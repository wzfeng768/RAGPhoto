ABSTRACT: For semiconducting polymers, such as regioregular poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (r-P3HT), the molecular weight has been corrlated to charge carrer feld-efect mobilities, surface morphology, and gelation rates in solution and therefore has important implications for long-term reliability, manufacturing, and future applications of electronic organic thin flms. In this work, we show that the molecular weight rr-P3HT in organic solar cels can also signifcantly change the internal cohesion of the photoactive layer using micromechanical testing techniques. Cohesive values ranged from ${ \sim } 0 . 5$ to $\sim 1 7 \mathrm { J } \mathrm { m } ^ { - 2 } ,$ following the general trend of greater cohesion with increasing molecular weight. Using nanodynamic mechanical analysis, we atribute the increase in cohesion to increased plasticity which helps dissipate the applied energy. Finally, we correlate photovoltaic effciency with cohesion to assess the device physics pertinent to optimizing device reliability. This research elucidates the fundamental parameters which affect both the mechanical stability and efficiency of polymer solar cell.

I t is well-known that the molecular weight of a polymer can significantly affect its physical, thermomechanical, and electrical properties. For semiconducting polymers, such as regioregular poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (rr-P3HT), which are used as active layer materials in bulk heterojunction (BHJ) solar cells, the molecular weight $\left( M _ { \mathrm { w } } \right)$ has been correlated to carrier field-effect mobilities, surface morphology, and gelation rates in solution.1-4 This has important implications for power conversion efficiency (PCE), large-scale manufacturing, and future large-scale applications of polymer-based organic electronics. For example, state-of-the-art rll-t-roll manufactured rolled sheets of organic solar cells (OsCs) allow for automated placement and replacement in large-scale solar parks.' Maintaining high values of internal mechanical cohesion along with optimized PCE's are naturally important for these applications. In this work, we show that the $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ of rr-P3HT in OSCs can significantly change the internal cohesion of the active layer even for relatively small increases in $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ By measuring the PCE of the resulting OSCs fabricated with rrP3HT of varying $M _ { w }$ we show how optimized combinations of PCE and cohesion can be achieved.

Recent reports on the mechanical reliability of BHJ layers in OSCs have revealed relatively low cohesion and/or adhesion values ranging from ${ \sim } 1$ to $2 ~ \mathrm { ~ J ~ } ~ \mathrm { m } ^ { - 2 }$ 8-10 By optimizing processing conditions, annealing temperature and time, and thin-film stacking order, cohesion and adhesion could increase to ${ \sim } 2 { - } 5 \mathrm { J } \mathrm { m } ^ { - 2 }$ 8-10 In this research, we demonstrate that active layer cohesion values as high as ${ \sim } 1 7 \mathrm { J } \mathrm { m } ^ { - 2 }$ can be achieved by increasing the $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ of rr-P3HT and adjusting active layer thickness. This dramatic increase is explained through a greater degree of plastic deformation in the BHJ layer during cohesive failure. For rr-P3HT, increased plasticity is realized through interchain $\pi - \pi$ stacking for the crystalline segments increasing the number of van der Waals bonds needed to be broken for chain disentanglement. In addition, more interlamellar amorphous regions with chain folds and tie molecules allow for polymer chain entanglements which would increase frictional molecular pullout during cohesive failure and may even require polymer chain scission.-13 This provides an opportunity to improve and optimize an important mechanical property that affects the reliability of the solar cell during fabrication and in-service.

For all devices, mechanical failure occurred within the BHJ layer as confirmed by elemental analysis of the cohesive surfaces with X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) which revealed an elemental composition of ${ \sim } 9 0 \%$ C, ${ \sim } 8 \% \ S$ and ${ \sim } 2 \%$ O (Table S3, Supporting Information). It is clear from Figure 1a that at the same BHJ layer thickness the $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ of $\mathrm { _ { r r } }$ -P3HT had a marked effect on the cohesive energy of the layer. Moreover, particularly at the higher $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ the cohesive energy was observed to increase markedly with increasing BHJ layer thickness.

The significant change in cohesive energy of the BHJ layer with $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ was most likely due to significant plasticity and viscoelasticity at the crack tip. Plastic deformation would also explain the thickness dependence on cohesive energy that we observed for OSCs made with ${ \ge } 5 3$ kDa rr-P3HT. By increasing the thickness of the BHJ layer, the plastic zone surrounding the crack tip in the BHJ layer can expand to a larger volume before being constrained by the stiff top and bottom glass substrates. In addition, studies have shown that the ultimate tensile stress, $\sigma _ { \mathrm { U T S } } ,$ for films of rr-P3HT exhibits an increase from 4 to 10 MPa for $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ of 22 and $1 0 2 \mathrm { \ k D a } ,$ respectively.14 The strain to failure was also observed to increase from $1 3 \%$ to $1 4 5 \%$ respectively, indicative of increased plastic deformation under tension.

The more pronounced sawtooth-like features in the load vs displacement plot (Figure 1b) for OSCs made with ${ \geq } 5 3 \ \mathrm { k D a }$ rr-P3HT are indicative of a time-dependent viscoelastic relaxation process at the crack tip. As loading occurs, a cohesive viscoelastic zone develops at the crack tip. At a critical load, the cohesive zone ruptures and the crack propagates rapidly with a corresponding drop in the applied load (Figure 1c). As the load drops, the crack slows and eventually arrests. On continued loading, the cohesive zone re-forms (along with crack tip blunting) until the instability is again reached and the crack propagates. The time-dependent viscoelastic properties together with the loading rate employed lead to the characteristic sawtooth features. This mechanism has been observed in other polymer systems such as poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA).1516 This is in contrast to OSCs made with $2 8 \ \mathrm { k D a }$ rr-P3HT, where we see a relatively flat load plateau indicating pure cohesive failure with no significant plasticity.

Nanodynamic mechanical analysis (nDMA) was employed on films of rr-P3HT: $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 0 } \mathrm { B M }$ to provide an indication of the viscoelastic behavior by measuring the storage modulus, $E ^ { \prime }$ , loss modulus, $E ^ { \prime \prime }$ , and loss tangent, tan $\delta ,$ for the varying BHJ films at room temperature. Increased molecular connectivity and associated viscoelasticity with increasing $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ would be more apparent under these conditions as revealed by molecular dynamics of polymers under tension." .18,19 However, it is interesting to note the increase in storage modulus as we decrease the $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ of rrP3HT. This may be indicative of the more crystalline nature of lower Mw rr-P3HT.20,21

To understand the effect of $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ on viscoelasticity and plasticity which ultimately affect cohesion, we need to understand how it affects the polymer on the molecular level. We know that for rr-P3HT, as $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ increases, the polymer chains are more likely to fold back on themselves, forming crystalline lamellae via $\pi - \pi$ stacking.2 Additionally, bridging between ordered crystalline domains of neighboring polymers via tie molecules is more likely to occur, thus increasing polymer chain interconnectivity.21 This bridging occurs more readily with increasing polymer $ { M _ { \mathrm { w } } } ^ { 2 1 , 2 2 }$ Studies utilizing transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) have confirmed that for $\mathrm { r r } { \mathrm { - } } \mathrm { P } 3 \mathrm { H T } { \mathrm { : } } \mathrm { P C } _ { 6 0 } \mathrm { B M }$ blends an increase in rr-P3HT $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ promotes interconnectivity at the molecular scale between polymer and crystalline domains for many different processing conditions. $2 , 4 , 2 2 - 2 5$ By increasing the degree of polymer chain folding and bridging, the layer becomes more resilient to chain pullout via greater degree of van der Waals interactions. Indeed, studies have shown that low $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ ${ < } 1 8$ kDa) rr-P3HT produce separated crystalline regions whereas higher $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ rr-P3HT produces crystalline regions connected by bridging polymer chains.13,22 Additionally, increased $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ raises the probability for interchain entanglements which would further resist chain pullout.26,27 These factors would describe why we see an increase in plasticity for OsCs made with progressively higher $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ rr-P3HT despite relatively similar viscoelastic properties for all films. A related phenomenon is well-known in bulk pure polymer materials like PMMA where increasing Mw results in an increase in cohesive fracture energy. 28

With increasing plasticity at the crack tip, we expect an increase in surface roughness after cohesive failure as polymer chains are stretched during pullout. By imaging the cohesive surfaces with optical microscopy (Figure 3a) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) (Figure 3b), we see significant differences in surface topography due to plastic deformation of the BHJ layer for OSCs made with 100 kDa rr-P3HT. The darker region is due to plastic deformation prior to rapid crack propagation which results in a localized region of thicker BHJ layer (more discussion in Supporting Information). OSCs made with low$M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ rr-P3HT produced relatively smooth, featureless cohesive surfaces (Figure S1, Supporting Information).

Finally, we investigated the correlation between PCE and cohesive energy. From Figure $4 \mathsf { a }$ we see that an optimal combination for the cohesion and PCE was apparent. These curves broaden with increasing rr-P3HT $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ A maximum PCE value was observed for the lowest rr-P3HT $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ This has been attributed to morphological traps of charge carriers due to increasing paracrystalinity with increasing $M _ { \mathrm { w } } \mathrm { \Omega } ^ { 2 1 }$ Figure 4b shows the dependence PCE dependence on BHJ thickness. The maximum PCE corresponded to BHJ layer thicknesses between ${ \sim } 6 0$ and $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ for these devices. As BHJ layers are made thicker, an increase in cohesion was observed $\left( > 2 8 \ \mathrm { k D a } \right)$ but with a steady decrease in PCE. This is due to a sharply declining short-circuit current $\left( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \right)$ (Table S4, Supporting Information). This has been attributed to charge carrier recombination processes. Because charge carriers have relatively short lifetimes due to a low dielectric constant of the organic polymer matrix, increasing the BHJ layer thickness means fewer carriers can be extracted before recombination.29,30 Below this optimum threshold, with decreasing BHJ layer thickness, we see a decline in PCE due to, again, a decrease in $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ However, the decrease in $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ is due to lower photon harvesting in such thin flms reducing overall charge carrier concentration.31 These results provide important insight into the trade-offs between making mechanically robust OSCs with high PCE which must be optimized prior to large-scale manufacturing.

In conclusion, we have demonstrated how tuning the $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ of rr-P3HT can greatly alter the cohesive energy in OSCs. In general, increasing the $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ increases intermolecular bonding and raises the probability for chain entanglement allowing for a greater degree of plastic deformation before cohesive failure can occur. Additionally, device performance was investigated, and it was determined that the most mechanically robust OsCs did not necessarily correspond to the most efficient. However, relatively high PCEs could be achieved with increases in cohesive energy of the BHJ layer. This presents significant opportunities that will impact large-scale manufacturing as studies have shown that device technologies with relatively low cohesive/adhesive energy $( < 5 \ \mathrm { ~ J ~ } \ \mathrm { m } ^ { - 2 } )$ become difficult to produce as mechanical failures can reduce production yield.8,32 As new semiconducting polymers are synthesized, balancing the electrical performance with mechanical reliability and ease of processing will remain a challenge.

# EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

OSC Preparation. Regioregular poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (rr-P3HT) (Rieke Metals) batches were used as received. All samples possessed a polydispersity index of ${ \sim } 2$ with a regioregularity of ${ > } 9 0 \%$ (Table S1, Supporting Information). All OSCs were fabricated on top of $5 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ of poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) covered indium tin oxide (ITO, $1 2 0 ~ \mathrm { { \ n m } }$ ) glass substrates. Devices for mechanical testing were built on $5 0 \ \mathrm { m m } \times 5 0 $ $\mathrm { m m \times 0 . 7 ~ m m ~ I T O / g l a s s }$ substrates. All other devices used $2 0 \mathrm { m m } \times$ $2 0 \ \mathrm { m m } \times 0 . 7 \ \mathrm { m m \ I T O / g l a s } .$ s substrates. The BHJ layer, consisting of rr-P3HT and phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester $( \mathrm { P C } _ { 6 0 } \mathrm { \bar { B } M } ,$ Solenne), was spun-cast from solutions with the solvent chlorobenzene at $6 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and allowed to dry for ${ \sim } 1 2 \ \mathrm { h }$ Solution concentrations and spin-casting parameters are in Table S2 (Supporting Information). Electrodes made of $7 \ \mathrm { n m }$ of Ca and $1 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ of Al were thermally evaporated onto the BHJ layer. The active area for current-voltage tested OSCs was $0 . 1 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { \tilde { 2 } }$

Adhesion/Cohesion Testing. Solar cells used for cohesion testing were further processed to produce four point bend (FPB) specimens as previously described.8,10 The FPB specimens were loaded under displacement control with a displacement rate of $0 . 2 5 \ \mu \mathrm { m } / s$ and moment arm, $L ,$ of $6 . 5 ~ \mathrm { m m }$ using a micromechanical testing machine

(DTS Delaminator, DTS). The load vs displacement curve for the FPB specimens was then taken and the critical load for stable crack growth, $P _ { \mathrm { c } }$ was then extracted. This allowed us to calculate the cohesive energy, $G _ { c }$ as the critical value of the strain energy release rate:32,33

$$
G _ { \mathrm { c } } = { \frac { 2 1 P _ { \mathrm { c } } ^ { 2 } L ^ { 2 } } { 1 6 b ^ { 2 } h ^ { 3 } E ^ { \prime } } }
$$

where $E ^ { \prime } , b ,$ and $h$ are the specimens plain strain modulus, width, and half-height, respectively. A schematic is given in the Supporting Information (Figure S3). For unstable crack growth (sawtooth pattern observed), $G _ { \mathrm { c } }$ was calculated by

$$
G _ { \mathrm { c } } = \sqrt { G _ { \mathrm { i } } G _ { \mathrm { a } } }
$$

where $G _ { \mathrm { i } }$ is the $G _ { \mathrm { c } }$ value at initiation (crest of sawtooth) and $G _ { \mathrm { a } }$ is the $G _ { \mathrm { c } }$ value at arrest (bottom of sawtooth).34,3s The cohesion values reported are taken from the average of at least three OsCs.

Surface Analysis. The BHJ layer thickness was measured with a profilometer (Dektak $^ { 1 5 0 + }$ Surface Profiler, Veeco). A scratch was induced on the portion of the OsCs without $\mathrm { C a } / \mathrm { A l }$ layer to give a combined thickness of PEDOT:PSS and BHJ layer. The average thickness of the PEDOT:PSs layer was subtracted from this measurement. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) (PHI VersaProbe XPS Microprobe) was performed on cohesive surfaces to identify failure pathways. X-ray beam spot was $1 0 0 \times 1 0 0 \ \mu \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 }$ with detection angle of $3 5 ^ { \circ }$ . Elemental composition was determined from resultant spectrum. Noncontact atomic force microscopy (AFM) (XE-70, Park Systems) was used to characterize surface topography of the cohesive surfaces and to obtain root-mean-squared roughness $( R _ { \mathrm { q } } )$

Nano-Dynamic Mechanical Analysis. Nano-dynamic mechanical analysis (nDMA) was performed using a Berkovich diamond tip with a nanoindentor (TI 750 Ubi, Hysitron) on specially prepared films of $\mathrm { P 3 H T : P C } _ { 6 0 } \mathrm { B M }$ ${ \sim } 4 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ Table S2). Tests utilized a quasistatic load of $5 0 \mu \mathrm { N }$ with a dynamic load of $5 \mu \mathrm { N }$ to get a displacement amplitude of ${ \sim } 1 { - } 2 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ This lead to a contact depth of less than 100 nm. Frequency sweep tests were ran from 10 to $2 0 0 ~ \mathrm { H z }$ Tests were performed in ambient conditions, and at least 30 tests were performed on each sample.

Current-Voltage Measurements. Photovoltaic performance was assessed via current-voltage measurements taken in a glovebox where the devices were fabricated. OSCs were annealed at $8 \bar { 5 } ~ ^ { \circ } C$ for $3 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ before current-voltage measurements were taken. A source meter (Keithley 2400) and solar simulator (Spectra-Physics 91160- 1000) were used to take current-voltage readings under 1 sun (AM 1.5G) using an NREL certified KG-5 filtered silicon diode.
