# A Narrow-Bandgap n-Type Polymer Semiconductor Enabling Efficient All-Polymer Solar Cells

Shengbin Shi, Peng Chen, Yao Chen, Kui Feng, Bin Liu, Jianhua Chen, Qiaogan Liao, Bao Tu, Jiasi Luo, Mengyao Su, Han Guo, Myung-Gil Kim, Antonio Facchetti, and Xugang Guo\*

Currently, n-type acceptors in high-performance all-polymer solar cells (all-PsCs) are dominated by imide-functionalized polymers, which typically show medium bandgap. Herein, a novel narrow-bandgap polymer, poly(5,6-dicyano-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole-alt-indacenodithiophene) (DCNBT-IDT), based on dicyanobenzothiadiazole without an imide group is reported. The strong electron-withdrawing cyano functionality enables DCNBT-IDT with n-type character and, more importantly, alleviates the steric hindrance associated with typical imide groups. Compared to the benchmark poly(naphthalene diimide-alt-bithiophene) (N2200), DCNBT-IDT shows a narrower bandgap (1.43 eV) with a much higher absorption coefficient $( 6 . 7 5 \times 7 0 ^ { 4 } \ : \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ . Such properties are elusive for polymer acceptors to date, eradicating the drawbacks inherited in N2200 and other high-performance polymer acceptors. When blended with a wide-bandgap polymer donor, the DCNBT-IDT-based all-PSCs achieve a remarkable power conversion efficiency of $8 . 3 2 \%$ with a small energy loss of $\pmb { 0 . 5 3 \ e V }$ and a photoresponse of up to ${ 8 7 0 } \ \mathsf { n m }$ . Such efficiency greatly outperforms those of N2200 $( 6 . 7 3 \% )$ and the naphthalene diimide (NDI)-based analog NDI-IDT $( 2 . 1 9 \% )$ . This work breaks the long-standing bottlenecks limiting materials innovation of n-type polymers, which paves a new avenue for developing polymer acceptors with improved optoelectronic properties and heralds a brighter future of all-PSCs.

Organic solar cells (OsCs) have achieved long-term and consistent advance with remarkable power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of greater than $1 6 \%$ obtained in nonfullerene solar cells, recently.[1-3] Among various types of OsCs, allpolymer solar cells (all-PsCs) consisting of polymers as both donor and acceptor semiconductors show pronounced advantages over other types of OsCs in terms of mechanical flexibility and remarkable device stability.[4-10] However, the PCEs of all-PSCs still lag behind those of OSCs based on fullerenes and nonfullerene small molecule acceptors, which is mainly due to the lack of high-performance n-type polymer semiconductors. So far, very few polymer acceptors can yield PCEs of ${ > } 8 \%$ in all-PSCs, and these polymers are mainly based on imide-functionalized (hetero) arenes, such as naphthalene diimide (NDI), perylene diimide (PDI), bithiophene imide (BTI), and their derivatives (Figure 1a).[8,11-13] In addition to the imidefunctionalized polymers, a new class of polymer acceptors based on 3,9-bis(2- methylene-(3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)- indanone))-5,5,11,11-tetrakis(4-hexylphenyl)-dithieno[2,3-d:2',3' d']-s-indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b'ldithiophene (ITIC)  derivatives is also developed, which enable promising all-PsC performance (Figure 1b).[14,15] However, these polymers are not $\pi$ conjugated along polymeric backbone, showing highly comparable absorption with the building blocks, i.e., ITIC derivatives, which should limit their usage in all-PsCs. The results demonstrate the unique status of imide-functionalized $\pi$ conjugated polymers for enabling state-of-the-art all-PSCs.

Despite the great successes of the NDI- and PDI-based acceptor polymers, their backbones are twisted due to their congested $\pi$ cores and the extruding carbonyl groups on the imide moiety, which enlarges optical bandgap, lowers absorption coefficient, and restrains charge carrier transport capacity,[6,8] thus limiting photocurrents and PCEs in all-PSCs.8,16,17] The relatively wide bandgap and small absorption coefficient are the long-standing bottlenecks, which constrain all-PSC device performance. Figure 1 summarizes the representative high-performance polymer acceptors enabling PCEs over $8 \%$ in all-PSCs reported in literature together with their optical bandgap $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } } )$ and absorption coefficient (s).[9,12,15,18,19] Recently, with the extensive efforts dedicated to polymer design and device optimization, Huang and co-workersl12,20] and Jenekhe and co-workers[9] have achieved all-PsC performance with PCE greater than $10 \%$ using poly(naphthelene diimide-alt-bithiophene) (N2200) and BSs10 as the acceptor, respectively (Figure 1a). However, most of the NDI- and PDI-based polymer acceptors exhibit e values about $3 . 0 { - } 4 . 8 \times 1 0 ^ { 4 } ~ \mathrm { c m ^ { - 1 } }$ , which are greatly lower than those $( 1 . 0 { - } 1 . 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \ \mathrm { c m ^ { - } } )$ of typical nonfullerene small molecules.13,21] A very recent study has shown that the imide-functionalized polymer f-BTi3-T (Figure 1a) can attain a PCE of $8 . 9 8 \%$ but with a wide $E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } }$ of $1 . 7 7 ~ \mathrm { e V . } ^ { [ 1 9 ] }$ Such a wide bandgap is not beneficial to photocurrent; hence, developing new polymer acceptors with narrower bandgap and increased absorption coefficient is imminent for further improving all-PsC performance, which relies on the invention of new electron-deficient building blocks with optimized molecular geometry and high electron affinity.

Among various electron-withdrawing functionalities, the cyano (CN) group is the strongest one and has been widely employed to lower the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy level of small molecules, such as cyano-functionalized air-stable n-type rylene diimides[22-24] and quinoidal type fused n-type semiconductors.[25,26] In addition, the CN group has an exceptional large dipole moment,[27] which can favor charge separation in bulk heterojunction blend films.28,29] Benzothiadiazole (BT) and its derivatives were also widely utilized as electron-deficient building blocks for constructing donor-acceptor (D-A) type copolymers for OSC applications.30-33] Nevertheless, these D-A type polymers typically functioned as p-type donor semiconductors owing to their relatively high-lying LUmO levels. Heeney and co-workers[34-37] recently reported a novel cyano-substituted electron-deficient building block 4,7-di(thiophen-2-yl)-5,6-dicyano-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole (DTDCNBT), and the DTDCNBT-based polymers show much suppressed frontier molecular orbital (FMO) energy level compared to their fluorine-substituted analogs owing to the strong electron-withdrawing capability of the cyano group. When applied in OSCs, the DTDCNBT-based polymers typically function as p-type electron-donating materials with a promising PCE $( 6 . 5 \% )$ achieved.35,37] Inspired by the pioneering works from Heeney and co-workers, our group[38,39] modified the DTDCNBT chemical structure and reported a cyano-substituted acceptor 5,6-dicyano-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole (DCNBT) without flanking thiophene moieties. The removal of the electron-rich thiophenes suppresses p-type performance and improves n-type characteristic. Hence, the DCNBT-based copolymers with bithiophene or bifuran donor co-units exhibited greatly lowered LUMO levels below $- 4 . 3 \mathrm { e V }$ and achieved substantial $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } } \mathrm { s }$ $( 0 . 3 - 0 . 5 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ in organic thin-film transistors (OTFIs).[38,39] Moreover, the incorporation of cyano groups into organic small molecules yielded the unprecedented fuse-ring electron acceptors (FREAs),[3,40] which dramatically changed the field of OSCs. These results clearly demonstrate the high efficacy of cyanofunctionalized (hetero)arenes for promoting n-type performance of the resulting semiconductors in organic solar cells.

Driven by the strong electron-withdrawing capability of the cyano groups and the remarkable success of benzothiadiazole-based polymers, herein we synthesized a new n-type polymer semiconductor poly(DCNBT-alt-indacenodithiophene) (DCNBT-IDT; Scheme 1a) by copolymerizing 5,6-dicyano-2,1,3- benzothiadiazole (DCNBT) with indacenodithiophene (IDT) and utilized it as the acceptor in all-psCs. Furthermore, the NDI-based analog poly(naphthelene diimide-alt-indacenodithiophene) (NDI-IDT) was also synthesized together with the benchmark n-type polymer $\mathrm { N } 2 2 0 0 ^ { [ 4 1 ] }$ to fabricate control cells for better understanding of structure-property correlations. In comparison to N2200, polymer DCNBT-IDT exhibits a narrower $E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } }$ of $1 . 4 3 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and a higher absorption coefficient of $6 . 1 5 \times 1 0 ^ { 4 } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ Both DCNBT-IDT and NDI-IDT show comparable $E _ { \mathrm { o p t } }$ (1.42-1.43 eV) but polymer DCNBT-IDT has a higher absorption coefficient, especially in the near-infrared region compared to the NDI-containing polymer NDI-IDT in film state. When combined with the well-known widebandgap polymer poly[(2,6-(4,8-bis(5-(2-ethylhexyl)thiophen-2-yl)- benzo[1,2-b:4,5- $b ^ { \prime }$ |dithiophene))-alt-(5,5- $^ { 1 ^ { \prime } , 3 ^ { \prime } }$ -di-2-thienyl- $. 5 ^ { \prime }$ $7 ^ { \prime }$ bis(2-ethylhexyl)benzo[ $1 ^ { \prime } , 2 ^ { \prime } { \cdot } c { : } 4 ^ { \prime } , 5 ^ { \prime } { \cdot } c ^ { \prime } ]$ dithiophene-4,8-dione)] (PBDB-T) (Scheme 1b) as the donor material, the DCNBT-IDTbased all-PsCs yielded an excellent photovoltaic performance with an optimal PCE of up to $8 . 3 2 \%$ along with a high opencircuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ of $0 . 9 0 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ which greatly outperforms both NDI-IDT and N2200-based all-PSCs showing PCE values of $2 . 1 9 \%$ and $6 . 1 3 \%$ , respectively. Photoresponse up to $8 7 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ was observed for the DCNBT-IDT-based cells. More importantly, the energy loss $( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } )$ in the DCNBT-IDT cell is only $0 . 5 3 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ which is among the smallest values reported to date for all-PSCs with efficiency $> 8 \%$ [5,7,16,42,43] Note, a PCE of $8 . 3 2 \%$ achieved by polymer acceptor DCNBT-IDT is also the highest reported value for all-PSCs except for those attained by imide-functionalized polymer acceptors and polymers based on ITIC derivatives, which indicates that incorporation of DCNBT into polymers paves a new avenue to develop n-type acceptor materials and also heralds a brighter future of all-PSCs owing to the improved optoelectronic properties of the DCNBT polymers.

The synthetic route to polymers DCNBT-IDT and NDI-IDT is depicted in Scheme 1a and the synthetic details are described in the Supporting Information. The dibrominated monomers DCNBT and NDI were copolymerized with the distannylated indacenodithiophene comonomer IDT via a Stille couplingbased polycondensation reaction under microwave irradiation to afford two new polymers DCNBT-IDT and NDI-IDT, respectively. After polymerization, the polymers were purified via Soxhlet extraction with different solvents. The number average molecular weights $( M _ { \mathrm { n } } \mathrm { s } )$ were determined using high-temperature gel permeation chromatography (GPC) at $1 5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and the $M _ { \mathrm { n } } \mathrm { s }$ are 59.7 and $7 9 . 5 \mathrm { \ k D a }$ for DCNBT-IDT and NDI-IDT, respectively, with a narrow polydispersity index (PDI) of 1.5-2.0 (Table 1). Both polymers show good solubility $( > 3 0 \ \mathrm { m g \ m L ^ { - 1 } } )$ in common organic solvents, such as chloroform, chlorobenzene (CB), and dichlorobenzene, which is beneficial to device fabrication and optimization. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA; Figure S3a, Supporting Information) of the polymers was carried out to investigate their thermal properties, and both DCNBT-IDT and NDI-IDT exhibited good thermal stability as evidenced by the high decomposition temperatures (the temperature with a $5 \%$ weight loss) of 426 and $4 4 2 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , respectively. Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC; Figure S3b, Supporting Information) measurement reveals that both DCNBT-IDT and NDI-IDT showed no obvious thermal transition, likely due to their limited crystallinity.

The normalized UV-vis absorption spectra of polymers in solution and film state are shown in Figure 2a, and the absorption coefficient in film state is displayed in Figure 2b. The corresponding data are summarized in Table 1. As shown, both DCNBT-IDT and NDI-IDT exhibit two distinct absorption peaks. The one located at ${ \approx } 4 1 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ is attributed to the $\pi - \pi ^ { \ast }$ transition of the polymer, while the other one around $7 2 0 { - } 7 7 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ corresponds to the intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) between the donor and acceptor units. Based on the temperature-dependent absorption measurements (Figure S4, Supporting Information), increasing the temperature minimally impacts both the absorption maximum $( \lambda _ { \mathrm { m a x } } )$ and absorption onset $( \lambda _ { \mathrm { o n s e t } } )$ for DCNBTIDT and NDI-IDT, which indicates that both polymers are not strongly aggregated in solution at room temperature. In addition, polymers DCNBT-IDT and NDI-IDT show similar absorption range in both solution and film, likely attributed to the fact that DCNBT and NDI have comparable electron deficiency, resulting in comparable ICT characteristics for the polymers. From solution to film, DCNBT-IDT exhibits an obvious redshift $\left( 3 4 \mathrm { n m } \right)$ of $\lambda _ { \operatorname* { m a x } } ,$ and the $\lambda _ { \mathrm { o n s e t } }$ in the film state is $8 6 6 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ leading to a narrow optical bandgap $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } } )$ of $1 . 4 3 \ \mathrm { e V } .$ In comparison, NDI-IDT displays a similar bathochromically shifted $\lambda _ { \operatorname* { m a x } }$ of ${ \approx } 3 7 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ going from solution to film state, and a slightly narrower $E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } }$ of $1 . 4 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ is derived from the $\lambda _ { \mathrm { o n s e t } }$ of NDI-IDT polymer film. Please note that the $\lambda _ { \mathrm { m a x } }$ of DCNBT-IDT is substantially redshifted by $6 2 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ compared to that of the benchmark polymer acceptor N2200 in film state (Figure 2b). The $E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } }$ of DCNBT-IDT $( 1 . 4 3 \mathrm { e V } )$ is smaller than that of N2200 (1.47 eV), which should be beneficial for light absorption in solar cells.

The most significant absorption feature of the new polymer DCNBT-IDT is the maximum absorption coefficient of

$6 . 1 5 \times 1 0 ^ { 4 } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ (Figure 2b) in film state, which is remarkably higher than that of NDI-IDT $( 3 . 1 7 \times 1 0 ^ { 4 } ~ \mathrm { { c m } ^ { - 1 } ) }$ and the benchmark polymer acceptor N2200 $( 3 . 4 8 ~ \times ~ 1 0 ^ { 4 } ~ \mathrm { { \ c m ^ { - 1 } } ) }$ ,likely originating from the higher backbone planarity of polymer DCNBT-IDT (Figure 2d,e, vide infra). It should be noted that the absorption coefficient of DCNBT-IDT is among the highest in polymer acceptors with $E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } } < 1 . 5 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and $\mathrm { P C E } > 8 \%$ . These results underscore that the DCNBT is a very promising building block to construct polymer acceptors with narrow bandgap and high absorption coefficient. In addition, DCNBT-IDT exhibits complementary absorption with that of the donor polymer PBDB-T, which ensures good harvesting of solar light from visible to near infrared region (Figure 2a).

The FMO energy levels of all polymers were determined by cyclic voltammetry (Figure S5, Supporting Information). Based on the reduction and oxidation onset potentials of the cyclic voltammograms, the LUMO energy levels $( E _ { \mathrm { L U M O } } \mathrm { s } )$ and highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels $\left( E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } ^ { \mathrm { S } } } \right)$ were determined to be $- 3 . 5 3 / - 5 . 3 3$ , 3.75/-5.59, $- 3 . 7 3 / - 5 . 5 6$ and $- 3 . 7 7 / - 5 . 7 8 \mathrm { e V }$ for PBDB-T,[44] DCNBT-IDT, NDI-IDT, and N2200, respectively (Figure 2c). The $E _ { \mathrm { L U M O } }$ of NDI-IDT is slightly higher $( 0 . 0 2 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ than that of DCNBT-IDT, while the $E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } }$ of DCNBT-IDT is slightly lower $( 0 . 0 3 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ versus NDI-IDT. These results indicate that DCNBT moiety is also a strongly electron-deficient building block for constructing n-type semiconductors, which is comparable to the widely used NDI unit. In comparison with the benchmark polymer N2200, which has achieved the record performance in all-PsCs,[12,16] DCNBT-IDT shows slightly higher-lying LUMO level, which should be beneficial to enlarging $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ in the DCNBT-IDT-based solar cells.

To gain further insights into the FMO energy levels and polymer chain geometries, the density function theory (DFT)-based calculations were carried out at the DFT//B3LYP/ 6-31G(d, p) level (Figure 2d,e; Figures S6 and S7, Supporting Information). Figure 2d,e reveals that there is a dihedral angle of $2 3 . 2 ^ { \circ }$ between DCNBT and IDT in polymer DCNBT-IDT. However, a much higher degree of backbone torsion with a calculated dihedral angle of about $3 5 . 5 ^ { \circ }$ was found between NDI and IDT in polymer NDI-IDT, which was attributed to the greater steric demand of the NDI unit compared to DCNBT. Metastable states were found at $1 5 5 ^ { \circ }$ and $1 3 0 ^ { \circ }$ for DCNBT and NDI containing trimers, respectively, and the energy barriers of 5.32 and $2 . 0 0 \ \mathrm { k c a l \ m o l ^ { - 1 } }$ were needed for the conversion of conformers from the energy minima to the metastable state for the DCNBT- and NDI-containing structures, respectively. The larger energy barrier of DCNBT structure suggests that the resulting DCNBT-IDT polymer will likely exhibit a more planar backbone and improved $\pi - \pi$ stacking compared to the NDI-based polymer NDI-IDT. Additionally, on the basis of the calculated orbital spatial distributions (Figure S7, Supporting Information), DCNBT-IDT shows a more delocalized HOMO and LUmO along polymer backbone, which should be beneficial to intrachain electron transport.

The photovoltaic performance of all-PSCs based on DCNBTIDT, NDI-IDT, and the control N2200 as the acceptor material was investigated by blending them with the well-known high-performance donor polymer PBDB-T (Scheme 1b),[45,46] which exhibits complementary absorption and matched energy levels with the polymer acceptors. The device with a conventional architecture of indium tin oxide (ITO)/poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS)/active layer/perylene diimide functionalized with amino N-oxide (PDINO)/Al was employed in this study, where PEDOT:PSS and PDINO served as the hole- and electron-transporting layers,47] respectively. Through systematic device optimizations (see Tables S1-S3, Supporting Information), it was found that a polymer donor:polymer acceptor ratio of 1:1 (wt:wt) and using CB as the processing solvent together with a conventional device configuration enhanced photovoltaic performance for all blends. By employing $1 \%$ volume ratio of 1,8-diiodooctane (DIO) as the processing additive and thermal annealing treatment could further boost the device performance. The current density-voltage $\left( J - W \right)$ characteristics of the bestperforming all-PSC devices are presented in Figure 3a and the corresponding photovoltaic performance parameters are summarized in Table 2. It was found that the all-PSCs based on DCNBT-IDT yielded an optimal PCE of up to $8 . 3 2 \%$ which is $\approx$ four times higher than that of the cell based on NDI-containing polymer analog NDI-IDT $( 2 . 1 9 \% )$ and also much higher than that of the cell based on N2200 as the acceptor $( 6 . 1 3 \% )$ The PCE increment is mainly attributed to the great enhancement of short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ . To the best of our knowledge, the $8 . 3 2 \%$ PCE is the highest reported to date for all-PsCs except for those from the most studied imide-based polymers and the polymers based on the ITIC derivatives.l7l In addition to the higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $1 4 . 2 0 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ (vs $1 0 . 9 4 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ the DCNBT-IDT-based devices also achieved a larger open-circuit voltage of $0 . 9 0 \mathrm { V }$ (vs 0.86 V) than the N2200-based all-PSCs, which is in good agreement with their LUMO levels. According to the equation: $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } = E _ { \mathrm { g } } - e V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ where $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ is the energy loss, the DCNBT-IDT and NDI-IDT-based solar cells showed $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } \mathrm { \bf S }$ of only 0.53 and $0 . 4 7 \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively. Such small $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } \mathrm { \bf S }$ achieved in this work are mainly attributed to the small energy offsets (both the HOMO-HOMO and LUMO-LUMO offsets, Figure 2c) between the donor and acceptor materials.[48,49] Please note that a state-of-the-art PCE and a small energy loss can be simultaneously realized in all-PSC field for the DCNBT-IDT-based devices, which demonstrates that the new electron-deficient building block DCNBT has great potentials for enabling n-type polymers with high efficiency and small $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ which have been elusive in all-PSCs to date.

For better understanding the DCNBT-IDT, NDI-IDT, and N2200-based all-PSC performances, the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of the best-performing cells were recorded. As shown in Figure 3b, for the NDI-IDT and N2200-based devices, there is a sharp EQE drop in the range of $6 5 0 { - } 8 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ which has been seen in all other high-performance all-PSCs containing NDI- or PDI-based polymer acceptors with a widebandgap polymer donor.[12,50,51] Remarkably, the DCNBT-IDTbased devices exhibit much higher EQE values, approaching $5 0 \%$ from 650 to $8 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ owing to the good absorption of this polymer acceptor in the region. The higher EQE values can be attributed to the distinctive advantages of this dicyanobenzothiadiazole-based polymer, DCNBT-IDT, which include a higher absorption coefficient and more ordered packing (vide infra). Furthermore, the DCNBT-IDT-based all-PSCs show a long-range photoresponse of up to $8 7 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . As a result, the all-PSCs achieve a substantially higher integrated $J _ { \mathrm { c a l } }$ value of $1 3 . 9 1 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ than that of the NDI-IDT-based $( 3 . 6 8 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ and N2200-based $( 1 0 . 5 2 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ cells. Please note that the deviations between the integrated $J _ { \mathrm { c a l } }$ values from EQE spectra and the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ values from $J { - } V$ characteristics are smaller than $3 \%$ demonstrating a good reliability of the results.

The photoluminescence (PL) measurements were carried out to evaluate the exciton dissociation efficiencies at the donor:acceptor interfaces, and the excitation wavelength of $5 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ is used for all the pure PBDB-T film, PBDB-T:DCNBTIDT blend film, and PBDB-T:NDI-IDT blend film. As shown in Figure 3c, after blended with the acceptor polymers, both PBDB-T:DCNBT-IDT and PBDB-T:NDI-IDT films exhibit a much lower PL intensity compared to the pure PBDB-T film. The greater PL quenching efficiency of $8 5 \%$ for the PBDBT:DCNBT-IDT film (vs the $7 5 \%$ quenching efficiency for the PBDB-T:NDI-IDT film) is in good agreement with its high $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ in solar cell devices, which is attributed to the more intermixing of donor and acceptor materials in blend film as revealed by morphology characterization (vide infra). The space charge limited current (SCLC) method was then utilized to study the charge carrier transport properties of both PBDB-T:DCNBTIDT and PBDB-T:NDI-IDT active layers. The fabrication details for the single carrier devices are reported in the Supporting Information. The $J ^ { 1 / 2 } – V$ curves measured under the dark condition for the hole-only and electron-only devices based on the optimized blend films are shown in Figure S8, Supporting Information, and the calculated hole/electron mobilities are summarized in Table S4, Supporting Information. It was found that both blend films display comparable hole mobility $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h , S C L C } } )$ of $2 . 6 0 \ \times \ 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \bar { \mathrm { V } } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for PBDB-T:NDI-IDT and $1 . 6 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for PBDB-T:DCNBT-IDT. In order to further probe exciton dissociation, charge extraction, and charge recombination in the DCNBT-IDT- and NDIIDT-based solar cells, photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ as a function of the effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ was first measured to study the exciton dissociation and charge extraction kinetics processes. $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ is defined as $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } = J _ { \mathrm { L } } - J _ { \mathrm { D } } ,$ where $J _ { \mathrm { L } }$ is defined as the current density under illumination and $J _ { \mathrm { D } }$ is defined as the current density in dark.[53] $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ is defined as $V _ { 0 } - V _ { \mathrm { a } }$ where $V _ { 0 }$ is denoted as the applied voltage when the value of $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ is equal to zero and $V _ { \mathrm { a } }$ is the applied voltage. The $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ versus $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ characteristics are shown in Figure 3d. It is clear that higher $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ values were recorded for the DCNBT-IDT-based solar cells, partially originating from the improved DCNBT-IDT light harvesting and exciton generation characteristics of this polymer. However, the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \mathrm { s }$ of both DCNBT-IDT and NDI-IDT-based devices exhibit no clear saturation $\left( J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } \right)$ under a high $V _ { \mathrm { a } }$ condition, especially for the NDI-IDT-based cells. The $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ of the PBDB-T:NDI-IDT film even shows a dramatic increase when $V _ { \mathrm { a } }$ is above $3 \mathrm { V } ,$ indicating a significant charge recombination in this blend.[52,54] Therefore, a larger $V _ { \mathrm { a } }$ is required to dissociate all the photogenerated charge carriers before recombination in the PBDB-T:NDI-IDT film. More severe charge recombination in the NDI-IDT containing all-PSCs results in a lower $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ in comparison with the DCNBT-IDT containing devices and thus leads to an inferior PCE.

Light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ -dependent $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ characteristics were subsequently measured to study the main charge recombination mechanism in our devices. The correlation between the $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ is described as $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } = \alpha ( k _ { \mathrm { B } } T / q ) \mathrm { l n } ( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ , where $k _ { \mathrm { B } }$ represents Boltzmann constant, T refers to absolute temperature, and $q$ is the elementary charge. The slope factor $\alpha ( k _ { \mathrm { B } } T / q )$ of the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ versus $\ln ( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ plot can be used to study bimolecular recombination versus monomolecular and/or trap-assisted recombination at the open-circuit condition. The slope factors for the DCNBTIDT- and NDI-IDT-based solar cells are determined to be 1.16 and $1 . 2 4 k _ { \mathrm { B } } T / q$ (Figure 3e), respectively. The smaller slope factor of the DCNBT-IDT-based all-PSCs indicates that bimolecular recombination is the dominant recombination pathway and there is negligible unwanted recombination like trap-assisted recombination at the open-circuit condition, in accordance with the higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and improved device performance of DCNBT-IDTbased devices. Additionally, the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ versus $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ measurement was conducted to evaluate the bimolecular charge recombination, and the experimentally derived $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } { - } P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ curves are shown in Figure 3f. In OSCs, $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ showed a power-law dependence on light density $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \propto P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } { } ^ { \alpha } ( \alpha \leq 1 )$ . For an OSC with negligible bimolecular recombination, the $\alpha$ value is approaching 1. The $\alpha$ values were calculated to be 0.983 and 0.964 for DCNBTIDT- and NDI-IDT-based devices, respectively. Compared to the PBDB-T:NDI-IDT devices, PBDB-T:DCNBT-IDT devices display a higher $\alpha ,$ suggesting a lower degree of bimolecular recombination, which is in good accordance with the higher fill factor (FF) and $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ values of the DCNBT-IDT-based devices.

The larger domain sizes are attributed to the stronger aggregation of polymer NDI-IDT (vs polymer DCNBT-IDT), which is induced by the extended and rigid $\pi$ conjugated core of the NDI moiety in NDI-IDT. Such a large domain size plays a detrimental role in charge carrier generation and transport for the active layer. As a result, the PBDB-T:NDI-IDT-based all-PSCs exhibit greatly reduced device performance with respect to the PBDB-T:DCNBT-IDT-based devices. Nevertheless, it should be noted that due to low electron scattering contrast between polymer donor and polymer acceptor, the TEM measurements should be challenging to well distinguish the polymer donor and polymer acceptor regions in blend films,7l although the darker region is typically attributed to the polymer acceptor-rich domain.17,56] Therefore, further studies by utilizing advanced morphological characterization techniques such as resonant soft X-ray scattering (RSoXs)[57] and photoinduced force microscopy (PiFM)[58] are highly desired, while these techniques are currently not widely available.

Grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) measurements were performed to investigate the molecular packing and ordering properties of the pure and blend polymer films. The 2D GIwAXs images and the corresponding line-cut profiles in both in-plane (IP) and out-of-plane (OOP) directions are depicted in Figure 5, and Figure S9, Supporting Information, and the detailed packing parameters are summarized in Tables S5 and S6, Supporting Information. Based on the 2D GIWAXS images of the pure DCNBT-IDT and NDIIDT polymers (Figure 5a,b), both polymers exhibit preferential face-on orientation of the polymer chain with respect to the substrate, evidenced from the more obvious $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak (located at around $q _ { z } \approx 1 . 4 8 \mathrm { \ : \mathring { A } ^ { - 1 } }$ in the OOP directions. Both (100) and (200) peaks with a higher intensity can be observed in the OOP direction for DCNBT-IDT, while the NDI-IDT only shows an arch-like (100) peak. The above results indicate that the polymer DCNBT-IDT is more textured compared with the polymer NDI-IDT. Moreover, $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances based on the scattering peak in out-of-plane (010) direction were found to be 4.25 and 4.22 A for DCNBT-IDT and NDI-IDT (Table S5, Supporting Information), respectively. Such a large $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance could be attributed to the out-of-plane orientation of the solubilizing chains on IDT. On the other hand, polymer DCNBT-IDT shows a narrower distributed (010) $\pi - \pi$ scattering peak in comparison with polymer NDI-IDT, indicating an improved $\pi - \pi$ stacking of polymer DCNBT-IDT.

After blending the new DCNBT-IDT (or NDI-IDT) acceptor polymer with the classical PBDB-T donor polymer, it can be found that the diffraction peaks originating from both the donor and acceptor polymers can be observed in the 2D GIWAXs images, and the blend films exhibit predominant face-on distributions of the polymer chains evidenced from the more pronounced (010) $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak located in the OOP direction compared to (010) stacking peak in the IP direction. For the PBDB-T:DCNBT-IDT blend, the (100) peak in the OOP direction contributed from the acceptor polymer DCNBT-IDT can be clearly observed, while this peak cannot be observed from the PBDB-T:NDI-IDT blend. This result suggests the maintenance of crystalline structure of polymer DCNBT-IDT in the blend, which will promote exciton dissociation and charge transport. Furthermore, the PBDB-T:DCNBT-IDT film exhibits much stronger $\pi - \pi$ stacking (010) peaks compared to the PBDB-T:NDI-IDT film. The face-on distribution and higher crystallinity will promote the charge transport and collection in the PBDB-T:DCNBT-IDT-based devices, and these results match pretty well with the higher $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } , \mathrm { S C L C } }$ and larger $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of the PBDBT:DCNBT-IDT-based devices versus the PBDB-T:NDI-IDT-based ones. The results demonstrate that the higher backbone planarity and more ordered packing structure of the DCNBT-IDT are the key to the impressive photovoltaic performance.

In summary, we have successfully devised a new n-type polymer DCNBT-IDT based on the highly electron-deficient building block DCNBT without the typical imide group. The dicyanobenzothiadiazole (DCNBT) enables polymer DCNBT-IDT with low-positioned FMO energy levels; hence, the polymer can function as an electron acceptor in all-PSCs. More promisingly, DCNBT-IDT exhibits a higher degree of backbone planarity and stronger absorption coefficient compared with the NDI-based analog polymer NDI-IDT. Such properties are elusive for highperformance polymer acceptors in all-PSC field to date, breaking the long-standing bottlenecks of the benchmark n-type polymer N2200 as well as all other high-performance imide-functionalized polymer acceptors. When applied in all-PsCs, a state-ofthe-art PCE of $8 . 3 2 \%$ is attained for the DCNBT-IDT-based cells, the highest value reported to date for all-PSCs except for those based on imide-functionalized polymers and polymers based on ITIC derivatives. In marked contrast, the analog polymer NDI-IDT and benchmark N2200-based all-PSCs show much reduced PCEs of $2 . 1 9 \%$ and $6 . 1 3 \%$ , respectively. Moreover, the DCNBT-IDT-based all-PSCs yield a large $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 9 0 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ with a small $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of $0 . 5 3 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and a photoresponse of up to $8 7 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ The results clearly demonstrate that DCNBT-IDT is one of the most promising polymer acceptors, breaking the domination of imide-functionalized polymers in all-PsCs. Hence, introducing DCNBT into polymers offers a new avenue for developing highperformance polymer acceptors for all-PSCs.
