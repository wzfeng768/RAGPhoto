ABSTRACT: Three wide-band-gap donor-acceptor (D-A) type polymers, PBA-TX, PBA-T2, PBA-T3, and PBA-T4, based on alkoxy-substituted anthrabisthiadiazole (ATz) and benzodithiophene (BDT) structures, were synthesized and characterized for the development of high-performance semiconducting polymers in non-fullerene organic photovoltaics (OPVs). The synthesized polymers PBA-TX possess deep highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels of $- 5 . 4 { - } 5 . 5 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and a relatively wide band gap of $1 . 8 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ attributed to the introduction of the alkoxysubstituted $\mathbf { A T z }$ core with a weak electron-acceptor character into polymer backbones. Additionally, the bulky alkylthienyl side chains on the BDT core provide sufficient solubility and suppress the formation of lamellar tructures, enabling the construction of asutable face-on orientation in the solid state and high miscibility with representative low-band-gap small-molecular acceptor (SMA) Y6. The halogenation of the $\beta$ -position of alkylthienyl groups on the BDT core further reduced the HOMO and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels. Solar cels fabricated with halogen atom-substituted polymers PBA-T3 (chlorine) and PBA-T4 (fluorine) exhibited a higher open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ than cells based on the nonhalogenated polymer PBA-T2. Interestingly, ll PBA-TX/Y6 blended flms formed almost the same molecular order and phase separation structures. As a result, ll fabricated solar cell based on PBA-TX/Y6 systems showed a similar power conversion efficiency (PCE) of around $8 - 9 \%$ . One possible reason for the small differences observed may be attributed to the presence of an ATz framework in the polymer backbone, as its rigid and $\pi$ extended manner can provide sufficient strong intermolecular interaction. The subtle halogenation efect here proves advantageous for crafting halogen-free wide-band-gap semiconducting polymers in non-fullerene organic photovoltaics (OPVs), steering clear of intricate synthetic pathways.

# INTRODUCTION

Due to their significant advantages, including lightweight, semitransparency, high mechanical flexibility, low cost, and the potential for large-area solar panel production, organic photovoltaics (OPVs) have garnered considerable interest among chemists and physicists.-- Over the past 7 years, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of OPVs has reached $1 9 \%$ in single junction solar cells, thanks to the development of a wide variety of semiconducting polymers and small-molecular semiconductors.6-12 Notably, the contribution of low-bandgap small-molecular electron acceptors (SMAs) developed as soluble fullerene derivatives has been remarkable, significantly improving the PCE of OPVs.3-17 In recent years, nonfullerene OPVs, consisting of a wide-band-gap semiconducting polymer and a low-band-gap SMA, have gained widespread recognition as a promising approach for developing highperformance OPVs.13-20 For instance, the complementary absorption of two photoactive materials with different spectral ranges can efficiently harvest sunlight, leading to a high shortcircuit current density $\left( J _ { s c } \right)$ in OPVs. Moreover, diverse molecular designs to tune the electronic properties of polymer donors and SMAs can offer a high open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ and minimal energy loss $\left( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } \right)$ in fabricated non-fullerene OPVs. However, further development of high-performance materials is highly necessary for commercial applications, as the PCE of OPV modules and their lifetime are still relatively low.4,21,22

Synthesis of Alkoxy-Substituted ATz-Benzodithiophene (BDT) Copolymers PBA-TXs. The synthesis of the target $\mathbf { A T z }$ -based copolymers, PBA-TXs, in combination with a benzodithiophene (BDT) donor unit, is illustrated in Scheme 1. Hexyloxy ATz 1 was synthesized following our previously reported method (Scheme S1).30 The ATz derivative 4 was synthesized through dibromination with iron(III) chloride $\left( \mathrm { F e C l } _ { 3 } \right)$ and bromine, followed by Migita-Kosugi-Stille coupling with 4-(2-butyloctyl)-2-(trimethylstannyl)thiophene (3) in $2 8 \%$ yield over two steps (Figure S1). Subsequent dibromination of compound 4 with $N$ -bromosuccinimide (NBS) resulted in ATz monomer 5 in $9 2 \%$ yield (Figure S2). Three stannylated BDT monomers, 6a-6c, were prepared using a reported procedure (see Supporting Information, Scheme S2 and Figures S3-S6).40-43 Finally, Pd-catalyzed copolymerization, facilitated by copper(I) iodide (CuI)- mediated Migita-Kosugi-Stille coupling of 5 and the corresponding BDT monomers 6a-6c, produced the three target $\mathbf { A T z }$ -based polymers, PBA-T2, PBA-T3, and PBA-T4, in yields ranging from 68 to $9 2 \%$ , respectively (Figures $\mathrm { { S 7 - } }$ S9). All ATz-BDT copolymers exhibit solubility in common organic solvents, such as chloroform, toluene, chlorobenzene, and $^ o$ -dichlorobenzene. The solubility of PBA-T2, PBA-T3, and PBA-T4 in chloroform at room temperature is 4.2, 12.4, and $1 2 . 0 \ \mathrm { g } \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively. However, in the case of PATz4To6OD, it is completely insoluble in both chloroform and chlorobenzene at room temperature. Physicochemical Properties of PBA-TX. The ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption spectra of PBA-TXs in chlorobenzene solution and in the thin film are illustrated in Figure $^ { 2 \mathrm { a } , \mathrm { b } } ,$ and the extracted optical parameters are summarized in Table 1. In addition, the optical properties of PATz4T-o6OD are also shown in Figure 2a,b and Table 1 for comparison. In chlorobenzene solution at room temperature, both PBA-T2 and PATz4T-o6OD exhibited broad and strong absorption across the entire absorption region, with the main absorption maxima $\left( \lambda _ { \operatorname* { m a x } } \right)$ around $6 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure 2a). After heating at approximately $8 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ a $4 0 \ \mathrm { ~ n m }$ blue-shift in absorption was observed in both PBA-T2 and PATz4To6OD solutions, but distinct shoulder peaks around $6 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ remained evident (Figure 2a). This suggests that PBA-T2 has a partially disaggregated structure in the heated solution at around $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ similar to that of PATz4T-o6OD. From this result, despite having a higher solubility, PBA-T2 exhibits sufficiently strong aggregation ability for the construction of an ordered structure. One possible reason for both high solubility and strong aggregation may be the presence of the rigid and $\pi$ extended BDT framework and the sterically bulky alkylthienyl side chains. In thin films, PBA-T2 and PATz4T-o6OD displayed nearly identical and slightly red-shifted absorption spectra compared to their corresponding solutions, but their absorption shapes were distinctly different (Figure 2b). PBAT2 exhibited a spectrum with a broad single peak, whereas PATz4T-o6OD exhibited two sharp peaks at 570 and $6 0 8 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ assignable to the $_ { 0 - 1 }$ and $_ { 0 - 0 }$ transition bands, respectively. This indicates that PBA-T2 formed poorer molecular ordering compared to that of PATz4T-o6OD. The change in absorption shape is likely due to the more twisted molecular structure of PBA-T2 compared to that of PATz4T-o6OD, as discussed below. The optical band gap of PBA-T2 $\left( 1 . 7 9 \mathrm { e V } \right)$ is almost the same as that of PATz4T-o6OD, likely due to a similar effective $\pi \cdot$ conjugation length or electron-donating nature of donor units.

Electrochemical measurements of PBA-TX in a thin film using cyclic voltammetry (CV) were conducted to assess their HOMO energy levels (Figure S10). The obtained HOMO and

LUMO energy levels are summarized in Table 1 and Figure 2c. PBA-T2 $( - 5 . 3 5 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ has a HOMO energy level similar to that of PATz4T-o6OD $\left( - 5 . 3 9 ~ \mathrm { ~ e V } \right)$ , likely due to the nearly identical electron-donating nature of alkylthienyl BDT and bithiophene donors, as will be discussed below. This suggests that the introduction of alkylthienyl BDT instead of bithiophene can improve solubility without any change in the electronic structures of $\mathbf { A T z }$ -based polymers. On the other hand, polymers PBA-T3 $( - 5 . 5 2 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ and PBA-T4 $( - 5 . 4 9 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ exhibit lower HOMO energy levels compared to PBA-T2 $\left( - 5 . 3 5 ~ \mathrm { ~ e V } \right)$ ,attributed to the substitution of electronwithdrawing fluorine or chlorine atoms onto the BDT framework. Although PBA-T4 incorporates the most electronegative fluorine atoms, its HOMO energy level is slightly higher than that of the chlorine-substituted polymer PBA-T3. This difference can be attributed to the p-n conjugation variance between fluorine and chlorine atoms. Fundamentally, the atomic radius of fluorine is smaller than that of chlorine, enhancing the contribution of p-n conjugation and leading to increased electron density in the BDT framework.44 Consequently, PBA-T4, with fluorine atoms, has a higher HOMO energy level than the chlorine-substituted PBA-T3.

Theoretical Calculations of the Dimer Structure in PBA-TX. To further assess the electronic structures of PBATX, density functional theory (DFT) calculations of their dimer structures were performed (Figure 3a). For the sake of simplicity in the DFT calculations, methyl groups were employed as alkyl groups. Figure 3a also shows the calculated dimer structure of PATz4T-o6OD for comparison. The calculated PBA-T2 dimer exhibited nearly identical HOMO $\left( - 5 . 0 8 \ \mathrm { e V } \right)$ and LUMO $\left( - 2 . 9 4 ~ \mathrm { e V } \right)$ energy levels to that of PATz4T-o6OD $( - 5 . 0 9$ and $- 2 . 9 5 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ ). This indicates that the electron-donating abilities of alkylthienyl-substituted BDT and bithiophene are almost the same. These findings align well with the results obtained by UV-vis absorption and CV measurements. On the other hand, the HOMO and LUMO energy levels of PBA-T3 ( $- 5 . 2 2$ and $- 3 . 0 3 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ and PBA-T4 $( - 5 . 2 0$ and $- 3 . 0 2 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ were downshifted compared to those of PBA-T2 due to the introduction of electron-withdrawing fluorine or chlorine atoms onto the BDT framework. The LUMO coefficients are predominantly located at the ATz core in all dimer structures, a characteristic feature common in thiadiazole-annulated molecules.30,45,46 1n contrast, the HOMO coefficients are delocalized over the entire dimer molecule. Hence, the incorporation of electron-withdrawing fluorine or chlorine atoms had a more significant impact on the HOMO than the LUMO, resulting in a slightly wider band gap. These findings are consistent with UV-vis absorption and CV measurements.

Based on the optimized molecular geometries, PBA-TX polymers exhibit similar dihedral angles and molecular conformations, as depicted in Figures 3b and S1la. This suggests that the effect of halogen atoms on their molecular conformation and aggregation ability is negligible. The dihedral angle $\left( \varphi _ { 1 } \right)$ between the $\mathbf { A T z }$ core and neighboring thiophene rings ranges from 10 to $1 2 ^ { \circ }$ owing to the conformational lock provided by $\mathrm { N } { \cdots } \mathrm { H }$ hydrogen bonding. Conversely, highly twisted structures between the BDT unit and the methylthiophene adjacent to the BDT core $\left( \varphi _ { 2 } \right. =$ ${ \sim } 2 8 ^ { \circ } ,$ were observed due to the steric repulsion of methyl groups on the thiophene ring and sulfur atoms of the BDT core. However, the overall dimer structures exhibit relatively coplanar configurations, which may contribute to the formation of effective $\pi - \pi$ overlaps, resulting in a strong aggregation tendency, as demonstrated by temperaturedependent UV-vis absorption. In comparison with PATz4To6OD, PBA-T2 possesses a more twisted molecular geometry and wavy-shaped backbone structure (Figures 3b and S11a). Such a twisted and wavy-shaped structure hinders effective $\pi \cdot$ orbital overlaps between the polymer backbone. Therefore, PBA-T2 exhibits weaker intermolecular interaction and poorer molecular ordering than that of PATz4T-o6OD.

Morphological and Structural Studies of PBA-TX/Y6 Blended Films by using Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) and Grazing Incidence Wide-Angle X-ray Scattering (GiwAxs) Analyses. To assess the correlation between thin-film structures and OPV performances in the PBA-TX/Y6 system, we conducted grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) measurements on both neat PBA-TX films and their blended films with Y6 (Figures 5, S15 and Table S5). Neat PATz4T-o6OD formed a crystalline edge-on orientation with a lamellar distance of $1 9 . 9 \ \mathrm { \AA } ^ { 3 0 }$ In contrast, distinctly different molecular orders were observed in PBATXs. All neat polymer films displayed distinct lamellar (100) diffraction at $0 . 3 0 \overset { \cdot } { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ along the $q _ { x y }$ axis and broad $\pi$ stacking (010) diffraction at approximately $1 . 6 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ } ^ { - 1 }$ along the $q _ { z }$ axis (Figure $\displaystyle { \mathfrak { s a } } - { \mathfrak { c } } )$ . This suggests that PBA-TXs adopt a preferential face-on orientation in the solid state, which is advantageous for hole transport in the vertical direction.34 Polymers PBA-T2, PBA-T3, and PBA-T4 exhibited nearly identical lamellar distances $( d _ { \mathrm { l m } } )$ of 20.9-21.7 A and $\pi \cdot$ stacking distances $( d _ { \pi } )$ of $3 . 8 6 { - } 3 . 9 4 \mathrm { ~ \AA } ,$ respectively (Table S5). Thus, the impact of halogen atoms on the crystallinity and molecular order of BDT-ATz-based polymers is negligible, aligning with their UV-vis absorption spectra in the thin flm. In comparison with PATz4T-o6OD, all PBA-TX polymers exhibit a 1-2 A longer lamellar distance despite having shorter alkyl side chains. This suggests that the presence of bulky alkylthienyl groups can prevent the interdigitated structure, resulting in the construction of a favorable face-on orientation.34 In the blended flm with Y6, a predominantly face-on orientation, akin to that of neat polymer films, was observed (Figure 5d-f). The 1D profiles of the $\pi$ stacking diffraction were asymmetric, and distinct lamellar diffractions originating from PBA-TX polymers were evident (Figure S15). Consequently, PBA-TX polymers somewhat crystallized in conjunction with Y6. However, the calculated $\pi$ stacking distances $\left( d _ { \pi } = 3 . 5 1 { - 3 . 6 4 } \mathrm { \AA } \right)$ are approximately $0 . 4 \textup { \AA }$ shorter than those of neat polymer films, corresponding to the $d _ { \pi }$ of Y6, as indicated by a GIWAXS image of neat Y6 film (Figure S16 and Table S5). Thus, the face-on crystallite of Y6 predominates in these PBA-TX/Y6 blended films. This difference in crystallinity may result in lower hole mobility compared to electron mobility in PBA-TX/Y6 blended films, leading to a relatively low FF. Nevertheless, the disparities in crystallinity and molecular orders among PBA-TX blended with Y6 are minimal. Consequently, we further explored their surface morphology using AFM measurements.

Figure $5 { \bf g - i }$ depicts the topographic and error-signal images of PBA-TX/Y6 blended films. All PBA-TX/Y6 blended films exhibited a similar surface morphology with nanofibril-like structures and nearly identical root-mean-square (RMS) roughness ranging from 0.6 to $1 . 0 \ \mathrm { ~ n m }$ This optimized morphology appears to feature a well-separated and suitable phase separation structure, which is advantageous for efficient charge separation and transport.25-27 Consequently, this optimized surface morphology has a minimal impact on the performance of PBA-TX/Y6. However, due to the electronwithdrawing fluorine or chlorine atom substitutions in PBA-T3 and PBA-T4, leading to a higher electrostatic potential (ESP) compared to PBA-T2 without halogen atoms (Figure S11b), the donor-acceptor interaction between PBA-T3 or PBA-T4 and Y6 is expected to be weaker than that between PBA-T2 and Y6.48,49 In such case, a weaker donor-acceptor interaction can result in poor miscibility and enhanced phase separation +84 To explore the miscbility between PBA-TX and Y6, we estimated their surface energies through contact angle measurements using two different solvents (Figure S17 and Table S6).5o A larger difference in surface energy corresponds to a greater interfacial energy and thus lower miscibility between the two materials.o The estimated surface energy $( \gamma _ { s } )$ of PBA-T3 and PBA-T4 is 25.15 and $2 6 . 9 2 ~ \mathrm { ~ m N ~ } ~ \mathrm { m } ^ { - 1 }$ respectively, which are higher than that of PBA-T2 $\left( \gamma _ { \mathrm { s } } = 2 5 . 0 2 \right.$ $\mathrm { m } \hat { \mathrm { N } } \mathrm { m } ^ { - 1 } .$ ) due to the increased polarity of halogen atoms.51,52 In comparison to the surface energy of Y6 $( \gamma _ { \mathrm { s } } = 2 5 . 1 7 \ \mathrm { m N }$ $\mathbf { m } ^ { - 1 } ,$ , PBA-T2 and PBA-T3 have nearly the same surface energy as Y6, indicating good miscibility with Y6. Conversely, PBA-T4 exhibits a slightly larger surface energy difference, indicating a slightly poor miscibility. We hypothesized that such differences in miscibility might lead to distinct internal morphologies compared to those of the PBA-T2/Y6 and PBAT3/Y6 blend systems. This could explain why the PBA-T4/ Y6-based cell exhibited a slightly lower PCE than the others. However, all PBA-TX polymers exhibit almost identical intermolecular interactions, coplanarity, surface morphology, and packing structures. Therefore, all of the fabricated devices demonstrated a PCE of around $8 - 9 \%$ and the influence of halogen atoms on their photovoltaic properties is relatively small. One possible reason for these minor differences may be attributed to their $\pi$ extended ATz framework. Since the introduction of a $\pi$ -extended ATz core can provide a sufficiently strong intermolecular interaction, the halogen atoms substituted at positions away from the main chain do not significantly affect the crystallinity or phase separation structure.

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we have detailed the synthesis, characterization, and solar cell applications of three $\mathbf { A T z }$ -based copolymers, PBA-TX, combined with an alkylthienyl-substituted BDT unit to enhance solubility and molecular orientation. Additionally, we explored the impact of halogen atoms on their physicochemical properties, thin-film structures, and solar cell performances. The incorporation of alkoxy-functionalized ATz cores, which exhibit weak electron-acceptor properties, into the polymer backbone results in PBA-TXs with large optical energy gaps (approximately $1 . 8 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ ) and deep HOMO energy. levels ranging from $- 5 . 4$ to $- 5 . 5 \mathrm { ~ \ e V } _ { \mathrm { ~ \scriptsize ~ } }$ similar to those of previously reported $\mathbf { A T z }$ polymer PATz4T-o6OD. These characteristics facilitate complementary absorption with the low-band-gap n-type semiconductor Y6 and yield high $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ Furthermore, the presence of bulky alkylthienyl side chains on the BDT framework enhanced the solubility of the synthesized polymers and prevented the formation of an interdigitated structure in the solid state, promoting a favorable face-on orientation. Moreover, halogenation of alkylthienyl side chains further deepened HOMO energy levels without altering the optical band gap, intermolecular interaction, and molecular packing structures. As a result, all fabricated PBA-TX-based solar cells exhibited a commendable PCE in the range of $^ { 8 - }$ $9 \%$ whereas the PATz4T-o6OD/Y6-based cell exhibited poor OPV performance with a PCE of less than $1 \%$ primarily due to its inhomogeneous blend film. This success can be attributed to the consistent molecular orders, crystallinity, and surface morphology of their films blended with Y6. The relatively small differences in solar cell performance in PBA-TX/Y6 systems may be ascribed to the presence of the $\pi \cdot$ -extended $\mathbf { A T z }$ framework. The introduction of this framework into the polymer backbone ensures sufficient strong intermolecular interaction, rendering the effects of halogen atoms relatively minor, especially considering their positions are distant from the main chain. In general, the halogenation of alkylthienyl side chains on BDT cores proves crucial in enhancing solar cell performances, underscoring its importance for high-performance non-fullerene OPVs. Consequently, our alkoxy-substituted ATz core demonstrates promising potential for the development of halogen-free semiconducting polymers, enabling the cost-effective synthesis of high-performance materials. Ongoing efforts in our laboratory involve further modifications to the ATz-based polymer backbone and continued evaluation of the fabricated cells.
