ABSTRACT: Organic solar cells (OSCs) with high performance were prepared using poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) and [2- (3,6-dibromo-9H-carbazol-9-yl)ethyl]phosphonic acid $\left( \mathrm { B r } { - } 2 \mathrm { P A C z } \right)$ double-layer flms as the anode interface. By spin-coating a layer of Br-2PACz on PEDOT:PSS to form a PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dual-anode interface, both the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF of the device can be increased simultaneously, resulting in a high $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 7 . 8 4 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and a high FF of $7 8 . 1 8 \%$ . The promising result indicates that the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dual-anode interface is an effective way to improve the performance of OsCs. The improvement of device performance is mainly attributed to (1) improved interface conductivity; (2) increased hole mobility and more balanced carrier transport efficiency; and (3) optimized morphology, which well explains the increase of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF of the device. In addition, the OSC based on the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dualanode interface exhibits exceptional stability, as it can maintain $9 4 . 7 \%$ of its initial efficiency even after $5 0 0 \mathrm { h }$ of storage in a nitrogen environment. This work provides a promising strategy for improving the efficiency and stability of OsCs by dual-anode interface modulatio.

# INTRODUCTION

Organic solar cells (OsCs) have gained significant attention due to their numerous advantages, such as low cost, lightweight, flexibility, and ease of fabrication.1-5 In recent years, OSCs have made remarkable progress in terms of the power conversion efficiency (PCE), with the highest reported value reaching $1 9 . 6 \%$ 6 To further improve its performance, researchers are focusing on designing new materials, as well as optimizing device structures and fabrication techniques.7-12 For example, new materials with better energy level alignment and light_absorption properties are continuously being developed.7-9 Additionally, the design of the device structure, such as the thickness and morphologies of the active layer, can also be optimized to improve the performance.10,1113 Another important factor affecting OsCs' performance is the optimization of interfaces, which are located between different functional layers in the devices and play a critical role in charge generation, transport, and collection process.14-19 Therefore, improving the interface performance and reducing interfacial losses are very important and necessary to enhance the device efficiency.

Anode interface, which facilitates the hole transport from active layer to the anode, is typically made of a transparent conducting oxide (TCO) such as indium tin oxide (ITO). Materials commonly used for the anode interface layer include metal oxides,20,21 conductive polymers like PEDOT:PSS,13 and polyelectrolytes.22 PEDOT:PSS is a commonly used hole transport layer (HTL) material in organic solar cells due to its excellent properties, such as high transparency, solution processability, and good energy level alignment with many organic semiconductors.23,24 However, it also has some drawbacks, including low electrical conductivity, chemical instability, and sensitivity to moisture and oxygen.25-27 These issues would limit the overall performance and stability of the solar cells.28,29

To address these challenges, researchers are exploring various approaches to improve the properties of PEDOT:PSs.3 or example, some studies have focused on enhancing the electrical conductivity of PEDOT:PSS by adding dopants or conductive nanomaterials such as carbon nanotubes or graphene.30-34 Other researchers are working on developing alternative hole transport materials that can offer better stability and performance, such as inorganic materials or conjugated polymers.37-39 Additionally, many efforts were devoted to modify the surface chemistry of PEDOT:PSS to reduce its corrosion of electrodes and sensitivity to moisture and oxygen, such as by using cross-linking agents or surface modifiers.35,36 Kim et al. designed a hybrid HTL of ultrathin tungsten oxide and PEDOT:PSS, which solved the device stability problem of PEDOT:PSS, and slightly improved the device eficiency.40 Wang et al. prepared PEDOT nanofibers with high crystallinity induced by cladophora nanocellulost.41 Xiao et al. reported that PEDOT:PSS and sulfonated polyaromatic ether sulfone bilayer flms were used as HTL.42 Due to the interaction between PSS and PEDOT, the insulators of PSS were removed and continuous PEDOT domain was formed; thus, the conductivity was improved and the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of the device was obviously improved. Lin et al. reported on self-assembled monolayer, using it as a $\mathrm { H T L } ,$ and found that devices perform better than devices based on traditional PEDOT:Pss.43 Hou et al. mixed solution-treated twodimensional $\mathrm { T i } _ { 3 } \mathrm { C } _ { 2 } \mathrm { T } _ { X }$ nanosheets with PEDOT:PSS to prepare OSCs with PEDOT:PSS and $\mathrm { T i } _ { 3 } \mathrm { C } _ { 2 } \mathrm { T } _ { X }$ mixed layer as the $\mathrm { H T L } ,$ which improved the conductivity of PEDOT:PSS flm and significantly improved the device performance.44 Therefore, interface performance optimization is of sufficient importance to improve the photoelectric performance of OSCs.

In this work, the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dual-anode interface was successfully prepared, and PBDBTClDTBT:BTP-4F was used as the active layer to fabricate organic solar cells. As a result, the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF of the device are significantly improved, and the highest efficiency is up to $1 8 . 6 1 \%$ , which is obviously higher than that of pure Br-2PACz $\left( 1 6 . 5 8 \% \right)$ and PEDOT:PSS $\left( 1 7 . 6 4 \% \right)$ anode interface. The PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz synergy results in cells with better conductivity, higher hole mobility, and more balanced carrier transport and appears to influence the PBDBTCl-DTBT:BTP4F-based blends to produce more uniform nanomorphology, ultimately leading to superior PCE.

Chemicals. ITO glass substrate $\left( 1 4 . 8 5 \ \times \ 1 4 . 8 5 \ \mathrm { \ m m } ^ { 2 } \right)$ was purchased from Yaoke Photoelectric Materials Company. Characterizations. External quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra were obtained on a solar cell quantum efficiency measurement system (QE $\cdot \mathrm { R } ,$ Enli Technology Co., Ltd.), which was calibrated with a Si probe (RC-S103011-G). The transmission and absorption spectra of the materials at the interface of the HTL were measured by an ultraviolet spectrophotometer (LAMBDALambda 950). The photoluminescence spectra of the thin flms were measured by a fluorescence spectrometer (FL3-111). The morphology of the films was characterized by a scanning probe microscope (SPM-Dimension ICON) and a transmission electron microscope (TEM-JEOL 2100), and the microstructure of the films was analyzed. # RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Figure 1a shows the preparation process of the PEDOT:PSS/ Br-2PACz dual-anode interface. First, the cleaned ITO substrate was blown clean and treated with UV-ozone for 25 min, and then the first layer of PEDOT:PSS was prepared by static spin-coating. After that, the PEDOT:PSS film was annealed at $1 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 5 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ and then transferred to a $\mathrm { N } _ { 2 ^ { - } }$ filled glovebox. Subsequently, the colorless and transparent Br2PACz alcohol solution prepared in advance was dropped on the prepared PEDOT:PSS film, which was spun into a film at a rate of $3 5 0 0 ~ \mathrm { r p m }$ . Finally, the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz film was annealed at $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ for further use.

The surface elements were analyzed by XPs,45,46 as shown in Figures $1 \mathrm { b - d }$ and S1. Figure 1b-d shows the XPs full spectrum and the Br 3d and $\mathrm { ~ \textbf ~ { ~ P ~ } ~ } 2 \mathrm { p }$ characteristic peaks, respectively. Based on the XPS full spectrum, it was observed that the presence of Br and P elements in the PEDOT:PSS/Br2PACz bilayer films indicates the successful deposition of Br2PACz on PEDOT:PSS. The decrease in the peak intensities of Br 3d and $\mathrm { ~ P ~ } 2 \mathsf { p }$ for PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz relative to Br2PACz is due to the presence of PEDOT:PSS. Similarly, the signals of $\mathrm { ~ P ~ } 2 \mathsf { p }$ and $\mathrm { ~ \normalfont ~ S ~ 2 p ~ }$ in Figure S1 are due to PEDOT:PSS and $\mathrm { B r } { - } 2 \mathrm { P A C z } ,$ respectively. In addition, the surface of the film was characterized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and surface scans and cross sections were obtained, as shown in Figure S2. The comparison of S2a and S2b also provides further evidence for the successful deposition of Br-2PACz.

Figure 1e displays the transmittance of three different HTLs. Studies have shown that Br-2PACz has a transmittance of almost $100 \%$ in the wavelength range of $3 0 0 { - } 1 1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , which means that it allows nearly all light to pass through. The PEDOT:PSS has an optical transmittal of over $9 2 \%$ in the $3 0 0 { - } 1 1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ wavelength range. When a layer of Br-2PACz film is deposited on PEDOT:PSS, the transmittance of the prepared bilayer film (PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz) is almost the same as that of the monolayer PEDOT:PSS. This indicates that Br-2PACz deposition on PEDOT:PSS has no significant effect on the overall light transmission of the interfacial layer.

To study the conductivity of different HTLs, a device with ITO/HTL/Ag structure+7 was prepared, and its conductivity was tested, as shown in Figure 1f. According to Figure 1f and film thickness (Figure S2) combined with the conductivity formula, the conductivity of the HTL can be calculated. Detailed data are listed in Table S1. The calculated results show that the conductivity of the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz film is $6 . 2 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { ~ S ~ m ~ } ^ { - 1 }$ , which is significantly higher than that of PEDOT: PSS film $\left( 3 . 3 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { ~ \bar { ~ } S ~ m ^ { - 1 } } \right)$ . This result is further supported by the analysis of the $\mathrm { ~ \normalfont ~ S ~ 2 p ~ }$ nuclear energy level XPS spectra of different HTL films. The test curves were fitted as shown in Figure $\mathrm { S 1 a - c } _ { i }$ and the results indicate that the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz films have significantly lower PSS peak intensities. The relative compositions of PSS and PEDOT on the film surface were calculated by using the area ratios corresponding to PSS and PEDOT. The ratio of PSS to PEDOT was calculated to be 7.5:1 and 5:1 for PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz films, respectively, which indicates that a mass of PSS was washed away during the deposition of Br-2PACz. The conductive part of PEDOT:PSS is PEDOT, and PSS acts as only a dispersed long chain. Therefore, the relative increase of the PEDOT content is also a sign of increased interface conductivity.

Then, the work functions of different HTLs were compared, and their ultraviolet photoelectron spectra (Ups) were characterized as shown in Figure 1g. The detailed data calculated from the UPS are summarized in Table S2. The results indicate that the WF values of PEDOT:PSS, the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dual-anode interface, and Br-2PACz are 5.03, 5.18, and $5 . 4 6 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively. Both Br-2PACz and the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dual-anode interface exhibit work functions higher than $5 . 1 1 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for ITO. However, the higher work function48,49 indicates that the HTL is more favorable for charge transfer. This result is consistent with the subsequent calculation results for hole mobility.

The active layer and interface transport layer materials used for device preparation are shown in Figures 2a and S4, respectively. The device structure used in this work is depicted in Figure 2b, and it consists of an ITO/HTL/PBDBTClDTBT:BTP-4F/PDINN/Ag traditional structure, where the HTL is a dual-anode interface composed of PEDOT:PSS and Br-2PACz. The mass ratio of the active layer was kept constant at 1:1.6, and the solution concentration of the second $\scriptstyle { \mathrm { H T L } } ,$ Br-2PACz, was varied to study the effects of different materials and concentrations on the photovoltaic properties of OSCs.

The $J { - } V$ curves of the devices are shown in Figure 2c,d, and the detailed PV parameters are summarized in Table 1. Figure 2c shows the $J { - } V$ curves of the different HTL devices. With PEDOT:PSS as the HTL, $J _ { s c } , V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ,$ and FF are $2 6 . 3 3 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ $0 . 8 6 0 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ and $7 6 . 6 6 \%$ respectively, resulting in a PCE of $1 7 . 3 7 \%$ . Meanwhile, the PCE of the device prepared with the Br-2PACz interface is $1 6 . 5 8 \%$ . However, the optimal PCE of the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dual-anode interface can reach $1 8 . 6 1 \%$ . The devices prepared with a double-HTL have a significant increase in their short-circuit current and fill factor. This is mainly because a portion of the PSS is washed out during the deposition of $\mathrm { B r } { - } 2 \mathrm { P A C z } ,$ resulting in an increase in the conductivity of the devices, which also coincides with the test of AFM below. Figure 2d shows the $J { - } V$ curves of devices with different Br-2PACz concentrations. The results show that the device performs best when the Br-2PACz concentration is $0 . 1 \ \mathrm { m g / m L } ,$ but $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF decrease significantly with a further increase or decrease of the concentration. Combined with Figure S5, it can be found that the device prepared by the dualanode interface is not only efficient but also has better repeatability. The $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF improvements of PEDOT:PSS/ Br-2PACz dual-anode interface devices are attributed to improved conductivity, higher carrier transport efficiency, and optimized morphology.

To demonstrate the improvement of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ the EQE of the device was further tested. As shown in Figure 2e, the integral current density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { c a l c } } \right)$ of the device was calculated and compared to the $J { - } V$ curve with good agreement within an allowable error range of less than $5 \%$ indicating the reliability of the device's photovoltaic performance. It is noted that the use of PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz as HTL showed higher EQE across the entire spectrum compared to devices using only PEDOT:PSS as HTL. Although the EQE spectral response of devices using Br-2PACz as HTL is also significantly higher than that of PEDOT:PSS, there is a significant decrease in the range of $6 0 0 { - } 8 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . The results show that the optical energy absorbed by the device with dual-anode interface can be converted into photocurrent more effectively, thus improving the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of the device. This is consistent with the results for the carrier mobility described below. In addition, the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dual-anode interface was applied to the devices with active layers of PM6:Y6 and PM6:BTP-eC9, respectively (Figures S6-S7 and Table S3). It is found that compared with the standard, the dual-anode interface can still improve the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF of the device and has better performance in terms of hole mobility, voltage, and current dependence on light intensity. This indicates that the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dual-anode interface can improve the photovoltaic performance of the device by increasing the short-circuit current of different nonfullerene donor-acceptor system devices.

The flm morphology was characterized by using atomic force microscopy (AFM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and the results are shown in Figure 3. Among them, Figure $_ { 3 \mathsf { a } - \mathsf { c } }$ shows the AFM height maps for each of the three different HTLs. The average surface roughness (RMS) of Br-2PACz is about $2 . 6 0 \ \mathrm { n m } ,$ which can explain the low FF of the device prepared by single-layer Br-2PACz as HTL. Compared with Br-2PACz, PEDOT:PSS films show less roughness and a smoother surface. The roughness of the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz film is between two monolayer films, about $1 . 5 7 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . The optimized morphology is more conducive to carrier transport,so thus improving the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF of device.

The influence of three different HTLs on the active layer was investigated, as shown in Figure $3 \mathrm { d - i } .$ The results of the study suggest that the roughness of the active layer films measured was found to be similar within the allowed error range. The phase diagrams of the interface and active layer films are shown in Figure S8. The results show that the phase separation morphology in the phase diagrams is consistent with this result. In a word, PEDOT:PSS, PEDOT:PSS/Br$2 \mathrm { P A C z } ,$ and Br-2PACz, have little to no significant effect on the active layer material. Figure $3 { \bf g - i }$ shows TEM images obtained after active layer coating on different HTL. It can be seen that image morphologies corresponding to PEDOT:PSS/ Br-2PACz/PBDBTCl-DTBT:BTP-4F are uniform, while those corresponding to PEDOT:PSS/PBDBTCl-DTBT:BTP-4F are scattered, which is not conducive to carrier transmission in the active layer. This explains well why the FF and $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of devices based on PEDOT:PSS/PBDBTCl-DTBT:BTP-4F are much lower than those based on PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz/ PBDBTCl-DTBT:BTP-4F.

In addition, the carrier recombination process of the OSCs was researched by the difference of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ with the variation of incident light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ 53 Quantitatively, the relationship between $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ (Figure 4b) can be indicated as $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \propto I ^ { \wedge ^ { \alpha } } ,$ where $\alpha$ indicates the degree of bimolecular recombination. The closer $\alpha$ is to 1, the device has negligible bimolecular recombination. Recombination results in a partial loss of carriers during charge transport. The $\alpha$ values of the devices based on PEDOT:PSS and the dual-anode interface PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz are 0.963 and 0.996, respectively. Obviously, the optimal-device $\alpha$ value based on PEDOT:PSS/ Br-2PACz is closer to 1, indicating that the second-order recombination generated during charge transfer is negligible. This means that the dual-anode interface used in the device is highly efficient in suppressing charge recombination and minimizing carrier losses during transport. Figure 4c shows the relationship curve of $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ with the light intensity. $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ dependence on $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ is proportional to trap-assisted recombination. Thus, a smaller slope (closer to 1) implies a more favorable device performance. As can be seen from the figure, the slope of the dual-anode interface device is small, indicating that it has less trap-assisted recombination, which is conducive to achieving better $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF.

The stability of OsCs is of great significance to their practical application and production. For this reason, we tested the stability of OSC devices based on the PEDOT:PSS/Br2PACz dual-anode interface in a nitrogen environment at room temperature for $5 0 0 \ \mathrm { h }$ . The normalization function of photovoltaic parameters is shown in Figure 5. As can be seen from the figure, the device with the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dual-anode interface still maintains an initial efficiency of $9 4 . 7 \%$ after $5 0 0 \mathrm { h }$ of storage, and its $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } , J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF all maintain excellent stability. In contrast, the device based on PEDOT:PSS only maintained an initial efficiency of $7 6 . 9 \%$ after $5 0 0 \mathrm { h }$ storage in a nitrogen environment, and its PCE and FF both had large error values, which also reflected the poor stability of PEDOT:PSS devices. PEDOT:PSS is a conductive polymer material, in which PEDOT has conductivity and hydrophobicity, and PSS has nonconductivity, hydrophilicity, and acidity. Due to the sulfonic acid group in PSS, the acidity of PEDOT:PSS will corrode the ITO electrode, and its hydrophilicity will also absorb water in the environment, which will affect the performance of the active layer, resulting in poor device stability. However, part of the PSS will be washed away in the process of spinning Br-2PACz on devices with dualanode interfaces (which has been explained in Figure 1). In addition, Br-2PACz will form a thin film on PEDOT:PSS, which can effectively protect the active layer from the erosion of water in the environment and thus improve the overall stability of the device.

# CONCLUSIONS

The dual-anode interface-based heterojunction OsCs with PCE up to $1 8 . 6 1 \%$ were successfully prepared using PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz as an HTL and PBDBTClDTBT:BTP-4F as an active layer. The high performance of the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dual-interface OSCs is attributed to the enhanced conductivity and more balanced carrier transmission, which effectively reduce the recombination loss. At the same time, the PEDOT:PSS/Br-2PACz dual interface provides a more suitable channel for hole transmission and optimizes the morphology of the active layer blend film, leading to better device performance. In terms of stability, Br2PACz can reduce the acid corrosion of ITO by PSS and form a protective layer on the active layer. Finally, the device with dual interface still maintains an initial efficiency of $9 4 . 7 \%$ after 500 h of storage in a nitrogen environment at room temperature. The results show that the dual-anode interface structure optimization strategy has significant potential in improving the performance of OsCs. Overall, this work provides new ideas for the design and manufacture of highefficiency and efficient solar cell materials, which could have a significant impact on the development of next-generation solar cells.
