# Unconjugated Side-Chain Engineering Enables Small Molecular Acceptors for Highly Efficient Non-Fullerene Organic Solar Cells: Insights into the Fine-Tuning of Acceptor Properties and Micromorphology

Tao Liu, Wei Gao, Yilin Wang, Tao Yang, Ruijie Ma, Guangye Zhang,\* Cheng Zhong, Wei Ma,\* He Yan,\* and Chuluo Yang\*

2D conjugated side-chain engineering is an effective strategy that is widely utilized to construct benzodithiophene-based polymers. Herein, an unconjugated side-chain strategy to design fused-benzodithiophene-based non-fullerene small molecule acceptors (SMAs) via vertical aromatic sidechain engineering on the ladder-type core is employed. Three SMAs named BTTIC-Th, BTTIC-TT, and BTTIC-Ph with thiophene, thieno[3,2-b]thiophene, and benzene, respectively, as side chains, are designed and synthesized. Three SMAs exhibit similar absorption ranges but different lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels due to the different strength of the &-inductive effect between vertical aromatic side chains and their electron-rich core. Organic solar cells based on PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT achieve a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of $1 3 . 4 4 \%$ , which is higher than the PCE of devices based on PBDB-T:BTTIC-Th $( 1 2 . 9 1 \% )$ and PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph $( 9 . 1 4 \% )$ -. The difference in device performance is investigated by electrical and morphological characterizations. A large domain size and different types of $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking are found in the bulk heterojunction layer of PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph blend film, which are detrimental to exciton dissociation and charge transport. Overall, it is demonstrated that when designing unconjugated side chains, thieno[3,2-b]thiophene is superior to thiophene and benzene through its dual roles of promoting the LUMO energy level and optimizing the morphology. These results shed light on the side-chain engineering of high-performance non-fullerene SMAs.

# 1. Introduction

The field of organic solar cells (OsCs) grows rapidly in the past few years due to the surge of non-fullerene acceptors,[1-11] which have been deemed to replace fullerene-based acceptors due to their wide absorption ranges,[216] tunable energy levels,[17-19] and controllable crystallization/aggregation properties.[20-26] Most of the progresses come from the design and synthesis of novel chemical structures that provide the molecules with properties superior to fullerene-based acceptors. Currently, the prevailing type of small molecule acceptor (SMA) is based on the A-D-A structure, that is, an electron-rich core flanked with two electron-deficient groups.[27] To further boost the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of a non-fullerene OSC device, which is proportional to the product of the open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ the short-circuit current $( J _ { \mathsf { S C } } )$ , and the fill factor (FF), deep insights into the structure-property-performance  relationship are needed, particularly for the emerging families of non-fullerene moieties that have shown great potential in achieving high-performance OsCs.2,12,18,20-2,24,25,2836] Some significant achievements have been made to this end. For example, introducing $\pi$ bridge with less hindrance or noncovalent interaction into the donor (D) and acceptor (A) moieties has been shown to be able to increase the coplanarity of the polymer, which promotes the stacking of molecules in the favorable $\pi - \pi$ direction.37,38] Furthermore, the 2D conjugated side-chain strategy is proved to be an effective method to modify the rigid and linear backbone of benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b'ldithiophene (BDT), which is considered a universal building block for constructing polymer donor to pair different electron-deficient units. The aromatic alkyl side chains not only enhance the intermolecular packing in the vertical direction but also adjust the crystallization property of the polymer, leading to an advancement of PCE in the resultant device.[39-43]

In 2015, Zhan and co-workers reported a narrow-bandgap SMA named ITIC, which was composed of a dithieno[2,3- $d ; 2 ^ { \prime } , 3 ^ { \prime }$ $d ^ { \prime } ]$ s-indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b']-dithiophene (IDTT) core and two 2-(3-oxo-2,3-dihydro-1H-inden-1-ylidene)malononitrile(INIC) end groups.[27] Since then, pairing ITIC with various types of polymer donors has led to the remarkably rapid enhancement of device performance and PCEs over $10 \%$ were reported by different groups. To make further breakthroughs, creating new cores (the D moiety) has been identified as an effective and sustainable approach as it is the most direct way to modulate the property considering the relatively limited choices of end groups. Among the emerging core units, benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b'] di(cyclopenta[2,1-b:3,4-b'ldithiophene (BDCD) is one of the isomers of IDTT with marshaling sequence of thiophene,[31,44-46] and the NFBDT, the SMA based on the BDCD core, possesses more modification sites and shows a redshifted absorption profile relative to ITIC.

Inspired by the 2D conjugated side-chain strategy of developing highly efficient BDT-based polymer donor, in this work, we implemented side-chain engineering on BDCD by employing frequently used thiophene, thieno[3,2-b]thiophene and benzene as building moieties, and designed and synthesized three new fused-ring electron acceptors, namely, BTTIC-Th, BTTIC-TT, and BTTIC-Ph. We show that BTTIC-TT and BT'TIC-Ph exhibit significantly higher lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels but similar absorption ranges to that of BT'TIC-Th in the meantime. OSCs based on PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT realized a PCE of up to $1 3 . 4 4 \%$ along with $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } ,$ and FF higher than those of PBDB-T:BTTIC-Th-based OSCs. However, PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph-based OSCs achieved remarkably lower PCE $( 9 . 1 4 \% )$ than those of PBDB-T:BTTICTh- and PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT-based OSCs due to the disordered $\pi - \pi$ stacking and large domain size. Through systematic elec trical and morphological characterizations, we provide the structure-property relationship of the series of molecules. Our results reveal that the 2D unconjugated side-chain strategy can optimize SMAs properties and also morphology of the bulk heterojunction layer and the new finding will guide our further design of high-performance non-fullerene SMAs.

# 2.2. Absorption and Energy Levels

Figure 1a shows the normalized UV-vis absorption spectra of BTTIC-Th, BTTIC-TT, and BTTIC-Ph measured in solution and neat films (Iable 1). In solution, BTTIC-Th, BTTIC-TT, and BTTICPh all exhibit wide absorption range with maximum absorption peaks located at 714, 718, and $7 2 9 \mathrm { n m }$ and molar extinction coefficients of $2 . 4 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ $2 . 5 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ , and $2 . 3 1 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { { M } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { { c m } ^ { - 1 } } }$ , respectively. When coming to the film state, the maximum absorption peaks redshift to 755, 754, and $7 6 6 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , for BTTIC-Th, BTTICTT, and BTTIC-Ph, respectively. The large redshifting along with the strengthened electronic vibration shoulder peaks indicate strong intermolecular stacking in their neat films. The absorption edges of BTTIC-Th, BTTIC-TT, and BTTIC-Ph neat films are located at 845, 841, and $8 5 2 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , corresponding to the optical bandgaps $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { { o p t } } } )$ of 1.467, 1.474, and $1 . 4 5 5 \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements of BTTIC-Th, BTTIC-TT, and BTTIC-Ph were carried out to evaluate the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO)/LUMO energy levels, which are shown in Figure 1b. The LUMOs/HOMOs of BTTIC-Th, BTTICTT, and BTTIC-Ph were determined to be $- 3 . 9 0 \mathrm { \ e V } \mathrm { - } 5 . 4 9 \mathrm { \ e V , }$ $- 3 . 8 0 \mathrm { \ e V } \mathrm { - } 5 . 5 4 \mathrm { \ e V } ,$ and $- 3 . 7 8 ~ \mathrm { e V } / - 5 . 4 8 ~ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively. These results show that the vertical aromatic side chains have little influence on the absorption spectra but a significant enhancement effect on the LUMO energy levels of the molecule, which is favorable for achieving a higher $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ in OSC devices.

# 2.3. Density Functional Theory (DFT) Calculations

To gain insights into the effect of the vertical aromatic sidechains on the properties of the acceptor, DFT calculations were performed at the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) level where the long alkyl chains were simplified to methyl groups. The optimal molecular configurations and LUMO distribution of BTTIC-Th, BTTIC-T'T, and BTTIC-Ph are displayed in Figure 2. It can be observed that the two end groups are distributed on both sides of the long axis of the three molecules with intramolecular O-S interactions. The small steric effect between the donor unit and end group, combined with the rigid ladder-type core, endow the three SMAs with excellent planarity (Figure S1, Supporting Information), which facilitates the $\pi - \pi$ stacking that is favorable for charge transport. The LUMO and HOMO are evenly distributed along the whole molecular backbone, which is beneficial for the intramolecular hole and electron transport. The calculated LUMOs/HOMOs are $- 3 . 3 5 ~ \mathrm { e V } / - 5 . 3 4 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for BTTICTh, $- 3 . 3 4 ~ \mathrm { e V } / - 5 . 3 5 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for BTTIC-TT, and $- 3 . 3 0 ~ \mathrm { e V } / - 5 . 3 0 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for BTTIC-Ph, which are consistent with the experimentally observed trend. Clearly, the aromatic side groups are forced to take the twisted configuration relative to the fused-ring backbone without HOMO and LUMO distributed on them, suggesting that the p-electrons ( $\pi$ electrons) between aromatic side chains and the fused-ring skeleton which are connected by single bond do not overlap at all, and thus the conjugation is interrupted. The twisted aromatic side chains affect the HOMO and LUMO energy levels of these SMAs through hyperconjugated effect (e.g., &-inductive effect, an effect usually happens between two unconjugated moieties and its strength is related to the angle and electron-donating ability of two moieties) not conjugated effect (an effect happens between two conjugated moieties connected by single bond). According to these features, we called them unconjugated side chain, which is very different from the 2D conjugated side chains on the BDT-based polymers because the sterically hindered phenyl groups of these SMAs cause the side chain to undergo a transition from conjugation to nonconjugation. The large thieno[3,2-b]thiophene and benzene can form a greater dihedral angle than thiophene, which weakens the &-inductive effect of the aromatic side groups on the backbone of BT'TIC-Th. Thus, BTTIC-TT and BTTIC-Ph show elevated LUMO energy levels compared to BTTIC-Th. Moreover, the weaker electron-donating ability of benzene than that of thieno[3,2-b]thiophene further lessens the &-inductive effect and therefore promotes the LUMO energy level, which is consistent with our calculation and experimental results. Overall, the DFT calculation reveals that optimizing the aromatic side group fine-tunes the LUMO levels of the SMAs, which could be employed to realize a higher $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ in OSC devices.

# 2.4. Photovoltaic Performance

OSC devices with a structure of indium tin oxide (ITO)/poly(3,4- ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate)(PEDOT:PSS)/ PBDB-T:acceptor/zirconium acetylacetonate (ZrAcac)/Al were fabricated to investigate the influences of aromatic side chains on the photovoltaic performance (Figure 3a), where ITO and Al serve as anode and cathode, respectively; PEDOT:PSS and ZrAcac were used as the hole transport layer (HTL) and the cathode interfacial layer (CIL), respectively; PBDB-T was selected as the donor to pair with three SMAs due to its suitable HOMO/ LUMO levels and complementary absorption. The optimal D:A weight ratio was 1:1 and the blend solution concentration was $1 5 ~ \mathrm { m g ~ m L ^ { - 1 } }$ . 1,8-Diiodooctane (DIO) was used as solvent additive $( 0 . 2 5 \% )$ during solution preparation and a thermal annealing $( 1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ step was performed after film formation. The details of device fabrication can be found in the Supporting Information.

The best-performing current density-voltage $\left( J - W \right)$ curves of the three SMAs-based OSCs and the corresponding external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra are shown in Figure 3b,c, respectively, and the key photovoltaic parameters are listed in Table 2. OSCs based on PBDB-T:BT'TIC-Th achieved a PCE of

# 2.5. Exciton Dissociation and Charge Recombination

In addition, we measured the photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ against effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ of three optimal OsCs to gain more insights into the effects of aromatic side chains on exciton generation, dissociation, and charge collection in the active layers, the result of which is shown in Figure 3d. $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ can be calculated by $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } = J _ { \mathrm { L } } - J _ { \mathrm { D } }$ where $J _ { \mathrm { L } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { D } }$ represent the current density measured under one standard sun illumination and in the dark, respectively. $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ is calculated by $V _ { \mathrm { { e f f } } } = V _ { 0 } - V _ { \mathrm { { b i a s } } }$ where $V _ { 0 }$ corresponds to the voltage when $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } = 0$ and $V _ { \mathrm { b i a s } }$ is the applied voltage. In principle, if the $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ is high enough, the bound excitons can be fully dissociated into free charge carriers and then efficiently collected by the individual electrode. Therefore, the probabilities of charge dissociation $\left( P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } \right)$ and charge collection $( P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } )$ can be evaluated by the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ values under shortcircuit and maximum power output conditions, respectively. At a high $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ value $( \geq 2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } )$ $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ of the three OsCs reach the saturated state $\left( J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } \right)$ , which are $2 0 . 1 3 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ for PBDB-T:BTTICTh-based OSCs, $2 0 . 2 7 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ for PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT-based OSCs, and $1 8 . 4 2 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph-based OSCs. According to $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } = q L G _ { \mathrm { m a x } } ,$ where $q$ is the elementary charge, L is the thickness of the active layer $( 1 1 0 \ \mathrm { n m } )$ , and $G _ { \mathrm { m a x } }$ is maximum exciton generation rate, the $G _ { \mathrm { m a x } }$ values were calculated to be $1 . 1 4 2 \times 1 0 ^ { 2 8 } \ \mathrm { m } ^ { - 3 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for PBDB-T:BT"TIC-Th-based OSCs, $1 . 1 5 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 2 8 } \ \mathrm { m } ^ { - 3 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT-based OSCs, and $1 . 0 4 5 \times 1 0 ^ { 2 8 } \ \mathrm { m } ^ { - 3 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for PBDB-T:BT"TIC-Ph-based OSCs, indicating that OSC based on PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT has the largest $G _ { \mathrm { m a x } }$ among the three different devices. The $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ under the shortcircuit and the maximum power output conditions are 19.45 and $1 6 . 3 5 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ for the PBDB-T:BTTIC-Th-based OSCs, 19.61 and $1 6 . 8 0 \ \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ for the PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT-based OSCs, and 16.47 and $1 2 . 0 8 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ for the PBDB-T:BTTICPh-based OsCs, corresponding to $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ of $9 6 . 6 \%$ and $8 1 . 2 \%$ $9 6 . 7 \%$ and $8 2 . 9 \%$ , and $8 9 . 4 \%$ and $6 5 . 6 \%$ , respectively. Compared to OSCs based on PBDB-T:BTTIC-Th and PBDBT:BTTIC-TT, the PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph-based OSC shows significantly lower $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } ,$ which are the main responsibility for its low $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF. Moreover, the photocurrent $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ as a function of light intensity $( P )$ of three OSCs was also measured, the results of which are plotted in a double logarithmic scale (Figure 3e). It can be clearly observed that the $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ is linearly correlated to $P$ with slopes (S) of 0.933 for the PBDB-T:BT'TICTh-based OSCs, 0.957 for the PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT-based OSCs, and 0.873 for the PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph-based OSCs. The highest S of the PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT-based OSC indicates that the thieno[3,2-bjthiophene side chain can effectively reduce bimolecular recombination that is beneficial for enhancing $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF.

# 2.6. Electron and Hole Mobility

To understand why thieno[3,2-bjthiophene performed better than the other two aromatic side chains, several characterization techniques were carried out to evaluate the active layer and the entire device. First, the space charge limited current (SCLC) method was used to measure the mobility of the three SMAs in neat and blend films. Electron-only devices with a structure of ITO/ZnO/active layer/ZrAcac/Al and hole-only devices with a structure of $\mathrm { I T O } / \mathrm { M o O } _ { x I }$ active layer/ $\mathrm { M o O } _ { x } / \mathrm { A l }$ were fabricated (Figure S3, Supporting Information). The electron mobilities $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ of BTTIC-Th, BTTIC-TT, and BTTIC-Ph neat films are $8 . 8 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ $9 . 2 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ , and $7 . 2 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively. When combined with PBDB-T, the blend films based on PBDB-T:BTTIC-Th, PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT, and PBDB-T:BTTICPh show bipolar transport with hole/electron $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ mobilities being $8 . 0 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } / 4 . 7 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } = 1 . 6 9 )$ $8 . 4 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } / 5 . 1 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } = 1 . 6 4 )$ , and $7 . 8 5 \times$ $1 0 ^ { - 4 } / 4 . 0 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } = 1 . 9 3 )$ , respectively. BTTICTT exhibits the highest mobility among the three SMAs not only in neat film but also in blend film, demonstrating that the thieno[3,2-b]thiophene side chain is beneficial for improving the carrier mobility, in turn positively affecting the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF of the OsC device. The low and imbalanced electron and hole mobilities are responsible for the poor $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF of the PBDBT:BTTIC-Ph-based OSCs.

# 2.7. Morphology Study

The surface morphology of the SMA neat films and the polymer:SMA blend films was investigated by atomic force microscope (AFM). As displayed in Figure 4a-c, BTTIC-Th, BTTIC-TT, and BTTIC-Ph neat films exhibit uniform morphology and smooth surface evidenced by their small rootmean-square (RMs) surface roughness of 0.49, 0.64, and $0 . 7 0 \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. Blending the polymer donor PBDB-T into the three SMAs (1:1 weight ratio) increases the RMS roughness to $1 . 0 7 \mathrm { n m }$ for PBDB-T:BTTIC-Th, 1.11 nm for PBDB-T:BTTICTT, and $1 . 2 7 \ \mathrm { n m }$ for PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph (Figure 4d-f). The small variation in RMS roughness between neat SMA films and their blend films indicates decent miscibility between PBDB-T and the three SMAs. In addition, it can be clearly observed from the AFM phase images (Figure S4, Supporting Information) that an interpenetrating fibrillar network is formed in the three blend films, which is favorable for charge transport. AFM measurements illuminate that the difference in aromatic side chains has only a small influence on the surface morphology of their blend films.

To reveal more details of the influence of the aromatic side chains on the phase separation scale between donor and acceptor, resonant soft X-ray scattering (RSoXS) experiments were carried out. The domain sizes of PBDB-T:BTTIC-Th, PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT, and PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph blend films calculated from the RSoXS profiles (Figure S5, Supporting Information) are 19.6, 15.3, and $2 6 . 3 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. The results indicate that the thieno[3,2-b] thiophene side chains on BTTIC-TT could reduce the domain size compared to the other two side chains, which facilitates the dissociation of excitons. In contrast, the domain size of $2 6 . 3 \ \mathrm { n m }$ for the PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph blend film lowers the probability for excitons to reach the donor-acceptor interface and thus increases exciton recombination. This is consistent with that the PBDBT:BTTIC-Ph-based OSCs displayed significantly lower $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ than PBDB-T:BTTIC-Th and PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT-based OSCs, which also agrees with its low EQEs.

Furthermore, grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) experiments were performed to better understand the side-chain effect on molecular packing. The 2D-GIWAXS patterns and the corresponding in-plane and out-of-plane linecuts are shown in Figure 5. The BTTIC-Th, BTTIC-TT, and BTTIC-Ph neat films and PBDB-T:BT'TIC-Th, PBDB-T:BTTICTT, and PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph blend films all exhibit a typical face-on orientation with intense (010) diffraction halos in the $q _ { z }$ direction (Figure 5a-f), which is favorable for carrier transport in the vertical direction between electrodes. It can be obviously observed from Figure 5c,f that there are two (010) diffraction halos for BTTIC-Ph neat and blend films in the $q _ { z }$ direction at about $1 . 6 \mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 }$ . This phenomenon indicates that different types of $\pi - \pi$ stacking exist in BTTIC-Ph neat and blend films, which is not conducive to efficient carrier transport. The positions of the two different (010) diffraction peaks are $1 . 5 3 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ (with a $d$ spacing of 4.11 A) and 1.77 $\mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 }$ (with a $d$ spacing of 3.54 A) for BTTIC-Ph neat film, and $1 . 5 2 \mathrm { ~ \AA ^ { - 1 } }$ (with a $d$ spacing of $4 . 1 4 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ and $1 . 7 3 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ (with a $d$ spacing of $3 . 6 3 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ for PBDBT:BTTIC-Ph blend film. The (010) diffraction peak of BTTICPh neat film at $1 . 5 3 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ is obviously different from and much stronger than those of the neat films based on the other two SMAs. The (01O) peak position for the PBDB-T:BTTIC-Th blend film is $1 . 7 0 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ with a $d$ spacing of 3.69 A while that for the PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT blend film is at $1 . 7 6 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ with a $d$ spacing of $3 . 5 7 \mathring \mathrm { A } .$ Furthermore, the coherence lengths (CLs) are 2.35, 3.35, and $2 . 5 3 \ \mathrm { n m }$ for the PBDB-T:BTTIC-Th, PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT, and PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph blend films. The result of CLs combined with the fact that PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph shows more types of $\pi - \pi$ stacking, shows that PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT has the most favorable (strong and more ordered) $\pi - \pi$ stacking in the faceon direction among the three different blend films, which is consistent with the higher mobility and better device.

# 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, molecular engineering on the vertical aromatic side chains was conducted to understand the effect of different side chains on the property of the non-fullerene SMAs and their photovoltaic device performance. Three non-fullerene SMAs, namely, BTTIC-Th, BTTIC-TT, and BTTIC-Ph, with thiophene, thieno[3,2-b]thiophene, and benzene units, respectively, as vertical side chains were designed and synthesized. The three SMAs show similar absorption spectra in both solution and neat films but different LUMO energy levels. The weaker &-inductive effect between the thieno[3,2-b]thiophene unit (or the benzene unit) and the ladder-type core promotes the LUMO energy levels. As a result, the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of OSCs based on PBDB-T:BTTIC-TT and PBDB-T:BTTIC-Ph were significantly enhanced. Moreover, the PBDB-T:BT'TIC-TT-based OSCs achieved the highest PCE of $1 3 . 4 4 \%$ which is higher than those of the PBDB-T:BTTIC-Th $( 1 2 . 9 1 \% )$ and PBDB-T:BTTIC-Phbased $( 9 . 1 4 \% )$ OSCs. Despite that BTTIC-Ph with benzene as aromatic side chain could obtain a good surface morphology in blend film, a large domain size and different types of $\pi - \pi$ stacking in its bulk blend film are detrimental for exciton dissociation and carrier transport. Overall, this study reveals that regulating the strength of $\delta$ inductive effect between the vertical aromatic side chain and the donor core is an effective method to fine-tune the energetics such as the LUMO energy levels and the morphology of the resultant SMAs. Our result shows that thieno[3,2-b]thiophene is better than thiophene and benzene when acting as the vertical aromatic side chain, which can be used to guide future molecular design.
