# Improvement in the Efficiency of Alkylsilyl Functionalized Copolymer for Polymer Solar Cells: Face-On Orientation Enhanced by Random Copolymerization

Hui Lei, Bin Huang, Lie Chen,\* Shanshan Chen, Guodong Xu, Shaorong Huang, Yun Tan, Changduk Yang, and Yiwang Chen\*

Herein, an alkylsilyl functionalized alternative (D-A1) copolymer with high crystallization property as the polymer matrix and planar [1,2-c:4,5-c]dithiophene-4, 8-dione (BDD) block as the second acceptor unit (A2) are selected to construct two D-A1-D-A2 type random copolymers PBDT-TZ-BDD-1/19 and PBDT-TZ-BDD-1/9. It is found that incorporation of a small amount of BDD block into the alkylsilyl functionalized copolymer by random copolymerization can effectively manipulate the energy levels, light absorption, molecular packing and the photovoltaic properties when blended with ITIC (indacenodithieno[3,2-b]thiophene (IT) as the central donor unit and 2- (3-oxo-2,3-dihydroinden-1-ylidene)malononitrile (IC) as end groups). More importantly, random copolymerization provides a beneficial trade-off that the slightly reduced periodic sequence promotes the compatibility with the acceptor, whereas introduction of planar BDD units allows a preferred face-on orientation with enhanced $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking of the random copolymer to facilitate the charge transfer. As a result, the random copolymer PBDT-TZ-BDD-1/19 delivers a significantly higher power conversion effciency $( 1 1 . 0 2 \% )$ than the alternative binary copolymer counterpart together with the remarkably improved short circuit current and fill factor. These results demonstrate that random polymerization of a small amount of planar units into the highly crystalline polymer matrix is a promising strategy to develop high-performance polymer solar cells.

# 1. Introduction

Polymer solar cells (PSCs) based on solution-processed bulk heterojunction (BHJ) have attracted much research attention for their low cost, lightweight, and potential for making large-area and flexible devices through roll-to-roll processing.1-5] A BHJ active layer in a PSC consisting of a p-type conjugated polymer as an electron donor blended with an n-type molecule as an electron acceptor has been the leading candidate in the organic photovoltaic technology. In particular, a breakthrough in nonfullerene acceptors occurred recently with the development of fused-aromatic-ring-based molecules, such as ITIC (indacenodithieno[3,2-b] thiophene (IT) as central donor unit and 2- (3-oxo-2,3-dihydroinden-1-ylidene) malononitrile (IC) as end groups), m-ITIC, and their derivatives, demonstrating an outstanding performance in blended films with polymer donors.[6-13] The nonfullerene acceptors are gradually emerging as competitors because of their advantages of strong absorption in the visible region, easily tunable energy levels, easy synthesis, and good stability of morphology.[14,15] At present, the efficiency of the nonfullerene organic solar cells based on a binary system has reached over $1 5 \%$ [16] To match with these fused-aromatic-ring-based acceptors, design and synthesis of efficient and stable polymer donors become more and more critical for high-performance nonfullerene PSCs.

A common strategy to develop a new polymer donor is accomplished by constructing the D-A copolymer through alternating the copolymerization of a donor unit (D) and an acceptor unit (A). Much effort is devoted to the optimization of the energy levels, light absorption, molecular packing, and solubility of the D-A copolymer by modulating the side chains of the molecules, introducing functional groups or designing new D or A blocks for the D-A copolymer. These methods are often complicated and involve a tedious synthesis. Recently, random copolymerization has emerged as an alternatively promising approach to develop highly efficient polymer donors for PsCs.17-20] At present, the random copolymer donors are mainly concentrated on two types of structures: one is D-A1-D-A2 type, where the random copolymerization of an electron-donating unit (D) is with two different electron-accepting units (A1 and A2); the other one is D1-A-D2-A type, where the random copolymerization of an electron-accepting unit (A) is with two different electron-donating units (D1 and D2).[17,21,22] In fact, random copolymer donors are achieved by introducing the third unit (D2 or A2) into the D-A binary copolymer. Therefore, by adjusting the structure and the quantity of the third unit, the electronic energy levels, light absorption, and molecular configuration of copolymer donors can be easily modulated, which is correlated with photovoltaic parameters, including the short-circuit current $( J { \mathrm { s c } } )$ , open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ , and fill factor (FF). Apart from the boosted efficiency, such covalentbond banded ternary random copolymers are expected to show a superior device stability than the physical blending ternary system.

Despite the improved photovoltaic properties of random copolymerization, nonperiodic sequence distribution caused by random copolymerization will dramatically interrupt the ordering packing of conjugated backbones. In 2013, our group has already prepared a random copolymerization PPC-T-BT for PSCs.231 We found that random copolymerization could effectively modulate the energy potentials of the copolymers, but the device performance still lags behind that of the alternating binary counterpart, due to the disordered sequence of the repeating units and twist backbones sharply reducing the charge transport. Therefore, random copolymerization offers a trade-off between the advantage from the third component and the interruption from the nonperiodic sequence distribution. A design of random copolymer donors relies on the rational selection of the polymer matrix, the third unit to be introduced as well as the component mole ratios, which will directly impact the device performance.

Alkylsilyl functionalized copolymers, such as J71 based on Si-containing benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b]dithiophene (Si-BDT) and fuorobenzotriazole (TZ), are promising candidates for highperformance solar cells.24] The $\sigma ^ { * }$ (Si)-n\* (C) bond interaction is demonstrated to not only effectively downshift the energy levels but also to improve the crystallinity of the copolymers and facilitate the charge transport.24,25] However, for the J71:ITICbased device, the unfavorable morphology with a relatively large phase separation limited the $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF enhancement.[25] Inspired by the morphology control on the BHJ by the random copolymerization, in this work, we introduced the benzo[1,2-c: 4,5-c|dithiophene-4,8-dione (BDD) block as the third unit (A2) into the J71 copolymer matrix to manipulate the energy levels, light absorption, molecular packing, and the eventually photovoltaic properties when blended with the ITIC acceptor. Two D-A1-D-A2-type random copolymers PBDT-TZ-BDD-1/19 and

PBDT-TZ-BDD-1/9 were prepared by a Si-containing (Si-BDT) as a D unit, a TZ as an A1 unit, and copolymerized with $5 \%$ or $10 \%$ mole ratios of BDD as an A2 unit. It can be found that due to the strong light absorption and strong electron affinity of the BDD block, incorporation of a BDD unit as the third unit (A2) can fine-tune the molecular frontier orbitals and improve light harvesting of the random copolymers. Nonperiodic sequence distribution of the random copolymers was discovered to effectively improve the compatibility with the ITIC acceptor, and the phase separation scale can also be appropriately reduced by controlling the content of the BDD unit during the random copolymerization process. More importantly, a BDD unit has a large conjugation plane, which could allow face-on orientation for efficient $\pi - \pi$ packing of the random copolymer for efficient charge transport, even with a nonperiodic sequence distribution. As a result, random copolymerization was revealed to dramatically boost the $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values for the solar cells. The best power conversion efficiency (PCE) of $1 1 . 0 2 \%$ was achieved for the PBDT-TZ-BDD$1 / 1 9$ : ITIC-based inverted device, which delivers ${ \approx } 1 6 \%$ improvement than the efficiency obtained from the J71: ITIC-based device.

Figure 1 and Figure S5, Supporting Information, show the ultraviolet visible near infrared (UV-vis-NIR) absorption spectra of the thin film and solution of the copolymers J71, PBDT-TZBDD-1/19, and PBDT-TZ-BDD-1/9, and their corresponding blend films with the ITIC acceptor. All copolymers showed the defined absorption profiles with two peaks at about 535 and $5 7 5 \mathrm { n m }$ , which can be ascribed to the vibronic bands associated with the (0-1) and (0-0) transitions, respectively. The (0-1) transition was attributed to the $\pi - \pi ^ { * }$ transition of the conjugated backbone, whereas the (o-o) transition was attributed to the intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) between electron-donating and electron-accepting units.19,2,27 The asorption of all copolymer films were red-shifted along with a stronger (0-0) transition peak in the long wavelength range from solution to film states (Figure 1 and Figure S5, Supporting Information). In general, random copolymers have a blue-shift absorption band with a lower coefficient than the alternating counterpart due to a nonperiodic sequence distribution, resulting in relatively weak interchain interactions.2,28-30 However, for both random copolymers PBDT-TZ-BDD-1/19 and PBDT-TZ-BDD-1/9, a slight redshift transition observed in the absorption spectroscopy relative to the alternative copolymer J71 (Figure 1a). Such a redshift phenomenon was more obvious with the increase in the BDD content.

Cyclic voltammetry was used to determine the highest occupied molecular orbital $( E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } } )$ and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital $\left( E _ { \mathrm { L U M O } } \right)$ levels of the copolymers. # 3. Conclusion

In conclusion, we developed two random copolymers by polymerization of Si-BDT with various proportions TZ and BDD. Incorporation of the BDD unit by random copolymerization into the copolymers effectively enhanced the light absorption of the copolymer and increased the compatibility of the copolymer with the ITIC acceptor, making the BHJ blend film the more reasonable phase separation scale. More interestingly, we also found that the addition of a small amount of the planar BDD unit by random copolymerization did not interrupt the face-on orientation arrangement but enhanced the $\pi - \pi$ stacking of the random copolymer. As a result, the $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } } ,$ FF, and PCE have been significantly improved for the random copolymer-based device than the alternative J71-based devices. In addition, the amount of BDD introduced is found to be carefully evaluated because too much third block will cause serious irregular sequence of the main chain, resulting in a decreased performance. These results demonstrated that random polymerization of a small amount of planar units into the highly crystalline polymer matrix is a facile and promising strategy to develop high-performance PSCs.
