# Achieving Balanced Crystallinity of Donor and Acceptor by Combining Blade-Coating and Ternary Strategies in Organic Solar Cells

Lin Zhang, Xianbin Xu, Baojun Lin, Heng Zhao, Tengfei Li, Jingming Xin, Zhaozhao Bi, Guanxian Qiu, Shengwei Guo, Ke Zhou, Xiaowei Zhan, and Wei Ma\*

As a prototype tool for slot-die coating, blade-coating exhibits excellent compatibility with Iarge-area roll-to-roll coating. A ternary organic solar cell based on PBDB-T:PTB7-Th:FOIC blends is fabricated by blade-coating and exhibits a power conversion efficiency of $1 2 . 0 2 \%$ , which is one of the highest values for the printed organic solar cells in ambient environment. It is demonstrated that blade-coating can enhance crystallization of these three materials, but the degree of induction is different (FOIC $>$ PBDB-T $>$ PTB7-Th). Thus, the blade-coated PBDB-T:FOIC device presents much higher electron mobility than hole mobility due to the very high crystallinity of FOIC. Upon the addition of PTB7-Th into the bladecoated PBDB-T:FOIC blends, the crystallinity of FOIC decreases together with the corresponding electron mobility, due to the better miscibility between PTB7-Th and FOIC. The ternary strategy not only maintains the well-matched crystallinity and mobilities, but also increases the photocurrent with complementary light absorption as well as the Forster resonant energy transfer. Furthermore, small domains with homogeneously distributed nanofibers are observed in favor of the exciton dissociation and charge transport. This combination of blade-coating and ternary strategies exhibits excellent synergistic effect in optimizing morphology, showing great potential in the large-area fabrication of highly efficient organic solar cells.

Bulk heterojunction (BHJ) organic solar cells (OsCs) have made great progress in recent years due to the rapid development of nonfullerene acceptors, which exhibit better tunability of energy levels and light absorption than the traditional fullerene acceptors.[1-4] Benefiting from the novel material design and morphology optimization as well as the device engineering, single junction Osc has achieved an impressive power conversion efficiency (PCE) of ${ > } 1 4 \%$ [5-8] However, most achievements are performed by experimental spin-coating on small-area devices, which is material consumptive and difficult to scale up to large-area fabrication.[9-11] Therefore, developing large-area manufacture technology of OsC is of great desire to satisfy the needs of wide application for OsCs in the near future. Fortunately, blade-coating (Figure 1b) serving as a prototype tool for slot-die coating exhibits excellent compatibility with large-area roll-to-roll coating.[9,12,13] Recently, the blade-coated device processed with ecofriendly solvents achieved a considerable PCE of $1 0 . 6 \%$ with a device area of $1 . 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ , showing a great prospect toward efficient large-area device fabrication.[14,15] It is well known that nanoscale morphology of phase separation, degree of intermixing, crystallization, and molecular orientation collectively affect the exciton generation and dissociation, as well as charge transport and extraction, so as to determine the photovoltaic performance.16-18] Interestingly, blade-coating can increase the degree of alignment in neat polymer thin film,[19] and slow blade-coating can induce more face-on orientation in fullerenebased OsCs as compared to spin-coating.[20] In addition, our previous study further demonstrated that blade-coating can inherently induce crystallization of both conjugated polymers and small molecular acceptors, which are beneficial for charge transport and extraction.21] Thus, blade-coating is deemed to be an efficient strategy to optimize the morphology of active layer in the process of fabricating OSCs.

Generally, charge carrier mobility is considered to be a pivotal characteristic to acquire high photovoltaic performance. Considerable research efforts have been dedicated to increase carrier mobility via material design and morphology optimization.[22] However, in the BHJ OSC, although some systems possess high electron mobility or hole mobility, the photovoltaic performance is also not too high, due to the unbalanced electron mobility and hole mobility.[23] For example, although the IDIC acceptor designed by Zhan and co-workers exhibits a much high electron mobility of $2 . 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { S } ^ { - 1 }$ the PCE based on the PDBT-T1:IDIC system is only $8 . 7 1 \%$ [23] Furthermore, in the PTB7-Th:ITIC system, the hole mobility is very high $( 2 . 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { S } ^ { - 1 } )$ , but the optimal device exhibits a PCE of $6 . 8 0 \%$ accompanied by a low fill factor (FF) of $5 9 \%$ [24] These poor photovoltaic performances derive from that the hole mobility or electron mobility is not big enough to maintain the balance, resulting in the charge accumulation. Therefore, aimed at balancing the hole mobility and electron mobility, increasing the relatively lower carrier mobility is of great significance to improve device performance.[24] Fortunately, crystallization is deemed to directly relate to the carrier mobility, and increasing crystallinity is considered to be an effective approach to improve carrier mobility.[25-27] As previously mentioned, the electron mobility is much lower than hole mobility in the PTB7-Th:ITIC device, so our group designed an in situ solvent annealing method to increase the electron mobility by improving the crystallinity of acceptor, leading to the improvement of device performance.[28] Hence, in order to achieve balanced mobility, the crystallizations of donor and acceptor should be finely controlled.

Ternary organic solar cells have emerged as a promising strategy to achieve high performance,l/ due to the enhanced light-harvesting efficiency with the complementary absorption of three materials. However, the prerequisite of improving photocurrent by using ternary strategy is to maintain efficient charge separation and transport properties. Impressively, the added third component can not only maintain the efficient optoelectronic process, but also improve the efficiency of the initial binary device through optimizing the morphology with controlling the crystallization and phase separation.[29] For example, a ternary system was designed by combining the characteristics of polymer with poor crystallinity and small molecule with strong crystallinity.[30] It was demonstrated that the $\pi - \pi$ stacking of PTB7-Th was improved by the incorporation of $p { \mathrm { - D T S } } ( \mathrm { F B T T H } _ { 2 } ) _ { 2 }$ into the PTB7-Th: $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ system. Similarly, a high crystalline small molecule BDT-3T-CNCOO was added into PBDTTPD-HT $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ blends to promote the crystallization of polymer, so as to facilitate the formation of bicontinuous interpenetrating network structure for highly efficient charge generation and transport.[31] In most cases, the addition of third component often increases the crystallinity of initial binary components. However, there are few effective approaches to reduce the crystallinity, especially for the nonfullerene system with both high crystalline polymer donor and small molecular acceptor. Therefore, an efficient strategy of controlling crystallization to maintain the balanced crystallinity and carrier mobility between donor and acceptor is of great desire for achieving high photovoltaic performance in the blade-coated ternary OSCs.

Herein, a balanced crystallinity of donor and acceptor was finely controlled by combining blade-coating and ternary strategies in the PBDB-T:PTB7-Th:FOIC ternary OSCs. It was demonstrated that blade-coating can induce crystallization of all these three materials accompanied by increased carrier mobility, and the degree of this induction is different (FOIC $>$ PBDB-T $>$ PTB7-Th). Combining blade-coating and ternary strategies, the PBDB-T:PTB7-Th:FOIC device exhibits a highest PCE of $1 2 . 0 2 \%$ , due to the balanced carrier mobilities and complementary light absorption as well as the Forster resonant energy transfer (FRET).

The crystallization coherence length (CCL) (Figure 1d) obtained from the 2D GIWAXS (Figure 1e) and the fitted 1D profile (Figure S1, Supporting Information) according to Scherrer equation[36] is a value that can quantify the crystallinity. All PBDB-T, FOIC, and PTB7-Th films exhibit highly ordered lamellar stacking (100) peak at low $q$ $( q = 0 . 2 9 \mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 }$ for PBDB-T, $q = 0 . 4 5 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ for FOIC, and $q = 0 . 2 6 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ for PTB7-Th) in the in-plane direction and pronounced $\pi - \pi$ stacking (010) peak at high $q$ position $( q = 1 . \bar { 6 } 7 \ \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ for PBDB-T, $q = 1 . 8 3 \ \mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 }$ for FOIC, and $q = 1 . 5 4 \mathrm { ~ \AA ^ { - 1 } }$ for PTB7-Th) in the out-of-plane direction, suggesting all these three organic semiconductor materials prefer a face-on orientation in the films. Notably, all the scattering peaks of blade-coated films become stronger and sharper than those of spin-coated films, indicating that bladecoating can induce a higher degree of molecular packing for these films. The calculated CCL values of spin-coated PBDB-T, FOIC, and PTB7-Th films are 2.10, 3.34, and $2 . 1 9 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. However, the corresponding blade-coated films present the CCL values of 2.20, 4.00, and $2 . 2 0 \mathrm { n m }$ signifying the strong induction of crystallization for FOiC and the weak induction effect for PTB7-Th $( \mathrm { F O I C } > \mathrm { P B D B } . \mathrm { T } > \mathrm { P T B } 7 . \mathrm { T h } )$

The blade-coated binary device based on PBDB-T:FOIC exhibits an average PCE of $1 0 . 7 0 \%$ with an open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ of $0 . 7 3 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ a short-circuit current $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ of $2 3 . 0 1 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , and an FF of $6 3 . 6 4 \%$ . As compared to the spin-coated devices, all the blade-coated devices exhibit better photovoltaic performance, especially for the improvement of FF, probably attributing to the improved morphology of active layer by blade-coating.

In order to better understand the improvement of photovoltaic performance, the charge carrier mobility was measured by using the space charge limited current method.37,38] As displayed in Figures S4 and S5 (Supporting Information) and Table 1 and Table S1 (Supporting Information), all the blade-coated devices exhibit higher electron mobility $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ and hole mobility $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } )$ than the spin-coated devices, attributing to the improvement of crystallization, so as to increase the FF of blade-coated devices. Upon the addition of increased PTB7-Th content into PBDB-T:FOIC devices, the electron mobility gradually decreases while the hole mobility first increases and then decreases, of which the variation may relate to the morphology. For the blade-coated devices, the improved $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ via the incorporation of $5 0 \%$ PTB7-Th into PBDB-T:FOIC blends can be confirmed by the external quantum efficiency (EQE) (Figure 2c) measurements, which match well with the UV-vis absorption spectra (Figure S6, Supporting Information) attributing to the complementary light absorption. Besides, the FRET should also be the reason of improved photovoltaic performance, demonstrated by the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and time-resolved PL (TRPL) (Figure S7, Supporting Information), due to the overlap between the absorption of PTB7-Th and the PL of PBDB-T (slash area in Figure 2d), as well as the overlap between the absorption of FOIC and the PL of PBDB-T (gray area in Figure 2d).[32] The charge carrier dynamics involving exciton dissociation and charge collection processes are performed to further clarify the improved photovoltaic performance.39-41] The exciton dissociation probabilities $P _ { i }$ determined by the normalized photocurrent density, $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ at the saturated current density) obtained from the profiles of $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ versus effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } ) ^ { [ 4 0 ] }$ (Figure 2e) were calculated to be $9 3 . 0 4 \%$ $9 4 . 5 7 \%$ and $9 3 . 0 0 \%$ for the blade-coated devices with $0 \%$ $5 0 \%$ , and $100 \%$ PTB7-Th content, respectively, revealing that the ternary device possesses the highest exciton dissociation and charge-extraction efficiencies. Generally, the light intensity dependent $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ is employed to study the nongeminate bimolecular recombination.39,41] As shown in Figure 2f, the fitted slopes $( \alpha )$ are 0.82, 0.90, and 0.87 for the blade-coated device with $0 \%$ $5 0 \%$ , and $100 \%$ PTB7-Th content, respectively, where 1 represents that all dissociated free carriers are collected at corresponding electrodes without charge recombination. This suggests that the blade-coated ternary device $( 5 0 \% )$ exhibits less nongeminate bimolecular recombination than the two binary devices $0 \%$ and $100 \%$ . Inspiringly, the PL spectra (Figure S8, Supporting Information) exhibit the weakest fluorescence intensity for the ternary film $( 5 0 \% )$ indicative of the low recombination and rapid charge transfer coinciding with the high and balanced carrier mobility as well as the optimal photovoltaic performance which may relate to the morphology of active layer.[39]

To correlate the improved photovoltaic performance and morphology, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and resonant soft X-ray scattering (RSoXs) were performed (Figure 3; Figure S9, Supporting Information). As shown in Figure 3, lots of fibril-like nanostructures through the whole film are observed in PBDB-T:FOIC $( 0 \% )$ film, which should be attributed to the crystalline PBDB-T polymer. Besides, another scale of phase separation with some large domains is visible contributing to the poor miscibility between PBDB-T and FOIC. However, the PTB7-Th:FOIC film exhibits almost one scale of domain, which is uniformly distributed in the whole film due to the relatively good miscibility between PTB7-Th and FOIC. Another difference is that there are almost no nanofibers due to the low crystallinity of PTB7-Th. Impressively, the nanofibers are kept in the ternary film $( 5 0 \% )$ while the large domains are absent. As is well known, the uniformly distributed nanofibers in BHJ are of great desire in the BHJ organic solar cells because it can facilitate charge generation and transport.[29,42] In the RSoXs measurement, an X-ray energy of $2 8 4 . 8 \ \mathrm { e V }$ was employed for the high scattering contrast of these organic materials.[43-46] Two scales of phase separations can be observed in PBDB-T:FOIC film due to the two scattering peaks, which is consistent with the TEM image. As expected, there is only one scattering peak in PTB7-Th:FOIC film indicating only one scale of phase separation. For the PBDB-T:PTB7-Th:FOIC ternary film, two scales of domains are observed, and the smaller one corresponding the scattering peak at $q ~ \approx ~ 0 . 0 7 4 { \mathrm { ~ \AA ^ { - 1 } ~ } }$ exhibits the smallest domain size $( 4 2 . 4 ~ \mathrm { n m } )$ among the three films. Moreover, the small domains dominate the whole film of PBDB-T:PTB7-Th:FOIC because of the pronounced scattering peak $( q \approx 0 . 0 7 4 \mathrm { \ : \mathring { A } ^ { - 1 } } )$ so that the large size domains can't be observed in the corresponding TEM image. Notably, the ternary film not only presents the smallest domain size, but also exhibits the highest purity (0.86 for PBDB-T:FOIC, 1.00 for PBDB-T:PTB7-Th:FOIC, and 0.97 for PTB7-Th:FOIC) according to the total scattering intensity analysis.[44]

In order to achieve the detailed information of molecular ordering and crystallization behavior, we performed the GIWAXs[35] measurements with various PTB7-Th contents (Figure 4; Figures S10 and S11, Supporting Information). All the blend films have preferential face-on orientation, evidenced by the pronounced (010) scattering peaks in the outof-plane profiles and the sharp (100) peaks in the in-plane direction. As compared to spin-coated films, all the bladecoated films exhibit stronger and more pronounced scattering peaks, indicating blade-coating can induce higher degree of molecular packing in the blend films. Meanwhile, it can be observed that the crystallinity of FOIC in the blade-coated films is much higher than that of spin-coated films (as shown in Figure S11 in the Supporting Information) while the crystallinity of PTB7-Th increases slightly, which agrees well with the improved crystallinity in the pure films (Figure 1d,e). Due to the similar alkyl side chain and $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance of PBDB-T and PTB7-Th, it is hard to differentiate the (100) peak and (010) peak. But the location of overlapped in-plane (100) peak at $q$ between 0.26 and $0 . 2 9 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ and the intensity of out-of-plane (100) peak show approximately linear variations as the PTB7-Th content increased due to the linear change of component for the ternary blend films. Notably, the crystallinity of FOIC decreases gradually with the increased PTB7- Th content, evidenced by the weakened peak 1 of FOIC and disappeared shoulder peak at $q \approx 0 . 4 2 \mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 }$ (the green arrow in Figure 4a). The crystallinity of acceptor and donor was quantified by fitting the peak 1 of FOIC and the overlapped (100) peak (at $q$ between 0.26 and $0 . 2 9 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 } \big )$ in the in-plane direction, respectively. As shown in Figure 4b,c, the variation trend of crystallinities agrees with the variation trend of carrier mobilities. Expectedly, the $5 0 \%$ specimen exhibits the balanced carrier mobilities and crystallinities of donor and acceptor, which may be the reason of the highest photovoltaic performance for the blade-coated ternary device with $5 0 \%$ PTB7-Th. To better understand the mechanism of morphology change, a clear and readily comprehensible diagram of blade-coated PBDB-T:FOIC film, PBDB-T:PTB7-Th:FOIC film, and PTB7-Th:FOIC film is shown in Figure 4d.

Considering the incorporation of PTB7-Th into PBDBT:FOIC blends can optimize the morphology of active layer by reducing the crystallinity of FOiC and decreasing the domain size of blend film; the contact angle measurement are employed to study the interaction and miscibility among different components.18,47,48] As shown in Figure 5 and Table 2, the surface free energy () calculated from the contact angles on water and diiodomethane are 44.1, 33.0, and $3 6 . 0 \ \mathrm { m N \ \bar { m } ^ { - 1 } }$ for FOIC, PBDB-T, and PTB7-Th, respectively. Therefore, the solubility parameters, [49] $( \delta )$ which are proportional to the square root of the surface energy, are calculated to be 24.36, 21.07, and 22.01 $\mathrm { M P a ^ { 1 / 2 } }$ . According to the Flory-Huggins model, the interaction parametersl50-52] (x) are calculated to be 1.01 for Xro1c, PBDB-r and 0.43 for Xro1c, PrB7-Th, respectively, suggesting the better miscibility between PTB7-Th and FOIC than that between PBDB-T and FOIC. As mentioned before, the crystallinity of FOIC is dramatically increased by bladecoating strategy in the PBDB-T:FOIC film, leading to much higher electron mobility than the hole mobility, and the addition of PTB7-Th into the blade-coated PBDB-T:FOIC blends reduces the crystallinity of FOIC. The reason is that the miscibility between PTB7-Th and FOIC is much better than that between PBDB-T and FOIC, resulting in a decreased electron mobility as well as much balanced electron mobility and hole mobility. Meanwhile, the domain size is also reduced upon the addition of PTB7-Th into PBDB-T:FOIC due to the better miscibility between PTB7-Th and FOIC. Furthermore, the differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) curves (Figure S12, Supporting Information) exhibit pronounced exothermal peaks in the heating process, which may be attributed to the cold crystallization.53-55] Apparently, as compared with the pristine FO1C, the cold crystallization temperature $( T _ { \mathrm { c c } } )$ of PTB7-Th:FOIC blends increases more than that of PBDB-T:FOIC blends, suggesting the better miscibility between PTB7-Th and FOIC than that of PBDB-T and FOIC. It agrees with the Flory-Huggins interaction parameters and can well explain the change of morphology and device performance.

In summary, we fabricated the ternary OsCs based on PBDB-T:PTB7-Th:FOIC blends with different PTB7-Th content by blade-coating in ambient environment. It was demonstrated that blade-coating can induce crystallization of all these three materials accompanied with increasing carrier mobility, but the degree of induction is different $( \mathrm { F O I C } > \mathrm { P B D B { \cdot } T } >$ PTB7-Th), resulting in an unbalanced mobility in the bladecoated PBDB-T:FOIC device, of which the electron mobility is much higher than the hole mobility. Upon the addition of PTB7-Th into the blade-coated PBDB-T:FOIC blends, the crystallinity of FOIC decreased together with the electron mobility, due to the better miscibility of PTB7-Th and FOIC than that of PBDB-T and FOIC, which provides more efficient charge transfer. Thus, the optimal blade-coated device based on PBDBT:PTB7-Th:FOIC blends at PTB7-Th content of $5 0 \%$ presents a highest PCE of $1 2 . 0 2 \%$ with a high FF of $6 5 . 7 8 \%$ . The addition of PTB7-Th not only maintains the well-matched mobility by balancing the crystallinity of donor and acceptor, but also increases the photocurrent with complementary light absorption and the Forster resonant energy transfer. In addition, small domains with homogeneously distributed nanofibers are observed in the ternary film, which facilitates exciton dissociation and charge transport. In future, for the other blade-coated nonfullerene devices, the unbalanced crystallinity and mobilities (small molecular acceptor $>$ polymer donor) can be optimized by adding a third component with well miscibility with acceptor to simultaneously achieve high current density and fill factor. This combination of blade-coating and ternary strategies exhibits excellent synergistic effect in optimizing morphology and improving device performance, showing a great potential in the large-area fabrication of highly efficient organic solar cells. Morphology evolution kinetics needs to be carefully considered in order to generalize this method.
