# Efficient and Scalable Large-Area Organic Solar Cells by Asymmetric Nonfullerene Acceptors Based on 9H-Indeno[1,2-b]pyrazine-2,3,8-Tricarbonitrile

Peddaboodi Gopikrishna, JinHyeong Rhee, Seongwon Yoon, DuHyeon Um, Hyunjung Jin, Yongseok Jun, Huijeong Choi, Hae Jung Son,\* and BongSoo Kim\*

It remains challenging to fabricate efficient, scalable large-area organic solar cells (OsCs) owing to the unfavorable morphology of photoactive blend flms. To address this challenge, two asymmetric nonfullerene acceptors (NFAs) IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F and IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl are synthesized, where 12,13-bis(2-butyloctyl)-3,9-diundecyl-12,13-dihydro-[1,2,5]thiadiazolo[3,4- e]thieno[2'",3'":4',5']thieno[2',3':4,5]pyrrolo[3,2-g]thieno[2' ${ \pmb 3 } ^ { \prime }$ :4,5]thieno-[3,2- b]indole (BBO) is the molecular core, and two types of end groups are appended to its ends, namely the 9H-indeno[1,2-b]pyrazine-2,3,8-tricarbonitrile (IPC1CN) end group and one of 2-(5,6-dihalo-3-oxo-2,3-dihydro-1H-inden-1-ylidene)malononitrile end groups (IC2F or IC2Cl). These NFAs facilitate effective tuning of light absorption and energy levels, offer high carrier mobilities, and allow for the formation of appropriate morphologies. Note that these benefits apply even to large-area devices, unlike typical Y6-based NFAs. In addition, a random copolymer PM6-PBDBT(55) is synthesized and its energy levels are optimally matched with those of the asymmetric NFAs. The blade-coated $\mathsf { 1 c m } ^ { 2 }$ -area OSCs based on PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl exhibit a PCE of $1 4 . 1 2 \%$ , which is higher than that of PM6-PBDBT(55)-IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F-based OSCs. More importantly, the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based large-area (58.50 $\mathsf { c m } ^ { 2 }$ modules yield an impressive PCE of 11. $28 \%$ with a small cell-to-module loss in fill factor. These results suggest that a combination of the asymmetric molecular design using the IPC1CN group and the terpolymer strategy will pave a new path for fabricating highly efficient and scalable large-area OSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Bulk-heterojunction organic solar cells (OSCs) have attracted considerable attention from both academia and industry owing to their advantages such as lowcost solution processibility, lightweight, semitransparency, and ability to form large-area devices.1-5] The photoactive film in an OsC consists of a nanoscopically phase-separated blend of $n$ -type and $p$ type organic materials. Over the past years, the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of OSCs have improved markedly owing to the development of photoactive materials.[6,7] Especially, nonfullerene acceptors (NFAs), which are $n$ type organic materials, have played a crucial role in improving the photovoltaic properties of OsCs.[8,9] NFAs have been developed actively for improving PCE because of their many favorable features, including high flexibility in terms of chemical structure modification, energy level tunability, and intense light absorption in the visible and near-infrared (NIR) region. Among the various types of NFAs, banana-shaped NFAs with the acceptor-donor-acceptor'-donor-acceptor (A-D-A'-D-A) structure have emerged as a prevailing class of NFAs, where A or $\aleph ^ { \prime }$ is an electron-deficient moiety, and D is an electron-rich moiety. In 2019, Yuan et al. reported a representative Y6 molecule (we call it IC2F-BEH-IC2F) composed of a fused-ring core, bulky alkyl side chains, and highly electron-deficient 2-(5,6-difluoro-3-oxo-2,3-dihydro-1H-inden-1- ylidene)malononitrile (IC2F) end groups. When IC2F-BEH-IC2F was blended with a representative $p$ -type polymer donor PM6, an impressive PCE of $1 5 . 7 \%$ was achieved.[10] Thereafter, the structure of Y6 was tuned by introducing linear or branched alkyl side chains of different lengths and modifying the core and end groups of the molecule. These efforts afforded PCEs of up to $1 9 \%$ for single-junction OSCs.[11-15]

Our group recently developed a tricyclic electron-deficient $9 H$ indeno[1,2-blpyrazine-2,3-dicarbonitrile (IPC) group and used it to replace one IC2F side group in the IC2F-BEH-IC2F to obtain an asymmetric end-group NFA (IPC-BEH-IC2F). When $p$ type PBDB-T polymer was blended with IPC-BEH-IC2F, a superior PCE was obtained compared to the PCEs of symmetric-NFA (i.e., IC2F-BEH-IC2F or IPC-BEH-IPC)-based OSCs owing to the resulting balanced short-circuit current $( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } )$ and open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ . Moreover, the strong intermolecular $\pi - \pi$ interaction between the tricyclic IPC end groups induced good long-term stability.[16] Nevertheless, IPC-BEH-IC2F has a higher-lying highest occupied molecular orbital (HOmO) level, which limits the selection of a good $p$ -type polymer donor for pairing with it. In general, the introduction of another electron-withdrawing group onto the end groups promotes the electron-deficient characteristic. Accordingly, the energy levels of the frontier orbitals of NFAs can be downshifted, which may increase the flexibility in terms of selecting high-performance polymer donors such as PM6 or D18, having lower HOMO levels than that of PBDB-T.[17,18] In this regard, halogenation and cyano group introduction in the end groups can be a good approach to modify the electronic properties of NFAs. For instance, fluorine substitution in the end group downshifts the HOMO and LUMO energy levels. In addition, it can enhance the intermolecular interactions and improve electron mobility as well, which is beneficial for realizing high $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and fill factor (FF).[19-22] Alternatively, chlorine substitution can promote strong intermolecular interactions because of the strong dipole moment of the C-Cl bond and the empty 3d orbital of chlorine, which can accommodate higher electron density. Hence, chlorination of the end groups can lower the energy levels to a greater extent than fluorination.[23] Furthermore, the introduction of cyano groups, in the end, groups can largely downshift the energy levels of the NFAs compared to that possible with halogenation owing to the stronger electron-withdrawing nature of the cyano group.[24] In this work, we carefully design asymmetrical NFA structures by employing IC2F or IC2Cl on the one end and an extra cyano-group-introduced IPC on the other end to ensure energy level tunability of the NFAs.

PBDB-T and its derivatives (e.g., PM6 or D18), which are synthesized by coupling two monomers, have been widely used as polymer donors in high-performance NFAbased OsCs. Recently, the random terpolymer strategy in which three monomers are used has yielded merits in terms of the fine-tuning of energy levels and alleviating the ag. gregation behavior of polymer donors.[25-29] For instance, Lu et al. synthesized three terpolymer donors (PL1, PL2, and PL3) by varying the 5,8-bis(4-(2-butyloctyl)thiophen-2- yl)dithieno[ $3 ^ { \prime } , 2 ^ { \prime } { : } 3 , 4 ; 2 ^ { \prime } { } ^ { \omega } , 3 ^ { \prime } { } ^ { \gg } ; 5 ,$ 6]benzo[1,2-c][1,2,5]thiadiazole

(DTBT) and 1,3-bis(2-ethylhexyl)-5,7-di(thiophen-2-yl)-4H,8Hbenzo[1 $\scriptstyle 2 - c : 4 , 5 - c ^ { \prime \prime } ]$ dithiophene-4,8-dione (BDD) acceptor ratios in the 4,8-bis(5-(2-ethylhexyl)-4-fluorothiophen-2-yl)benzo[1,2- $b { : } 4 , 5 { - } b { ^ \prime } 1$ dithiophene (BDT2F) polymer backbone, which were derived from the polymers PM6 and D18. The solubilities of these terpolymers in a nonhalogenated solvent (o-xylene) were superior to those of PM6 and D18, and the PL1-based OSCs presented the highest PCE of $1 8 . 1 4 \%$ with an improved $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 7 ~ \mathrm { V }$ relative to those of the PM6- and D18-based OSCs because of optimal phase separation and the fibrous-type morphology of the PL1:NFA blend film.[30] Herein, we synthesize a random terpolymer by using 4,8-bis(5-(2-ethylhexyl)thiophen2-yl)benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']dithiophene (BDT), BDT2F, and BDD monomers to tune the energy levels with respect to the energy levels, especially HOMO, of the newly synthesized NFAs and to demonstrate effective usage of the terpolymer strategy.

High-performance OsCs have been developed mostly for small-area $( < 0 . 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ devices, and their photoactive layers have been deposited by means of spin-coating, which allows for uniform film formation but is unsuitable for high-throughput production. Moreover, significant PCE drops were observed when these high-performance photoactive layers were used for fabricating large-area OSCs because of excessive aggregation and high roughness of the blend films.[31] This is a major challenge in the commercialization of OsCs. To overcome this challenge, the introduction of long-branched chains has been attempted.32] For instance, Dong et al. reported the DTY6 molecule by appending 2-decyltetradecyl (DT) side chains on the Y6 core in place of 2-ethylhexyl side chains. Because of the longer branched alkyl side chains, DTY6 exhibited improved solubility and effective suppression of excessive aggregation. Consequently, PM6:DTY6- based medium-area OsCs with a photoactive area of $1 8 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ yielded a PCE of $1 4 . 4 \%$ [33] Although this alkyl chain modification represents an important milestone, it is desirable to develop other means, especially by employing the ingenious design concept of NFAs.

In this work, we developed an end group of IPC1CN by introducing a strongly electron-withdrawing cyano group at the $8 ^ { \mathrm { { t h } } }$ position of the IPC structure and used it to synthesize two asymmetric NFAs, namely IPC1CNBBO-IC2F and IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl (Figure 1a). These molecules consisted of the core structure of 12,13-bis(2- butyloctyl)-3,9-diundecyl-12,13-dihydro-[1,2,5]thiadiazolo[3,4- e]thieno[2'" $, 3 ^ { \prime \prime \prime } { : } 4 ^ { \prime } , 5 ^ { \prime }$ thieno[2',3':4,5]pyrrolo[3,2- g|thieno $[ 2 ^ { \prime } , 3 ^ { \prime } { : } 4 , 5$ thieno[3,2-b]indole (BBO) and a combination of end groups (IPC1CN/IC2F or IPC1CN/2-(5,6-dichloro-3-oxo-2,3- dihydro-1H-inden-1-ylidene)malononitrile (IC2Cl), respectively). Upon introduction of the cyano group into the IPC, the HOMO and LUMO energy levels of IPC1CN deepened compared to those of the corresponding IPC end group, and the IC2Cl end group lowered the energy levels compared to those with the IC2F end group. Both the NFAs possessed highly planar structures with noncovalent intramolecular interactions $( \mathsf { S e o s e N }$ and $\mathrm { S } { \bullet } { \bullet } { \bullet } 0 )$ between the core and end groups. Moreover, the cyano group at the $8 ^ { \mathrm { t h } }$ position of the IPC end group can prevent the formation of NFA isomers, whereas the IPC end group yields two isomers.16] By employing the aforementioned combination of electron-withdrawing end groups, the light absorption, energy levels, and molecular packing of the IPC1CN-based molecules were finely tuned. Moreover, we synthesized a random copolymer PM6-PBDBT(55) composed of three monomeric units (BDT, BDT-2F, and BDD) in a ratio of 0.5:0.5:1 (Figure 1b). The energy levels of the PM6-PBDBT(55) polymer were between those of the PBDB-T and PM6 polymers with negligible changes in the light absorption spectrum. The asymmetric NFAs IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F and IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl exhibited better energy level matching with PM6-PBDBT(55) than with the well-known PM6 or PBDB-T. By using the synthesized PM6-PBDBT(55) and the two asymmetric NFAs, blade-coated OsCs with an active area of 1 $\mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ were fabricated. The PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Fbased OSCs yielded an improved PCE of $1 3 . 0 2 \%$ with a largely improved $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 4 . 3 8 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ relative to the $1 1 . 2 7 \%$ PCE of the PM6:IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F OSCs. The PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CNBBO-IC2Cl-based OSCs offered an even higher PCE of $1 4 . 1 2 \%$ with a further improved $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ value of $2 5 . 9 4 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . Moreover, the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based large-area modules with an active area of $5 8 . 5 0 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ yielded an excellent PCE of $1 1 . 2 8 \%$ . Notably, the PCEs were retained well with a minor change in the fill factor, even after the area extension from 1 $\mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ to $5 8 . 5 0 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ , which demonstrated the good scalability of the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based OSCs. Overall, the use of the IPC1CN end group served as an effective strategy for designing high-performance asymmetric NFAs, and it can be employed to realize efficient and scalable large-area organic solar modules.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Synthesis of IPC1CN, NFAs and PM6-PBDBT(55) and Thermal Properties

The chemical structures of the NFAs and polymer donors developed in this work are depicted in Figure 1. The synthetic routes of IPC1CN, the asymmetric NFAs, and PM6-PBDBT(55) are illustrated in Scheme S1, Supporting Information. The hydroxyimine group was first introduced to compound 1 by using isobutyl nitrite and hydrochloric acid, and it was subsequently converted to ketone through acid hydrolysis in the presence of formaldehyde. Then, compound 3 was reacted with 2,3-diamino malononitrile to produce the IPC1CN molecule. Separately, CHO-BBOCHO was synthesized as described in the literature.l34] IPC1CNBBO-CHO was synthesized through Knoevenagel condensation between CHO-BBO-CHO and IPC1CN. The target asymmetric NFAs were finally synthesized through Knoevenagel condensation between IPC1CN-BBO-CHO and IC2F or IC2Cl. The IPC1CN, IPC1CN-BBO-CHO, and asymmetric NFAs were characterized with H-nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), C-NMR, F-NMR, and matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-offlight mass analyses (Figures S1-S12, Supporting Information). Additionally, PM6-PBDBT(55) was prepared through Stille coupling, and the number-average molecular weight of the polymer was determined by means of gel permeation chromatography (Figures S13 and S14, Supporting Information). Details of the synthesis are provided in the Supporting Information.

Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) were used to investigate the thermal properties of the asymmetric NFAs (Figure S15, Supporting Information). Both asymmetric NFAs showed sufficient thermal stability for OsC applications, exhibiting less than a $5 \%$ weight loss at a temperature of $3 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ in TGA (Figure S15a, Supporting Information). In DSC, IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F exhibited a sharp endothermic peak at $2 4 2 . 4 9 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ with an enthalpy change of $1 8 . 2 5 \ \mathrm { J / g }$ By contrast, IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl did not exhibit any melting peak before $3 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ indicating that its intermolecular interactions may be stronger than those of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F (Figure S15b, Supporting Information).

# 2.2. Density Functional Theory Calculations

Density functional theory (DFT) calculations for the IPC1CN and asymmetric NFAs were performed at the B3LYP/6-31G(dp) level by using the Gaussian 09 program. As illustrated in Figure 2a, the optimized structures of IPC and IPC1CN possessed planar molecular geometries owing to the tricyclic fused ring framework. To investigate the electron density distributions of IPC and IPC1CN, their electrostatic potential surface (EPS) characteristics were analyzed using their respective optimized structures. The blue and red colors indicate the positive and negative electrostatic potential regions, respectively (Figure 2a). The positive electrostatic potential was spread throughout the molecule, and the negative electrostatic potential was present only on the nitrogen atoms, that is, cyano groups in the IPC end group. In other words, the electron-accepting ability of the IPC unit improved owing to the attachment of another cyano group to IPC, which lowered the HOMO and LUMO energy levels (from $- 7 . 1 1$ to $- 7 . 5 8 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and $- 2 . 8 4$ to $- 3 . 2 4 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively).

In addition, relaxed potential energy surface (PES) scans were performed to identify the stable conformer for these NFAs. TT-IPC1CN, which contains thieno[3,2-b]thiophene (TT), and IPC1CN moieties was selected as the model compound for these NFAs (Figure S16, Supporting Information). In our previous study, IPC-BEH-IC2F exhibited two isomeric forms (IPC-BEHIC2F-I and IPC-BEH-IC2F-III), which were formed because of the condensation reaction between IPC and CHO-BEH-CHO.[16] However, we believe that the introduction of a cyano group at the $8 ^ { \mathrm { t h } }$ position of IPC allows for only one conformer owing to steric hindrance between the cyano group of IPC1CN and the sulfur atom of TT. The PES scans of TT-IPC1CN were performed by means of rotation along the double bond between TT and IPC1CN. As illustrated in Figure S16, Supporting Information, the TT-IPC1CN-I conformer exhibited the lowest energy near the torsion angle of $0 ^ { \circ }$ , while the energy of the T'T-IPC1CN-III conformer was 48.93 kJ $\mathrm { m o l ^ { - 1 } }$ at the torsion angle of $1 8 0 ^ { \circ }$ owing to steric hindrance between the cyano group and the sulfur atom of TT. These results suggest that the conformer of TT-IPC1CN-I is the most stable. Accordingly, the chemical structures of IPC1CNBBO-IC2F and IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl, presented in Figure 2b, are the most stable conformers. Moreover, we confirmed from the HNMR analysis that both IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F and IPC1CN-BBO1C2Cl had only one stable conformer each (Figures S6 and S10, Supporting Information).

Further, DFT calculations were performed to predict the optimized geometries and electronic structures of the NFAs. To simplify the DFT calculations, the $n$ -undecyl and 2-butyloctyl side chains on the TT and pyrrole rings were replaced with methyl groups. As illustrated in Figure 2b, both NFAs possessed highly planar molecular geometries with small dihedral angles between the core and end groups, which might be because of possible noncovalent intramolecular interactions $( \mathsf { S e o s e N }$ and $\mathrm { S } { \bullet } { \bullet } { \bullet } \mathrm { O } \rangle$ between the $^ { \prime \prime } { \sf S } ^ { \prime \prime }$ atoms of the core and $ { \mathrm { ~  ~ \ell ~ } } ^ { \prime } \mathrm { ~ N ~ } ^ { \prime \prime }$ and $^ { \omega } \mathrm { O } ^ { n }$ atoms of the end groups. The planar molecular structure facilitates $\pi$ -electron delocalization and improves charge carrier transport.35] In addition, the core of the NFAs exhibited a slightly twisted conformation with the twist angles of $1 4 ^ { \circ }$ and $1 5 ^ { \circ }$ for IPC1CN-BBOIC2F and IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl, respectively, and these angles were close to that of IPC-BEH-IC2F $( 1 3 ^ { \circ } )$ . This finding suggests that the introduction of the cyano group in the end groups did not change the overall planarity of the NFAs. On the other hand, molecular dipole moments and energy levels were affected by the introduction of an extra cyano group in the IPC end group. The IPC end group exhibited a higher dipole moment of 8.98 D compared to that of the IPC1CN end group (6.44 D) because of an uneven charge distribution induced by the presence of electrondeficient nitrogen atoms and cyano groups in the pyrazine ring, which resulted in a more negatively charged pyrazine ring and a more positively charged phenyl ring. By contrast, the extra cyano group at the ${ 8 ^ { \mathrm { { t h } } } }$ position of IPC1CN reduced the positive charge on the phenyl ring, resulting in a lower dipole moment compared to that of IPC. Nevertheless, owing to the asymmetric chemical structure, both NFAs possessed still high dipole moments of 9.77 and 10.49 D for IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F and IPC1CN-BBOIC2Cl, respectively, which were slightly lower than that (13.30 D) of IPC-BEH-IC2F. The dipole moment of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl was higher by 0.72 D owing to the larger dipole of the C-Cl bond than that of the C-F bond. Typically, the high dipole moments of NFAs are beneficial for strong intermolecular $\pi - \pi$ stacking and efficient exciton dissociation in photoactive blend films.[36] As illustrated in Figure S17, Supporting Information, the electronic distributions in the HOMO and LUMO orbitals are mainly located on the core and end groups, respectively, implying efficient intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) from the core to the end groups. In addition, compared to IPC-BEH-IC2F, IPC1CN-BBOIC2F exhibited downshifted HOMO (from $- 5 . 4 5$ to $- 5 . 5 5 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ and LUMO (from $- 3 . 4 2$ to $- 3 . 5 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ ) energy levels, which indicated the stronger electron-withdrawing ability of IPC1CN relative to that of IPc.16] Benefiting from the strong electronaccepting ability of the chlorine atoms in IC2Cl, the HOMO and LUMO energy levels of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl downshifted further $_ { ( - 5 . 5 8 }$ and $- 3 . 5 6 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively). Overall, the theoretical results suggest that the introduction of the cyano group in the IPC and halogen atoms in the IC end groups can effectively tune the molecular properties of the synthesized NFAs.

# 2.3. Optical and Electrochemical Properties of the Active Materials

The ultraviolet-visible (Uv-Vis) absorption spectra of the asymmetric NFAs and polymer donors in chloroform solutions and films were recorded (Figure 3a), and the related parameters are listed in Table 1. In chloroform solution, both the NFAs exhibited similar absorption spectral shapes between 550 and $7 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ . The absorption maxima of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F and IPC1CN-BBOIC2Cl were located at 732 and $7 4 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. The molar extinction coefficient values increased from $1 . 7 4 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } ~ \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 }$ $\mathrm { c m ^ { - 1 } }$ for IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F to $1 . 8 7 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ for IPC1CNBBO-IC2Cl (Figure S18a, Supporting Information). In the film state, the absorption spectra of both NFAs were red-shifted; the absorption maxima of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F and IPC1CN-BBOIC2Cl shifted to 815 and $8 2 1 \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively, owing to the strong intermolecular $\pi \cdot \pi$ interactions in the film state compared to those in the solution state. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was performed to identify the electronic structures of the NFAs and polymer donors. The CV curves of the asymmetric NFAs are depicted in Figure 3b. The HOMO and LUMO energy levels were estimated from the onset oxidation and reduction potentials. The HOMO/LUMO levels of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F and IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl were $- 5 . 6 5 / - 3 . 8 9$ and $- 5 . 6 9 / - 3 . 9 1 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively. Benefiting from the strong electron-accepting ability of the chlorine atoms in IC2Cl, the HOMO and LUMO energy levels of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl were deeper than those of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F.[23] These results are consistent with the trend of the calculated theoretical energy levels. The HOMO energy levels of the PBDB-T, PM6-PBDBT(55), and PM6 polymers were $- 5 . 3 1$ $- 5 . 4 0$ , and $- 5 . 5 1 \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively (Figure S18c, Supporting Information). As the proportion of the BDT-2F moiety increased in the polymer backbone, the HOMO levels gradually downshifted owing to the electron-withdrawing nature of the fluorine atoms. The LUMO energy levels of the polymers were estimated from the difference between their HOMO levels and optical band gaps. The determined energy levels are depicted in Figure 3c and summarized in Table 1. Note that for the OSCs, the film absorption feature and energy levels of IPC1CNBBO-IC2F and IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl agree well with those of the PM6-PBDBT(55).

# 2.4. Photovoltaic Properties of Blade-Coated OsCs

To investigate the photovoltaic properties of the NFAs, large-area inverted-type OSCs $( 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ were fabricated with a device structure of ITO/ZnO/active layer/ $\mathsf { M o O } _ { 3 }$ /Ag using blade-coating technique. 1-chloronaphthalene (1-CN) was used as a solvent additive, and various conditions for fabricating OsCs, including processing solvent, donor-acceptor ratio, and the ratio of solvent additive, were carefully optimized. The optimum donor-acceptor ratio and the ratio of 1-CN was $1 { : } 1 \ \mathrm { w } / \mathrm { w }$ and $1 \ \mathrm { v o l . } \%$ , respectively, and the OsC processed with chloroform showed better performance than that processed with chlorobenzene (Figure S19 and Table S1, Supporting Information). The current density-voltage $\left( J - W \right)$ characteristics of the optimized OsCs are illustrated in Figure 4a and their photovoltaic parameters are summarized in

Table 2. As shown in Figure 4a, the resulting OSC yielded a PCE of $1 1 . 2 7 \%$ , with a $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 4 8 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 0 . 8 ~ \mathrm { m A ~ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and an FF of $6 3 . 7 6 \%$ , which are considerably lower than the corresponding values of the reported PM6-based OSCs.137,38] The lower PCE of the PM6:IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F-based OSCs can partly be attributed to the small energy offset of $0 . 1 4 \mathrm { e V }$ between the HOMO level of PM6 and that of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F, which may be inadequate for efficient charge transfer and separation at the polymer donor/NFA interface.

Therefore, the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F-based OSCs were fabricated by replacing PM6 with PM6-PBDBT(55), which has a slightly higher HOmO energy level $( - 5 . 4 0 \ : \mathrm { e V } )$ and, therefore, can provide a larger driving force for charge separation. The PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F blend film was prepared with the polymer donor/NFA weight ratio of 1:1. The PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F-based OSCs yielded an improved PCE of $1 3 . 0 2 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 3 3 \mathrm { V } ,$ $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 4 . 3 8 \mathrm { m A }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and an FF of $6 4 . 1 0 \%$ . The improved $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ indeed suggested that charge separation at the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBOIC2F interface occurred more efficiently than that at the PM6:IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F interface. The PCE is further increased to $1 4 . 1 2 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 3 6 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ a $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 5 . 9 4 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ and an FF of $6 5 . 0 9 \%$ when IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F was replaced with IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl. The PCE enhancement was mostly ascribed to the higher $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values. The increase in the FF may be attributed to the more favorable morphology of the blend film (the blend film morphology is discussed in the following section). To investigate the light-harvesting properties of the fabricated OSCs, external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of the best OsCs were recorded, and the corresponding spectral data are presented in Figure 4b. The IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based OSC exhibited a higher average EQE value than those of the IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F-based OSC over the visible and NIR ranges, as well as more red-shifted onsets (which is consistent with the wider light-absorbing feature of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl relative to that of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F). The $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ values calculated from the EQE spectra of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F- and IPC1CN-BBOIC2Cl-based OSCs were 21.93 and $2 3 . 9 9 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , respectively, which agree well with the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ trend obtained from the $J { - } V$ characteristics. The improvement in the EQE spectrum of the IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based OSC was mainly ascribed to the superior light-harvesting ability of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl, such as a more red-shifted absorption spectrum and greater absorption coefficient (Figures 3a and S18b, Supporting Information, respectively).

The PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl blend film exhibited even higher average hole and electron mobilities of $3 . 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ $( \mathrm { V s } ) ^ { - 1 }$ and $3 . 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { ( V s ) ^ { - 1 } }$ , respectively than those of the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F blend films $( 2 . 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } { \mathrm { c m } } ^ { 2 }$ $( \mathrm { V s } ) ^ { - 1 }$ and $2 . 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ( \mathrm { V s } ) ^ { - 1 } )$ . The more efficient charge transport in the IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based blend film owing to its superior charge carrier mobility can be related to the better $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF of the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based OSCs compared to those of the other types of OsCs, which would be beneficial for fabricating large-area devices.

To further understand the difference between the recombination behaviors of the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F- and PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based OSCs, their $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ values were collected under various irradiation light intensities $( P _ { \mathrm { i n } } \mathbf { s } )$ . The slope of the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } { - } P _ { \mathrm { i n } }$ plot is proportional to $n k _ { \mathrm { B } } T / e$ where $n$ $k _ { \mathrm { B } }$ $T ,$ and $^ { e }$ denote the ideality factor, Boltzmann's constant, temperature, and elementary charge, respectively.[39] The $n$ of an ideal OsC is unity, indicating that only bimolecular recombination is dominant. Typical OsCs have n values between 1 and 2, and Shockley-Read-Hall recombination (trap-assisted recombination) becomes dominant when $n$ is close to 2. The light-intensity dependence of the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the bladecoated OSCs is illustrated in Figure 4c. The slope of the IPC1CNBBO-IC2Cl-based OSC is smaller (i.e., $n = 1 . 2 7$ ) than that (i.e., $n$ $= 1 . 7 5 )$ of the IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F-based OSC. This means that trap-assisted charge recombination decreased in the IPC1CNBBO-IC2Cl-based OSCs. In addition, the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ depends on $P _ { \mathrm { i n } }$ in the relationship $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \propto ( P _ { \mathrm { i n } } ) ^ { \alpha }$ , where $\alpha$ is a recombination parameter related to the degree of bimolecular recombination.[40] $\alpha =$ 1 indicates the absence of bimolecular recombination. The $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } -$ $P _ { \mathrm { i n } }$ plot is shown in Figure S21, Supporting Information. The $\alpha$ value of the IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl based OSC (0.951) is greater than that of the IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F-based OSC (0.932), which indicates a lower degree of bimolecular recombination when using IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl. The suppressed recombination behavior of the IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based OSC may be attributed to its higher and more balanced charge carrier mobilities and better morphological features, which resulted in improved photovoltaic performance.

# 2.5. Morphology of the BHJ Films

The surface morphology of the optimized blend flms was investigated using atomic force microscopy (AFM). Topography images of the blade-coated blend films are presented in Figures 5a,b. Notably, the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl blend film possessed clearer and narrower fibrillar structures with a more uniform distribution on the surface than that of PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F. This feature may imply that IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl would form larger interfaces and more percolating networks between the polymer donor and acceptor domains, which is conducive to better charge transport, as confirmed by better hole and electron mobilities in Table 3 and Figure S20, Supporting Information. The root-mean-square roughness $( R _ { \mathrm { r m s } } )$ of the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl blend film was $1 . 3 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , which was smaller than that of PM6- PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F $( 1 . 6 7 \mathrm { n m } )$ . The lower $R _ { \mathrm { r m s } }$ value of the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl blend film could be attributed to the better-mixed blend morphology associated with more efficient charge separation.

To investigate the miscibility between PM6-PBDBT(55) and the asymmetric NFAs, the contact angles of water and ethylene glycol (EG) on the respective neat films were measured. Surface energy was calculated using the measured contact angles with dispersive and polar components.[41] Figure S22, Supporting Information, depicts the contact angles of water and EG on the PM6-PBDBT(55), IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F, and IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl films, and the calculated surface energy values are summarized in Table S2, Supporting Information. The difference between the surface energies of IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl (surface energy $= 3 6 . 5 7$ $\mathrm { m N \ m ^ { - 1 } }$ ) and PM6-PBDBT(55) $( 2 5 . 0 3 \mathrm { ~ m N ~ m ^ { - 1 } } ,$ was smaller than that between IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F $( 4 0 . 2 5 \mathrm { m N } \mathrm { m } ^ { - 1 }$ ) and PM6- PBDBT(55) $( 2 5 . 0 3 \mathrm { m N } \mathrm { m } ^ { - 1 } )$ . This aspect indicated that IPC1CNBBO-IC2Cl was more miscible with PM6-PBDBT(55), thereby resulting in the more well-mixed surface morphology depicted in Figure 5b.

# 2.6. Photovoltaic Properties of Large-Area Organic Solar Module

High device performance of the blade-coated PM6- PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based OSCs with an active area of $1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ was motivated us to fabricate a large-area organic solar module by using the abovementioned optimized device conditions. The organic solar module consisted of 10 sub-cells, and the total active area was $5 8 . 5 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ . Figure 6a depicts the current-voltage $( I { - } V )$ characteristics corresponding to the best performance of a PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based organic solar module. Impressively, the resulting large-area organic solar module yielded a remarkable PCE of $1 1 . 2 8 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ , a short-circuit current $( I _ { \mathrm { S C } } )$ , and an FF of 8.16 V, $1 2 9 . 2 \mathrm { m A }$ and $6 3 . 7 \%$ , respectively. To the best of our knowledge, this is one of the highest PCE values with the smallest cell-to-module loss in FF among the reported binary large-area organic solar modules with active areas exceeding $2 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ (Figure 6b and Table S5, Supporting Information). Notably, the FF of the organic solar module remained higher and similar to the average FF value $( 6 3 . 4 6 \% )$ of its $1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ OSCs. This result indicates that the morphology of PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl blend film can homogeneously form optimal morphology even in a large area over $5 0 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ by the blade-coating technique. This reproducible morphology of the PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl blend film is one of the most important factors that minimize the FF loss during the process of upscaling from cell to module.[43] Typically, FF loss can be caused by inhomogeneous phase separation, structural defects, and material aggregation, which are generated more often as the active area increases.44] Hence, it is essential to develop photoactive materials that can form the optimal blend film morphology in large-area organic solar modules to ensure the commercialization of OsC technology.

# 3. Conclusions

We synthesized two asymmetric NFAs (IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F and IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl) based on the IPC1CN/IC2F or IPC1CN/IC2Cl end groups to systematically investigate the effect of the cyano group in the IPC1CN and halogen atoms in the IC end groups of the NFAs on the photovoltaic properties of the OSCs. In addition, a PM6-PBDBT(55) copolymer donor was synthesized to maximize the benefits of the asymmetric NFAs. It was found to be better suited than the wellknown PM6 and PBDB-T polymer donors in terms of energy levels and charge separation. The PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO1C2F-based large area $( 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ OSCs yielded an improved PCE of $1 3 . 0 2 \%$ compared to that of the PM6:IPC1CN-BBO-IC2F (PCE $= 1 1 . 2 7 \% )$ control device. The PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBOIC2Cl-based OSCs yielded an even higher PCE of $1 4 . 1 2 \%$ with concurrent improvements in the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values. The enhanced photovoltaic properties of the asymmetric NFAs were attributed to our strategy of introducing electron-withdrawing groups, namely cyano group and halogen atoms, in the NFA structure. This led to efficient charge separation and high carrier mobility with a good control over miscibility in the polymer donor:NFA blend film. More importantly, the large-area organic solar modules based on PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBOIC2Cl with an active area of $5 8 . 5 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ yielded an excellent PCE of $1 1 . 2 8 \%$ The PM6-PBDBT(55):IPC1CN-BBO-IC2Cl-based module exhibited good scalability, retaining $80 \%$ of the PCE of its $1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ counterpart. Overall, these results demonstrated that rational chemical tuning of asymmetric NFAs through the introduction of electron-withdrawing groups and energetic tuning of polymer donors with the terpolymer strategy are effective methods for fabricating high-performance large-area OsCs with high scalability.
