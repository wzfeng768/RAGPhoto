By introducing acceptor groups to the terminal ends, it has been noticed that all the optoelectronic parameters of designed molecules have improved to a considerable extent when compared with the reference molecule, e.g., absorption properties, exciton mobility, molecular electrostatic potential (MEP), HOMO-LUMO band gap, light-harvesting efficiency (LHE), etc. Amongst all the designed molecules, T3 possesses the maximum absorption $( \lambda _ { \mathrm { m a x } } = 5 4 1 \ \mathrm { n m } )$ with the smallest bandgap (4.21 eV), least excitation and binding energy i.e., $( \mathrm { E _ { x } = 2 . 2 9 ~ e V } )$ and (1.92 eV) respectively, as well as the smallest interaction coefficient (0.5877). T1 with the least hole $( \lambda _ { \mathrm { h } } = 0 . 0 1 5 9 4 )$ and electron $( \lambda _ { \mathrm { e } } = 0 . 0 1 7 2 3 )$ reorganization energy values possesses better hole as well as electron mobility when compared with all other designed molecules. $\mathtt { V _ { O C } }$ is calculated by making a complex of investigated acceptor molecules with PTB7-Th donor molecules and the values obtained range from 2.27 to $3 . 2 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ Additionally, the T4 molecule has a maximum open-circuit voltage $( \mathsf { V } _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ of $2 . 6 9 \mathrm { e V }$ $\mathtt { V _ { O C } }$ is directly related to the normalized $\mathtt { V _ { O C } }$ and FF so, T4 also possesses proficient results for both (normalized $\mathrm { V _ { O C } = 1 0 4 . 0 5 3 8 ~ e V }$ and $\mathrm { F F } = 0 . 9 4 6 2 )$ . These results illustrate that the introduction of new acceptor groups to small molecules is worthwhile for organic solar cells (OSCs).

# 1. Introduction

The utilization of fossil fuels for energy production is a serious threat to the atmosphere, so the main focus of many researchers has shifted toward energy sources that are renewable [1]. As compared to fossil fuels, there are many efficient sources of renewable energy, e.g., hydropower, wind energy, solar energy, and geothermal energy [2]. Possibly the most energy-rich source, which is also free from carbon is solar energy. The amount of energy received by the Earth in a single hour $( 4 . 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 2 0 } \mathrm { J } )$ is more than the per year need of the Earth [3]. The utilization of solar energy is a productive substitute for fossil fuels as the sunlight is a simply utilizable, unpolluted, modest, rich as well as constant source of energy [4,5]. Photovoltaics (PVs) uses solar energy and convert them into electrical one, and solar energy is among the best potential energy sources that are renewable [6,7]. Solar cells (SCs) work by the absorption of photons, proceeding with the creation of electronhole pairs or combined called excitons, and then these pairs are separated and moved toward their corresponding electrodes for electricity production [8].

Solar cells based on silicon as well as other inorganic materials have been excessively utilized in the photovoltaic industry for the last many years because of their high power conversion efficiencies (PCE) of about $1 5 { - } 2 0 \%$ [9]. SCs of these types are effective, but because of their high expenses, complex synthetic approaches, and non-upgradable energy levels, they have left a substantial vacancy for organic solar cells [10]. For the last many years, organic solar cells (OsCs) have progressed a lot. OSCs are eco-friendly, semitransparent, and flexible, and because of these properties, the desire of scientists and financiers to initiate them on a much larger scale is constantly high [11,12]. Although, the PCEs of the best OsCs are still lower than that of perovskite or inorganic SCs. Generally, the PCE of an OSC has a direct relation with short-circuit current density $\left( \mathrm { J } _ { \mathrm { s c } } \right)$ , fill factor (FF) as well as open-circuit voltage $\mathrm { ( V _ { o c } ) }$ . Considerable enhancements have been attained in two $( \mathrm { J } _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FFs) out of those three factors [13,14].

OSCs are utilized due to their better morphology and enhanced optoelectronic properties after absorption of sunlight [15]. The chief method to manufacture the OsC is the p-n junction method [16]. Photovoltaic cells of a single layer are the simplest ones and possess a working efficiency of $^ { 1 5 \% }$ [17]. The second type of photovoltaic cell contains donor and acceptor in two layers for improved working efficiency. Bulk heterojunction (BHJ) is the third type, it is a mixture of donor and acceptor components placed between two electrodes, possesses high working efficiencies, and is slender as well as economical [18,19]. The efficiency of solar cells is greatly dependent upon the level of exciton separation as well as the separately shifting of charges toward their corresponding electrodes [20]. Furthermore, the proficiency of OSCs can be determined by some main aspects, i.e., fill factor (FF), short circuit current density $\left( \mathrm { J } _ { \mathrm { s c } } \right)$ and open-circuit voltage $\mathrm { ( V _ { o c } ) }$ [21]. The main types of OsCs, based on their acceptors attached, are fullerenebased OSCs and non-fullerene-based OSCs [22]. The PCE of fullerenebased [23] OsCs is restricted to much increase due to their characteristic limitations, i.e., poor light absorption in the region of UV -- Visible, high reactivity with oxygen, and fewer possibilities for the up-gradation of molecules by alteration in the energy levels [24-30]. Non-fullerenebased small molecular acceptors (SMAs) have placed the stage for the advancement in OSCs, with power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of more than $1 7 \%$ stated by many researchers. SMAs possess a variety of notable properties like their strong and adjustable absorption, easily modifiable energy levels as well as enhanced device and chemical stability. Many of these tremendous aspects are allowed due to the flexibility and facile fabrication of sMAs that often lead to a variety of possibly designed molecular structures [31]. Commonly known nonfullerene based small molecular acceptors are, [32] diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP), [33] rylene diimide, [34] and naphthalene diimides (NDI), etc [35].

This research intends to improve the optical properties of organic small molecules utilized in solar cells so, we have taken $\mathrm { D } { \cdot } \mathrm { A } _ { 1 }$ -D type reference R molecule containing core of 5,6-Difluoro-benzo[1,2,5]thiadiazole as an acceptor and alkyl substitutes bithiophenyl as side group donor [36] and designed five new $\mathbf { A _ { 2 } { \cdot } D { \cdot } A _ { 1 } { \cdot } D { \cdot } A _ { 2 } }$ type molecules T1-T5, by adding strong electron acceptors side group to the terminal ends of thiophenes i.e., 2-Methylene-melononitrile (T1), 2-(5,6-Difluoro-2- methylene-3-oxo-indan-1-ylidene)-malononitrile (T2), 2-(1-Chloro-5- methylene-6-oxo-5,6-dihydro-cyclopenta[c]thiophen-4-ylidene)-malononitrile (T3), 3-Methyl-5-methylene-2-thioxo-thiazolidin-4-one (T4) and 2-Cyano-acrylic acid methyl ester (T5) groups respectively, highlighted red color in Fig. 1. The designed molecules possess better optical aspects than the R molecule i.e. low band gap, remarkable light absorption with improved light harvesting efficiency, along with better charge mobility.

# 2. Computational studies

In this research, all the theoretical investigations were performed by using GaussView 6.0. [37] and Gaussian 09 w [38] programs. Because of the results' reliability, the density functional theory (DFT) method was employed to evaluate different aspects of reference as well as synthesized molecules, such as electronic, thermodynamic, vibrational, and spectroscopic ones [39]. Among the variety of available functionals of the DFT approach [40], ground state geometry of R was optimized with four disparate ones, i.e., B3LYP [41], CAM-B3LYP [42], MPW1PW91 [43], and oB97XD [44] (spin restricted) because of their accuracy and extensive utilization for the illustration of the optoelectronic properties of OSCs [45-49]. Two different basis sets (6-31G, 6-311G) with different solvents (Chloroform, 1,4-dioxane, chlorobenzene, and dichloromethane) were used to examine their influence on absorption spectra. The values of maximum absorption $\lambda _ { \mathrm { { m a x } } }$ with different solvents at both basis sets i.e., 6-31G and 6-311G given in Table 1. The combination of the basis set 6-31G (d, p) and chloroform solvent generated the most accurate results for both geometrical optimization and electrical performances [50-52]. So, this basis set along with the elected solvent (chloroform) was employed to meet the aspects of results' accuracy. Another shred of evidence towards the selection of chloroform solvent is the fact that it was used in the cited literature of reference molecule R as well.

TD-SCF method was employed to record the absorbance spectrum in solvent form [53], using Swizard program. To detect the solvent (Chloroform) effect, the IEFPCM [54] was applied with the TDDFTmethod. The Origin 6.0 software was used for the graphical representation of the UV-Visible absorption and easier estimation of the $\lambda _ { \mathrm { { m a x } } }$ [55]. The theoretical values of $\lambda _ { \mathrm { { m a x } } }$ of the R were compared with its experimental one [36]. The CAM-B3LYP functional with 6-31G (d, p) basis set was considered as the most reliable method for more investigation because it has the $\lambda _ { \operatorname* { m a x } }$ of $\mathbf { 4 7 5 \ n m }$ , which is the closest one to the experimentally performed value of $\mathbf { 5 2 0 \ n m }$ . By using the aforesaid functional and basis set, all the designed molecules T1-T5 were optimized at the ground as well as the excited state. Also, the same functional was utilized for the analysis of transition density matrix (TDM), reorganization energies (RE) and the density of states (DOs). The density of states DOs [56] was analyzed to perceive the effect of the absorption of radiation on every orbital, while PyMOlyze-1.1 [57] software was used to plot the spectrum of these DOS.

Two types of energies, intrinsic and extrinsic sphere energies sum up to form the total reorganization energies. Instead of determining the extrinsic sphere electronic charge for the calculation of activation free energy and the polarization properties, the intrinsic energies are investigated here. To elaborate on the intrinsic (electron and hole mobility), as well as intra-molecular charge mobility of the designed molecules [58], Marcus theory is applied [59]. In this research, the calculations of electron $\left( \lambda _ { \mathrm { e } } \right)$ and hole $( \lambda _ { \mathrm { h } } )$ RE for the $\mathbf { R }$ molecule and the designed T1-T5 molecules were performed by using the following equations (1) and (2) [60].

Thus, CAM-B3LYP method was selected as the best method for further investigation on all the developed molecules. # 3.2. Analysis of frontier molecular orbitals (FMOs)

The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOmO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LUmO) are collectively called the frontier molecular orbitals (FMOs). The analysis of FMOs is a mandatory step to investigate the optoelectronic properties of OsCs by exploring the charge density distribution on the molecule when some new moieties are introduced to it. On the absorption of a specific wavelength of light, the excitation of electrons take place from HOMO to LUMO intramolecularly The band gap is the measurement of the difference between these energy levels , represented as $\mathrm { E _ { g } }$ $( \mathrm { E } _ { \mathrm { g } } = \mathrm { E } _ { \mathrm { L U M O } ^ { - } } \mathrm { E } _ { \mathrm { H O M O } } )$ . The chemical reactivity increases if the bandgap is shorter, and a considerable increment in the charge transfer takes place that causes more photons to be absorbed, correspond to the increment in the PCE [62]. Fig. 4 shows the FMOs of all the investigated molecules estimated by using the DFT/CAM-B3LYP /6-31G (d, p) method.

In the five newly designed molecules (T1-T5), the acceptor core attached to the donor thiophene groups, that are further linked to the peripheral acceptor groups at the terminal ends, overall made the symmetry of $\mathbf { A _ { 2 } { \cdot } D { \cdot } A _ { 1 } { \cdot } D { \cdot } A _ { 2 } }$ The charge shift starts from the core to the thiophene donors and then moves towards the terminal acceptor groups, because of the existance of these robust electron-withdrawing acceptor moieties at the terminals, due to which, the delocalization of charge over the molecule increased through facilitation in the conjugation. Charge mobility in the developed molecules is also facilitated by their rigid and planner structure. Because of the absence of an electron acceptor group in the peripheries of the reference molecule R, the charge density in LUMO is concentrated in its central area. However, in the situation of HOMO, the charge accumulation is dispersed majorly over the whole molecule, except for the electronegative sulfur atom. The energy levels of HOMO and LUMO for R molecule are $- 6 . 2 6 \ \mathrm { ~ e V }$ and $- 1 . 6 8 \ \mathrm { \ e V }$ respectively. The energy levels of HOMO for the designed molecules T1- T5 are $- 7 . 0 1$ eV, $- 6 . 8 6 \ \mathrm { ~ e V } _ { ; }$ $- 6 . 8 4 \ \mathrm { ~ e V }$ $- 6 . 5 7$ eV and $- 6 . 7 5 \ \mathrm { ~ e V }$ respectively. While the energy levels of LUMO are $- 2 . 5 4 \mathrm { e V }$ $- 2 . 6 3 \mathrm { e V }$ $- 2 . 6 3 \mathrm { e V }$ $- 2 . 2 1 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and $- 2 . 2 5 \mathrm { e V }$ , respectively as given in the Table 3. The sequence of energy levels of HOMO for all the investigated values is $\mathbf { T 1 } < \mathbf { T 2 } < \mathbf { T 3 } < \mathbf { T 5 } < \mathbf { T 4 } < \mathbf { R } ,$ while the energy levels of LUMO have the sequence of $\mathbf { T 2 } = \mathbf { T 3 } < \mathbf { T 1 } < \mathbf { T 5 } < \mathbf { T 4 } < \mathbf { R } .$ The above-mentioned energy levels of HOMO and LUMO of T1-T5 molecules are lower than that of R molecule, indicating that the designed molecules possess improved optical properties, because a lower bandgap shown in Fig. 4. makes the sequence of $\mathbf { R } > \mathbf { T } \bar { \mathbf { 5 } } > \mathbf { T } \mathbf { 1 } > \mathbf { T } \mathbf { 4 } > \mathbf { T } \mathbf { 2 } > \mathbf { T } \mathbf { 3 } .$ Here, the existence of strong electron-withdrawing group 2-(1-Chloro-5-methylene-6-oxo-5,6-dihydro-cyclopenta[c]thiophen-4-ylidene)-malononitrile at the terminal site of the T3 molecule can be credited for its smallest bandgap (4.21 eV) amongst all. Other designed molecules still possess a narrower band gap than R , again because of the existence of robust electron-withdrawing acceptor end-groups. In the aspect of charge transfer properties of molecules, electron affinity (EA), as well as ionization potential (IP), matters a lot because both these are directly related to HOMO and LUMO respectively. # 3.9. Exciton binding energies and transition density matrix

After the absorption of a photon, chromophores generate excitons, i. e., a pair of charge carrier species. These charges are separated from each other for their successive transport to their respective electrodes. Organic molecules with significant conjugation possess robust Coulombic attractive forces among the charge carrier species because of their weak dielectric constant. As the binding energy is inversely proportional to exciton dissociation, this is a challenging dissociation. Attachment of new acceptor groups at the terminal ends of the designed molecules have resduced their Coulombic forces and subsequently, dissociation becomes facile [5]. Exciton binding energy $\left( \mathrm { E _ { b } } \right)$ is the least amount of energy that is needed for the generation of the free hole and electron careers. The value of $\mathrm { v _ { O C } }$ depends upon a variety of factors such as HOMO (donor) LUMO (acceptor) difference, charge carrier recombination, light intensities, charge mobility, as well as the temperature of the device.
