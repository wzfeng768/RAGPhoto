# Achieving 19% Power Conversion Efficiency in Planar-Mixed Heterojunction Organic Solar Cells Using a Pseudosymmetric Electron Acceptor

Wei Gao, Feng Qi, Zhengxing Peng, Francis R. Lin, Kui Jiang, Cheng Zhong,\* Werner Kaminsky, Zhiqiang Guan, Chun-Sing Lee, Tobin J. Marks, Harald Ade,\* and Alex K.-Y. Jen\*

A record power conversion efficiency (PCE) of over $19 \%$ is realized in planarmixed heterojunction (PMHJ) organic solar cells (OSCs) by adopting the asymmetric selenium substitution strategy in making a pseudosymmetric electron acceptor, BS3TSe-4F. The combined molecular asymmetry with more polarizable selenium substitution increases the dielectric constant of the D18/ BS3TSe-4F blend, helping lower the exciton binding energy. On the other hand, dimer packing in BS3TSe-4F is facilitated to enable free charge generation, helping more efficient exciton dissociation and lowering the radiative recombination loss $( \Delta E _ { 2 } )$ of OSCs. As a result, PMHJ OSCs based on D18/BS3TSe-4F achieve a PCE of $1 8 . 4 8 \%$ . By incorporating another mid-bandgap acceptor Y6-O into D18/BS3TSe-4F to form a ternary PMHJ, a higher open-circuit voltage $( \boldsymbol { v } _ { \infty } )$ can be achieved to realize an impressive PCE of $1 9 . 0 3 \%$ . The findings of using pseudosymmetric electron acceptors in enhancing device efficiency provides an effective way to develop highly efficient acceptor materials for OSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Organic solar cells (OsCs) are very promising due to their combined solution processability, light-weight, good mechanical flexibility, and tunable optical transparency, which are advantageous for applications in building-integrated photovoltaic (PV) and wearable electronics compared with conventional inorganic cells.1-5] To achieve high efficiency, it is essential to develop suitable donor (D) and acceptor (A) materials that can be easily processed into a finely phase-separated morphology in active layer with low energy loss $( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } )$ in OsCs.69] Recently, the vibrant progress made on nonfullerene acceptors (NFAs),[10,11] especially the Y-series NFAs,[12] with facilely tailored chemical structures and precisely tuned bandgap, energy levels, and crystallization properties, has enabled OSCs to achieve very high power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) ${ \approx } 1 9 \%$ [13-32]

The bulk-heterojunction (BHJ) architecture-based devices prepared from mixing D and A in solution is the most widely adopted method for depositing photoactive layer and have successfully increased the PCE of single-junction OSCs to $1 9 \%$ recently.[31] Another emerging strategy is to deposit neat D and A materials sequentially by using orthogonal solvents to realize a planar heterojunction-like configuration,[33] known as planar-mixed heterojunction (PMHJ) because the interdiffusion between two components will create a p-i-n-like three-layer structure. PMHJ presents several unique advantages: i) layer-by-layer processing allows D and A layers to be controlled and optimized independently; ii) separated holes and electrons respectively migrate into pure D and A domains, effectively reducing bimolecular recombination; iii) preferred vertical phase distribution significantly enhance the charge collection efficiency. These advantages, especially the more tunable and flexible processing conditions of PMHJ, are of great potential for scalable production of large-area OsCs.

While PMHJ architecture has received increasing popularity with Y6 derivatives based material systems,[34 41] the highest performance of reported PMHJ OSCs is still barely over $1 8 \%$ PCE,35,36] lagging behind the state-of-the-art BHJ ones which has the champion efficiency of $19 \%$ . An important consideration arises from the much less abundant $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ interfacial areas in PMHJ than those in BHJ, which strongly affects the exciton dissociation.(4243] However, if exciton dissociation can be guaranteed, the PMHJ architecture can potentially help improve transporting properties to elongate carrier lifetime and promote the charge collection by electrodes.37 Therefore, the key to realizing high-performance PMHJ devices lies on developing rationally designed materials that can improve the intrinsic exciton dissociation process.

Being well known by the research community, the success of Y6 lies on its unique packing pattern, which originated from the crescent-shaped molecular structure and electrondeficient benzo[c][1,2,5]thiadiazole moiety on the $\pi$ core.[12] On one hand, the continuous and highly regular 3D molecular packing framework of Y6 provides separated transporting channels for holes and electrons, respectively, distributed in central cores and end groups.27] On the other hand, such packing pattern leads to formation of different types of compact Y6 dimer stacking, which induce strong coupling between locally-excited state and charge-transfer (CT) state, promoting spontaneous exciton dissociation in Y6 in condensed phase.44 46] These very important findings help guide us to design materials that are intrinsically prone to generating free charges similar to inorganic semiconductors.

To realize this goal, we have rationally designed a pseudosymmetric electron acceptor, BS3TSe-4F (Figure 1a), combining our previous findings on selenium-substitution[47-53] and breaking the symmetry of Y6 derivatives.7,8,54 60] By regio-selectively replacing the two sulfur atoms in the central and lateral positions with selenium on the $\pi$ core of previously reported BP4T-4F,7 the typical molecular packing mode of Y6 derivatives is maintained and the exciton binding strength is further reduced for more efficient generation of free charges under illumination. For comparison, another symmetric acceptor S9TBO-F with selenium substitution only in the central position of $\pi$ core was also synthesized for comparison.

The integrated molecular design strategy of asymmetric selenium substitution redshifts the optical absorption of BS3TSe4F very slightly compared to that of S9TBO-F[61] but increases its dielectric constant to help reduce exciton binding strength in both neat acceptor film and donor/acceptor blend, which should improve the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of D18/BS3TSe-4F based PMHJ OSCs. The results from quantum chemical calculations based on the single-crystal structure of BS3TSe-4F reveal that it is more likely to generate free charge carriers due to the enhanced dimer stacking patterns arising from molecular asymmetry. This can help promote exciton dissociation and lower the $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of PMHJ OSCs through suppressed radiative $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } \ ( \Delta E _ { 2 } )$ . Moreover, the asymmetric selenium substitution of $\pi$ core endows BS3TSe-4F with proper crystallinity for achieving optimized morphology in D18/BS3TSe-4F blend. As a result, PMHJ OSCs based on D18/BS3TSe-4F achieved a $1 8 . 4 8 \%$ PCE, which is significantly higher than that of D18/S9TBO-F $( 1 7 . 7 \% )$ Furthermore, by incorporating a mid-bandgap acceptor $\mathrm { Y } 6  – \mathrm { O } ^ { [ 6 2 ] }$ $1 0 \%$ in weight) into D18/BS3TSe-4F to make a ternary composite, a higher open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ could be achieved to realize an impressive PCE of $1 9 . 0 3 \%$ . Our findings shed light on the design principles of acceptor molecules toward more efficient PMHJ OSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The two acceptor molecules S9TBO-F and BS3TSe-4F were synthetized according to the synthetic routes shown in Scheme S1 (Supporting Information). Considering the poor solubility of 4,7-dibromo-5,6-dinitrobenzo[c][1,2,5]selenadiazole in toluene, the Stille coupling reaction were optimized by first completely dissolving two reactants at high temperature and then controlling the reactivity with $\mathrm { P d } ( \mathrm { P P h } _ { 3 } ) _ { 2 } \mathrm { C l } _ { 2 }$ as a catalyst, offering a reasonable yield for the asymmetric intermediate compound 2. Compound 4 was further obtained via Stille coupling reaction between compound 2 and tributyl(6-nonylselenopheno[3,2-b] thiophen-2-yl)stannane. The latter synthetic procedures for targeted molecules were optimized from that of $\mathrm { Y } 6 ^ { [ 1 2 ] }$ except for the formylation step, which was accomplished by quenching the lithiated intermediate with N,N-dimethylformamide.

The optical absorption properties of S9TBO-F and BS3TSe4F in diluted solution and thin-films were investigated with their absorption profiles shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information); and Figure 1b, respectively, with the corresponding parameters summarized in Table 1. Due to more electron-rich selenium atom than sulfur atom, the more selenium-substituted $\pi$ core achieves a slightly enhanced electron-donating ability to give an ${ \approx } 1 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ redshift for BS3TSe-4F in solution and films. Both S9TBO-F and BS3TSe-4F exhibit strong near-infrared (NIR) absorbing properties with their onsets extending to 958 and $9 6 9 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , corresponding to an optical bandgap $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } } )$ of 1.294 and $1 . 2 8 0 ~ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively. Ultraviolet photoelectron spectrometry (UPS) was employed to accurately determine the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels of D18, S9TBOF, and BS3TSe-4F in condensed phase, which were identified as 5.24, $- 5 . 6 1$ , and $- 5 . 6 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ (Figure 1c), respectively, by fitting the UPS curves (Figure S2, Supporting Information). Moreover, the fundamental gaps $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { f u n d } } )$ of D18, S9TBO-F, and BS3TSe-4F were quantified by carrying out cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements which show oxidation/reduction potentials of $1 . 2 4 / - 1 . 0 0$ $1 . 3 3 / - 0 . 4 4 $ , and $1 . 3 1 / - 0 . 4 3 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ respectively (HOMO/LUMO energy levels estimated from CV method are $- 5 . 5 4 / - 3 . 3 0 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for D18, $- 5 . 6 3 / - 3 . 8 6 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for S9TBO-F, and $- 5 . 6 1 / - 3 . 8 7 \ : \mathrm { e V }$ for BS3TSe-4F, Figure S2, Supporting Information). $E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { f u n d } }$ of D18, S9TBO-F, and BS3TSe-4F were calculated to be 2.24, 1.77, and $1 . 7 4 \mathrm { e V } ,$ thus, corresponding to the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels of $- 3 . 0 0$ $- 3 . 8 4$ and $- 3 . 8 6 \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively, based on $E _ { \mathrm { L U M O } } = E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } } + E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { \ f u n } }$ 1 [63] The narrow $E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { { o p t } } }$ together with deeplying energy levels of S9TBO-F and BS3TSe-4F are advantageous for achieving good photovoltaic performance in OsC devices.

The exciton dissociation in organic absorbers is not only related to the driving force at $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ interface, but also largely dependent on the binding energy of excitons themselves. For example, excitons generated in silicon and perovskite solar cells have intrinsically small binding energies to facilitate the generation of free charges without external driving forces. On the other hand, the binding energy of excitons in organic semiconductors decreases as the dielectric constant of surroundings increases, according to the following Equation (1)[64]

$$
E _ { \mathrm { B } } ^ { \mathrm { C T } } = \frac { q ^ { 2 } } { 4 \pi \varepsilon _ { 0 } \varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } } r }
$$

where $q$ is the elemental charge, $\varepsilon _ { 0 }$ is the vacuum dielectric constant, $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ is the relative dielectric constant of surroundings, and $r$ is the average radius for electron-hole separation. Therefore, $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ as a function of frequency could be measured to provide insight for understanding the exciton binding strengths in different materials (details regarding measurement of $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ can be found in Figure S3, Supporting Information).

Figure 1d shows the $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ values of D18, S9TBO-F, BS3TSe-4F, D18/S9TBO-F, and D18/BS3TSe-4F, which are 3.94, 2.36, 2.50, 2.15, and 2.80, respectively, under the frequency of $1 0 ^ { 5 } ~ \mathrm { H z }$ . The average values of $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ for these neat and PMHJ films were also counted based on six or eight measured results as summarized in Table S1 (Supporting Information). The small enhanced $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ from S9TBO-F to BS3TSe-4F may result from slightly increased conjunction length and dipole moment of molecule endowed by asymmetric Se-substitution. Compared to D18/S9TBO-F PMHJ film that showed a low $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ even smaller than that of pure S9TBOF, D18/BS3TSe-4F PMHJ film showed a significantly larger $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ than that of BS3TSe-4F, implying a more effective interpenetration between D18 and pseudosymmetric acceptor, which is beneficial for high-quality $\mathtt { p - i - n }$ structure. Moreover, corresponding exciton binding energies (without considering polarization energy) can be estimated based on the retrieved $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ values by using approximate Equation (1) with $r = 1 . 5 \ \mathrm { n m } , ^ { [ 6 4 ] }$ giving $0 . 2 5 1 \mathrm { e V }$ for D18, $0 . 4 3 2 \mathrm { e V }$ for S9TBO-F, $0 . 4 0 8 \mathrm { e V }$ for BS3TSe-4F (the exciton binding energies of D18, S9TBO-F and BS3TSe-4F estimated from difference between $E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { f u n d } }$ and $E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { { o p t } } }$ are 0.310, 0.476, and $0 . 4 6 0 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively), $0 . 4 9 5 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for D18/S9TBO-F and 0.381 eV for D18/BS3TSe-4F (Figure 1e). The smaller exciton binding strength in D18 should facilitate electron transfer from D18 to acceptor and avoid charge recombination for hole transport in pure D18 domain, indicating the compatibility of D18 with the PMHJ architecture. Likewise, the significantly lower exciton binding energy of D18/BS3TSe4F than that of D18/S9TBO-F should benefit efficient exciton dissociation and suppress charge recombination in the blend to contribute to $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ improvement.

To gain more insights into the molecular microstructure and self-assembly, single-crystals of S9TBO-F and BS3TSe-4F were grown and resolved (Figures S4, S5, and Tables S2, S3, Supporting Information). It was found that both single-crystals of S9TBO-F and BS3TSe-4F present a monoclinic crystal system with a $\mathrm { C } 2 / \mathrm { c }$ space group and consist of a pair of M- and $P$ enantiomers originating from twisted backbone of central $\pi$ core. The $M / P$ enantiomer possesses D-A dihedral angles of $9 . 5 ( 8 ) ^ { \circ }$ and $3 . 6 ( 2 ) ^ { \circ }$ for S9TBO-F, and $9 . 5 ( 5 ) ^ { \circ }$ and $5 . 5 ( 0 ) ^ { \circ }$ for BS3TSe-4F, corresponding to S-O distance of 2.67(9) A and 2.63(8) A for S9TBO-F, and S-O distance of 2.58(1) A and Se-O distance of 2.36(1) A for BS3TSe-4F (Figure 2a). It was observed that asymmetric selenium substitution not only affects the interactions between the $\pi$ core and terminal groups of BS3TSe-4F (slightly increased D-A dihedral angles with decreased Se-O distance), but also flattens the $\pi$ core by reducing the torsion angle from $6 . 5 ( 7 ) ^ { \circ }$ to $5 . 3 ( 8 ) ^ { \circ }$ , which may result from enhanced quinoid character of multiple Se-substituted molecule for better molecular rigidity[65] and help form a more compact $\pi - \pi$ stacking between the molecules.

Meanwhile, the overall packing modes and long-range self-assembly in single-crystals of S9TBO-F and BS3TSe-4F remain similar to those typical patterns of Y6 derivatives.[47] Highly regular stacking of dimers established by interactions between $\pi$ cores and end groups in S9TBO-F and BS3TSe-4F can be clearly observed (Figure 2b,c). The $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances between $M / M$ (or $P / P )$ and $M / P$ enantiomer pairs are calculated to be 3.48(1) and 3.35(2) A (average $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance is 3.415 A) for S9TBO-F dimer, respectively, and 3.76(4) A and 3.23(2) A (average $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance is 3.495 A) for BS3TSe-4F dimers, respectively. The Se-N interaction distance between selenadiazole units of $M / M$ (or $P / P )$ enantiomer pairs are found to be 3.76(9) A for S9TBO-F and 3.97(9) A for BS3TSe-4F. Moreover, it was also observed that the pore size of BS3TSe-4F single crystal is 28.00(4) A in length and 19.63(0) A in width, which is slightly larger than that of S9TBO-F (25.79(6) A in length and 18.87(6) A in width). These slightly increased stacking distances and pore size of BS3TSe-4F are most likely caused by the larger atomic radius of Se than S atoms.

By fitting the 1D sector-averaged $( \pm 1 0 ^ { \circ } )$ scattering profiles (Figure 3h), the (100) diffraction peaks at $q \approx 0 . 4 1 1 / 0 . 4 0 7 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ $d$ spacing of 15.3/15.4 A) with coherence lengths (CLs) of $9 0 / 7 7 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ in in plane (IP) direction and (010) diffraction peaks at $q \approx 1 . 8 3 7 / 1 . 8 2 3 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { d }$ spacing of 3.42/3.44 A) with CLs of 17.6/17.4 A in out of plane (OoP) direction were recorded for S9TBO-F and BS3TSe-4F, respectively. It can be observed that the $\pi - \pi$ stacking spacings of S9TBO-F and BS3TSe-4F neat films are in good agreement with parameters retrieved from their single-crystal structures. The asymmetric selenium substitution only affects minimally the ordering of vertical $\pi - \pi$ stacking, but slightly reduces the ordering of horizontal lamellar packing of BS3TSe-4F film. Moreover, atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements were conducted to examine the surface morphology of S9TBO-F and BS3TSe4F neat films (Figure $3 \mathrm { d } \mathrm { - g } )$ , which revealed a more uniform and smoother surface of BS3TSe-4F than that of S9TBO-F. The appearance of coarser fibrous and granular structures on the surface of S9TBO-F film indicates that S9TBO-F has a faster crystallization rate and/or agglomeration tendency than that of pseudosymmetric BS3TSe-4F.

Silicon and perovskites can generate free charge carriers upon light irradiation, which facilitates them to achieve high efficiencies with low $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ [66] The results from ultrafast absorption experiments have shown that Y6-type acceptors are also able to generate free charge carriers under light irradiation, which enables them to be used for PMHJ devices.144 45] To figure out whether the asymmetric selenium substitution strategy can enhance the tendency of free charge generation of Bs3TSe4F molecule, we have combined theoretical simulations with single-crystal data (Figure 4). Figure 4a shows there are four different types of dimer packings in S9TBO-F and BS3TSe-4F single-crystals, which are named as Dimer A (half of $\pi$ core packed in parallel manner), Dimer B (end groups packed in antiparallel manner), Dimer C (half of $\pi$ core packed in antiparallel manner), and Dimer D (end groups packed in parallel manner). The asymmetric substitution of Se atom in BS3TSe4F results in indeterminate positions of lateral Se atom in its single-crystal, which gives rise to 16 possible dimer patterns.

According to the order of S and Se atoms at the lateral position on $\pi$ core of BS3TSe-4F, we defined S-Se-Se-S as the first type (represented by 1) of a certain dimer (A, B, C, and D). The following second (2), third (3), and fourth (4) types are, respectively, Se-S-Se-S, S-Se-S-Se, and Se-S-S-Se, which are shown in Figure S6 (Supporting Information). Density functional theory (DFT) simulations were employed to calculate the electron-hole overlapping (first excited state, S1) of the 20 dimers, where less electron-hole overlapping implies easier free charge generation inside dimers. Electron and hole distribution within dimer and corresponding electron-hole overlapping values were obtained and provided in Figure S7 (Supporting Information); and Figure 4c, respectively. It was found that only Dimer D in S9TBO-F single-crystal can achieve a small electron-hole overlapping (value of 0.313) for obvious charge separation (Figure 4b), while the other three types of dimers show large electron-hole overlapping (0.540 for A, 0.591 for B, and 0.604 for C).

It is worth to mention that the value of electron-hole overlap. ping does not seem to have much relationship with the degree of overlapping in molecular stacking, but should be more related to the packing mode of dimer, which is consistent with previous reports.45] However, the electron-hole overlapping of Dimer B (0.325 for B2 and B3) and Dimer C (0.464 for C1, 0.536 for C2, and C3) can be reduced by the asymmetric selenium substitution, in which B2 and B3 could achieve better charge separation. Moreover, the electron-hole overlapping of Dimer D can be further minimized in D2 (0.241) and D4 (0.306). Theoretical calculation results show that pseudosymmetric BS3TSe-4F has a stronger tendency to generate free charge carriers than S9TBO-F.

The introduction of Y6-O blueshifts the EQE spectrum of D18/BS3TSe4F devices, however, it improves the EQE response between 500 and $9 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ , leading to a comparable $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ $\mathrm { ~ ; ~ c ~ } ( 2 9 . 4 1 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ to the binary system. The EQE-integrated $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ for PMHJ OSCs based on D18/S9TBO-F, D18/BS3TSe-4F, and D18/BS3TSe-4F:Y6-O are 27.10, 27.64, and $2 8 . 1 2 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , respectively, which are consistent with the measured $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \mathrm { s }$ within acceptable error.

Additionally, the charge recombination and transporting properties of the two OsCs were examined. $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ dependence on light intensities $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ of both devices were obtained by measuring the $J { - } V$ curves under different $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ (Figure S10, Supporting Information). Linear fitting of $\ln ( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } ) { \cdot } \ln ( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ gave the slopes (S) of two curves that indicate the degree of bimolecular recombination, which are 0.996 for D18/S9TBO-F and 0.998 for D18/BS3TSe-4F. It is noteworthy that the lower degree of recombination degree benefited from PMHJ concept is conducive to realizing high FF. The charge carrier mobilities were further measured, where electron-only devices with a configuration of ITO/ZnO/D18/acceptor/PNDIT-F3N/Ag, and hole-only devices of ITO/MoO3/D18/acceptor $\mathrm { M o O } _ { 3 } / \mathrm { A l }$ were fabricated. By fitting the $J { - } V$ curves according to space-chargelimited current (SCLC) model (Figure S11, Supporting Information), electron $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ and hole $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } )$ mobilities of two diodes were obtained, which are $2 . 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 }$ and $1 . 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for D18/S9TBO-F, and $3 . 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 }$ and $5 . 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for D18/BS3TSe-4F with $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ of 2.3 and 0.6, respectively. The more balanced electron and hole transporting in D18/BS3TSe4T device can effectively reduce charge recombination and thereby enhance FF.

To better understand the effect of enhanced exciton dissociation on $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ highly sensitive EQE $\left( \mathrm { E Q E _ { P V } } \right)$ , and electroluminescence (EL) measurements were performed to analyze $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of S9TBO-F- and BS3TSe-4F-based OSCs (Figure 5d-f). The bandgaps $( E _ { \mathrm { g a p } } )$ of D18/S9TBO-F and D18/BS3TSe-4F were obtained by considering both EL and EQE spectra of two devices, to be 1.385 and $1 . 3 6 8 \ : \mathrm { e V } _ { \mathrm { { i } } }$ corresponding to $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } \mathrm { s }$ of $0 . 5 4 7$ and $0 . 5 4 0 \mathrm { e V }$ $( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } = E _ { \mathrm { g a p } } - q V _ { \mathrm { O C } } $ where $q$ is elementary charge), respectively. By fitting the low-energy part of highly sensitive EQE and EL spectra, CT state energy $( E _ { \mathrm { C T } } )$ was estimated to be $1 . 1 2 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for D18/S9TBO-F and $1 . 1 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for D18/BS3TSe-4F. Thus, the radiative recombination loss $( \Delta E _ { 1 } )$ defined as difference between $E _ { \mathrm { g a p } }$ and $E _ { \mathrm { C T } }$ were determined to be 0.266 and $0 . 2 6 5 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for D18/S9TBO-F and D18/BS3TSe-4F, respectively. Moreover, the electroluminescence EQE $( \mathrm { E Q E _ { E L } } )$ was also measured as shown in Figure 5f, which is $2 . 0 9 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ for D18/S9TBO-F and $1 . 8 7 3 \ \times \ 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ for D18/BS3TSe-4F. It was found that the D18/S9TBO-F-based device exhibits a higher $\mathrm { E Q E _ { E L } }$ than that of D18/BS3TSe-4F-based device, suggesting a slightly smaller nonradiative $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ $( \Delta E _ { 3 } = - k \mathrm { T l n } ( \mathrm { E Q E _ { E L } } )$ . where $k$ is the Boltzmann constant and $T$ is absolute temperature) of $0 . 2 1 9 \ \mathrm { e V }$ compared to $0 . 2 2 2 \mathrm { e V }$ for D18/BS3TSe-4F based device. To further explore the origin of larger $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ in pseudosymmetric acceptorbased PMHJ OSCs, the exponential fittings of sub-bandgap of $\mathrm { E Q E } _ { \mathrm { P V } }$ spectra were carried out based on the Urbach rule.[68] It was revealed that the PMHJ OSC based on D18/BS3TSe-4F shows a slightly higher Urbach energy $( E _ { \mathrm { U } } )$ of $2 3 . 0 ~ \mathrm { m e V }$ than that of D18/S9TBO-F-based OSCs $( 2 2 . 6 \ \mathrm { m e V } )$ , which indicates a negligible change of energetic disorder from symmetric to pseudosymmetric acceptors (Figure 5d,e) and also accounts for the slightly increased $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ . Importantly, these $E _ { \mathrm { U } }$ of two PMHJ OsCs are significantly lower than thermal energy $k _ { \mathrm { B } } \mathrm { T }$ $( 2 5 . 8 \mathrm { \ m e V } )$ at room temperature and are one of the lowest values among reported results,25] suggesting great potential of Se-substituted electron acceptors. Overall, although D18/BS3TSe-4F device has a higher $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ , its lower radiative recombination loss $\langle \Delta E _ { 2 }$ $E _ { 2 } , ~ 0 . 0 5 3 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ compared to $0 . 0 6 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for D18/S9TBO-F) helps compensate this disadvantage and leads to a lower $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ in its derived device.

Above discussions have illustrated the merits of asymmetric selenium substitution on influencing acceptor properties and photovoltaic performance. To further validate its effects on morphology of PMHJ active layer, the combined AFM, GIWAXS, and resonant soft X-ray scattering (RSoXS) characterizations were conducted (Figure 6). PMHJ active layers exhibit smooth and uniform surfaces with small root-mean-square (RMS) roughness ( $0 . 8 5 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ for D18/S9TBO-F and $0 . 8 6 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ for D18/BS3Tse-4F), which is good for forming ohmic contact with charge-transporting layers (Figure 6a,b). Importantly, PMHJ active layers show well-defined nanofibers with interpenetrating networks and high continuity and orientation, which is different from what commonly observed in BHJ morphology which should facilitate charge transport. Moreover, the incorporation of Y6-O into D18/BS3TSe-4F further compacts the nanofiber structure which may help reduce defects and flatten the surface for higher FF (Figure 6c).

GIWAXS data showed that D18/S9TBO-F, D18/BS3TSe-4F, and D18/BS3TSe-4F:Y6-O films all possess "face-on" orientation with a regular diffraction halo in the OoP direction (Figure 6d-f), which is beneficial to the vertical charge transport. Although the packing ordering of BS3TSe-4F neat film is slightly inferior to that of S9TBO-F, this was reversed in the PMHJ film. D18/ BS3TSe-4F film achieved larger CLs with similar packing distances (located at $q = 0 . 3 1 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ and $q = 1 . 8 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ ) compared to those of D18/S9TBO-F (CLs in IP and OoP directions are 77|20.4 A for D18/BS3TSe-4F and $6 4 / 1 9 . 4 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ for D18/S9TBO-F), indicating a better morphology compatibility between BS3TSe-4F and D18, which will facilitate for obtaining higher charge carrier mobilities in devices. The adding of Y6-O into BS3TSe-4F was found to improve the CLs of BS3TSe-4F to $8 7 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ for lamellar stacking and 18.1 A for $\pi - \pi$ stacking (Figure 3c,h), which further enhances the CL of 20.6 A for D18/BS3TSe-4F:Y6-O ternary film, benefiting FF improvement.

# 3. Conclusions

Pseudosymmetric electron acceptor BS3TSe-4F was designed and synthesized via asymmetric selenium substitution. Compared to the symmetric S9TBO-F, BS3TSe-4F shows enhanced dielectric constant, which facilitates exciton dissociation for the generation of free charge carriers to achieve higher EQE response and lower $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ Moreover, the asymmetric selenium substitution endows BS3TSe-4F with suitable crystallization properties to attain an optimized morphology. As a result,

PMHJ devices based on D18/BS3TSe-4F realized an impressive PCE of $1 8 . 4 8 \%$ with a $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ as high as $2 9 . 4 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . By incorporating Y6-O as a ternary additive further elevates the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and optimizes the morphology of D18/BS3TSe-4F to achieve a record PCE of over $1 9 \%$ for PMHJ OSCs. These encouraging results and insights generated by using asymmetric selenium substitution are very beneficial for further design of high-performance acceptors for PMHJ OSCs.
