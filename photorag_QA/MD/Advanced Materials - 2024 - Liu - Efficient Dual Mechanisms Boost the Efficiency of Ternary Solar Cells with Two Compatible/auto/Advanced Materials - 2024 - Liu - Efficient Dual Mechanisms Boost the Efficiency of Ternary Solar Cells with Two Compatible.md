# Efficient Dual Mechanisms Boost the Efficiency of Ternary Solar Cells with Two Compatible Polymer Donors to Exceed 19%

Shizhao Liu, Junjie Wang, Shuguang Wen, Fuzhen Bi, Qianqian Zhu,\* Chunpeng Yang, Chunming Yang,\* Junhao Chu, and Xichang Bao\*

Ternary strategyopens a simple avenue to improve the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic solar cells (OSCs). The introduction of wide bandgap polymer donors (PDs) as third component canbetter utilize sunlight and improve the mechanical and thermal stability of active layer. However, efficient ternary OSCs (TOSCs) with two PDs are rarely reported due to inferior compatibility and shortage of efficient PDs match with acceptors. Herein, two PDs-(PBB-F and PBB-Cl) are adopted in the dual-PDs ternary systems to explore the underlying mechanisms and improve their photovoltaic performance. The findings demonstrate that the third components exhibit excellent miscibility with PM6 and are embedded in the host donor to form alloy-like phase. A more profound mechanism for enhancing efficiency through dual mechanisms, that are the guest energy transfer to PM6 and charge transport at the donor/acceptor interface, has been proposed. Consequently, the PM6:PBB-Cl:BTP-eC9 TOSCs achieve PCE of over $19 \%$ . Furthermore, the TOsCs exhibit better thermal stability than that of binary OsCs due to the reduction in spatial site resistance resulting from a more tightly entangled long-chain structure. This work not only provides an effective approach to fabricate high-performance TOsCs, but also demonstrates the importance of developing dual compatible PD materials.

# 1. Introduction

Organic solar cells (OsCs) have garnered significant attention due to their advantageous features, including light-weight, ease of large-scale solution-processed fabrication, flexibility, and translucence.!1,2] Significant advancements have been achieved in recent years, and the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of OSCs have exceeded $1 9 \%$ due to materials innovation and device optimization.[3-6] However, organic conjugated materials often exhibit narrow absorption range with a full width at half maximum of approximately $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ , which imposes the limitations on photon harvest in traditional binary systems.17,8] The ternary strategy by introducing a third component in binary systems is one of the simple and effective ways to broaden optical absorption, which can optimize photon harvest in related ternary organic solar cells (TOsCs).[9-13]Recently, two small molecule acceptors have been widely used in the study of TOsCs due to their g00d compatibility.[1416] Despite the narrow band gap small molecule acceptors can extend absorption in the near infrared region, it remains challenging to achieve a wide and high spectral response across solar spectrum due to the fact that the maximum solar radiation is located at approximately $5 0 0 { - } 6 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ [17-20] Therefore, introducing materials that cover such band radiation will more efficiently to improve the performance of photovoltaic devices, and these materials are currently typically polymer donors (PDs).

As is widely recognized, TOsCs possess several operational mechanisms, such as Forster resonance energy transfer[21] and charge transfer.[22] Typically, only one operational mechanism is usually involved to improve device efficiency in ternary systems in the current literature.23.24] Moreover, the limited range of energy transfer and the significantly faster energy transfer process at 10 ps compared to charge transfer within the few 100 ps necessitate close proximity and substantial interfaces between the "energy donor" and the "energy acceptor".[25,26] According to the aforementioned requirements for time scales of transmission, energy transfer may require closer distances compared to charge transfer. However, due to the entanglement between long polymer chains caused by complex chain behaviors, introducing PDs as the third component into the host system can lead to larger steric hindrance, which can disrupt energy transfer feasible channels, thereby failure of two PDs-based TOSCs. Recently, a number of efforts have been made by researchers to alleviate this problem.27-291 For instance, Zhang et al. have developed the alloy structure that enhances the efficiency of two PDs-based to $1 9 . 1 3 \%$ [30] In addition, Liu et al. demonstrated the cell with an efficiency of $1 9 . 3 \%$ based on a dual fiber network structure using ternary donor-acceptor morphology.[31] For two acceptors based TOSCs, not only is the compatibility of small molecules generally good, but charge transfer also has a relatively long diffusion life. Additionally, the guest-host compatibility and guest location exhibit less sensitivity compared to those in two PDs-based TOSCs.5-32] This could potentially elucidate the numerous successful cases of two acceptors based TOsCs. Therefore, a comprehensive investigation into the interplay relationships and inherent enhancement mechanisms in TOsCs are still highly desired to gain a deeper understanding of the structure-function relationship. Hence, it can be seen that reasonable design and selection of materials, achieving complementary absorption spectra, matched energy level, and fine-tuned micromorphology are important issues for further improving the performance of TOSCs. In this work, two PDs PBB-F and PBB-Cl based on benzo(1,2- d:4,5-d')dithiazole (BBT) as acceptor unit were selected, which exhibit excellent absorption within strong solar radiation.133] And the PD PM6 and small molecular acceptor BTP-eC9 were selected as the host system. Due to the excellent miscibility and intermolecular interactions between two PDs, excessive aggregation can be effectively prevented, which leads to the attainment of appropriate phase separation and thereby inhibits carrier recombination. Meanwhile, there is an efficient energy transfer between the host donor and third component, providing additional exciton dissociation pathways. In addition, each component has good crystallinity, which forms close packing of materials and constructs network structure conducive to charge transport, thereby enhancing the charge transfer ability. Hence, the observed efficient dual mechanisms drive the PCE of TOSC with PBB-Cl to over $1 9 \%$ with enhanced high short circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } )$ $( 2 8 . 5 3 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } } .$ , open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ of $0 . 8 5 3 \mathrm { V } ,$ and fill factor (FF) of $7 8 . 2 3 \%$ . Additionally, the thermal stabilities of TOSCs based on PBB-F and PBB-Cl exhibit significant enhancements compared to the binary OSCs. The TOSCs with PBB-Cl exhibited remarkable retention of over $90 \%$ PCE even after undergoing $7 2 0 \mathrm { h }$ at $6 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ surpassing the binary OSC (500 h). This is attributed to the good interactions between the two PDs, resulting in tightly entangled long chains that enhance spacefilling density within the active layer. The findings provide valuable insights for the widespread applications of two PDs system in future TOSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The chemical structures of the materials used in this study are depicted in Figure 1a. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was employed to determine the energy levels of PBB-F and PBB-Cl (Figure S1, Supporting Information). It is observed that PBB-F and PBB-Cl possess deeper HOMO energy levels compared to that of PM6, as shown in Figure 1b. Compared with PM6, the guest PDs show a downshifted HOMO level, implying that the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of TOSCs should be higher than that of binary devices. The lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy cascades for the PM6, PBB-Cl, and BTP-eC9 are conducive to charge extraction and transport. The UV-Vis absorption spectra of the PM6, PBB-F, PBB-Cl, and BTP-eC9 neat films and the related ternary blend films with $1 5 \mathrm { \ w t \% }$ PBB-F or PBB-Cl were measured, as depicted in Figure 1c,d. PBB-F and PBB-Cl exhibit similar absorption region, and the addition of PBB-Cl (or PBB-F) into the PM6:BTPeC9 blends can not only enhance absorption at about $5 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ but also achieve higher molar absorption coefficients (Figure 1d, Figures S2 and S3, Supporting Information), which contribute to enhancing $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of the TOSCs.

To elucidate the influence of guest PDs on the photovoltaic performance, a series of OsCs with a conventional configuration of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/active layer/PDINN[34]/Ag were prepared, as shown in Figure 2a. The $J { - } V$ curves of the binary devices and ternary devices with PBB-F and PBB-Cl are shown in Figure 2b and Figure S4 (Supporting Information), and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are listed in Table 1. The optimal binary PM6:BTP-eC9 OSC demonstrates a PCE of $1 7 . 7 9 \%$ that is comparable to the previous report,35] with $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ % $2 7 . 4 1 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 } , V _ { 0 }$ of $0 . 8 3 8 \mathrm { V } ,$ and FF of $7 7 . 4 8 \%$ while for PBB-F and PBB-Cl-based binary OSC, the inferior PCEs of $1 0 . 9 6 \%$ and $1 0 . 9 9 \%$ are yielded. Notably, PBB-Cl:BTP-eC9-based OSC exhibits a higher $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of 0.910 V. As presented in Tables S1 and S2 (Supporting Information), TOsCs were fabricated by adjusting the proportion of guest PDs in the PM6:BTP-eC9 host devices. After adding $1 5 \mathrm { w t \% }$ PBB-Cl (compared to host donor), the optimized TOsC shows an outstanding PCE of $1 9 . 0 4 \%$ with $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 8 . 5 3 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 5 3 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ and FF of $7 8 . 2 3 \%$ , to be one of the highest PCEs in the ternary systems based on two PDs. The external quantum efficiencies (EQEs) of corresponding devices are presented in Figure 2c and Table 1, where the integrated $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } } \mathrm { \bf s }$ are in accordance with those of $J { - } V$ measurements. With the incorporation of PBB-Cl, the EQE values in the range of $5 0 0 { - } 6 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ are improved, which agree with the absorptivity of the ternary blend film, contributing to the achieved $\mathrm { \ h i g h { \it J _ { \mathrm { S C } } } }$ . Nevertheless, it should be noted that the lower responses at full regions of binary OSCs often suffer from inferior photocurrents, ascribed to the reduced molecular aggregation, enlarged stacking spacing, and incomplete absorptions. What's more, the increased $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ can be well explained by the lower HOMO level of PBB-Cl than that of PM6. With the increase in PBB-F or PBB-Cl content from 10 to 50 $\mathrm { w t \% }$ the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } \mathrm { s }$ in TOsCs show a gradual improvement, exhibiting a quasi-linear dependence on PBB-F/Cl ratios, which suggests the presence of the alloy-like mode in ternary cells (Figures S5,S6, Supporting Information). In addition, the incorporation of PBB-Cl can effectively optimize the morphology of the active layer, which consequently enhances charge transfer and extraction, thereby improves FF in TOsCs. At the same time, the fuorine-substituted donor polymer as the third component can also improve all important photovoltaic parameters of related devices. The optimized TOsCs containing $1 5 ~ \mathrm { w t \% }$ PBB-F in the donor materials demonstrate a PCE of $1 8 . 6 9 \%$ , with $\mathrm { \ h i g h \ } J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 8 . 1 6 ~ \mathrm { m A ~ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , improved $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 4 8 \mathrm { V } ,$ and enhanced FF of $7 8 . 2 8 \%$ . Furthermore, the repeated experimental data was statistically analyzed, as shown in Figure 2d. The strategy with introduce guest PDs can significantly improve the device performance with good repeatability. Furthermore, the efficiency of the unencapsulated PBB-Cl based TOSC in $\Nu _ { 2 }$ -filled glove box can be maintained at over $90 \%$ for more than $7 0 0 \mathrm { { h } }$ at $6 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (Figure 2e), owing to the enhanced entanglement and reduced slip resulting from two PDs-based active layer. More encouragingly, as suggested the PCE against FF was plotted in Figure 2f and Table S3 (Supporting Information), the efficiency of PBB-Cl-based TOSC ranks the highest value among two PDs TOSCs, which declares the great potential for practical application.

To discern the work mechanisms of PBB-F and PBB-Cl based ternary systems, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra of PM6, PBB-F, PBB-Cl, and PM6:PBB-F and PM6:PBB-Cl $( w / w = 1 { : } 0 . 1 5 )$ films were measured. As shown in Figure 3a,b, the main characteristic emission peak of PBB-Cl neat film is located at $6 2 5 \mathrm { n m }$ when excited at $5 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ , whereas PM6 neat film exhibits a characteristic emission peak at $6 7 5 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . And the partially overlapping emission spectrum of PBB-Cl (or PBB-F) with the absorption spectrum of PM6 suggests the potential for energy transfer from PBB-Cl (or PBB-F) to PM6, in accordance with Forster theory.[36] Interestingly, adding PBB-Cl into PM6 enhances the PL peak of PM6, with the quenching of the PL intensity of PBB-Cl at the same time, proving efficient Forster resonance energy transfer from PBB-Cl to PM6. To determine whether incidental charge transfer occurs from PBB-Cl to PM6, the $J { - } V$ curves of devices based on PBB-Cl, PM6, and PBBCl:PM6 were probed, as exhibited in Figure 3c. The $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ values of the devices with PM6:PBB-Cl as active layer are between those with PM6 and PBB-Cl as active layer, manifesting the insignificant charge transfer at PBB-Cl/PM6 interfaces. Additionally, both the binary and ternary blended films were excited at $5 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ to further determine the charge dissociation situations. It can be seen that the emission of donors can be almost completely quenched by PM6 (Figure S7, Supporting Information), indicating efficient electron transfer occurs in both binary and ternary devices. It should be noted that more effective emission quenching can be observed after the addition of $1 5 \ \mathrm { w t \% }$ PBB-Cl into PM6:BTP-eC9 blend film. This indicates that there is good charge transfer at the interface of PM6 and PBB-Cl with BTP-eC9.[37] Thus, according to the PL results mentioned above, when accompanied by two phenomena, namely the significant enhancement of PM6 excitation peak with the addition of PBB-Cl in two PDs-based films and the extinguishment enhancement of the PM6 with PBB-Cl added in PM6:BTP-eC9 blend film, the working mechanisms that promote improvement in $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } } \mathrm { s }$ in TOSCs often involve dual mechanisms (Figure 3d). PBB-Cl can not only directly generate excitons and dissociate at PBB-Cl/BTP-eC9 interfaces but also transfer its excited energy state to PM6, enabling efficient charge separation at PM6/BTP-eC9 interfaces. Interestingly, PBB-F has similar results and phenomena as the third component.

To understand the influence of different PDs PBB-F and PBB-Cl on the exciton dissociation and charge collection behaviors, the photocurrent density $( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } )$ as a function of the effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ curves of the devices was employed.138] As displayed in Figure 4a, the charge generation and collection probability $P ( E , T )$ was calculated using the equation $P ( E , T )$ $= J _ { \mathrm { p h } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } ,$ where $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ is the saturated photocurrent density under $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ approaches $1 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ or more. Contrastingly, the $P ( E , T ) s$ of the binary and PBB-F and PBB-Cl-based TOSCs are $9 6 . 3 \%$

$9 7 . 2 \%$ , and $9 8 . 3 \%$ , respectively, demonstrating more efficient exciton dissociation of TOsCs, which is conducive to get high $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF. And, charge transfer kinetics was studied by timeresolved photoluminescence (TRPL), with decay curves shown in Figure 4b and relevant data summarized in Table S4 (Supporting Information). The PBB-Cl-based ternary blend has an accelerated emission decay with fuorescence lifetime () calculated to be 0.89 ns, which is smaller than those of binary (1.14 ns), and PBB-F-based ternary blend (1.06 ns), and suggests more comprehensive exciton dissociation and faster charge transfer at donor/acceptor (D/A) interfaces. Thus, adding PBB-Cl into PM6:BTP-eC9 blend is conducive to enhance charge collection and transfer, which is consistent with the acquired $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of each device.

The electron/hole mobilities $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } )$ of the devices based on binary and ternary devices are measured by space charge limited current (sCLC) method with device structures of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/active layer, $/ \mathrm { M o O } _ { 3 } / \mathrm { A g }$ and $\mathrm { I T O } / \mathrm { Z n O } _ { I }$ active layer/PDINN/Ag, respectively, and the results are shown in Figure 4c, Figure S8 and Table S4 (Supporting Information). Despite the same batch of BTP-eC9 acceptor, the binary and ternary devices exhibit largely different $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ value. Notably, the PBB-Clbased TOSC exhibits a $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ of $5 . 9 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of $5 . 7 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively, to be the highest values among the three devices. Of note is that the PBB-F-based and PBB-Cl-based TOSCs display a $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ ratio of 1.06 and 1.03, respectively, which are more balanced than that of the binary device (1.16), facilitating the charge extraction and collection, thus guaranteeing outstanding FF in the device.139]

Furthermore, to investigate the impact of PBB-F or PBB-Cl on the charge recombination behavior, the dependence of J-V on the light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ was measured. The $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } { - } P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } { - } P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ curves are described in Figure 4d,e, respectively. The relationship between $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ can be expressed as the equation of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } \propto$ $n k T / q$ ln $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ , where $k , T ,$ and $q$ represent Boltzmann constant, Kelvin temperature, and elementary charge, respectively.[40] The $n$ values of PM6:BTP-eC9, and TOSCs with PBB-F and PBB-Cl are 1.12, 1.09, and 1.09, respectively. The $n$ values of PBB-Clbased TosCs exhibit a closer to 1, indicating the effective suppression of trap recombination in the TOsCs. Subsequently, the relationship between $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ can be expressed as the equation of $\begin{array} { r } { J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \propto P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } { } ^ { \alpha } } \end{array}$ [41] The $\alpha$ values of PM6:BTP-eC9-based binary, PBB-F and PBB-Cl-based TOSCs are 0.96, 0.97, and 0.99, respectively. The larger $\alpha$ values indicate that bimolecular recombination can be effectively inhibited. Subsequently, to further figure out the charge extraction and recombination mechanisms of different OSCs, the transient photocurrent (TPC) and transient photovoltage (TPV) are shown in Figure 4f and Figure S9 (Supporting Information), and the detailed parameters are listed in Table S3 (Supporting Information). The charge extraction time $( \tau _ { \mathrm { e x t } } )$ values estimated from fittings TPC decays under short-circuit conditions for the binary, TOSCs with PBB-F and PBB-Cl are fitted to be 3.79, 1.63, and 0.89 s, respectively. Obviously, the shortened $\tau _ { \mathrm { e x t } }$ in PBB-Cl-based TOSCs indicate faster charge extraction process and promoted efficiency of charge sweep-out than that of binary devices. The estimated photocarrier lifetimes $( \tau _ { \mathrm { r e c } } )$ of PM6:BTP-eC9, TOSCs with PBB-F and PBB-Cl are 41.6, 45.0, and $5 0 . 6 \mu \mathrm { s }$ respectively. The ternary devices obtained longer $\tau _ { \mathrm { r e c } }$ is likely to stem from the ameliorative charge recombination in comparison with the binary devices, which is in accord with the enhancement of FF.[42]

To discuss the impact of PBB-Cl and PBB-F on the morphology of blend films, atomic force microscopy (AFM) was used to study surface morphology. As depicted inFigure 5a-c, All AFM images and corresponding phase images (Figure S10, Supporting Information) demonstrate comparable fibrous microstructures. The binary, PBB-F-based, and PBB-Cl-based ternary films all show smooth surface morphology with root-mean square roughness $( R _ { \mathrm { q } } )$ values of 0.95, 1.15, and $1 . 3 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. The slight enhancement in roughness by introducing PBB-Cl could be attributed to the improved crystallinities of the blends, thus facilitating charge transport. The components are well blended in ternary systems with no large aggregates observed, enabling sufficient contact area between donors and acceptor, which corresponds well with the efficient exciton dissociation probed by PL. Furthermore, the transmission electron microscopy (TEM) bright-field images (Figure 5d-f) of the three films show delicate bright and dark regime interpenetration, indicating the uniform distribution of donor and acceptor. Subsequently, it can be observed that the ternary film displays a more uniform and distinct bi-continuous network in comparison with the binary device and should be one aspect to the favorable performance as confirmed above. Based on the above underlying driving forces for distributing the guest PD in the ternary matrix, we explored a wider range of applications for this third component polymer. Therefore, the results indicate that PBB-Cl has good universality in state-of-the-art PDs.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, we screened and studied a class of wide bandgap PDs PBB-Cl and PBB-F applied to TOSCs with two PDs. The excellent compatibility between the two PDs resulted in the formation of alloy-like phase structures, and a more fine-divided uniform network with slightly enhanced crystalline occurs in the ternary blend film, which can form more excellent charge transfer channels. More interestingly, the synergistic effect of efficient energy transfer and charge transfer dual mechanisms has been revealed in depth, the third polymer component can effectively enhance the performance of TOsCs. Meanwhile, compared with the host donor PM6, the guest PD PBB-Cl possesses a downshifted HOMO level and blue-shifted absorption with higher maximum absorption coefficients, and the PCE of PM6:PBB-Cl:BTP-eC9 ternary solar cell reached $1 9 . 0 4 \%$ , significantly higher than that of the binary OsCs, and is one of the highest values of TOSCs based on two PDs. In addition, the improvements of compatibility enhance the thermal stability of TOsC, and the PCEs of the PBB-Cl-based TOSCs were still higher than $90 \%$ after annealing at $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $7 2 0 \mathrm { { h } }$ . This result indicates that reasonable design of compatible PDs and development strategies with multiple work mechanisms have important guiding significance for improving the efficiency of TOsCs.
