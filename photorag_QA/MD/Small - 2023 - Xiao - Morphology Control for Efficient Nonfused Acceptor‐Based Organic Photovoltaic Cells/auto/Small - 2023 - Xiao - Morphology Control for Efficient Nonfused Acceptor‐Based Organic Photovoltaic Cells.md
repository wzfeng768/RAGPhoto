# Morphology Control for Efficient Nonfused Acceptor-Based Organic Photovoltaic Cells

Yang Xiao, Huifeng Yao,\* Zhihao Chen, Ni Yang, Chang Eun Song, Jingwen Wang, Zi Li, Yue Yu, Du Hyeon Ryu, Won Suk Shin, Xiaotao Hao, and Jianhui Hou\*

Non-fused electron acceptors have huge advantages in fabricating low-cost organic photovoltaic (OPV) cells. However, morphology control is a challenge as non-fused C--C single bonds bring more molecular conformations. Here, by selecting two typical polymer donors, PBDB-TF and PBQx-TF, the blend morphologies and its impacts on the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of non-fused acceptor-based OPV cells are studied. A selenium-containing non-fused acceptor named ASe-5 is designed. The results suggest that PBQx-TF has a lower miscibility with ASe-5 when compared with PBDB-TF. Additionally, the polymer networks may form earlier in the PBQx-TF:ASe-5 blend film due to stronger preaggregation performance, leading to a more obvious phase separation. The PBQx-TF:ASe-5 blend film shows faster charge transfer and suppressed charge recombination. As a result, the PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based device records a good PCE of $1 4 . 7 \%$ with a higher fill factor (FF) of 0.744, while the PBDB-TF:ASe-5-based device only obtains a moderate PCE of $12 . 3 \%$ with a relatively low FF of 0.662. The work demonstrates that the selection of donors plays a crucial role in controlling the blend morphology and thus improving the PCEs of non-fused acceptor-based OPV cells.

# 1. Introduction

Organic photovoltaic (OPV) cells are a promising technology for harnessing solar energy with the advantages of flexibility, spectral tunability, and solution processability.[1-5] Most highly efficient OPV cells adopt multilayer structures, and the photon-to-electron conversion mainly occurs in the active layer.[6] Generally, the active layer consists of blended $p$ type electron donors and $n$ -type

Seleniumcontaining (Se-containing) nfEAs show redshifted absorption spectra, higher carrier mobilities, and lower nonradiative energy loss compared with their thiophene analogs, which has proved as an effective modification strategy.[32] Meanwhile, although the introduction of Se atoms may increase the synthetic cost, its cost will still be lower than that of fEAs.[33]

A prominent feature of nfEAs is that their aromatic rings are linked by rotatable ${ \mathrm { C } } { \mathrm { - } } { \mathrm { C } }$ single bonds.[30] To ensure a planar conjugated backbone, two strategies, including noncovalent interactions and large steric hindrance groups, are usually introduced to increase the rotational barrier.[26,28] Even so, morphology control of nfEA-based blends remains a huge challenge, impeding the improvement of PCEs. PBDB-TF and PBQx-TF are two representative polymer donors (Figure 1a), and both of them achieved outstanding PCEs, making them excellent candidates for fabricating high-efficiency nfEA-based OPV cells.[15,34] Although both polymers possess the preaggregation effect in solution, the thermodynamics and kinetics of the film-forming process are quite different, which will affect the blend morphology greatly. Therefore, it is highly needed to explore the morphology evolution of blend films that consist of these polymers and nfEAs.

In this work, we synthesized a Se-containing nfEA named ASe5 and studied the influence of blend morphology on the photovoltaic performance, trying to provide a feasible guideline for donor selection for highly efficient nfEA-based OPVs. The in situ ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption and photoluminescence (PL) spectra results suggest that the aggregation of ASe-5 experiences a longer time when blended with PBQx-TF, and more ordered intermolecular packing of ASe-5 in PBQx-TF:ASe-5 blend film was formed. In addition, compared with PBDB-TF, PBQxTF shows a suppressed miscibility with ASe-5, which is beneficial for forming suitable phase separation. Pronounced fibrous morphology is observed in the PBQx-TF-based blend film. Transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy measurements show faster hole transfer and more inhibited charge recombination in the PBQxTF:ASe-5 blend film. In the OPV cells, PBQx-TF:ASe-5 combination records a high PCE of $1 4 . 7 \%$ with a fill factor (FF) of 0.744, which is much higher than that of PBDB-TF:ASe-5 $( 1 2 . 3 \% )$ . Our work demonstrates that morphology modulation has great influences on the photovoltaic performance of polymer donor: nfEA systems.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Synthesis

Compared with their thiophene counterparts, Se-containing materials have redshifted absorption spectra and higher carrier mobility, which will be beneficial for achieving higher short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } )$ [32] In addition, the chlorination of terminal groups is beneficial for further expanding the absorption spectrum and reducing energy loss.35] To achieve both high $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and low energy loss $( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } )$ , here, we designed and synthesized a Se-containing nfEAs with

1,1-dicyanomethylene-3-indanone (IC-2Cl) as the terminal group, named ASe-5 (Figure 1a). The synthetic route of ASe5 is shown in Scheme S1 (Supporting Information). 3-(2- butyloctyl)selenophene (compound 1) was prepared according to the similar procedures of our previous literature.331 Starting with compound 1, bromination of the 2-position of compound 1 was performed via N-Bromosuccinimide. Then, the aldehyde group was introduced to the 5-position of compound 1 by lithium diisopropylamide and N,N-Dimethylformamide, thereby the important intermediate 5-bromo-4-(2-butyloctyl)selenophene-2- carbaldehyde (compound 3) was obtained. A Stille cross-coupling between compounds 3 and 4 afforded the dialdehyde intermediates (compound 5) with a yield of $8 5 \%$ . Finally, ASe-5 was synthesized by the Knoevenagel reaction using the electronwithdrawing terminal group IC-2Cl. More detailed synthesis and characterization of the chemical structure are provided in the Supporting Information. ASe-5 has excellent solubility in chloroform, toluene, chlorobenzene, and o-xylene, and exhibits good processability. Thermogravimetric analysis was applied to evaluate the thermal stability of the ASe-5 (Figure S1, Supporting Information), and the result shows that the thermal degradation temperature at $5 \%$ weight loss is $3 6 3 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ To understand the impact of the introduction of Se atoms on the cost of nfEAs, we calculated the material-only cost (MOC) of ASe-5 according to previous literature.[30] The MOCs of Y6, A4T-16, and ASe-5 are estimated to be 146.9, 89.3, and $1 0 3 . 1 \ \ S \ \mathrm { g } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively (Scheme S2 and Tables S1 and S2, Supporting Information). The result suggests that the incorporation of Se atoms leads to a slight increase in the cost when compared to its thiophene analog A4T-16. However, it is noteworthy that the cost of nfEAs (A4T-16 and ASe-5) remains significantly lower than that of fEAs (Y6).

# 2.2. Single Crystal Structure

We conducted the single crystal X-ray diffraction measurement to determine the stacking model of ASe-5. As displayed in Figure 1b; and Figure S2 (Supporting Information), the two central thiophene units are in the same plane, which should be ascribed to the large steric hindrance effect of triisopropylbenzene. The triisopropylbenzene is nearly perpendicular to the thiophene with a dihedral angle of $8 8 . 0 ^ { \circ }$ The torsion angle between thiophene and selenophene is $3 0 . 4 ^ { \circ }$ . In addition, a dihedral angle of $1 . 1 7 ^ { \circ }$ is observed between selenophene and the terminal group. We find that not only the terminal groups are involved in $\pi - \pi$ stacking, but the $\pi$ bridges also exhibit partial participation. ASe5 forms a 2D stacking structure and the $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance is ${ \approx } 3 . 4 0 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$

# 2.3. Optical and Electrochemical Properties

The UV-vis absorption spectra of materials are shown in Figures 1c and S3a (Supporting Information). In diluted chloroform solution, ASe-5 exhibits an absorption peak at ${ \approx } 7 2 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ and an absorption edge at around $8 2 2 \mathrm { n m }$ . From the dilute solution to the thin film, the absorption edge of ASe-5 extends to ${ \approx } 9 0 8 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , indicating an optical bandgap $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { \ o p t } } )$ of $1 . 3 7 \mathrm { e V } .$ The main absorption bands of PBDB-TF and PBQx-TF films are in the range of

500-700 and $4 0 0 { \mathrm { - } } 6 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. The absorption ranges between the donors and ASe-5 are well complementary. Meanwhile, the absorption coefficients of the thin films are measured (Figure S3b, Supporting Information). Cyclic voltammetry measurements were performed to determine the electrochemical energy levels of the two polymer donors and ASe-5 (Figure 1d). The ionization potential (IP) of PBDB-TF is $- 5 . 4 9 \mathrm { e V } ,$ slightly deeper than that of PBQx-TF $( - 5 . 3 6 ~ \mathrm { e V } )$ . The electron affinity (EA)/IP of ASe-5 is found to be $- 4 . 0 1 / - 5 . 7 0 ~ \mathrm { e V } ,$ demonstrating a good match with the two polymer donors.

# 2.4. Photovoltaic Performance

We fabricated OPV cells using a conventional device structure of ITO/poly(3,4-ethylene-dioxythiophene):(polystyrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS)/donors:ASe-5/PDINN/Ag to assess the photovoltaic performance of ASe-5 when blended with PBDB-TF and PBQx-TF. The processing conditions, such as solvents, donor/acceptor ratios, additives, and annealing conditions, were meticulously optimized to achieve optimal photovoltaic performance (Tables S3-S10, Supporting Information). The best current density-voltage $\left( J - W \right)$ curves and corresponding parameters are depicted in Figure 2a and Table 1, respectively. The device based on PBDB-TF:ASe-5 achieves a moderate PCE of $1 2 . 3 \%$ with an open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ of $0 . 8 4 9 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ a $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 1 . 9 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and a FF of 0.662. In contrast, the PBQx-TF:ASe5-based cell exhibits the highest PCE for selenium-containing nfEAs, with a PCE of $1 4 . 7 \%$ $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } = 0 . 8 3 6 \mathrm { V } ; J _ { \mathrm { S C } } = 2 3 . 6 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ $\mathrm { F F } = 0 . 7 4 4$ . The relatively lower $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ in the PBQx-TF-based cell is attributed to its high-lying ionization potential. The disparity in $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values should be related to the variations in morphology, which will be discussed in detail later. Moreover, considering the importance of green solvents for the industrialization of OPV cells,[36,37] we have optimized PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based cells using o-xylene as the processing solvent, and the results are shown in Tables S11-S13 (Supporting Information). The PBQxTF:ASe-5-based cells using o-xylene achieve a moderate PCE of $1 2 . 2 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 3 0 \mathrm { V } ,$ $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 0 . 3 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and a FF of 0.724.

From the external quantum efficiency (EQE) curves (Figure 2b), the PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based device exhibits high response in the wavelength range of 450-600 and $7 0 0 { - } 8 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ while the PBDB-TF:ASe-5-based cell possesses a higher EQE response in the wavelength range of $6 0 0 { - } 7 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ . The integrated current densities $\left( J _ { \mathrm { c a l } } \mathbf { s } \right)$ of PBDB-TF- and PBQx-TF-based devices are 21.4 and $2 2 . 7 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , which are in an error range of $5 \%$ with the $J { - } V$ results. To further understand the difference in $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ , we analyzed the photon absorption rate distribution of the two devices by the transfer matrix method (Supporting Information).[38,39] The refractive index $n$ and extinction coefficient $k$ of the active layers were measured by the spectroscopic ellipsometer (Figure S4, Supporting Information). As displayed in Figure 2c,d, the PBDB-TF:ASe-5-based OPV device shows higher photon absorption rates in the wavelength range of $6 0 0 { - } 7 0 0 \ \mathrm { ~ n m }$ which is related to its stronger absorption in this region. Differently, the higher photon absorption rates are observed in the wavelength range of 450-600 and $7 0 0 { - } 9 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ in PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based OPV devices. The photon absorption rate distribution intuitively explains the difference in EQE responses of the two devices at different wavelengths.

# 2.5. Morphological Characterization

Electrostatic potential (EsP) calculations of the two polymers and ASe-5 were carried out, and the results are shown in Figure S9 (Supporting Information).[48-50] We calculated the averaged ESP values of the polymer models and their constituent donor/acceptor moieties (Figure 3a). The averaged ESP of PBDBTF, PBQx-TF, and ASe-5 are -0.315, 0.029, and $4 . 5 4 \mathrm { k c a l m o l ^ { - 1 } }$ respectively. We can speculate that the intermolecular electrostatic attraction between PBQx-TF and ASe-5 is decreased when compared with that in the PBDB-TF:ASe-5 combination.[51] The contact angle measurements were conducted to determine the surface energy of ASe-5 and two polymer donors. As shown in Figure 3b; and Table S15 (Supporting Information), the surface energy of ASe-5 is calculated as $4 6 . 5 \ \mathrm { m N \ m ^ { - 1 } }$ . For the donors, PBDB-TF shows a surface energy of $3 5 . 8 \mathrm { ~ m N ~ m ^ { - 1 } }$ which is slightly higher than that of PBQx-TF $( 3 5 . 1 \ \mathrm { m N \ m ^ { - 1 } }$ By utilizing the surface energy of donors $( \gamma _ { \mathrm { { D } } } )$ and acceptor $( \gamma _ { \mathrm { A } } )$ , the Flory-Huggins interaction parameter $( \chi _ { \mathrm { \scriptscriptstyle D A } } )$ between donors and the acceptor can be estimated by the formula $\chi _ { \mathrm { D A } } ~ =$ $K ( \sqrt { \gamma _ { \mathrm { D } } } ~ - ~ \sqrt { \gamma _ { \mathrm { A } } } )$ 2, where $K$ is a constant.[52] The $\chi _ { \mathrm { D A } }$ values between PBQx-TF and ASe-5 is higher than that of the combination of PBDB-TF:ASe-5. These results suggest that a lower miscibility and stronger phase separation can be expected in the PBQxTF:ASe-5 blend film.

The shift of UV-vis absorption spectra can reflect the aggregation behavior of materials in the solvent evaporation process.53,54] We performed in situ measurements to investigate the morphology evolution during spin-coating. The time-dependent absorption spectra are shown in Figures 3c,d, and the corresponding UV-vis absorption spectra are shown in Figure S10 (Supporting Information). In both systems, the absorption peaks belonging to the donors experience a slight redshift. By contrast, the sig. nificant redshift of the absorption peaks are observed in ASe-5, with the red shift range of ${ \sim } 6 8 0$ to $7 9 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . To further understand the influence of two polymer donors on the aggregation morphology of the active layer, the absorption peak location of acceptor ASe-5 is extracted over time, and the corresponding results are shown in Figure 3e. For PBQx-TF: ASe-5 blend film, the absorption peak location of ASe-5 first undergoes a slow red shift stage from $6 8 5 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , and then rapidly moves to $7 9 2 \mathrm { n m }$ . For the PBDB-TF:ASe-5 blend film, the absorption peak location rapidly moves from 683 to $7 8 7 \mathrm { n m }$ . The duration of the redshift process of the ASe-5 absorption peak in the PBQx-TF:ASe-5 blend is 1.34 s, which is longer than that of the PBDB-TF:ASe-5 blend (1.02 s). To explain the experimental results, we measured the molecular weight and preaggregation performance of the two polymers by gel permeation chromatography and temperature-dependent absorption measurements, respectively, and the results are shown in Figures S11 and S12 and Table S16 (Supporting Information). We find that the molecular weight and distribution differences between the two polymers are not significant; PBQx-TF still maintains a preaggregation peak at around $6 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ at high temperatures, suggesting that PBQx-TF has a stronger preaggregation performance compared with PBDB-TF. According to previous literature,[53] PBQx-TF may form polymer networks earlier in high-temperature toluene solution due to stronger preaggregation compared with PBDB-TF. The formed polymer network will limit the movement of acceptor ASe-5, making the redshift caused by aggregation more difficult and time-consuming for ASe-5. However, benefiting from the earlier formation of polymer networks, the phase separation of PBQx-TF:ASe-5-blend is more significant. In addition, the maximum absorption peak of acceptor ASe-5 is $7 9 2 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ after the formation of PBQx-TF:ASe5 blend film, which is a redshift of $5 \ \mathrm { n m }$ compared with that of the PBDB-TF:ASe-5 blend film $[ 7 8 7 \mathrm { n m } ]$ . This indicates that the ASe-5 of the former forms stronger $\pi - \pi$ interactions during phase separation, which is beneficial for charge transport and transfer.[55]

The in situ PL spectra were also measured to confirm the morphology evolution of the active layer during the film formation process,[56] and the results are displayed in Figure $3 \mathrm { f - h }$ ; and Figure S13 (Supporting Information). The maximum emission peak redshift of acceptor ASe-5 in the PBDB-TF:ASe-5 blend film lasts for 3.15 s with the final peak position at $8 6 5 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , while the duration in the PBQx-TF:ASe-5 blend film is 4.76 s with the final peak position at $8 6 9 \mathrm { n m }$ . The in situ PL spectra further confirm the phase separation results of the in situ UV-vis absorption spectra, indicating that better morphology is formed in the PBQxTF:ASe-5 blend film.

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements were used to characterize the surface morphology of the blend films and further verify the phase separation differences between the two blend systems. As shown in Figure 4a-d, both PBDBTF:ASe-5- and PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based blend films exhibit uniform fibrillar surfaces, but the latter shows more pronounced and coarser fibrillar morphology, corresponding to more obvious phase separation. In addition, the root-mean-square surface roughness $( R _ { \mathrm { q } } )$ of PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based blend film is $2 . 0 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ which is higher than that of PBDB-TF:ASe-5-based blend film $( 1 . 2 8 \ \mathrm { n m } )$ . To further validate the fibrillar morphology, we also conducted transmission electron microscope (TEM) experiments to characterize the morphology of the two blend films. As shown in Figure 4e,f, more obvious fibrous morphology is observed in the PBQx-TF:ASe-5 blend film. The above results indicate that the miscibility between PBQx-TF and ASe-5 is lower, and the blend film of the two is conducive to achieving more obvious phase separation compared with PBDB-TF:ASe-5-based blend film.

The grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) experiment was carried out to analyze the molecular packing structures of PBQx-TF:ASe-5- and PBDB-TF:ASe-5-based blend films. The corresponding 2D scattering patterns and 1D line-cut profiles of the active layers are shown in Figure S14 (Supporting Information). The GIWAXS results reveal that both blends have an obvious (010) peak in the out-of-plane (OOP) direction, indicating that the face-on molecular orientations are predominant. The PBDB-TF:ASe-5-based blend film exhibits a $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance of $3 . 7 2 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ with a coherence length of $2 . 2 8 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . By contrast, PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based blend film has a slightly smaller $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance of $3 . 6 9 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ , accompanied by a coherence length of $2 . 2 4 \ \mathrm { n m }$ There are negligible changes in the coherence length between the PBDB-TF:ASe-5 and PBQx-TF:ASe-5 blend films. However, the increased intensity of both OOP and in-plane diffraction peaks is observed, indicating that the molecular packing and orientation are enhanced with PBQx-TF-based blend film. The GIWAXS results indicate that the PBQx-TF:ASe5-based blend film may have a better intermolecular charge transfer, leading to an increase in $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values.

To further understand the relationship between morphology and charge transfer processes, we carried out TA spectroscopy measurements.157,58] As shown in Figure S15a-c (Supporting Information), the $4 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ pump pulse was used to excite the neat donor films and the $8 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ pump pulse was used to excite the neat acceptor film. The main negative signals of PBDB-TF, PBQx-TF, and ASe-5 films are ground state bleaching (GSB) signals. The decay signals at ${ \approx } 1 0 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ belong to the photoinduced absorption (PIA) of singlet excitons. Using the $8 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ excitation pump pulse, we excited the ASe-5 solely to study the hole transfer process in blends and the results are displayed in Figure 5a-d and Figure S15d-S16 (Supporting Information). In the two blends, the signals around $1 0 1 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ represent the PIA signals of the acceptor singlet excitons. Compared with PBDBTF:ASe-5-based films, PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based films exhibit faster singlet exciton decay signal. The shorter lifetime of the latter shows that the singlet excitons in ASe-5 undergo hole transfer more rapidly to the donor PBQx-TF, which is conducive to obtaining more effective exciton dissociation. After the hole transfer process, the GSB signal of the donor gradually appears at $5 7 5 \mathrm { n m }$ In the initial stage of increased signal intensity, there is no significant difference between the two blend films, but PBQx-TF:ASe5-based film reached the signal peak earlier. This result is consistent with the faster signal decay of PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based film at $1 0 1 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , confirming its faster hole transfer process. In the signal decay stage, PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based film exhibits a longer lifetime of 1071 ps compared with that of PBDB-TF:ASe-5-based films (565 ps), suggesting a more suppressed charge recombination in PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based film. The TA results further explain the higher FF and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based devices.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we investigated the impacts of blend morphology on the photovoltaic performance of nfEA-based OPV cells by synthesizing a new acceptor ASe-5 and matching it with two typical polymer donors (PBDB-TF and PBQx-TF). ASe-5 shows strong optical absorption in the range of $4 5 0 { \mathrm { - } } 9 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ with an $E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { \ o p t } }$ of $1 . 3 7 ~ \mathrm { e V } .$ The contact angle measurement shows that PBQx-TF has lower miscibility with ASe-5 than PBDB-TF. By conducting in situ absorption spectra measurements, we can find that ASe-5 can form more ordered intermolecular packing in its blend with PBQx-TF. Morphology characterizations show more pronounced fibrous phase separation in the PBQx-TF:ASe-5 blend film, which benefits faster hole transfer and suppressed charge recombination. Consequently, the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values can be promoted in the PBQx-TF:ASe-5-based OPV cells. As a result, the PBQx-TF:ASe5-based device records a high PCE of $1 4 . 7 \%$ with an FF of 0.744, which is among the top values for nonfused acceptor-based devices and is the highest one for selenophene-containing nfEAbased cells. By contrast, the PBDB-TF:ASe-5-based device only gets a moderate PCE of $1 2 . 3 \%$ with a relatively low FF of 0.628. Our results demonstrate that the selection of donors should consider the donor: acceptor interaction to ensure a good phase separation morphology, which is of great importance for improving the photovoltaic performance of nfEA-based OPV cells.
