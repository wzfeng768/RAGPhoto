# Highly Ordered Supramolecular Materials of Phase-Separated Block Molecules for Long-Range Exciton Transport

Martin H. C. van Son, Anton M. Berghuis, Bas F. M. de Waal, Felix A. Wenzel, Klaus Kreger, Hans-Werner Schmidt, Jaime Gomez Rivas, Ghislaine Vantomme, and E. W. Meijer\*

Efficient energy transport over long distances is essential for optoelectronic and light-harvesting devices. Although self-assembled nanofibers of organic molecules are shown to exhibit long exciton diffusion lengths, alignment of these nanofibers into films with large, organized domains with similar properties remains a challenge. Here, it is shown how the functionalization of $c _ { 3 }$ -symmetric carbonyl-bridged triarylamine trisamide (CBT) with oligodimethylsiloxane (oDMS) side chains of discrete length leads to fully covered surfaces with aligned domains up to $1 2 5 \times 7 0 ~ \mu \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 }$ in which Iong-range exciton transport takes place. The nanoscale morphology within the domains consists of highly ordered nanofibers with discrete intercolumnar spacings within a soft amorphous oDMS matrix. The oDMS prevents bundling of the CBT fibers, reducing the number of defects within the CBT columns. As a result, the columns have a high degree of coherence, leading to exciton diffusion lengths of a few hundred nanometers with exciton diffusivities $( \approx 0 . 0 5 \ \mathsf { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathsf { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ that are comparable to those of a crystalline tetracene. These findings represent the next step toward fully covered surfaces of highly aligned nanofibers through functionalization with oDMS.

# 1. Introduction

The transport of excitation energy over length scales beyond the optical absorption depth of ${ > } 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ can greatly enhance fluorescence sensors or light-harvesting devices such as solar cells.1,2] Crucial for longrange energy transport is the arrangement of the system at the nanoscale; densely packed molecules provide strong electronic Coulomb coupling, while minimization of both electronic and structural disorder improves exciton delocalization.[3] In solution, these requirements of strong coupling and low disorder can be met with supramolecular systems. Directed noncovalent interactions between $C _ { 3 }$ or $C _ { 4 }$ -symmetric molecules provide a dynamic system with a well-defined spatial arrangement of the building blocks.14,5] In defect-free single crystals, these requirements are met, leading to singlet excitons diffusion lengths $( L _ { \mathrm { D } } )$ of a few $1 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ [6] However, the processing of these crystals remains challenging due to their polydisperse dimensions, while devices require precisely defined dimensions.l7l In contrast, most organic semiconducting conjugated polymers can be easily processed for solid-state applications. However, local imperfections or variations in the crystalline packing will lead to exciton trapping at low-energy sites. This disorder limits their $L _ { \mathrm { D } }$ to only ${ \approx } 1 0 { - } 2 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ [8,9]

In attempts to combine processability with defect-free nanofibers, previous research has used solution-based selfassembly to obtain nanofibers up to $5 ~ \mu \mathrm { m }$ in length consisting of ordered aggregates.[4,7,10] These nanofibers remain intact when deposited on a surface, and their structural order leads to long diffusion lengths and large exciton diffusivities $( 0 . 5 { - } 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ . These nanofibers have only been studied as isolated fibers on a surface, as bundling of fibers increases the energetic disorder, making them unsuitable for full surface coverage.[10-14] This was clearly demonstrated for carbonylbridged triarylamine trisamides (CBT), where single fibers were found to have an exciton diffusivity of ${ \approx } 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , while the exciton diffusivity of bundled CBT fibers was lower but still high for organic molecules at $0 . 0 5 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { \mathbf { s } } ^ { - 1 }$ . This reduction for bundles is attributed to the interacting periphery leading to local electronic perturbations.[10] Similar high exciton diffusion lengths and diffusivities are found in films of poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT), where nanofibers are embedded in disordered P3HT domains.[9] Although alignment of these nanofibers is possible through blade coating, the aligned nanofibers possess a lower structural order than nanofibers isolated on a surface.17,15] Thus, the next step toward organic energy transport materials is to combine the highly ordered structures of supramolecular fibers with the film-forming ability of semiconductor polymers to form large domains of aligned structures, where the high degree of order leads to high exciton diffusivities.[3]

Recently, oligodimethylsiloxane (oDMS) side chains have been attached to various aromatic cores to create highly ordered structures at the nanoscale.116-22] In one such example, domains of millimeter-sized aligned 2D crystalline sheets could be obtained by slow cooling from their melt.116] For this purpose, oDMS blocks of discrete length are covalently linked to diphenylanthracene (DPA). In this system, the nanophase separation of oDMS with the aromatic core leads to highly ordered 2D DPA sheets separated by oDMS layers. As a result of the welldefined crystalline DPA packing within these monolayers, energy transport was found to be highly effective. This was shown by two orders of magnitude increase in triplet-triplet annihilation photon upconverted photoluminescence (PL) of the aligned material compared to its amorphous counterpart. In addition, phase separation with oDMS prevents interaction between adjacent nanostructures, reducing electronic disorder in the system. Furthermore, oDMS side chains are found to enhance energy transport in semiconducting polymers compared to their alkyl counterparts.23-26] These properties make functionalization with oDMS promising for creating large domains of aligned nanofibers with favorable energy transport characteristics. Although functionalization of "hard" crystalline molecular blocks with soft oDMs blocks has been used to form highly ordered aromatic nanofibers in an oDMS matrix, this approach has mainly been used for asymmetric, $C _ { 2 }$ or $C _ { 4 }$ -symmetric systems where the nanostructure formed depends on the oDMS volume fraction.19-27] However, $C _ { 3 }$ symmetric molecules such as CBT have an inherent preference to form nanofibers and are thus more suitable to combine with oDMS functionalization to achieve micrometer-sized domains of highly ordered nanofibers for energy transfer applications.[28]

Here we report on the synthesis, assembly and molecular and photophysical characterization of $C _ { 3 }$ -symmetric CBT functionalized with linear and branched discrete length oligodimethylsiloxane. We show how phase separation induced by the peripheral oDMS chains leads to better order within the columns by preventing fiber bundling, thereby effectively increasing the degree of coherence (exciton delocalization). The CBT derivatives form micrometer-sized domains with the same hexagonally arranged columnar arrangement for which high exciton diffusivities and long exciton diffusion lengths are reported.

The molecular structure and synthetic route of the CBT derivatives with discrete oDMS chains are shown in Scheme 1. Two different oDMS chains are used that have the same length and molecular formula but are attached to the CBT alkene either at the first silicon atom $( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 } )$ or at the seventh silicon atom $( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } )$ . These two different chains make it possible to study the effect of branching of the oDMs chain on the physical behavior and nanoscale morphology of $C _ { 3 }$ -symmetric molecules, illustrated here with a CBT core. The strong core-core interactions between the CBT cores along with the flexible nature of oDMS make these block molecules ideal for creating hard, isolated aromatic columns in a soft matrix.

The synthesis of these oDMS-functionalized CBT molecules begins with synthesizing the oDMS side chains. The linear oDMS chain $\mathbf { S _ { i } } _ { 1 5 } \mathbf { H }$ is synthesized according to a previously described procedure.[29] The branched oDMS chain $\mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } \mathbf { H }$ is synthesized by coupling $\mathbf { S i } _ { 7 } \mathbf { O H }$ on either side of dichloromethylsilane to obtain the desired branched oDMs.29] The hydride-functionalized discrete oDMS is then coupled with Karstedt's catalyst to the alkenefunctionalized 2,6,10-triamino-CBT $( \mathbf { C B T } ( \mathbf { A l k } ) _ { 3 } )$ , which is synthesized similarly to reported literature procedures.[30] Although this type of hydrosilylation reaction is generally rapid,[31] the reaction here was let to react for 2 weeks because strong supramolecular aggregation of the cores is thought to prevent easy access to the terminal alkenes. The final oDMs-functionalized CBT molecules were purified with recycling gel permeation chromatography (GPC) to yield $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ and $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ , containing linear and branched oDMS, respectively, as orange waxy compounds. The yields were low $( 1 - 6 \% )$ but with high purity of the material, as the strong core aggregation prevented easy separation. The structure and the purity of the molecules were confirmed by $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR, $^ { 1 3 } \mathrm { C }$ NMR, MALDI-TOF-Mass spectrometry, and GPC. Details on the synthesis and molecular characterization can be found in the Supporting Information.

The thermal properties of $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ and $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ were characterized by differential scanning calorimetry (DsC) and polarized optical microscopy (POM). Similar to the properties of other CBT molecules reported previously, no obvious thermal transitions were observed up to $3 5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ using DSC (Figure S4, Supporting Information).[32] The lack of discrete thermal transitions is attributed to very broad transitions, which were identified using POM measurements at variable temperature (Figure 1a). The width of these transitions is attributed to the strong aggregation of the core. For the POM measurement, the compound is placed between two microscopy slides and heated at $1 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } \ \mathrm { m i n ^ { - 1 } }$ until the isotropic melt. At room temperature (RT), both molecules $\mathbf { C B T } ( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ and $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ showed clear birefringence and do not flow. Upon heating, material flow was observed at $1 0 0 { - } 1 1 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $\mathbf { C B T } ( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ and at $1 3 0 { - } 1 4 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ upon an applied external pressure. This flow is attributed to softening of the side chains, as the birefringence, which indicates core-core interactions, was maintained. The higher softening temperature of the side chain $( T _ { \mathrm { e x o - R } } )$ for $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ $( 1 3 0 -$ $1 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ compared to $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ $( 1 0 0 { - } 1 1 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ indicates that the $T _ { \mathrm { e x o - R } }$ is not determined by the absolute length of the oDMS side chain, but rather by its relative length. Branched $\mathrm { \bf S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } }$ can also be considered as two oDms side chains of length 7, and it is known that shorter oDMS side chains often lead to higher melting temperatures.[16] Upon further heating, the materials became isotropic between 210 and $2 5 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $\mathbf { C B T } ( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ and between 180 and $2 5 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ . The loss of birefringence is related to the breaking of the CBT-CBT interactions and the resulting loss of order. The lowest isotropization temperature reported refers to the initial loss of birefringence, while the highest temperature indicates the isotropic melt. The lower initial isotropization temperature of $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ is attributed to the greater bulkiness of branched oDMS compared to linear oDMS (Figure S5, Supporting Information). Both molecules are completely isotropic at the same temperature, indicating similar core- core interactions once the side chains become flexible. Upon cooling the material from their isotropic phase at $1 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } \ \mathrm { m i n } ^ { - 1 }$ , the birefringence is restored for both materials. This occurred faster for $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ due to a larger number of nucleation points of the core. The birefringent domains averaged $2 0 { - } 5 0 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } }$ in length, although highly ordered domains up to $1 0 0 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } }$ in length were observed for both compounds (Figure 1b,c). The largest domains of $1 2 5 \times 7 0 \mu \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 }$ were observed for CBT $( \mathbb { S } \mathbf { i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ (Figure S6, Supporting Information).

Upon rotation of the material between the crossed polarizers of the POM setup, bright regions become dark and dark regions become bright. This observed anisotropy in the film occurs through spontaneous directional alignment of the molecules during the domain formation. This alignment was further studied with polarized PL measurements (Figure 1d-f). As can be seen in Figure 1d,e, a dark line is visible through the center of a single domain when a polarizer is present between the material and the detector. This dark line becomes as bright as the surrounding material upon rotation of $9 0 ^ { \circ }$ (Figure S7, Supporting Information). Thus, the PL in this region is highly polarized, as confirmed by polarizer angle-dependent PL measurements that can be fitted with a sinusoidal wavefunction (Figure 1f). For CBT $( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathbf { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ , a slight decrease in PL intensity is observed across the different polarizer angles due to bleaching, whereas bleaching was not observed for $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ . These results, combined with the different isotropization behavior, indicate different core-core packing due to the different oDMS chains. In agreement with the POM results, the highest degree of polarization is observed on the dark line (Figure S8, Supporting Information). In these measurements, the area whose PL is measured is slightly larger than the width of the dark line. As such, the polarization angle dependence of emitted light shown in Figure 1f is only a lower value but still indicates the high degree of alignment inside a domain. Although the microscopic domains could not be aligned over millimeter distances with either shear force or rubbed polyimide layers, the domains of aligned columns are several orders of magnitude larger than the length of the previously reported nanofibers with high exciton diffusivities.[7,9,10]

The nanoscale morphologies of $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ and $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ and their precursor $\mathbf { C B T } ( \mathbf { A l k } ) _ { 3 }$ were examined using mediumand wide-angle X-ray scattering (MAXS and WAXS, Figure 2a). In the MAXS region $( 1 < q < 7 ~ \mathrm { n m ^ { - 1 } } )$ , both $\mathbf { C B T } ( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ and $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ show a sharp principal scattering peak $q ^ { * }$ , followed by reflection peaks indicative of long-range columnar packing $( q ^ { * } , \sqrt { 3 q ^ { * } } , \sqrt { 4 q ^ { * } } , \sqrt { 7 q ^ { * } } , \sqrt { 9 q ^ { * } } , . . . )$ [33] The shoulder of the principal scattering peak at lower $q$ values is present because the sample could not be brought completely to its isotropic state due to the high-temperature requirement. The order measured with this technique is therefore lower than in the samples used for the optical characterizations that were brought to their isotropic phase. The average center-to-center distance of adjacent CBT columns is calculated from the principal scattering peak to be $d _ { \mathrm { c o l } } = 2 \pi / q ^ { * } =$ $4 . 3 \pm 0 . 1$ nm with the packing schematically shown in Figure 2d. Both the sharpness and number of scattering peaks indicate longrange columnar ordering with little to no defects.[29] In contrast, for $\mathbf { C B T } ( \mathbf { A l k } ) _ { 3 }$ , only a broad initial scattering peak is observed without clear reflection peaks, indicating a poorly defined morphology. In the WAXS region $( 7 < q < 2 5 \ \mathrm { n m ^ { - 1 } } )$ , a broad bump centered around $8 . 6 \ \mathrm { n m ^ { - 1 } }$ is observed for both $\mathbf { C B T } ( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ and $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ , which corresponds to the amorphous halo of oDMS. For all three molecules, a pronounced $\pi - \pi$ distance is observed around ${ \approx } 0 . 3 4 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , comparable to other CBT compounds.[10,34-36] These results show that functionalization with oDMS greatly improves ordering at the nanoscale, which translates to a reduced number of defects in the CBT columns, while favorable orbital overlap between adjacent CBT molecules is maintained (Figure 2b).

The high structural order and close $\pi - \pi$ distance make these materials promising for long-range exciton migration.37-40] To first understand the photonic properties of these molecules, absorption and PL spectra are recorded in $4 0 ~ \mu \mathbf { M }$ solutions of tetrahydrofuran (THF) and dodecane and annealed films (Figure 1g; Figures S9, Supporting Information). In THF, a clear vibronic progression can be observed with PL maxima at 487 and $5 3 3 \mathrm { n m }$ for both $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ and $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ . These peaks are attributed to the electronic $\pi - \pi ^ { * }$ transition of the CBT core in a molecularly dissolved state.[10] In contrast, the absorption spectrum in dodecane is broader than in THF and blueshifted, while the PL spectrum is broader and redshifted to ${ \approx } 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , indicating aggregation of the CBT cores. Aggregation is further confirmed by measuring the fluorescence lifetime. The molecularly dissolved compound in THF follows a single exponential decay, whereas in dodecane an additional longer lifetime component indicates aggregation in this solvent (Table 1; Figure S10, Supporting Information). Comparing the absorption and emission spectra of the annealed films with the aggregates in solution, a significant suppression of the (0,0) transition at $5 3 3 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ can be observed for $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ , while the (o,0) transition is even absent for $\mathbf { C B T } ( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ . The greater suppression of the (0,0) transition for $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ indicates a greater degree of electronic order within the $\pi$ stacked columns than for $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ , due to a lower number of defects. The formation of $_ \mathrm { H }$ type aggregates indicates significant electronic Coulomb coupling between adjacent cores and strong exciton delocalization.41-44] Together with the micrometer-sized domains of perfectly aligned columns, these supramolecular materials are ideal for studying their long-range energy transport.13,9,10,35,44,45]

With this detailed knowledge of the photonic properties of the system in hand, we measured the exciton diffusivity in the solid state. For this purpose, the annealed thin films obtained during the optical microscopy measurements are used. Exciton diffusivity was measured by a combination of a fixed pulsed laser excitation $( \lambda = 4 7 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ with a spatially resolved emission detection that measures the exciton lifetime using a time-correlated single-photon counting setup. After initial excitation, photoluminescence occurs mainly at the excitation site. However, as the excitons diffuse through the material before their (radiative) de. cay, the area from where PL occurs widens over time. By plotting the broadening of the Gaussian fits of the emission as a function of time, the exciton diffusivity can be derived (Figure 3a,b). Very low exciton fluxes are used for these measurements to exclude artifacts due to saturation, photobleaching, and exciton-exciton annihilation.10] Although the POM and PL measurements showed anisotropy of the material, while anisotropy was not observed in the diffusivity measurements. We hypothe. size that the degree of diffusion anisotropy is smaller than the detection limit of our setup. The averaged exciton diffusivities were determined to be $D ( \mathbf { C B T ( S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 } ) = 0 . 0 4 7 \pm 0 . 0 3 6 ~ \mathrm { c m ^ { 2 } ~ s ^ { - 1 } }$ and $D ( { \bf C B T ( S i _ { 1 5 b } ) _ { 3 } } ) = 0 . 0 2 8 \pm 0 . 0 2 1 ~ \mathrm { c m ^ { 2 } ~ s ^ { - 1 } }$ as evaluated for t $< 2$ ns. As can be seen in Figure 3c, for $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ also exciton mobilities above $0 . 1 \thinspace { \mathrm { c m } } ^ { 2 } \thinspace { \mathbf { s } } ^ { - 1 }$ were measured. The slightly higher average exciton diffusivity of $\mathbf { C B T } ( \mathbf { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ than $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ can be explained by the lower number of defects, based on the larger suppression of the (0,0) transition in the emission spectra. When taking an average diffusion constant of $0 . 0 4 7 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ $\mathrm { . } 4 . 7 \ \mathrm { n m } ^ { 2 }$ $\mathsf { p s } ^ { - 1 } \bigr \rvert$ and the intrinsic radiative lifetime of molecularly dissolved $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 } ) _ { 3 }$ or $\mathrm { C B T } ( \mathrm { S i } _ { 1 5 \mathrm { b } } ) _ { 3 }$ at $\tau = 2 9 0 0$ ps (Table S1, Supporting Information), a diffusion length $L _ { \mathrm { D } } = ( D \tau ) ^ { 1 / 2 }$ of ${ > } 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ can be estimated.146,47] This corresponds to an exciton transport over more than $3 4 0 \mathrm { C B T }$ cores that are aligned within 1D fibers in an isolating oDMS matrix, as calculated with their $\pi - \pi$ distance. The exciton diffusivity and diffusion length values correspond with the theoretically calculated values for CBT nanofibers.35] The measured diffusion values are comparable to tetracene crystals $( 0 . 0 4 \ \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { \ s } ^ { - 1 } )$ , polyfluorenes $( 0 . 0 2 9 \ \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { \ s } ^ { - 1 } )$ , or zinc porphyrins $( 0 . 0 1 4 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ , while the diffusion length is an order of magnitude larger than typically reported for amorphous and polycrystalline organic semiconductors.140,46,48,49] These results clearly show how the functionalization of organic $\pi$ -conjugated molecules with oDMS side chains can result in fast exciton diffusion over long distances without the requirement of 3D crystallinity, covalent polymerization, or heavy atoms. Although higher diffusion coefficients have been reported for organic nanofibers $( 0 . 5 { - } 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ ,[7,10] these values were obtained for isolated nanofibers on a surface while the approach reported here with $1 2 5 \times 7 0 \mu \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 }$ domains is the next step toward complete surface coverage by isolating the nanofibers on the nanoscale.

This manuscript is the next step to bridge the gap between isolated organic fibers on a surface and processable materials with complete and homogeneous surface coverage. The work reported here extends the general approach to functionalization with welldefined oDMS from $C _ { 2 ^ { - } }$ to $C _ { 3 }$ -symmetric molecules, where the high chemical dissimilarity of the flexible oDMS with aromatic groups repeatedly leads to highly ordered nanophase-separated structures and long-range alignment. Moreover, oDMS functionalization induces intermolecular interactions that maximize energy transport, which, combined with the thermal processability of the materials, is promising for nanophotonics or nanoelectronics.
