ABSTRACT: Two new two-dimensional (2D) conjugated polymers (Z3 and Z4) with different side chains were synthesized and applied as donor materials for organic solar cells (OsCs). The alkylthiophenyl side chains in Z4 conferred an appropriate absorption spectrum and morphology, whereas the extra thienyl incorporated in the side chains of Z3 caused stronger $\pi - \pi$ interaction, excessive molecular aggregation, and further inferior $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF. Therefore, upon blending with the acceptor Y6, $\mathbf { Z 4 }$ -based OSCs achieved a power conversion efficiency of $1 5 . 1 2 \%$ which was almost $3 0 \%$ higher than that of Z3-based devices $\left( 1 0 . 6 8 \% \right)$ . The result indicated that side-chain modification is a fine-tuning strategy for the aggregation of 2D-conjugated polymeric donors in OSCs.

# INTRODUCTION

Given the advantages in making large-area flexible solar panels, organic solar cells (OsCs) hold great promise for promoting sustainable and green solar energy into electric energy.1-4 With the structural innovation of the materials and the improvement of device fabrication techniques, the advancement of OSCs has reached a new level in recent years, and the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of single-junction cells has boosted and exceeded $1 8 \%$ 5-13 In particular, two-dimensional (2D) conjugated $\mathrm { D } { - } \mathbf { A }$ polymer donors which possess two planes in the conjugate side chain and the molecular main chain, has the irreplaceable status in this field owing to the strong intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) effect and tunable energy levels.8,10

Li et al.14,1s frst reported polythiophene derivatives containing vinyl-conjugated side chains, and since then, 2Dconjugated structures have been extensively used in the design of D-A-type photovoltaic materials. On the one hand, the enhanced electron delocalization in the $\mathrm { D } { - } \mathbf { A }$ alternating copolymer could weaken the Coulombic attraction between electron-hole pairs, which resulted in a lower driving force threshold for exciton dissociation, thereby increasing the exciton dissociation rate under short-circuit conditions.18,17 In addition, the 2D conjugated framework constructed in the D or A unit could extend the electron delocalization length on the whole conjugated skeleton, which increased the possibility of electronic transitions between the ground state and the excited state, thereby narrowing the band gap.18 18,19 On the other hand, the expanded conjugation planes could increase the effective contact between the polymer donor and the acceptor, which promoted higher short-circuit currents $\left( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \right)$ 20 Therefore, polymers with a 2D conjugated structure generally have a large carrier mobility, broad absorption spectrum, and high exciton dissociation rate. Moreover, several studies have reported outstanding achievements in OSCs when they are paired with acceptors. For example, Yu et al.2' modified the side chain to optimize the physical properties of polymers and further finely tuned the coverage of absorption, hole mobility, and miscibility with an acceptor. Subsequently, Chen et al.? 22 replaced the side chain of PTB7 with the aromatic thiophene alkyl group onto the benzodithiophene (BDT) unit and synthesized a 2D polymer PTB7-Th. Upon blending with fullerene $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M } _ { \mathrm { \ell } }$ the optimized PTB7-Th showed better performances in absorption, energy level, and migration rate. Furthermore, in non-fullerene systems, such 2D materials exhibited an excellent performance as the PCEs reached 12.00, 13.10, and 14.57% in PTB7-Th:F101C,23 PBDB-T-SF:IT-4F,24 and PBDB-TF:IT-4F,25 respectively.

Based on the abovementioned studies, we synthesized two 2D polymer donors (Z3 and Z4) with dithienobenzothiadiazole (DTBT) as the electron-withdrawing unit, which has strong crystallinity and molecular packing, and classical BDT as the electron-donating unit, accompanied by 2-hexyldecylthiophenylthienyl or 2-hexyldecylthiophenyl on the side chain (Figure 1). Upon adjusting the groups and lengths of the conjugated side chains, the materials could achieve favorable energy levels, spectra, and molecular packing. Based on the basic principles of energy-level matching and spectral complementarity between the donor and acceptor, the efficient and narrow-band-gap Y6 was selected as the acceptor to prepare binary OSCs. The morphologies, molecular aggregations, charge mobilities of blend flms, and photovoltaic properties of devices were systematically studied. Consequently, the device based on Z4 with alkylthiophenyl side chains achieved a significantly higher PCE of $1 5 . 1 2 \%$ compared with Z3 $( 1 0 . 6 8 \% )$ with alkylthiophenylthienyl side chains. These results indicate that the groups and lengths of the conjugated side chains could be adjusted to develop an efficient 2D polymer donor for high-performance OSCs.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Synthesis and Characterization. The molecular structures of Z3, Z4, and Y6 are presented in Figure 1. Polymers were synthesized by Stille coupling, and the specific synthetic procedures are displayed in the Supporting Information. In addition, their monomers $\mathrm { M 1 } , \stackrel { 2 6 } { \mathrm { M 2 } } , \stackrel { 1 0 } { \mathrm { ~ } }$ and $\mathbf { M } 3 ^ { 2 7 }$ were synthesized in accordance with the relevant literature. The number-average molecular weights $\left( M _ { \mathrm { n } } \right)$ tested by gel permeation chromatography for Z3 and $\mathbf { Z 4 }$ were 63.1 and $3 1 . 2 \mathrm { \ k D a }$ , along with polydispersity indexes of 3.15 and 1.62, respectively. Both copolymers Z3 and Z4 exhibit good solubility in $\mathrm { C H C l } _ { 3 }$ . The $5 \%$ weight loss temperatures $\mathsf { \bar { \Gamma } } ( T _ { \mathrm { d } } )$ of Z3 and Z4 measured by thermogravimetric analysis in a nitrogen atmosphere at $2 0 \mathrm { \Omega ^ { \circ } C \ m i n ^ { - i } }$ were 386 and $4 0 3 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ respectively (Figure S1 in the Supporting Information), displaying their excellent thermodynamic stability.

Density functional theory at the B3LYP/6-31G(d) level was utilized to analyze the geometrical structures of Z3 and Z4 with methyl substituent in replacement for long alkyl ones for simplification (Figure 2a). In Z3, the dihedral angle of $5 4 . 3 6 ^ { \circ }$ is observed between the alkylthiophenylthienyl side chain and BDT. In addition, the dihedral angle between BDT and the thiophene $\pi$ bridge in its conjugate skeleton is $2 2 . 6 2 ^ { \circ }$ . In Z4, upon getting rid of the thiophene units in the side chain, the dihedral angle between the side chain and the BDT increases to $5 8 . 3 8 ^ { \circ }$ . However, the dihedral angle in the main skeleton has changed slightly by $2 2 . 0 4 ^ { \circ }$ .Therefore, removal of the thiophene groups in the conjugated side chain could increase the degree of twist between the main and side chains, which would prevent the excessive molecule aggregation.

Optical and Electrochemical Properties. Based on the UV-vis absorption measured in chloroform solution $\mathrm { ~ \ ' ~ } _ { 1 } \mathrm { ~ m g / }$ $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ Figure S2), Z3 shows an absorption ranging from 370 to $6 3 0 ~ \mathrm { n m } ,$ with evident absorption shoulder peaks at 550 and $5 9 5 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ , which are attributed to the intramolecular ICT effect and the absorption caused by $\pi - \pi$ stacking, respectively. Compared with Z3, the absorption range of Z4 is blue-shifted to $3 7 0 { - } 5 8 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ with the maximum absorption located at 491 nm and the maximum absorption coefficient of $4 1 . 2 3 \textrm { L g } ^ { - 1 }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ . Compared with their absorption in dilute solution, the maximum absorption wavelength of the Z4 film is red-shifted by $8 4 \ \mathrm { n m }$ with the absorption peaks moved to $5 7 5 ~ \mathrm { { \ n m } }$ However, the absorption spectrum of the Z3 film is not significantly different from that of the solution, and the wavelength of the maximum absorption peak remains unchanged at $5 9 5 \ \mathrm { n m }$ which implies that the polymer Z3 has strong molecular pre-aggregation in its solution. In addition, there are obvious shoulder peaks at $5 5 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ that could be observed in the shorter-wavelength region in both solution and film of Z3, which might be assigned to the more pronounced H-aggregation resulting from the introduction of thiophene units in the side chain.28

In exploring the molecular aggregation, the temperaturedependent absorption spectra of Z3 and Z4 in chlorobenzene were measured (Figure 2c,d). Within the temperature test scope $\left( 2 0 - 9 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } \right)$ , Z3 continuously showed two main evident absorption peaks generated by ICT and $\pi - \pi$ stacking at approximately 550 and $5 9 5 ~ \mathrm { { \ n m } }$ respectively. The barely changed outline of the curves indicates the almost unbreakable aggregation of Z3. For comparison, Z4 exhibits a more pronounced disaggregation and blue-shifting, with the disappearance of the shoulder peak at elevated temperatures (approximately $7 0 { - } 9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ . With the decrease of temperature, the aggregation enhanced, which was reflected in the redshifted absorption profile with distinct spectral features.29 These results, which signify that Z4 has more controllable aggregation than Z3, could be ascribed to the larger twist angle between the BDT core and the conjugated side chain (Figure 2a). The presence of these features is beneficial for the formation of a proper phase-separated morphology in Z4- based devices.30,31

The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels of the copolymers determined by cyclic voltammetry are shown in Figure S3 and Table 1. The HOMO/LUMO levels of Z3 and Z4 are $- 5 . 4 2 / - 3 . 3 8$ and $- 5 . 5 9 / - 3 . 4 1 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively. Their levels are higher than the HOMO/LUMO levels $( - 5 . 6 5 / - 4 . 1 0 ~ \mathrm { e V } )$ of acceptor $\Upsilon 6 , ^ { 3 3 }$ thereby ensuring exciton dissociation and transfer between the polymer and Y6. In particular, the difference between the HOMO energy level of Z4 and the LUMO energy level of Y6 is larger than the corresponding level between Z3 and Y6, predicting that a relatively higher open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ could be obtained in the Z4-based device.

Film Morphologies. The morphologies of Z3- and Z4- based blend flms were measured by tapping mode atomic force microscopy (AFM) and transmission electron microscope. As shown in the AFM images in Figure 3, the surface root-mean-square roughness of Z3- and Z4-based films are 8.62 and $5 . 2 6 ~ \mathrm { { \ n m } }$ respectively. In addition, particle aggregation and a large phase separation structure are observed in the Z3-based blend film. In the TEM image (Figure 3f), evident black particles could also be seen in the Z3-based blend flm, which might be due to the excessive molecular aggregation. In contrast, the Z4-based film exhibited a more homogeneous and smaller phase domain size, accompanied by the interpenetrating network structure and appropriate phase separation, which is desirable for more efficient exciton separation and charge transport, and then a higher $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and fill factor (FF) could be expected.34,35

In investigating the molecular orientation, pure and blend films were obtained by grazing incidence wide angle X-ray scattering, and the 2D diffraction images and line-cut scattering profiles are shown in Figure 4 and Table S7. All films formed a face-on orientation. In the IP direction, the lamellar stacking peaks (1o0) could be observed clearly in pure films, and the $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances of Z3 and Z4 located at 0.20 and 0.24 $\mathbf { A } ^ { - 1 }$ respectively, are 31.05 and $2 6 . 2 7 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ,$ respectively. The large distance of Z3 might be due to the great steric hindrance caused by the insertion of thiophene in its side chain, forcing a large intermolecular distance. This phenomenon could also be observed in their blend films, indicating that the diffraction signals might be derived from the ordered backbone. Moreover, in the OOP direction, in Z3 and Z4 pure films, the $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances of (o10) diffraction signals located at 1.70 and $1 . 6 2 ~ \mathrm { ~ A ~ } ^ { - 1 }$ are 3.70 and $3 . 8 7 \mathrm { ~ \ r ~ { ~ \AA ~ } ~ } ,$ respectively. Furthermore, the corresponding peaks in ${ \bf Z } 3 \mathrm { - }$ and Z4-based blend films at 1.73 and $1 . 7 7 ~ \mathrm { ~ A } ^ { - 1 }$ are 3.63 and 3.55 A, respectively. The small $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance of Z4-based blend flms indicated its closer $\pi - \pi$ packing, which is conducive to the vertical charge transport and inhibition exciton recombination.

Photovoltaic Properties. The photovoltaic performances of Z3 and Z4 were evaluated by fabricating bulk-heterojunction OSCs with an architecture of ITO/PEDOT:PSS (30 nm)/active layer $\mathbf { \bar { \Omega } } _ { 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { ~ n m } } ,$ /PFN-Br $( 5 \ \mathrm { n m } ) / \mathrm { A l }$ $\mathrm { ~ \ ' ~ } 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { ~ n m } )$ 3 under illumination of AM 1.5G $( 1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m W \ c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ . The device fabrication conditions of the OsCs were systematically screened by varying the $\mathrm { D } { - } \mathbf { A }$ ratios, post-treatments, and different ratios of additive, in which the optimal active layers were spin-coated from chloroform solution (total concentration, $\mathrm { D - A } \left[ \mathbf { w } / \mathbf { w } \right] ~ = ~ 1 { : } 1 . 2 \AA$ with 0.5 vol $\%$ additive 1,8- diiodooctane and thermally annealed at $1 1 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ Based on the density-voltage curves and the corresponding device parameters shown in Figure 5 and Table 2, the Z3- based device delivered a moderate PCE of $1 0 . 6 8 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 0 \mathrm { V } ,$ a $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 0 . 0 7 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and a FF of $6 6 . 7 1 \%$ . The Z4-based blend film exhibited a higher PCE of $1 5 . 1 2 \%$ with a $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 6 . 2 7 ~ \mathrm { m A ~ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ a $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 1 \mathrm { V } _ { ; }$ and a FF of $7 1 . 0 6 \%$ . Its significantly enhanced $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF might be due to the ideal morphology and molecular aggregation of the Z4 and Y6 blend film, which promotes the exciton separation and charge transport. In addition, the difference between the HOMO of

Z4 and the LUMO of Y6 is larger than that of Z3, which also leads to the slightly higher $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the Z4-based device.

The external quantum efficiency spectrum, which was confirmed for $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of optimized devices, is shown in Figure 5b. The Z3- and Z4-based active layers exhibited a similar photoresponse as their spectra ranged from 350 to $9 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ In addition, the Z4-based device exhibited a higher response intensity with integrated $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 5 . 2 3 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ than Z3 (19.22 mA $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ). In comparison with $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ obtained from $J { - } V$ measurements, these parameters are within $5 \%$ relative error.

The space-charge-limited current method was used to reveal a major difference in the charge-carrier mobilities of the blend films. The hole mobilities were estimated to be $1 . 4 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ and $2 . 2 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ s ^ { - 1 }$ for neat polymer flms of Z3 and Z4 (Figure S4), respectively. The higher mobility of Z4 could be attributed to the more tightly stacking of the polymer. The hole/electron mobilities of Z3- and Z4-based blend films shown in Figure Sc,d are $2 . 2 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } / 1 . 5 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ and $5 . 7 7 \times$ $1 0 ^ { - 4 } / 5 . 2 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m ^ { 2 } \ V ^ { - 1 } \ s ^ { - 1 } } ,$ respectively. Accordingly, the ratios of the hole-to-electron mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } \right)$ are 1.44 and 1.09, respectively. The higher and more balanced mobility of Z4:Y6 is consistent with its film morphology and molecular aggregation, which would inhibit charge recombination and increase the FF and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$

To understand the energy transfer between the polymer donor and acceptor, the photoluminescence spectra of pure and blend films were performed at the excitation wavelength of $5 3 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ As shown in Figure 6a, the pure films of Z3 and Z4 showed the emission peaks at 643 and $6 1 9 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively, and a significantly large intensity could be observed in Z4. After blending with Y6, the emission peaks of both materials are quenched completely, signifying an efficient photoinduced charge transfer between the donor and the acceptor.

The dependence of $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ versus light density $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ was investigated to probe the charge recombination of the devices. As reflected in the power law connection $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \propto P ^ { \alpha } ,$ when the exponential factor $\alpha$ approaches 1, a low bimolecular recombination is achieved.6 As shown in Figure $6  { \mathbf { b } }$ compared with the Z3-based device $\prime \alpha = 0 . 9 4 7 ,$ , the Z4-based device exhibits $\alpha$ closer to 1, which is 0.988, indicating that the bimolecular charge recombination is suppressed more effectively in OsCs, thereby improving the morphology and balancing the charge transfer. Meanwhile, based on the linear law $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } \propto \operatorname { I n } ( P )$ shown in Figure 6c, the slopes of the fitting lines of Z3- and Z4-based OSCs are 1.47 and $1 . 2 6 ~ k T / q ,$ respectively. The smaller slope of the Z4-based device indicated the lower monomolecular recombination. Finally, the charge collection probability $P ( E , \ T )$ was investigated (Figure 6d) to comprehensively understand charge extraction and exciton dissociation. Considering that the incident photons are the only factor affecting the photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { { s a t } } } \right)$ , the photocurrent $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ can be equal to the saturated photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { { s a t } } } \right) ^ { \mathrm { { } } }$ when the effective voltage $\left( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } \right)$ approached $2 . 0 \mathrm { V } .$ In this case, $P ( E , T )$ , featured by the ratio of $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ is 0.874 and 0.964 for Z3- and Z4-based devices, respectively, indicating the high efficiency of exciton dissociation into free charges, which are then collected by the electrodes in Z4:Y6 devices.

# CONCLUSIONS

We synthesized two 2D conjugated polymers (Z3 and Z4) based on the good coplanarity BDT and high mobility DTBT units with different conjugated side chains. Polymer Z3 containing alkylthiophenylthienyl side chains showed the stronger $\pi - \pi$ interaction, excessive molecular aggregation, and further inferior $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF. After getting rid of the thienyl in the side chains, polymer Z4 with alkylthiophenyl side chains achieved an appropriate $\pi - \pi$ stacking and a favorable morphology. Consequently, the bulk heterojunction devices of Z4:Y6 exhibited an impressive PCE of $1 5 . 1 2 \%$ , which was nearly $3 0 \%$ higher than that of Z3-based devices, with a significantly higher $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 6 . 2 7 ~ \mathrm { m A ~ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and FF of $7 1 . 0 6 \%$ These findings show how to finely tune the aggregation of 2D polymeric donors with the appropriate intermolecular packing through modifying the conjugate side chain for OSCs.
