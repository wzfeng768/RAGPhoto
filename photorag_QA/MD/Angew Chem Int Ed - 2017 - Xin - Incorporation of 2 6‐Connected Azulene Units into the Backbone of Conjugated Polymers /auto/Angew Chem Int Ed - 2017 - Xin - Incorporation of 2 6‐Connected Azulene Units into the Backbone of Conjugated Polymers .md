# Incorporation of 2,6-Connected Azulene Units into the Backbone of Conjugated Polymers: Towards High-Performance Organic Optoelectronic Materials

Hanshen Xin, Congwu Ge, Xuechen Jiao, Xiaodi Yang, Kira Rundel, Christopher R. McNeill and Xike Gao\*

Abstract: Azulene is a promising candidate for constructing optoelectronic materials. An effective strategy is presented to obtain high-performance conjugated polymers by incorporating 2,6-connected azulene units into the polymeric backbone, and two conjugated copolymers P(TBAzDI-TPD) and $P ( T B A z D I \cdot T F B )$ were designed and synthesized based on this strategy. They are the first two examples for 2,6-connected azulene-based conjugated polymers and exhibit unipolar ntype transistor performance with an electron mobility of up to $ { 0 . 4 2 ~ c m ^ { 2 } } V ^ { - I }  { s ^ { - I } }$ which is among the highest values for n-type polymeric semiconductors in bottom-gate top-contact organic field-effect transistors. Preliminary all-polymer solar cell devices with $P ( T B A z D I \cdot T P D )$ as the electron acceptor and PTB7-Th as the electron donor display a power conversion efficiency of $1 . 8 2 \%$

Azulene, a nonbenzenoid aromatic hydrocarbon with beautiful blue color and a large dipole moment of $1 . 0 8 \mathbf { D } _ { \mathbf { \nu } } ^ { [ 1 ] }$ has attracted particular attention owing to its unique electronic structure and unusual physical properties. The nonalternant hydrocarbon character of azulene results from the fusion of an electron-deficient seven-membered ring and an electronrich five-membered ring (Figure 1a), affording a dissimilar distribution of electron density between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO).] This non-mirror-related frontier orbital geometry endows azulene with a small energy gap. Owing to its unique optical and electronic properties, azulene has been long considered to be a promising candidate for application in materials science.l In the last few years, azulene has gained increasing attention for its applications in organic field-effect transistors (OFETs)[4) and solar cells.] These developments highlight the attractive properties of azulene and its ability to construct novel $\pi$ -conjugated systems for organic semiconductors, but many difficulties and challenges are still being faced to effectively design and synthesize azulene-based conjugated molecules/polymers. In particular, the lack of azulene-containing polymers (especially the unreported ones incorporating 2,6-connected azulene units), limits the availability of azulene in numerous materials application.

Conjugated polymeric semiconductors have been receiving increasing attention in the field of organic optoelectronics such as OFETs and polymer solar cells (PSCs).] However, there are few n-type polymers for high performance OFETs and PSCs. Up to now, the design and synthesis of azulenebased conjugated polymers have been focused on the modification of either the five-membered ring or sevenmembered ring of azulene;3-i4d,8] those incorporating 2,6- connected azulene units have not been reported. Herein, we report first two azulene-containing conjugated copolymers, P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB) (Figure 1b) for high performance n-type OFETs, and all-polymer solar cells (allPSCs),where $\mathrm { T B A z D I } = N , N ^ { \prime }$ -bis(2-decyltetradecyl)-6,6'- di(thiophen-2-yl)-[2,2'-biazulene]- $^ { . 1 , 1 ^ { \prime } , 3 , 3 ^ { \prime } }$ -tetracarboxdiimide, $\mathrm { T P D } = N .$ -dodecylthieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6-dione and $\mathrm { T F B } = 1 , 2 , 4 , 5$ -tetrafluorobenzene. There are two significant considerations for this molecular design: 1) conjugated oligomers/polymers with 2- and/or 6-connected azulene units could completely employ the donor-acceptor (D-A) structures of azulene moities to provide more efficient $\pi - \pi$ stacking,4,8] which is crucial for achieving high-performance OFETs and organic solar cells; and 2) $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -biazulene- $\cdot 1 , 1 ^ { \prime } , 3 , 3 ^ { \prime } .$ tetracarboxylic diimides (BAzDIs)[4e] were selected as new electron acceptor building blocks for constructing conjugated polymers incorprating 2,6-connected azulene units. OFET devices based on P(TBAzDI-TPD) exhibit electron mobilities of up to $0 . 4 2 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathbf { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathbf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ which is among the highest mobility values for bottom-gate top-contact (BGTC) n-channel OFETs based on polymers. Furthermore, an all-PSC device with polymer P(TBAzDI-TPD) as the electron acceptor and PTB7-Th as the electron donor exhibits a preliminary power conversion efficiency (PCE) value of $1 . 8 2 \%$ , indicating its great potential for electron acceptor of all-PsCs. Our studies demonstrated that incorporation of 2,6-connected azulene units into the backbone of conjugated polymers is an efficient strategy for achieving high performance organic optoelectronic materials.

The synthesis routes of P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDITFB) are shown in Scheme 1. Compound 1 was prepared according to the previous reports. The Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling reaction of 1 and 2-thiopheneboronic acid was then carried out to afford compound 2 in $9 1 \%$ yield. Our first attempt to chlorination of 2 via Sandmeyer reaction using isoamyl nitrite and hydrogen chloride gas failed. After some exploration, intermediate 3 was synthesized efficiently with a yield of $9 3 \%$ by using isoamyl nitrite and chlorotrimethylsilane. Compound 3 was then underwent a self-coupling reaction to give the key precursor 4 in $8 9 \%$ yield. Hydrolysis of 4 produced acid 5 in $8 2 \%$ yield, which was then treated with acetic anhydride, affording dianhydride 6 in $8 6 \%$ yield. The imidization reaction of dianhydride 6 with 2-decyltetradecan-1-amine successfully gave TBAzDI building unit 7 in $4 2 \%$ yield. Dibromination of diimides 7 provided monomer 8 in nearly quantitative yield $( 9 6 \% )$ .The copolymers P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB) were synthesized via palladium catalyzed direct $\mathrm { C - H }$ arylation polymerization $( \mathrm { D A r P } ) ^ { [ 1 0 ] }$ using 8 to react with TPD and TFB, respectively. The chemical structures of P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB) were characterized by $\mathrm { ^ { 1 } H N M R }$ and $^ { 1 9 } \mathrm { F }$ NMR (for P(TBAzDI-TFB)) spectroscopy, as well as elemental analysis. The $^ { 1 9 } \mathrm { F }$ NMR spectrum exhibits a signal at $\delta = - 1 3 8 \mathrm { p p m }$ , suggesting the existence of TFB units in the polymeric backbone of P(TBAzDI-TFB) (see the Supporting Information). The number-average molecular weight $( M _ { \mathrm { n } } )$ and polydispersity index (PDI) determined by gel permeation chromatography (GPC) were $2 2 . 1 \mathrm { K D a }$ and 3.68 for P(TBAzDI-TPD) and $1 3 . 5 \mathrm { K D a }$ and 3.26 for P(TBAzDITFB), respectively. P(TBAzDI-TPD) is soluble in hot chlorobenzene (CB), while P(TBAzDI-TFB) has good solubility in chloroform (CF) and CB $( > 5 \mathrm { \ m g { m L } ^ { - 1 } ) }$ at $5 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ which may be ascribed to the fact that P(TBAzDI-TFB) has lower $M _ { \mathfrak { n } }$ than P(TBAzDI-TPD). Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) measurements revealed that both polymers have excellent thermal stability with decomposition temperatures of over $4 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (Supporting Information, Figures S1a and S1b). As shown in Figures S1c and S1d, no thermal transitions were observed for both of two polymers by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC).

To investigate the electronic structures of P(TBAzDITPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB), density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed on their model compounds at the B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) level using the Gaussian09 program.11] As shown in the Supporting Information, Figure S2, for both polymers, the electron density distribution of HOMO and LUMO is well delocalized over the TBAzDI and TPD (or TFB) moieties. This is different from those of NDI-, PDI-, and IID-based n-type donor-acceptor copolymers, where the LUMOs are usually localized on the acceptor units of the polymeric backbones.[12] Therefore, the LUMO levels of BAzDI-based copolymers are determined by both the BAzDI unit and the co-monomer unit and could be efficiently tuned by changing the co-monomer units. Moreover, the wellspread frontier orbitals would be beneficial for effective charge carrier transport. The calculated HOMO/LUMO energies of P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB) are $- 5 . 6 6 / - 3 . 3 7 \ : \mathrm { e V }$ and $- 5 . 7 3 / - 3 . 3 3 \mathrm { e V }$ respectively.

The UV/Vis absorption spectra of the two polymers in $^ o$ dichlorobenzene $^ o$ -DCB) solutions and in thin films are shown in Figures 2a. The data are listed in Table 1. Both P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB) exhibit dual-band absorptions in the range of $3 0 0 { - } 8 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , where the former bands correspond to the $\pi - \pi ^ { * }$ transitions of the conjugated building block, such as oligothiophene unit,[13] and the bands appearing at the higher wavelength are intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) absorption of the polymeric backbone. Notably, at the higher wavelength region, the absorption spectrum of P(TBAzDI-TPD) shows a strong band at $6 6 1 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ , while that of P(TBAzDI-TFB) is blue-shifted to $6 1 6 \mathrm { n m }$ . As shown in Figure 2a and Table 1, only a slight red-shift $( < 1 0 \mathrm { n m } )$ is detectable for these two polymers in going from solution to thin films. Optical band gaps $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { \ o p t } } )$ of P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB) estimated from the onset absorption of the thin films are 1.57 and $1 . 6 0 \mathrm { e V } .$ respectively.

To elucidate the electrochemical behaviors of the polymers containing the 2,6-connected azulene units, cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements of thin films of these polymers were implemented. As shown in Figure 2b, both P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB) show an irreversible oxidation wave with the onset oxidation potential ( $\cdot { E _ { \mathrm { o n s e t } } } ^ { \mathrm { o x } }$ vs. $\mathrm { F c / F c ^ { + } ) }$ of $1 . 0 2 \mathrm { V }$ and $0 . 9 6 \mathrm { V } ,$ respectively, and multiple quasireversible reduction waves with the first onset reduction potential $( E _ { \mathrm { o n s e t } } \mathrm { ^ r e d } 1 )$ of $1 . 0 4 \mathrm { V }$ and $1 . 2 0 \mathrm { V } ,$ respectively. The HOMO/LUMO levels of P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDITFB) estimated from CV are $- 5 . 8 2 / - 3 . 7 6 \mathrm { e V }$ and $- 5 . 7 6 /$ $- 3 . 6 0 \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively, which is consistent with the DFT calculated results, suggesting their potential applications as ntype polymers in OFETs and PSCs.

To investigate the charge transport properties of P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB), we fabricated their BGTC thin film transistors and measured the device performance under nitrogen atmosphere. Figure 3 displays the typical transfer and output characteristics of OFET devices based on P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB), respectively, and the data for device performance are summarized in Table2. Both of two polymers show unipolar n-channel charge transport behaviors When annealed at $1 2 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ thin films of P(TBAzDI-TPD) exhibited an optimized electron mobility of $0 . 4 2 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathbf { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathbf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and a current on/off ratio of $1 0 ^ { 5 } -$ $1 0 ^ { 6 }$ To the best of our knowledge, there are few pure electron transport polymeric  semiconductors with mobilities $\geq 0 . 3 \ : \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathbf { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathbf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ in BGTC OFET devices,[14] thus, P(TBAzDI-TPD) is among the best unipolar n-type polymers. Thin films of P(TBAzDI-TFB) also displayed nice device performance with electron mobilities of up to $0 . 2 4 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathbf { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathbf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ when annealed at $2 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ The results demonstrate that incorporation of 2,6-connected azulene units into the backbone of conjugated polymers could afford excellent electrontransporting polymers. Furthermore, the lower $M _ { \mathfrak { n } }$ of P(TBAzDI-TFB) relative to P(TBAzDI-TPD) may be one of the reasons for its relatively lower electron mobility.[15]

The microstructures and surface topographic features of the as-spun and thermal annealed thin films of P(TBAzDITPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB) were investigated by grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) and atomic force microscopy (AFM). The 2D GIWAXS patterns and the corresponding 1D out-of-plane and in-plane scattering profiles are provided in Figure 4 and the Supporting Information, Figure S3 and S4. The crystallographic parameters are presented in the Supporting Information, Table S1. Both polymers show multiple orders of alkyl (lamellar) stacking along $q _ { z }$ indexed as $( h 0 0 )$ , indicating a high propensity of these two polymers to crystallize and a predominantly "edgeon" orientation. The $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking peaks (010) are observed in-plane consistent with this orientation. This packing pattern is beneficial to achieve higher charge mobility in the direction parallel to the substrate.16] The (010) peaks are observed for both two polymers with value of $q \approx 1 . 7 7 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ corresponding to $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking distance of about $3 . 5 5 \mathrm { \AA }$ which is among the shortest value for the polymers with high mobilities.[17] This results demonstrated that employing the D-A structures of azulene units can provide more efficient $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking. Furthermore, the thermal annealing treatments of thin films lead to different changes in scattering patterns for these two polymers (see the Supporting Information for detail). The scattering patterns of thin films of P(TBAzDI-TPD) showed limited changes upon thermal annealing, whereas thin films of P(TBAzDI-TFB) were found to exhibit stronger and sharper scattering peaks with the increased thermal annealing temperature, which correlates well with the steady improvement of the device performance of P(TBAzDI-TPD) and the rapid increase of the device performance of P(TBAzDI-TFB) upon thermal annealing. AFM images of the as-spun and thermal annealed films of P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB) are shown in the Supporting Information, Figure S5. Both of two polymers provided smooth and uniform films with no evidence of pinholes and a root-mean-square (RMS) roughness of $< 1 \mathrm { n m }$ and there are no obvious changes upon thermal annealing.

For P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB), their suitable HOMO/LUMO levels, high electron mobilities as well as strong absorption in visible region encouraged us to fabricate all-PSC devices with these two polymers as electron acceptor and PTB7-Th (Figure 5a) as electron donor. As shown in Figure $^ { 5 \mathrm { b } }$ the ALUMO and $\Delta \mathrm { H O M O }$ between these two polymers and PTB7-Th are larger than $0 . 3 \mathrm { e V } ,$ suggesting that exciton dissociation and fluorescence quenching (Supporting Information, Figure S6) might be efficient in blend with appropriate donor polymers.[18] Figures 5c and d depict the current density-voltage $( J { - } V )$ curve and external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of devices based on PTB7- Th:P(TBAzDI-TPD) and PTB7-Th:P(TBAzDI-TFB) blend, respectively. The photovoltaic parameters as well as charge mobilities of blend films measured by space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method are presented in the Supporting Information, Table S2. An all-PSC device with PTB7- Th:P(TBAzDI-TPD) blend as active layer exhibits an opencircuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ of $0 . 7 7 \mathrm { V } ,$ a short-circuit density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ of $5 . 7 6 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and a PCE value of $1 . 8 2 \%$ , making P(TBAzDITPD) among the best non-naphthalene diimides-based polymer acceptors for all-PSCs.[19] The device based on PTB7- Th:P(TBAzDI-TPD) blend shows an EQE response from $3 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ to $8 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ with the maximum value of $3 2 \%$ . AFM image of the blend film of PTB7-Th and P(TBAzDI-TPD) is shown in the Supporting Information, Figure S9, the crude featureless film morphology is not optimal for charge transport, and there is still much room to improve the device performance by controlling the thin film morphology.

In summary, we demonstrate an efficient strategy to obtain high performance organic optoelectronic materials by incorporation of 2,6-connected azulene units into the backbone of conjugated polymers. Following this strategy, the first two examples for 2,6-azulene-based copolymers, P(TBAzDITPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB), have been designed and synthesized by DArP. Both P(TBAzDI-TPD) and P(TBAzDI-TFB) exhibited excellent OFET performance with electron mobilities of $0 . 4 2 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathbf { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathbf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $0 . 2 4 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathbf { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathbf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ ,respectively, making these two polymers among the best unipolar n-type polymers for BGTC OFETs. Furthermore, an all-PSC device based on PTB7-Th: P(TBAzDI-TPD) blend showed a PCE value of $1 . 8 2 \%$ . The results demonstrate that our strategy could afford high performance polymeric optoelectronic materials and would contribute to the development of azulene chemistry. Further studies on design and synthesis of novel conjugated polymers containing 2,6-connected azulene units are currently underway in our laboratory.
