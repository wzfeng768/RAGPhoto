# Conversion of 2-Iodobiaryls into $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -Diiodobiaryls via OxidationIodination Sequences: A Versatile Route to Ladder-Type Heterofluorenes\*\*

Bin Wu and Naohiko Yoshikai\*

Abstract: Even though 2,2’-diiodo- and $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -dibromobiaryls represent accomplished precursors for heterofluorenes and other extended $\pi$ -conjugated systems, their preparation still remains nontrivial when structural diversity of the biaryl backbone is required. Herein, we report a convenient method for the preparation of various 2,2’-diiodobiaryls from 2- iodobiaryls via cyclic diaryliodonium intermediates. An iodinative ring-opening of the diaryliodonium salts, mediated by a copper/diamine catalyst system, is able to afford the corresponding $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -diiodobiaryls under mild conditions. The versatility of this two-step approach is demonstrated by the preparation of hitherto unexplored tetraiodoteraryls and their conversion into ladder-type $\pi$ -conjugated systems.

Heteroatom-bridged biaryls (heterofluorenes or dibenzoheteroles), containing Group 13–16 heteroatom elements, have garnered significant attention as building blocks of pconjugated functional materials with optical and electronic applications.[1–4] Over the years, a variety of preparative methods for the synthesis of heterofluorenes have been established, especially well for the naturally occurring nitrogen- and oxygen-containing analogues (carbazoles and dibenzofurans).[5] But regardless, synthetic methods for the inclusion of other bridging heteroatoms in heterofluorenes still represent desirable research targets, as they provide a means to modulate the electronic nature of the $\pi$ -conjugated systems.[6] The most frequently used precursors to such heterofluorenes are $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -dihalobiaryls $\mathbf { \boldsymbol { X } } = \mathbf { \boldsymbol { B } } \mathbf { \boldsymbol { r } }$ or I), which are usually subjected to halogen–lithium exchange, followed by trapping with boron,[1a] silicon,[7] phosphorus,[8] or other heteroatom electrophiles (route A, Scheme 1 a).[9, 10] Recently, approaches based on $\mathrm { C - H }$ functionalizations have emerged. For example, the Pd-catalyzed intramolecular $\mathrm { C - X / C - H }$ coupling of silicon-,[11] phosphorus-,[12] or sulfur-tethered[13] arenes allows the efficient preparation of the corresponding heterofluorenes (route B, Scheme 1 a). Alternatively, biaryls bearing appropriate ortho-substituents (Si, P, S) may undergo an intramolecular heteroatom–aryl bond formation, thus affording dibenzosiloles,[14] dibenzophospholes,[15] or dibenzothiophenes (route C, Scheme 1 a).[16] Even though each synthetic route has its own advantages, they also have individual drawbacks. Route A, for example, allows the conversion of common $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -dihalobiaryl precursors into a variety of heterofluorenes, but the synthesis of a structurally diverse set of $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -dihalobiaryls is laborious and nontrivial.[17] Routes B and C are suitable for the synthesis of specific heterofluorenes with various substituents, but are not a priori divergent with respect to the heteroatom. Consequently, a practical solution to enable diversity of both the central heteroatom and the peripheral substituents would be attractive.[18]

Herein, we disclose an expansion to route A for the synthesis of heterofluorenes enabled by the conversion of readily available 2-iodobiaryls (1) into the corresponding $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ - diiodobiaryls (3) in two steps through oxidation to cyclic diaryliodonium triflates (2),[19] followed by a copper/diaminecatalyzed iodinative ring-opening reaction (Scheme 1 b). Although conversion of 1 to 3 was previously achieved by the thermal decomposition of cyclic diaryliodonium iodide at

$2 0 0 { - } 2 5 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ ,[20] this classical method requires several steps under harsh conditions, and has hence found limited applications. In contrast, the present approach allows the facile preparation of a wide variety of $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -diiodobiaryls, as well as the generation of hitherto unexplored tetraiodoteraryl derivatives, which may serve as versatile precursors for the construction of ladder-type $\pi$ -conjugated systems.[6b]

The key to the present two-step preparation of 2,2’- diiodobiaryls is the iodinative ring-opening of the cyclic diaryliodonium salts. We envisioned that an oxidative addition of the aryl¢ $- \mathrm { { I } } ^ { \mathrm { { I I I } } }$ bond to a $\mathrm { C u ^ { I } }$ catalyst should generate a 2- iodo- ${ \boldsymbol { \cdot } } { \boldsymbol { 2 ^ { \prime } } }$ -cuprio(III)biaryl intermediate (A),[19a, 21] which should subsequently be intercepted by an iodide anion $\mathbf { \delta } ( \mathbf { B } )$ to furnish the desired $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -diiodobiaryl through $\mathrm { C } \mathrm { - } \mathrm { I }$ bond-forming reductive elimination (Scheme 1 b). On the basis of this hypothesis, dibenzo[b,d]iodol-5-ium triflate (2 a) was treated with tetrabutylammonium iodide in dioxane at room temperature in the presence of various copper catalysts $( 1 0 \mathrm { m o l } \%$ ; Table 1). Using CuI in the absence of any additives afforded $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -diiodobiphenyl (3 a) merely in low yield $1 0 \%$ ; entry 1, Table 1). However, using a combination of $\mathrm { C u I }$ and N,N’- dimethylcyclohexane-1,2-diamine (L1, $2 0 \mathrm { m o l } \%$ )[22] resulted in the clean conversion of 2 a to 3 a in $9 5 \%$ yield as determined by $\mathrm { ^ { 1 } H N M R }$ spectroscopy $8 9 \%$ yield of the isolated product; entry 2, Table 1). Other 1,2-diamine ligands (L2–L4, Table 1), $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -bipyridine, and 1,10-phenanthroline also promoted the iodinative ring-opening, albeit in somewhat lower yields $( 5 7 - 8 3 \%$ ; entries 3–7, Table 1). As alternative sources of iodide, NaI and KI could be used instead of $[ ( n \mathrm { B u } ) _ { 4 } \mathrm { N } ] \mathrm { I }$ (entries 8 and 9, Table 1). Even though reduced loadings of CuI $( 5 \mathrm { m o l } \% )$ and L1 $( 1 0 \mathrm { m o l \% } )$ resulted in significantly decreased yields (entry 10, Table 1), the reaction can be readily scaled up to 5 mmol (entry 11, Table 1). It is worth noting that the use of $[ ( n \mathbf { B } \mathbf { u } ) _ { 4 } \mathbf { N } ] \mathbf { B } \mathbf { r }$ instead of $[ ( n \mathrm { B u } ) _ { 4 } \mathrm { N } ] \mathrm { I }$ promoted the brominative ring-opening of $\mathbf { 2 4 }$ to afford 2-iodo- $. 2 ^ { \prime }$ -bromobiphenyl ( $5 3 \%$ yield; data not shown).

We next explored the scope of the iodinative ring-opening reaction (Scheme 2). Various 2-iodobiaryls (1 b–1 q) were readily oxidized using a mixture of mCPBA (1.5 equiv) and TfOH (3 equiv) in $\mathrm { C H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { C l } _ { 2 }$ (room temperature, 1 h),[19, 23] thus affording the corresponding cyclic diaryliodonium triflates $( 2 \mathbf { b } \mathbf { - } 2 \mathbf { q } )$ . We extended the present method to the conversion of diiodoteraryls into the corresponding tetraiodoteraryls (Scheme 3). The oxidation of $^ { 2 ^ { \prime } , 5 ^ { \prime } }$ -diiodo- $p$ -terphenyl derivatives $\bf { 4 a - 4 d }$ proceeded cleanly to afford bis-iodonium triflates $\mathbf { 5 4 - 5 d }$ in high yields $( 8 7 - 9 8 \%$ ; Scheme 3 a). The ladder-type structure of these bis-iodonium triflates was confirmed by X-ray crystallographic analysis of single crystals of 5 b (see inset in Scheme 3 a).[24] A twofold iodinative ringopening of $\mathbf { 5 4 - 5 d }$ took place in acetonitrile at $6 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } .$ affording the desired $2 , 2 ^ { \prime } , 2 ^ { \prime \prime } , 5 ^ { \prime }$ -tetraiodo- $p$ -terphenyl derivatives $\bf { 6 4 - 6 d }$ as light-sensitive solids in moderate to good yields $( 5 1 - 7 2 \%$ ; Scheme 3 a). Furthermore, 2,5-bis(2-iodophenyl)thiophene (7) was also amenable to a twofold oxidation/ring-opening, which resulted in the formation of 3,4-diiodo-2,5-bis(2- iodophenyl)thiophene (9) in moderate yield $4 4 \%$ ; Scheme 3 b).

The diiodobiaryl and tetraiodoteraryl derivatives synthesized by the present method should be able to serve as versatile starting materials for the synthesis of heterofluorenes and other extended $\pi$ -conjugated systems. This idea was validated by conversion of tetraiodoteraryls 6 b and 9 into various ladder-type $\pi$ -conjugated systems (Scheme 4a,b). Exposure of 6 b to a catalytic amount of CuI, sulfur powder, and ${ \bf K } _ { 2 } { \bf C } { \bf O } _ { 3 }$ in $\mathrm { D M F } ^ { [ 2 5 ] }$ resulted in a fourfold $\mathrm { C } { - } \mathrm { \bf { S } }$ bond formation to afford benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b’]bis[b]benzothiophene[26] 10 in $8 6 \%$ yield. A similar Cu-catalyzed method using selenium and ( $N$ -methyl-2-pyrrolidone) (NMP) instead of sulfur and $\mathrm { D M F } ^ { \mathrm { [ 1 8 a ] } }$ allowed conversion of 6 b and 9 into benzo[1,2-b:4,5- $^ { b ^ { \prime } }$ ’]bis[b]benzoselenophene[26a] (11) and dibenzo $[ d , d ^ { \prime } ]$ thieno[3,2-b;4,5-b’]diselenophene (15). These results suggest great promise for a catalyst system based on copper and elemental sulfur/selenium to prepare dibenzothiophenes and dibenzoselenophenes directly from $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -diiodobiaryls.[27] A twofold iodine–lithium exchange/electrophilic trapping sequence proved effective for the preparation of germanium- and phosphoryl-bridged $p$ -teraryls 12 and 13,[15a, 28] respectively. The latter product was obtained as a separable mixture of syn- and anti-isomers. Besides the heteroatom-bridged $p$ -teraryls, the Pd-catalyzed reaction of 6 b with 5-decyne, using WuÏs conditions,[29] resulted in a twofold annulation to afford benzo[k]tetraphene derivative 14. Single-crystal X-ray diffraction analysis was performed for all of these ladder-type products except anti-13, which invariably demonstrated high levels of coplanarity of their skeletons in the solid state (see Scheme 4 c for 11, 12, and syn13, and the Supporting Information for further details).[24]

In summary, we have developed a convenient two-step method for the preparation of $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -diiodobiaryls from readily accessible 2-iodobiaryls through oxidative cyclization/Cucatalyzed iodinative ring-opening sequences. Even though cyclic diaryliodonium intermediates have previously been reported to serve as direct precursors to carbazoles and some other bridged biaryl compounds,[19, 30] the method presented herein should substantially enhance their utility for the synthesis of a wide range of heterofluorenes. Indeed, the versatility of this method has been demonstrated by the preparation of novel tetraiodoteraryls and their conversion into various ladder-type $\pi$ -conjugated systems. Furthermore, the present method may also be useful for the diversification of KitaÏs $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -diiodobiaryl-based organocatalysts for oxidation reactions.[31]
