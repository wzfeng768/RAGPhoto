# Heteroatom-Bridged Benzothiazolyls for Organic Solar Cells: A Theoretical Study

ABSTRACT: On the basis of a typical organic photovoltaic (OPV) building block of 4,7-di(thiophen-2-yl)benzo[c][1,2,5]thiadiazole (DTBT), a series of novel DTBT derivatives were designed following a heteroatom-bridging strategy to take advantage of the diversified interactions between heteroatoms and $\pi$ -conjugated systems. These heteroatom-bridged DTBTs, whose outer electronrich thiophene moieties are covalently fastened to the central electron-deficient benzothiadiazole with additional heteroatom bridges, exhibit promising features for OPV applications with rigid molecular structures, properly lain frontier molecular orbitals (FMOs), broad and intense absorption spectra, and adequate charge transport properties, as revealed by systematic theoretical calculations on molecular geometries, FMOs, absorption spectra, and relaxation and reorganization energies. The structure-property relationship investigations show that the mono-/di-heteroatom bridging iseffective not only in tuning the rigidity of the molecular geometries but also in adjusting the optoelectronic properties of the resulting material. Among the studied heteroatoms, the C and Si were found to be the most eficient in designing novel molecules for OPV applications. These theoretical insights may provide a solid basis for experimental synthesis and device investigations of the proposed heteroatom-bridged DTBTs as potential high-performance building blocks for bulk heterojunction OPV molecules.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Cost-effective organic photovoltaic (Opv) devices, based on composites of electron-donating conjugated polymers and electron-accepting fullerenes, are promising for the realization of printable, portable, and flexible renewable energy sources due to their inherent advantages over their inorganic counterparts.-3 The device performance with bulk heterojunction (BHJ) structure has been steadily improved with power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) over $1 0 \% , ^ { 4 , 5 }$ fueled by the continuous efforts in the molecular structure engineering of conjugated polymers used as the electron-donating materials.6-- However, the building blocks for high performance OPV materials (PCE ${ > } 8 . 0 \%$ ) are still limited to several intensively investigated ones, such as difluorobenzothiadiazole,4,10 4,7- di(thien-2-yl)-5,6-difluoro-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole,'1 and thieno[3,4-b]thiophen-4,6-diyl.12

To design new building blocks for polymer-based BHJ solar cells with high PCE, the following issues need to be particularly considered. First, the open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ is tightly correlated to the energy level difference between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of the donor and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of the acceptor but is also affected by a couple of other factors. When the widely used [6,6]-phenyl- $C _ { 6 1 }$ -butyric acid methyl ester $\big ( \mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M } \big )$ with a LUMO level of $- 4 . 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ is adopted as the acceptor, the lowest possible LUMO level of the donor would be near $- 3 . 9 \ \mathrm { e V }$ to offer a minimum energy difference of ${ \sim } 0 . 3$ $\mathtt { e V }$ to facilitate efficient exciton splitting and charge dissociation.4 The low-lying HOMO level will lead to high $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ but the HOMO cannot go too low in order to effectively absorb sunlight with a suitable optical bandgap.15 Second, the theoretical upper limit of the short-circuit current $\left( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \right)$ is determined by the number of excitons created after the photon absorption of solar illumination. Roughly. $7 0 \%$ sunlight energy is distributed in the wavelength region from 380 to $9 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m } ,$ which can be hardly absorbed by $\mathsf { \Gamma } _ { \mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M } } ^ { \mathrm { o } }$ 16 An ideal donor should have a broad and strong absorption in this range, which requires a bandgap of $1 . 4 { - } 1 . { \stackrel { \smile } { . } } { \mathrm { \ e V . } } ^ { 1 7 }$ Third, a high fill factor (FF) requires a small series resistance and a large shunt resistance that are impacted by the morphology of the donor/ $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ blend.18 Promoted charge separation and favorable transport of photogenerated charges under optimized morphology of the active layer are highly preferred for maximized FF and attainable $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } .$ Finally, solution processability with good solubility in common solvents and long-term stability of OPV devices are of equal importance for future commercialization. 19 If one assumes an external quantum efficiency of $6 5 \%$ an optimal bandgap of $1 . 5 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ and a HOMO level around $- 5 . 4 \mathrm { e V }$ of an ideal donor, its $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ based BHJ solar cell can achieve a PCE of $1 0 \%$ with a FF of 0.65.

The heterocyclic quinoid compound of 4,7-dithien-2-yl-2,1,3- benzothiadiazole (DTBT), first introduced by Dhanabalan in 2001,' has demonstrated a high PCE in typical BHJ solar cells as a famous strong acceptor for constructing ideal OPV polymers following the "weak donor (D)--strong acceptor (A) strategy.' A large variety of donors such as thiophene, fluorene, silafluorene, carbazole, cyclopentadithiophene, bithiophenzosilole, benzodithiophene, etc., have been conjugated to DTBT for OPV applications with PCE up to $6 . 2 \%$ 20 Also, with the incorporation of metallopolyyne, the DTBT-based metalated conjugated polymers show a high PCE with an average of $4 . 1 \%$ I8 Impressed by the particular molecular structure and properties of DTBT, a large number of researchers were devoted to the chemical modification of DTBT for improved device performance. You et al.21 replaced the benzene in 2,1,3- benzothiadiazole (BT) with the $\pi$ electron-deficient pyridine to construct a stronger acceptor unit; their polymers show a highly respectable PCE over $5 . 5 \%$ . They also replaced the two hydrogen atoms on the BT unit with two fluorine atoms to take advantage of the special features of fluorine; the overall PCE of the BHJ device achieved $7 . 2 \%$ 22 The substitute groups and the substitution sites on the two thiophenes also exhibit profound effects on the device performance.23

Besides tailoring the electronic structures of the $\pi$ conjugated molecules, the control of the solid-state structure to obtain appropriate intermolecular interactions is another aspect of the extensive investigations of optoelectronic materials.44 Multifused aromatic and heteroaromatic compounds with flat and rigid frameworks possess effectively extended $\pi$ conjugation and dense molecular packing, leading to spectacular properties such as high carrier mobility. Inspired by the 2D collinearly fully fused molecular structures of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs),5 Hsu et al.26 bridged the surrounding thiophenes and BT with nitrogen to construct a multifused dithienopyrrolobenzothiadiazole; the forced planarization by covalently fastening adjacent aromatic units provides an additional way to control the bandgap and to enhance the intrinsic charge mobility. A PCE of $3 . 4 \%$ has been reached. The nitrogen bridges not only planarize the molecular structure to induce stronger intermolecular $\pi - \pi$ interaction but also play an important role in determining the electronic and photophysical properties of the resulting polymers for OPV applications. The relatively low PCE of the N-bridged DTBT is mainly due to the high-lying HOMO and LUMO resulting from the strong electron donating ability of the N atom.?' According to the studies of heterofluorenes,2 \$,28-32 many other heteroatoms with characteristic electronic properties can also be adopted. In addition, the specific interactions between the bridging heteroatom and the $\pi \cdot$ conjugated organic framework may induce novel electronic states with significantly modified optoelectronic properties for various device applications.33 However, the experimental attempts to use other heteroatom bridges have not been reported yet, presumably due to the difficulties in synthesis. Herein, we theoretically investigated the mono- and di-bridged DTBT by atoms of carbon (C), silicon (Si), nitrogen (N), oxygen (O), and sulfur (S) to give a computational scan on the structural, electronic, and optical properties of the heteroatom-bridged DTBTs (Figure 1), intending to provide theoretical insights into the structure-- property relations and to suggest suitable heteroatoms as bridges for high-performance OPV building blocks that are worthy of experimental explorations.

# 2. COMPUTATIONAL DETAILS

Theoretical calculations were carried out using the Gaussian 09 package.34 The molecular geometries at neutral, cationic, and anionic states were carefully optimized using the B3LYP (Becke's hybrid exchange functional with the Lee-Yang-Parr correlation functional) at the standard ${ 6 } { \cdot } 3 1 \mathrm { G } ^ { \ast }$ basis set. The symmetric constraint was not used in geometry optimization. Harmonic vibrational frequency analysis was performed to confirm that no imaginary vibrational frequency has been reported to make sure that the optimized stationary points represent the true minima on respective potential energy surfaces.s The frontier orbitals of the highest occupied molecular orbitals (HOMOs) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LUMOs) were calculated by B3LYP/6- 31G(d) on the basis of optimized structures at the ground states $( \mathsf { S } _ { 0 } )$ of the OPV building blocks. The bandgap $( { \bar { E } _ { \mathrm { g } } } )$ was obtained from the energy difference between HOMO and LUMO of the compound. The HOMO, LUMO, and $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of the polymers were achieved by plotting the HOMO, LUMO, and $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of the corresponding monomers, dimers, trimers, tetramers, and pentamers against their reciprocal chain length and extrapolating to infinite chain length (representing the corresponding polymers). Very good linearity between HOMO/LUMO and reciprocal chain length was observed with the correlation coefficients $\left( R ^ { 2 } \right)$ mostly higher than $9 7 \%$ The electronic absorption in a vacuum was carried out using the time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT) method of B3LYP/6-31G(d) on the basis of optimized structures at the ground state $( \boldsymbol { \varsigma } _ { 0 } )$ . The absorption spectrum was simulated by Gaussian functions with a half-width of 3000 $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ based on 20 lowest singlet energies from the TD-B3LYP/ 6-31G\* calculations.36

# 3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

3.1. Optimized Molecular Geometries. The molecular geometry impacts significantly the electronic structures and optoelectronic properties of organic functional materials. The effects of various calculation methods on the molecular geometry predictions were investigated and compared with the experimental structure revealed by single crystal structure analysis based on 4,7-bis(4-methylthiophen-2-yl)benzo[c]- [1,2,5]thiadiazole (Me-DTBT).43 The optimized molecular geometries calculated by the ab initio HF and density functional theory (DFT) of B3LYP using different basis sets are listed in Table 1. Compared to the experimental data, a larger basis set for HF and B3LYP calculations generally leads to better prediction of the molecular geometry, but when the basis set is larger than $6 { \cdot } 3 1 \mathrm { G } ^ { \ast }$ , the geometry changes slightly; the diffuse function is essential for good geometry optimization. In DTBT, there are hydrogen bond interactions between the hydrogen atoms in thiophene and the nitrogen atoms in benzothiadiazole (BT),23 leading to a relatively planar geometry of DTBT. The lack of enough considerations on the effects of this hydrogen bonding in $a b$ initio HF calculation leads to overestimated $\pmb \theta$ at large basis sets. However, the B3LYP methods produce close but slightly smaller $\pmb \theta$ (close to zero) in comparison with the experimental result. For an ideal OPV donor, its LUMO energy level should be $0 . 3 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ higher than that of $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M } ,$ i.e., LUMO $>$ $- 2 . 7 9 \ \mathrm { e V }$ - $- 3 . 0 9 + 0 . 3 = - 2 . 7 9 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ , while the HOMO should be about $1 . 5 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ lower than LUMO, i.e., LUMO  HOMO $\approx$ $1 . 5 \ \mathrm { e V }$ .Compared to this criterion, the Si- and C-bridgings must be better than the N-, $\mathrm { O } _ { \cdot } ,$ and S-bridgings and the Nbridged DTBT (TNBNT) seems to have the lowest OPV performance. However, the experimental results reveal that the TNBNT-based OPV device showed a moderate PCE of $3 . 4 0 \%$ 26 indicating that the other heterobridged DTBTs, especially Si- an C-bridged ones, may have a much more improved OPV performance than TNBNT.

To give a more in-depth investigation of their potential OPV applications, carbazole, which is a high-performance building block for OPV materials, was chosen as a comonomer for the proposed new OPV building blocks of heteroatom-bridged DTBTs. From Figure 3, the carbazole moiety has a limited effect on the LUMO, since the LUMO is dominated by the heteroatom-bridged DTBT, as demonstrated in Figure S1 (Supporting Information). However, the HOMO is significantly increased due to the typical electron-donation feature of the carbazole unit which significantly participates in the HOMO formation (Figure S1, Supporting Information) with a high lying energy level. In comparison with the optimal HOMO and LUMO of ideal donors for OPV using $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ as the acceptor,46 CzTTCBCTT and CzTTSiBSiTT are very promising candidates.

Consequently, the alternative conjugated polymers based on carbazole and heteroatom-bridged DTBT of TCBT, TSiBT, TCBCT, TSiBSiT, TTCBT, TTSiBT, TTCBCTT, and TTSiBSiTT were further investigated via plotting the HOMO, LUMO, and $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ against the reciprocal chain length of the monomer, dimer, trimer, tetramer, and pentamer and extrapolating to the infinite chain length of a polymer47 (Figure 4a). Due to the rigid molecular structure of the bridged DTBTs, the extrapolating slope is very small and the HOMO, LUMO, and $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of the pentamers are quite close to that of the polymer (Table S3, Supporting Information). Thus, these heteroatom-bridged DTBTs, particularly Si-bridged ones, should have high OPV performance and are highly worthy of experimental synthesis and device investigations.

3.4. Absorption Spectra. For a highly efficient OPV material, the high absorption ability in a wide range of wavelength that covers the whole sunlight spectrum is highly desired. The simulated absorption spectra of DTBT and heteroatom-bridged DTBTs were presented in Figure 5. The absorption peak $( \lambda _ { \mathrm { a b s } } )$ ,oscillator strength $( f )$ and main configuration of the absorption were listed in Table S5 (Supporting Information).

From Figure 5, the two predicted absorption bands (321 and ${ 5 1 9 } \ \mathrm { n m } \stackrel { . } { }$ ) of DTBT, which are quite close to the experimental measurement (310 and $4 4 5 ~ \mathrm { \ n m }$ in Figure S3, Supporting Information), show a negligible change in TNBT, TOBT, and TSBT, while, in TCBT and TSiBT, these two bands were obviously red-shifted. When thiophene is connected to the compounds, both of the two bands are further red-shifted but with the same main transition configuration (Table S5, Supporting Information); the most significant red-shift was observed from around 540 to $6 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ in C- and Si-bridged DTBTs. The same changing trend can also be concluded from the dibridged DTBTs. The sensitivity of the absorption spectrum to the varied heteroatom bridges demonstrates the strong interactions between the heteroatoms and the $\pi$ -electron system, resulting in diversified optical properties of the heteroatom-bridged DTBTs. The absorption band in TTCBCTT and TTSiBSiTT can reach up to 800 nm, indicating again their great potential in OPV applications. 50 When conjugated with carbazole, the absorption bands can be further red-shifted up to $1 0 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ (Table S6, Supporting Information); this strong absorption in a wide wavelength range is highly attractive for OPV applications.

# 4. CONCLUSIONS

A series of heteroatom-bridged DTBTs have been designed and investigated by a comprehensive DFT calculation of their structural, electronic, and optical properties for consideration as potential building blocks for $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ based bulk heterojunction OPVs. Compared to the widely used OPV building block of DTBT, the newly proposed heteroatom-bridged DTBTs, especially C- and Si-bridged DTBTs, exhibit more rigid molecular structures with broader and stronger absorption spectra and with more suitably lain FMOs and adequate reorganization energies, suggesting their great potential as new building blocks for high-performance OPV devices that are worthy of experimental explorations. The single-molecular DFT investigation demonstrates that (1) the DTBT framework is a good platform in the construction of high performance OPV molecules, capable of finely tuned electronic and optical properties; (2) the heteroatom bridging is effective not only in tuning the rigidity of the molecular geometries but also in adjusting optoelectronic properties through the strong and diversified interactions between the varied heteroatoms and the $\pi \cdot$ electron systems; (3) the mono- and dibridging of the thiophene and BT in the DTBT is a successful strategy in constructing high-performance OPV building blocks with optimal FMOs and sunlight absorptions; (4) the C and Si were found to be the most efficient bridging atoms, and the dibridging and thiophene end-capping were advisible in designing effective building blocks for OPV molecules. These theoretical insights may offer important guidance in experimental exploration of such kind of materials for highperformance OPV devices.
