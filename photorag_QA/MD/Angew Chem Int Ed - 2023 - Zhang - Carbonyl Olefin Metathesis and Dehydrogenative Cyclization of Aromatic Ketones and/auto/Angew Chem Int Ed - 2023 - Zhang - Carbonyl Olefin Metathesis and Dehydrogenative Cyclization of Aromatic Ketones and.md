# Carbonyl Olefin Metathesis and Dehydrogenative Cyclization of Aromatic Ketones and gem-Difluoroalkenes

Yunxiao Zhang, Jiaxin Wang, Youyuan Guo, Shanshan Liu, and Xiao Shen\*

In memory of Professor Qing-Yun Chen.

Abstract: The beauty of one-pot cascade reaction lies in the efficient disconnection and construction of several bonds in a single reaction flask, without the isolation of any intermediates. Herein, we report the first photoinduced thermally promoted cascade reactions of readily available aromatic ketones and aromatic gem-difluoroalkenes for the synthesis of phenanthrenes which possess potential utility in drug design and materials science. The reaction combines carbonyl-olefin metathesis (cascade photoinduced $[ 2 + 2 ]$ cyclization and thermally controlled retro $[ 2 + 2 ]$ cyclization) and dehydrogenative cyclization (cascade photoinduced conrotatory $6 \pi$ electrocyclization and collidine-promoted dehydrogenative aromatization) together in one pot. The oxidant-free, acid-free and metal-free reaction shows broad substrate scope and wide functional group tolerance.

$\pmb { O } _ { \mathrm { n e } }$ long-term goal in organic synthesis is the facile construction of more than one bond in a single reaction flask starting from simple substrates.1-5 To address this challenge, cascade reaction is an attractive strategy, since no reaction intermediate is needed to be isolated or purified to facilitate the following reaction steps (Scheme 1a).[6-8] In this context, the development of synthesis of phenanthrene derivatives through one-pot cascade reactions of readily available starting materials is of great value, because these compounds containing conjugated $\pi$ systems have increasing applications in drug design and material development.-i4] Among early developed methods  for the  synthesis of phenanthrenes,[1-39] the Scholl-type oxidation reactions with stilbenes as substrates are the most widely used.25-29] But these reactions usually require excessive strong acids and/or strong oxidants, thus resulting in unwanted side reactions and inseparable byproducts, leading to low reaction efficiency (Scheme 1b).25-29] Therefore, Scholl-type oxidation generally prefers electron-neutral arenes, but electron-poor and highly electron-rich/heterocyclic arenes are not suitable substrates. Photochemical Mallory-Katz reaction features milder conditions, but the dilute reaction concentrations (\~ $1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathbf { M } )$ limit its synthetic potential.30-38] The Dong's improvement in dehydrogenative cyclization of $o$ -teraryls required complicated Co complexes as catalysts.39] Moreover, the difficulty in the access to stilbenes limits the applications of both methods. Conceptually, carbonyl olefin metathesis should be a good strategy for the synthesis of stilbenes, but the intermolecular carbonyl olefin metathesis[40 42] between aromatic ketones and styrenes to deliver stilbenes is still an unsolved task (Scheme1c). Hoffmann and co-workers found that diaryl ketones and trifluoroalkanes could undergo photo-induced Paterno-Buchi reaction[43-45] to prepare fluorinated oxetanes,[46-52] and alkenes were sometimes generated as minor sideproducts.46] $\mathrm { ~ I n ~ } 2 0 2 2$ , our group reported an intramolecular [2+2] cycloaddition of aromatic ketones and gem-difluoroalkenes without observation of carbonyl olefin metathesis products.52] We envisioned that if we could combine the [2 $+ 2 ]$ cycloaddition, retro [2+2] cycloaddition, 6 electrocyclization and aromatization steps of the reactions between ketones 1 and gem-difluoroalkenes 2 in one-pot, practical synthesis of phenanthrene derivatives 3 could be achieved (Scheme 1d). Herein, we report our success in tackling this task under oxidant-free, acid-free and metal-free conditions with normal reaction concentrations through a one-pot cascade reaction.

With the idea in mind, we investigated the reaction conditions with ketone 1a and gem-difluoroalkene 2a as the model substrates. After careful screening of the reaction parameters including additives, solvents, light sources, reaction time, the ratio of the reactants and concentrations (for details, see the Supporting Information), we found that when collidine (3.5equiv.) was used as an additive, the reaction of 1a (2 equiv.) and 2a in MeCN $( 0 . 1 { \bf M } )$ at room temperature under $3 6 5 \mathrm { n m }$ LEDs $( 6 \mathrm { ~ W } )$ for $3 6 \mathrm { h }$ generated product 3a in $8 0 \%$ yield $8 1 \%$ isolated yield, Table 1, entry 1). Intermolecular $[ 2 + 2 ]$ cycloaddition product gemdifluorooxetane 4a and triarylalkene 5a were completely converted to phenanthrene 3a (Table 1, entry 1). Lutidine, 4-OH-lutidine, 4-Cl-lutidine and pyridine were less efficient additives (Table 1, entries 2-5), and $2 \%$ or $8 \%$ of triaryl alkene 5a was observed in the reactions with 4-Cl-lutidine or pyridine as the additive. With the optimized conditions in hand, we studied the substrate scope of gem-difluoroalkenes (Figure 1). Various gem-difluoroalkenes containing electron-neutral, -donating, or -withdrawing groups on the aromatic rings performed well in the reaction with ketone 1a, affording the phenanthrenes 3a-3t in $4 0 \% { - } 8 5 \%$ yield. The reaction could tolerate $\mathrm { C } ( \mathrm { s p } ^ { 2 } ) { \cdot } \mathrm { F }$ $\mathrm { C } ( \mathrm { s p } ^ { 2 } )$ -Cl, $\mathrm { C } ( \mathrm { s p } ^ { 2 } )$ -Br, $\mathrm { C } ( \mathrm { s p } ^ { 2 } )$ -CN, $\mathrm { C } ( \mathrm { s p } ^ { 2 } )$ COOMe and $\mathrm { C } ( \mathrm { s p } ^ { 2 } )$ -OMe bonds. It is worth noting that traditional Scholl-type oxidation reactions usually showed low efficiency for electron-rich substrates,[26] but various electron-rich aromatic gem-difluoroalkenes are good substrates in our reaction ( $\mathbf { 3 e { - } } 3 \mathbf { m }$ $5 1 \% { - } 8 5 \%$ yield). In general, increasing the loading of ketone and collidine could increase the yield for the electron-rich substrates (e.g. 3i, from $3 0 \%$ to $7 7 \%$ ). The structure of compound 3i was determined by $\mathbf { X }$ ray crystal structure analysis.3] Notably, alkynyl group could be tolerated (31, $7 6 \%$ ), which can be employed in further downstream transformations, thanks to the rich synthetic chemistry of alkynes. Electron-rich alkenes are generally good substrates in traditional Paterno-Buchi reaction to synthesize oxetanes,4344] but gem-difluoroalkene moiety was found to be more reactive than the normal alkene part $( 3 \mathbf { m } , 5 1 \% )$ , and no formation of ${ \bf 4 m ^ { \prime } }$ and $\pmb { 5 } \mathbf { m } ^ { \prime }$ were observed, indicating the unique reactivity of gemdifluoroalkene moiety.

Subsequently, we studied the influence of the positions of substituents on the site selectivity of the reaction. When the substituent is in the meta-position, only the isomer resulting from less sterically hindered $\mathrm { C - H }$ bond was obtained (3n-3r, $4 0 \% { - } 8 3 \%$ yield, $\operatorname { r r } > 9 8 / 2$ ). The ortho-Br substituted aromatic gem-difluoroalkenes also worked well, affording compound 3s in $3 9 \%$ yield, with $1 4 \%$ yield of 4s remained. When the reaction mixture was heated under $1 1 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ with silica to promote the generation of triaryl alkene 5s, the yield of 3s increased to $6 0 \%$ .Furthermore, a complex molecule 3t was prepared in $4 7 \%$ yield, and the intramolecular alkyl ketones were all tolerated.

Disubstituted aromatic gem-difluoroalkenes were then studied, and the reactions were also efficient, affording phenanthrene derivatives $\mathbf { 3 } \mathbf { u } \mathbf { - } \mathbf { 3 } \mathbf { y }$ in $5 1 \mathrm { - } 6 7 \%$ yield. Since the fully substituted gem-difluorooxetanes were stable at room temperature, heating at $1 1 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ was needed to promote the decomposition of the $[ 2 + 2 ]$ cyclization intermediates to tetra-substituted alkenes in the presence of silica, thus facilitating the one-pot synthesis of phenanthrene derivatives.

We also studied the scope of diaryl ketones and the results are shown in Figure 2. Various diaryl ketones were appropriate substrates for photo-induced cascade reactions, affording the corresponding phenanthrenes $\mathbf { 3 . z { - } 3 . 2 r }$ in $4 4 \% -$ $7 6 \%$ yield. Functional groups on aromatic rings of the ketones such as alkyl, OMe, OTBS, F, Cl, Br, $\mathrm { C F } _ { 3 }$ were all tolerated. However, when non-substituted benzophenone and halogenated benzophenones were employed as the substrates, the gem-difluorooxetanes were stable at room temperature, thus high temperature was needed to promote the retro $[ 2 + 2 ]$ cyclization step, and the reaction could still be performed in one-pot, without isolation of reaction intermediates, affording the desired products $\mathbf { 3 2 - 3 a c }$ in $5 8 \% { - } 6 7 \%$ yield. para-Alkyl substituted diaryl ketones performed well at room temperature, affording $\mathbf { 3 } \mathbf { a d } \mathbf { - } \mathbf { 3 } \mathbf { a h }$ in $5 8 \% { - } 7 5 \%$ yield. Again, our photothermal cascade reaction provides an efficient access to the electron-rich phenanthrenes 3ai-3al in synthetically useful yield. When there were meta-substituents on the aromatic rings of diaryl ketones, regioselectivity was found, favoring the formation of product from the less sterically hindered reaction site $( 3 \mathbf { a m } \mathbf { - } 1 + 3 \mathbf { a m } \mathbf { - } 2$ $6 4 \%$ yield, $3 \mathbf { a m } { - } 1 / 3 \mathbf { a m } { - } 2 = 6 0 / 4 0$ . When there were two electronically different substituents of each aromatic ring of the diaryl ketones, the products derived from the more electron rich ring were favored (3an, $7 6 \%$ yield with 75/25 regioselectivity; 3ao, $7 2 \%$ yield with 63/37 regioselectivity; 3ap, $7 5 \%$ yield with 81/19 regioselectivity). Unfortunately, when one of the aryl groups is extremely steric hindered 2,4,6-trimethylphenyl group, the in situ generated gem-difluorooxetane 4aq was relatively stable under room temperature, although there was $8 \%$ yield of 3aq formed. Again, when $1 1 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ was used in combination of addition of silica, the efficiency of the one-pot reaction was significantly improved, resulting in the formation of phenanthrene 3aq in $7 2 \%$ yield, which further indicates that the reaction was photothermally controlled.

Recently, various polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons have been investigated in medicinal chemistry and many bioactive molecules contain phenanthrenyl group.19,10,54-58] For example, aryl substituted phenanthrene 9-1 was found to show anti-inflammatory property.58] Therefore, we applied our reaction in the synthesis of compound 9-1 to demonstrate the synthetic potential (Figure3a). Compound 8 was prepared from compounds 6 and 7 in $8 6 \%$ yield through copper-catalyzed Huisgen's reaction.59] The reaction of 8 and ${ \bf 2 \cdot z }$ afforded compound 9-1 and 9-2 in $7 2 \%$ yield, with 75/25 regioselectivity. This modular synthesis provides an access to the analogs of compound 9-1 through changing the substituents on gem-difluoroalkenes or aromatic ketones.

In order to shine some light on the possible mechanism of the reaction, we performed preliminary studies (Scheme2). Firstly, we monitored the model reaction through performing a set of experiments which were quenched at different reaction time to determine the conversion of $\mathbf { 2 4 }$ and the yield of 3a, 4a and 5a (Scheme 2a). We found that the formation of 4a was faster than 5a and the formation of 5a was faster than 3a. After about 9 hours, 4a and 5a reached the highest yield and then decreased to nearly $0 \%$ after 36 hours. These data supported that the 4a and 5a were probably the intermediates for the formation of 3a. It is worth noting that, in the reaction for the preparation of compound 3as, the addition of 2 equivalents of TEMPO after the in situ generation of alkene can significantly improve the yield of 3as. The effect of TEMPO was then studied. We found that the yield of 3a decreased when TEMPO was added into the reaction mixture, indicating that the reaction might undergo a radical process, which is consistent with the traditional PaternoBuchi reaction (Scheme 2b). However, 20 equivalents of TEMPO were needed to completely shut down the reaction (Scheme 2b, entry 4), which led us to study the reaction of 5as. Under $3 6 5 \mathrm { n m }$ irradiation in the presence of 3.5 equivalents of collidine in MeCN at room temperature, compound 5as was transformed to phenanthrene 3as in $5 6 \%$ yield (Scheme 2c, entry 1), which supported that triaryl alkenes 5 were key intermediates for the formation of phenanthrenes 3. Control experiments showed that both light and collidine were important for conversion of 5as to 3as (Scheme 2c, entries 2 and 3). Besides, inorganic bases such as $\mathbf { N a } _ { 2 } \mathbf { C O } _ { 3 }$ and MeONa were much less efficient additives than collidine (Scheme 2c, entries 4 and 5). However, TEMPO was a much better additive than collidine (Scheme 2c, entry 6). Further study found that gem-difluorooxetane ${ \pmb 4 } \mathbf { z }$ was completely transformed to triphenyl olefin ${ \pmb 5 } { \pmb z }$ in the absence of collidine and light, and carbonyl fluoride gas was detected in MeCN in $4 2 \%$ yield (Scheme 2d). In contrast, gem-difluorooxetane ${ \bf 4 \cdot }$ was stable in the presence of light and collidine at room temperature, indicating that retro $[ 2 + 2 ]$ process was a thermal decomposition process. Moreover, we isolated $1 7 \%$ yield of hydrogenation product $\pmb { 5 } \mathbf { a s } ^ { \prime }$ ,together with $5 6 \%$ yield of phenanthrene 3as (Scheme 2e). These data support that dehydrogenative cyclization step probably proceeds through a single-electron process. Based on the above experimental results and related literature, a plausible mechanism was proposed in Figure 4. Light-induced intermolecular PaternoBuchi type reaction leads to the formation of gem-difluorooxetane 4a from ketone 1a and gem-difluoroalkene 2a. Compound ${ \bf 4 a }$ is unstable under the reaction conditions and undergoes retro $[ 2 + 2 ]$ process to generate triaryl olefin 5a and releases carbonyl fluoride gas. The substituents on compound 5 significantly influence their stability and sometimes heating at higher temperature or the addition of silica are needed to promote the retro $[ 2 + 2 ]$ cyclization processes. Triaryl olefin 5a would then undergo conrotatory 6n electrocyclization to form reactive dihydrophenanthrene intermediate $\mathbf { I } , ^ { [ 6 0 - 6 2 ] }$ which might undergo hydrogen atom abstraction by excited collidine to form radical-like intermediates II, II, III and $\mathbf { I I } ^ { \prime }$ , and phenanthrene 3a and dihydrocollidne were generated after another hydrogen atom abstraction. This proposal was also supported by the fact that collidine had absorption at $3 6 5 \mathrm { n m }$ and we detected dihydrocollidine by HRMS (for details, see Supporting Information). We can not rule out the possibility of dehydrogenative cyclization through oxidation of I by excited collidinium salts which might be generated through the reaction of collidine, carbonyl fluoride and traces of water. But we think this possibility might not be the major pathway, because compound 5as was transformed to compound 3as in $5 6 \%$ yield in the present of collidine (Scheme 2c, entry1); there was no carbonyl fluoride generated in this reaction, thus there was much less possibility to generate collidinium salts.

In conclusion, we have developed the first photothermal cascade reaction of readily available diaryl ketones and gemdifluoroalkenes to synthesize phenanthrenes. The photoinduced reaction avoids using stoichiometric strong acids or oxidants, which allows the reaction to tolerate not only electron-neutral and electron-deficient substrates but also electron-rich substrates. The mechanistic study supports a photothermal cascade process that combines photoinduced $[ 2 + 2 ]$ cyclization, thermally controlled retro $[ 2 + 2 ]$ cyclization, photoinduced conrotatory $6 \pi$ electrocyclization and photo-promoted dehydrogenative aromatization together in one pot, without isolation of any reaction intermediates. We believe this rare photothermal cascade reaction would pave the way for the design of novel transformations through the combination of photochemical transformations and thermal reactions.
