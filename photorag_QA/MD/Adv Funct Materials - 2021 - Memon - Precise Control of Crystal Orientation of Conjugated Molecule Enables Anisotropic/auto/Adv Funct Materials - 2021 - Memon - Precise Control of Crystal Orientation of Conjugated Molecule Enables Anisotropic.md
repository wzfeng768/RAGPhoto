# Precise Control of Crystal Orientation of Conjugated Molecule Enables Anisotropic Charge Transport Properties

Waqar Ali Memon, Ruimin Zhou, Yajie Zhang,\* Yuheng Wang, Lixuan Liu, Chen Yang, Jianqi Zhang, Adeel Liaqat, Liming Xie, and Zhixiang Wei\*

The crystal orientation of small molecules is one of the key parameters for developing high-performance small molecule based organic field-effect transistors. However, achieving a high degree of backbone alignment still remains a challenge, due to the sensitive nature of small molecules to processing conditions. Herein, the thin films of three conjugated small molecules, named as ZR1, ZR2-C3, and Y6 are prepared by epitaxial-crystallization on a highly oriented polyethylene (PE) substrate, resulting in the significant enhancement in charge transport properties. The maximum mobilities for aligned edge-on molecules in the films enhance by up to $9 . 9 7 \times 7 0 ^ { - 2 }$ and $8 . 9 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 2 } \mathsf { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathsf { V } ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 }$ for ZR1 and ZR2-C3 films with conducting channel along small molecule backbones, which are 35- and 37-fold that of the values evaluated in perpendicular to molecular backbones and nearly 23-and 19-fold beyond the values measured from unaligned films. Furthermore, the electron and hole mobilities of the small molecule Y6 with face-on nature increase by nearly fourfold the values measured in perpendicular to the backbone direction and from unaligned films. This work demonstrates the epitaxial crystallization of thin films on oriented PE, offers a critical understanding of small molecules with different orientations and an effective approach for tuning charge transport toward high-performance organic electronic applications.

# 1. Introduction

In recent years, organic semiconductor thin films have been comprehensively studied because of their prospective for diverse electronic applications including organic solar cells (OSCs),[1] organic field-effect transistors (OFETs),[2] and organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs).[3] The specific arrangement of molecular orientation can play an essential role in improving the performance of these organic electronic devices. For example, it is well established that the horizontal orientation of light-emitting dipoles in OLEDs is important to enhance their outcoupling efficiency.4 As for OFETs, the crystalline grains and orientation are the crucial parameters to obtain high charge transport and hence to fabricate high-performance OFET devices.[5] The different molecular orientations of donor and acceptor molecules significantly influence the charge generation, resulting in high-performance OsCs.[6] Furthermore, balancing charge transport characteristics of organic semiconductor thin films by controlling molecular packing precisely without altering the chemical structure of the molecules is interesting for key research dedicated to the structureproperty correlation of organic semiconductors and realizing its practical uses including optical recording and organic complementary logic circuits.7

Up to now, numerous methods have already been introduced to control the orientation and enhance the molecular packing in organic semiconductor thin films. For instance, uniaxially aligned organic thin films have been produced through nanogrooved surfaces that are utilized to enhance the coherence of molecular arrangement within the films by capillary effects on molecule chains.8l The magnetic-field guided solvent vapor annealing technique is suitable for producing the alignment of molecules in semiconductor films. However, this method exhibits the lack of orientation control for large-area aligned films in a simple and scalable way.l9] The printing flow methods have also been employed to fabricate oriented films in a broad range of organic semiconductors. But it is difficult to control the twisting conformation of organic semiconductor films during preparation, in a resultant dramatic decrease in charge carrier mobility and charge transport anisotropy[5d,10] Regarding the high-temperature mechanical rubbing method, several studies have showed the alignment of molecular chains arranged parallel to the rubbing direction and the crystal orientation changes from preferential edge-on to face-on orientations,[11] this method however produces well-aligned films with a rough surface, which is unfavorable for charge transport in high-performance OFETs. A very recent study by Imamishi et al. has used a soft friction transfer method to produce aligned semiconductor thin films. Although the orientation obtained by using this method is somewhat improved compared to the conventional transfer method, but it is inadequate as expected.12] To prepare solution-processed organic electronic devices, simultaneously maintaining device performance and consistency, demands the emerging solution-process based fabrication method that can easily prepare large-area and highly aligned organic films with uniform thickness. Though, there are methods that allow to produce highly aligned organic films just in a single step including controlled dip-coating,[13] zone-casting,[14] and bar-coating.[15] The realization of efficient, highly alignment, and ordered molecule packing still remain a challenge.

Herein, we introduce a solution-processed method that efficiently produces uniaxially aligned films with a small roughness by epitaxial crystallization of conjugated small molecule films on a highly oriented polyethylene (PE) substrate. Two similar small molecules ZR1, ZR2-C3, which varied in the alkyl side chains as shown in Figure 1a, exhibit edge-on orientations and excellent crystallinity are systematically investigated. A fused-ring electron accepter Y6 exhibiting face-on orientation is selected for comparative study. The results show that the conjugated small molecules with edge-on orientation can epitaxially grow on the oriented PE substrate and the well-aligned edgeon molecules in the films display a significant improvement in charge carrier mobilities by 35- and 37-fold, reaching values as maximum as $9 . 9 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 2 }$ and $8 . 9 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 2 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for ZR1 and ZR2-C3 films along the direction of the small molecule backbone. On the other hand, Y6 small molecule with face-on orientation shows the random alignment of molecules in the films exhibiting poor charge transport anisotropies of 3.83- and

1.09-fold, for electron and hole mobilities. In general, this study offers a clear demonstration that compared to the small molecule with face-on orientation, the oriented PE substrate can efficiently align the molecules with edge-on orientation and enhance their charge transport properties. This technique also offers a potential opportunity in the large-area alignment of small molecules with edge-on orientations and advances its electronic applications.

# 2.1. Preparation and Characterization of Aligned Small Molecule Films

The polymeric substrate highly oriented PE is prepared according to the melt-drawn technique with the thickness of resultant films ranging in $3 0 { - } 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . The sketch illustration is shown in Figure S1, Supporting Information. TEM bright-field electron micrograph and corresponding electron diffraction patterns of oriented PE substrate are shown in Figure S2, Supporting Information. The sharp and well-defined spots that appeared in electron diffraction denote the high orientation of prepared PE substrate with the molecular chain alignment along the drawing direction during sample preparation. As spin-coating is a generally employed technique to fabricate organic thin films, we have investigated the epitaxial efficiency of small molecules on oriented PE substrate during the spin-coating process in the present work. The epitaxial crystallization of small molecules ZR1, ZR2-C3, and Y6 on oriented PE substrates is achieved simply by spin-coating small molecule solutions prepared in chlorobenzene solvent. To disclose the different crystallization behaviors of small molecules having different orientations, the polarized optical micrographs of ZR1, ZR2-C3, and Y6 small molecule films on the oriented PE substrates are recorded using polarized optical microscopy (POM) as shown in Figure 1b-d. It is clearly seen that the uniform and large-area small molecule films are obtained. The occurrence of light extinction when the samples are rotated $\pm 4 5 ^ { \circ }$ about the light beam axis demonstrates the exceptional anisotropic optical properties of ZR1 and ZR2-C3. On the contrary, due to the face-on crystal orientation, the resultant Y6 small molecule aligned film exhibits isotropic features with unchanged birefringent during the rotation of the sample about the light axis, demonstrating the random orientation of molecules on oriented PE substrate.

# 2.2. Anisotropic Orientation Structures by Grazing-Incidence Wide Angle X-Ray Scattering and Selected-Area-Electron-Diffraction

To further understand the degrees of alignment in the crystalline domains of small molecule ZR1, ZR2-C3, and Y6 films, 2D grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) has been performed. Figure 3 shows 2D-GIWAXS maps of ZR1, ZR2-C3, and Y6 films with incidence X-ray beam parallel (ll) and perpendicular () to the drawing direction of highly oriented PE. The 1D correlation profiles along $q _ { \mathrm { x y } }$ and $q _ { z }$ are presented in Figure S5, Supporting Information, as integrated from 2D-GIWAXS maps. The crystal structure of small molecule ZR1 is shown in Figure S6, Supporting Information. The diffraction patterns of ZR1 or ZR2-C3 molecules exhibit preferable edge-on molecular orientation with the multiple peaks in the out-of-plane (OOp) direction and an evident of $\pi - \pi$ stacking in the in-plane (IP) direction on silicon substrates. On the other hand, the arc-like diffraction peak $( d = 2 2 . 2 8 3 \textrm { \AA }$ - in the IP and $\pi - \pi$ stacking in OOP for Y6 molecules on silicon substrate indicating the preferential face-on orientation of Y6 molecules as shown in Figure S6, Supporting Information.17 For the aligned ZR1 and ZR2-C3 films, two well-defined and sharp peaks at 1.544 and $1 . 6 8 8 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ are associated with (110) and (200) peaks of oriented PE observed in OOP under the incident X-ray beam perpendicular to the oriented PE (as shown in the right panel of Figure 3a). These two diffraction peaks in OOP disappear under the incident X-ray beam parallel to the oriented PE (as shown in the left panel of Figure 3a) due to the high uniaxial $c$ -axis orientation along the drawing direction.18] when the incident X-ray beam is parallel to the oriented PE, the obtained diffraction patterns are different from those with the incident X-ray beam perpendicular to the oriented PE. The diffraction peak of the first ring $( d = 2 0 . 8 0 7 \textrm { \AA }$ observed in the IP under incident X-ray beam parallel to oriented PE (as shown in the left panel of Figure 3a), disappeared under perpendicular incident X-ray measurement. It suggests that the $c$ axis of face-on (with $\pi - \pi$ stacking direction perpendicular to the substrate) orientation of ZR1 is parallel to the drawing direction $( c$ -axis) of PE, in resultant, the epitaxial crystallization of small molecules on the oriented substrate may be formed during spin-coating process. Simultaneously, enhanced the third order of diffraction peaks in the OOP direction of aligned small molecule films, implies the increase in the crystallinity compared to the films spin-coated on the silicon substrate.19 The epitaxial mechanism can be crystal lattice matching, considering that the $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance $( { \approx } 3 . 6 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ } )$ is nearly similar to the lattice distance of (200) diffraction peak of oriented PE substrate $( \approx 3 . 7 { \mathrm { ~ \AA } } )$ . Therefore, it may be speculated that within this range of difference $( { \approx } 0 . 1 \mathring \mathrm { A } )$ for small molecules and PE, the anisotropic structures of small molecules with edge-on orientation might be induced by the epitaxial effect of oriented PE substrate. Moreover, It can be seen that the $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak of aligned films is stronger under parallel X-ray measurement than the perpendicular X-ray measurement for ZR2-C3 aligned film and completely disappeared for ZR1 aligned film. This shows that the alignment direction of molecule backbones is parallel to the drawing direction of oriented PE. Alignment, as evaluated by GIWAXS, is better for ZR1 molecule than ZR2-C3. This is consistent with polarized UV-vis absorption and PL spectrums. The determined $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances under incident X-ray beam parallel to the direction of the small molecule backbone are found as 3.593 and $3 . 5 9 8 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ for aligned ZR1 and ZR2-C3 films, which are smaller than the films prepared on silicon substrates found as 3.674 and $3 . 6 2 8 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ , showing that the oriented PE substrate significantly impacts the molecular packing within the unit cell. Moreover, the crystallite coherence length (CCL) of the crystalline aggregates is estimated from full-width at half-maximum of the $\pi - \pi$ stacking diffraction peaks using the Debye-Scherrer equation.117a,20] The analysis reveals that the CCL for small molecule ZR1 and ZR2-C3 films on oriented PE and silicon substrates are found as 75.53, $7 3 . 8 4 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ and 42.12, 52.81 A, respectively. This indicates that the oriented substrate can simultaneously reduce the twisting of small molecule backbones, $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance and increases the CCL, which is advantageous to enhance the charge carrier mobility of small molecule-based transistors.

# 3. Organic Field-Effect Transistor Device Performance and Charge Transport Anisotropy

In order to investigate the electrical characteristics of epitaxially aligned all small molecule films, the charge transport properties are measured with bottom-gate-top-contact (BGTC) OFET device configuration as shown in Figure 4a. The small molecule solutions are spin-coated on oriented and unoriented PE covered OTS treated $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ substrates. The aligned and unaligned small molecule films are thermally annealed at $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 5 ~ \mathrm { m i n }$ . The typical transfer curves of the corresponding ZR1, ZR2-C3, and Y6 aligned and unaligned thin films are shown in Figure 4e-l. The OFET devices are fabricated to measure anisotropic charge transport with conducting channels parallel (ll) and perpendicular () to the direction of small molecule backbones, respectively. The maximum and average charge carrier mobilities of small molecule-based aligned and unaligned thin film transistors and their corresponding essential characters, including on/off ratio $( I _ { \mathrm { O N } } / I _ { \mathrm { O F F } } )$ , threshold voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { T H } } )$ , and charge transport anisotropy are summarized in Table 1. The average charge carrier mobilities are obtained from 15 independent OFET devices. All the devices display nice transfer curves without any detectable double-slop phenomenon, indicating that the curves are reasonable for charge carrier mobility extraction.19] The transfer curves in Figure 4e shows that the maximum charge carrier mobilities $( \mu )$ at the saturated regime for aligned ZR1 film along parallel $( \mu _ { \parallel } )$ and perpendicular $( \mu _ { \perp } )$ to the molecular backbones increase up to $9 . 9 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 2 }$ and $2 . 8 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ that is, the mobility $( \mu _ { \parallel } )$ enhanced nearly 35-fold to the $( \mu _ { \perp } )$ . The maximum charge carrier mobility of unaligned ZR1 film is $4 . 2 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { S } ^ { - 1 }$ (as shown in Figure 4i), which is 23 times lower than that of $( \mu _ { \parallel } )$ aligned ZR1 film. Figure $4 \mathrm { f }$ shows the calculated charge carrier mobilities for aligned ZR2-C3 film along parallel $( \mu _ { \parallel } )$ and perpendicular $( \mu _ { \perp } )$ to the molecular backbones increase up to $8 . 9 7 \times$ $1 0 ^ { - 2 }$ and $2 . 4 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ which indicate the charge carrier mobility is typically 37 times higher along the small molecule backbones direction than in the direction of $\pi - \pi$ stacking. The maximum charge carrier mobility of unaligned ZR2-C3 film is $4 . 8 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ (as shown in Figure 4j), which is 18-19 times lower than that of $( \mu _ { \parallel } )$ aligned ZR2-C3 film. The charge transport within crystalline domains is confirmed by the combination of interchain and intrachain processes, while the transport between crystalline domains depends on a combination of domain boundary hopping and the tie-chains which link crystalline domains.[23] The tie-chains between crystalline domains predominate the charge transport in the amorphous zones as outlined in recent studies.20,23,24 According to the results discussed above, we observed that the considerable improvement in charge carrier mobilities of the small molecule aligned ZR1 and ZR2-C3 films is not only due to the $\pi - \pi$ stacking, but rather it is probably due to the alignment of ZR1 and ZR2-C3 molecules along drawing direction of PE and the presence of tie-chains which offer additional conductive routes connecting neighboring ordered crystalline domains. This definitely illustrates that the small molecule films having a high degree of alignment, interconnecting tie-chains, and high crystallinity, which boost charge carriers to travel more effectively along the molecular backbone in 1D with less possibility of hopping and traps at grain boundaries. The slight difference in charge carrier mobilities of ZR1 and ZR2-C3 is mainly related to their molecular structures and their distinct characteristics, reflected by polarized UV-vis absorption, PL, and molecular crystallinity.[25]

It is worth noticing that the electron and hole mobilities along parallel direction are higher than that of perpendicular direction and those of reported in the literature,[26] this small improvement in ambipolar charge transport properties can be assigned to its compacted $\pi - \pi$ stacking mode shaped by multiple intermolecular interactions. In addition, the charge transport anisotropic ratios $( \mu _ { \parallel } / \mu _ { \perp } )$ are consistent with the trend of characterization results of the polarized optical micrographs, polarized UV-vis spectrums, GIWAXS, and electron diffraction patterns obtained by TEM, indicating the overall poor anisotropic electrical properties of aligned Y6 small molecule films.

To further interpret the charge transport mechanism, the temperature-dependent OFET characteristics of aligned and unaligned ZR1, ZR2-C3, and Y6 films are measured over a range from room temperature to nearly the melting temperature of oriented PE substrate. In order to reduce the influence of the contact resistance effects, we measured the charge carrier mobility in the saturation regime. The corresponding transfer curves of all small molecules with aligned and unaligned films are shown in Figures S9-S12, Supporting Information. The extracted maximum/average charge carrier mobilities along with their important characters are summarized in Tables S2-S5, Supporting Information, respectively. It is worth noticing that for the all thermal annealing range of measurements, the magnitude of charge carrier mobilities of small molecule ZR1 and ZR2-C3 aligned films along the backbone alignment direction is greater than that of evaluated for the devices with channels perpendicular to the backbone alignment direction, justifying the effectiveness of the oriented substrate to enhance the charge carrier mobility along the backbone alignment direction. The maximum charge carrier mobilities for ZR1 and ZR2-C3 aligned films are achieved at the thermal annealing temperature of $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ which is nearer to the melting temperature of oriented PE substrate as shown in Figure 5a,b. However, a possible explanation may be that at this annealing temperature, the oriented substrate extensively aligns the small molecule backbones in the drawing direction of PE and the mobility for transport along molecule backbone direction gets a significant boost from charges being able to access delocalized electronic states along the small molecule backbone. On the contrary, the electron $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ and hole $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } )$ mobilities of aligned Y6 films confirm the weak charge transport anisotropy at low temperatures, while further increase in annealing temperature the mobilities along both parallel and perpendicular to the small molecule backbone direction are nearly identical as shown in Figure 5c,d. This observation can be attributed to the randomly face-on orientation distribution of Y6 molecules on oriented PE substrate. The above results indicate that the temperature-dependent charge transport performance of small molecules exhibiting edge-on orientation confirm the charge transport anisotropy even at low temperatures. With a increase in annealing temperature, the largescale interconnected alignment of small molecule backbones along the oriented PE substrate is achieved, resulting in the higher charge carrier mobilities and charge transport anisotropies. However, the mobility anisotropy of small molecule with face-on orientation is negligible at all annealing temperatures as shown in Figure 4e, demonstrating the random alignment of Y6 small molecule backbones on oriented PE substrate.

# 4. Conclusion

In this work, we have successfully realized the fabrication of uniform and highly aligned films based on conjugated small molecules with different orientations through epitaxial crystallization on highly oriented PE substrate. The edge-on molecules in ZR1 and ZR2-C3 films are well-ordered along the drawing direction of oriented PE substrate. Such a high degree of alignment in ZR1 and ZR2-C3 films ensure the anisotropic optical (POM) and structural (GIWAXS) properties with the UV-vis absorption DRs reaching as maximum as 5.06 and 4.17. These high-quality small molecule aligned films lead to an increase in the charge carrier mobilities with the conducting channel along aligned molecular backbones by up to 0.0991 and $0 . 0 8 9 7 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ which are 35- and 37-fold to the values evaluated in perpendicular to the molecular backbones and nearly 23-fold, 19-fold to values measured from the unaligned films. In addition, the face-on orientation of Y6 small molecule is inappropriate for the alignment of molecules on the oriented PE substrate, as a resultant, electron $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ and hole $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } )$ mobilities of Y6 film along parallel to drawing direction of PE substrate increase up to 0.0425 and $0 . 0 3 0 2 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ $\mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } { \mathsf { S } } ^ { - 1 } .$ which are one to four fold to mobilities measured in perpendicular to drawing direction of PE. Low anisotropic charge transport demonstrates the disordered orientation of Y6 small molecule film on oriented PE substrate. These results demonstrate the great potential of the oriented PE substrate as alignment-inducing substrates which can be combined in organic electronic devices, for example, OFET constructions. It also offers a prospective approach to produce large-area, uniform, and highly aligned small molecule films for application of sophisticated organic electronics.
