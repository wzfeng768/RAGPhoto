# An Unfused-Core-Based Nonfullerene Acceptor Enables High-Efficiency Organic Solar Cells with Excellent Morphological Stability at High Temperatures

Shuixing Li, Lingling Zhan, Feng Liu,\* Jie Ren, Minmin Shi,\* Chang-Zhi Li, Thomas P. Russell, and Hongzheng Chen\*

Most nonfullerene acceptors developed so far for high-performance organic solar cells (OsCs) are designed in planar molecular geometry containing a fused-ring core. In this work, a new nonfullerene acceptor of DF-PCIC is synthesized with an unfused-ring core containing two cyclopentadithiophene (CPDT) moieties and one 2,5-difluorobenzene (DFB) group. A nearly planar geometry is realized through the F..H noncovalent interaction between CPDT and DFB for DF-PCIC. After proper optimizations, the OSCs with DF-PCIC as the acceptor and the polymer PBDB-T as the donor yield the best power conversion efficiency (PCE) of $1 0 . 1 4 \%$ with a high fill factor of 0.72. To the best of our knowledge, this efficiency is among the highest values for the OsCs with nonfullerene acceptors owning unfused-ring cores. Furthermore, no obvious morphological changes are observed for the thermally treated PBDB-T:DFPCIC blended films, and the relevant devices can keep ${ \approx } 7 0 \%$ of the original PCEs upon thermal treatment at $1 8 0 ^ { \circ } \mathsf { C }$ for $\lnot \textrm { \ r { h } }$ . This tolerance of such a high temperature for so long time is rarely reported for fullerene-free OSCs, which might be due to the unique unfused-ring core of DF-PCIC. Therefore, the work provides new idea for the design of new nonfullerene acceptors applicable in commercial OsCs in the future.

Fullerene-free organic solar cells (OsCs) have gained great progresses in recent years with the enhanced efficiencies of over $1 3 \%$ [1-3] Compared to traditional fullerene-based acceptors,[4,5] or indacenodithieno[3,2-b]thiophene (IDTT) core, e.g., 3,9-bis(2- methylene-(3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)-indanone))-5,5,11,11- tetrakis(4-hexylphenyl)-dithieno[2,3-d:2',3'-d']-s-indaceno[1,2- b:5,6-b']dithiophene (ITIC, Figure 1a).[29-32] The fully fused structure of IDT and IDTT is beneficial for efficient intramolecular charge transfer and face-on molecular arrangement, which broaden the absorption range and improve charge transport in OSCs, respectively. In the meanwhile, the design of efficient nonfullerene acceptors with new structures should be meaningful for further advancing OsCs. We think that the introduction of unfused-ring core would lead to diversified nonfullerene acceptors constructed by smaller building blocks. In addition, the device stability is another issue that is to be solved for the commercialization of OsCs.33,34] Relative to fullerene derivatives, the reduced crystallinity or amorphous nature of many nonfullerene acceptors enables the improved morphological stability for the relevant OSCs, thus potentially extending device lifetime.113,35,36]

Based on the abovementioned considerations, we here design and synthesize a new nonfullerene acceptor with a planar geometry,2,2'-((2Z,2'Z)-(2,5-difluoro-1,4-phenylene)bis(4,4-bis(2- ethylhexyl)-4H-cyclopenta[2,1-b:3,4-b']dithiophene-6,2-diyl)) bis(methanylylidene))bis(3-oxo-2,3-dihydro-1H-indene-2,1- diylidene))dimalononitrile (DF-PCIC, Figure 1b), which owns the same A terminal groups (2-(3-oxo-2,3-dihydroinden-1-ylidene) malononitrile, IC) as ITIC, but a new D core constructed by connecting two cyclopentadithiophene (CPDT) moieties[37-42] with one 2,5-difluorobenzene unit through simple and routine reactions (Figure 1c). The fluorine atoms on 2,5-difluorobenzene facilitates the F...H noncovalent interaction with the hydrogen atoms on adjacent CPDT units, therefore locking the molecular conformation with a planar geometry(43-45] (Figure 1b) as indicated by $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR and $^ { 1 9 } \mathrm { F }$ NMR spectra. And the fluorine atoms on 2,5-difluorobenzene help deepen the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of DF-PCIC, leading to a deep value of -5.49 eV. The bulky branched alkyl chains, ethylhexyl groups on CPDT units, which are used to ensure the solubility in the solution and hold back the formation of large aggregates in the film, can be attached easily. The obtained DF-PCIC possesses similar absorption bands and energy levels as ITIC. Then, DF-PCIC is used as an electron acceptor to pair with a medium bandgap polymer, poly[(2,6-(4,8-bis(5-(2-ethylhexyl)thiophen-2-yl)-benzo[1,2-b:4,5- b']dithiophene))-alt-(5,5-(1',3'-di-2-thienyl- $\cdot 5 ' , 7 '$ -bis(2-ethylhexyl) benzo[1,2'-c:4',5'-c']dithiophene-4,8-dione)](PBDB-T),[46,47] and a high power conversion efficiency (PCE) of $1 0 . 1 4 \%$ is achieved with an excellent fill factor (FF) of 0.72 for the relevant OSCs. It turns out that the PBDB-T:DF-PCIC blended films show excellent morphological stability, and the related devices can also bear high temperature treatment for $^ { 1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ with $8 5 . 5 \%$ $8 6 . 2 \%$ and $6 9 . 8 \%$ initial efficiencies remained at 130, 150 and $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ respectively, which outclass those based on the fullerene acceptor, [6,6]-phenyl ${ \cdot } { \mathrm { C } } _ { 7 1 }$ -butyric acid methyl ester $( \mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M } )$

The synthetic route of DF-PCIC is described in Figure 1c and the detailed information can be found in the Supporting Information. At first, ethylhexyl groups are attached to CPDT unit easily at room temperature in a high yield of $7 7 \%$ . After introducing trimethyl tin group on one side of CPDT unit, Stille coupling reaction is used to synthesize intermediate 4, followed by lithiation with $n$ -butyllithium and quenching with dimethylformamide to get intermediate 5. Finally, Knoevenagel condensation between intermediate 5 and IC is conducted, yielding the target molecule, DF-PCIC. Chemical structure of DF-PCIC is fully characterized by $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR, $^ { 1 3 } \mathrm { C }$ NMR, $^ { 1 9 } \mathrm { F }$ NMR, and matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time of filght (MALDI-TOF) Mass Spectroscopy. Besides, DF-PCIC shows good solubility in common solvents, such as dichloromethane, chloroform, and chlorobenzene.

Optical properties of DF-PCIC are investigated by UV-vis spectroscopy and relevant results are displayed in Figure 2a. A sharp peak appears at $6 7 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ for DF-PCIC solution. DF-PCIC film shows broader absorptions, and two peaks are observed at 645 and $6 9 6 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. These changes indicate the intermolecular interaction or $\pi - \pi$ stacking of DF-PCIC is enhanced in the condensed state.(48-50] Thus, from the film absorption onset at around $7 8 1 \mathrm { n m }$ , the optical bandgap is calculated to be $1 . 5 9 \mathrm { e V } .$ The narrow bandgap of DF-PCIC allows us to pick a wide or medium bandgap polymer donor to pair with. Besides, we measure the absorption coefficients of DF-PCIC both in solution and film (Figure S2, Supporting Information) and find that DF-PCIC solution shows a maximum absorption coefficient of $1 . 9 6 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { { M } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { { c m } ^ { - 1 } } }$ at $6 7 0 \mathrm { n m }$ which is comparable to that of ITIC solution $( 2 . 0 7 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } ~ \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { C m } ^ { - 1 }$ at $6 7 9 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . The relative relation between the absorption coefficients of DF-PCIC and ITIC films is similar to those of DF-PCIC and ITIC solutions. Cyclic voltammetry measurements (Figure 2b) are used to check the energy levels of DF-PCIC. We use the equation $E _ { \mathrm { L U M O / H O M O } } = - \mathrm { e } ( E _ { \mathrm { r e d / o x } } + 4 . 3 6 )$ (eV) to calculate the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) and HOMO levels, since the redox potential of $\mathrm { F c / F c ^ { + } }$ is measured as $0 . 4 4 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ (the energy level of $\mathrm { F c / F c ^ { + } }$ as $4 . 8 \ \mathrm { e V }$ below vacuum(51) in our lab. Then, the onset reduction potential $( E _ { \mathrm { r e d } } )$ and onset oxidation potential $( E _ { \mathrm { o x } } )$ of DF-PCIC are found to be $- 0 . 5 9$ and $1 . 1 3 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ respectively. Thus, the LUMO and HOMO are calculated as $- 3 . 7 7$ and $- 5 . 4 9 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively, which are close to those of ITIC $( - 3 . 8 2$ and $- 5 . 5 4 \mathrm { e V } ,$ Figure S3, Supporting Information). The deep HOMO of DFPCIC allows us to choose a polymer donor with the deep HOMO level to pair with.

To investigate the geometry of DF-PCIC, density functional theory calculation is carried out at the B3LYP/6-31G level, and the ethylhexyl alkyl chains are replaced by methyl for simplifying the calculation. The results are displayed in Figure 1b. It shows that DF-PCIC possesses a nearly planar geometry and the dihedral angle between the 2,5-difluorobenzene and CPDT units is found to be around $5 . 1 5 ^ { \circ }$ . The almost planar geometry is formed with the help of F...H noncovalent interaction between the fuorine atoms on 2,5-difluorobenzene and the hydrogen atoms on CPDT units. To experimentally prove the existence of hydrogen bonds, we compare $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR spectra of DF-PCIC at $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and room temperature (Figure S5, Supporting Information) and $^ { 1 9 } \mathrm { F }$ NMR spectra of DF-PCIC at $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and room temperature (Figure S6, Supporting Information). The results show that a large shift of around $0 . 1 6 ~ \mathrm { p p m }$ happens for the CPDT units' hydrogen atoms that close to the fuorine atoms and a comparable shift of around 0.23 ppm happens for the fuorine atoms, indicating that there indeed exist F...H noncovalent interaction between 2,5-difluorobenzene and CPDT units, since F.-.H noncovalent interaction would be broken at high temperature.

According to the absorptions and energy levels of DF-PCIC, we select a medium bandgap polymer with the lower-lying HOMO level, PBDB-T, as the donor to match DF-PCIC, and evaluate its photovoltaic performances while used as an electron acceptor. An inverted device structure of indium tin oxide $( \mathrm { I T O } ) /$ ZnO/poly[9,9-bis( $3 ^ { \prime }$ -(N,N-dimethylamino)propyl)-2,7-fluorene)- alt-2,7-(9,9-ioctylfluorene](PFN)/PBDB-T:DF-PCIC/ $\mathrm { M o O } _ { 3 } / \mathrm { A g }$ is adopted to fabricate the OsCs (Figure 3a,b).[16,52] Relevant $J { - } V$ curves are displayed in Figure 3c and the photovoltaic parameters of the OsCs are summarized in Table 1. We first vary the weight ratios of the donor (D) and acceptor (A) from 1:1 to 1:1.5, and find 1:1.2 of D/A weight ratio is the best composition. Under this condition, a PCE of $6 . 2 1 \%$ is obtained with an open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ of $0 . 9 2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } ,$ a short-circuit current $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ of $1 4 . 2 2 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ and an FF of 0.48. To further improve the device performance, two post-treatment methods, thermal annealing and 1-chloronaphthalene (CN) additive, are performed, respectively. The annealing temperature is changed from 130 to $2 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and the best performance is achieved when an annealing temperature of $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ is used. Under this condition, the PCE is improved to $9 . 1 0 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 8 5 ~ \mathrm { V } ,$ a $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $1 5 . 8 6 ~ \mathrm { m A ~ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and an FF of 0.68. Obviously, the improvement of PCE is mainly attributed to the enhancement of $\scriptstyle J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF. We also observe that thermal annealing would cause the decrease of $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } .$ As for the CN additive processing, we change the amounts of CN additive from $0 . 2 \%$ to $0 . 8 \%$ and find that $0 . 5 \%$ amount of CN additive works best. When $0 . 5 \%$ CN is added, the PCE can be elevated to $8 . 1 0 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 9 5 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } ,$ a $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $1 3 . 3 8 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and an FF of 0.63. Thus CN additive can help improve the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and FF, but the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ is reduced to some extent. So, we combine thermal annealing and CN additive together, expecting that high $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ can remain with the help of CN additive and high $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ can increase with the help of thermal annealing. We fix the amount of CN additive at $0 . 5 \%$ and change the annealing temperature from 120 to $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ If the annealing temperature is $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ the PCE is found to be $9 . 6 0 \%$ The best device performance is reached when $1 3 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ annealing temperature is applied. Thus, we achieve the highest PCE of $1 0 . 1 4 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 9 1 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ a $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $1 5 . 6 6 ~ \mathrm { m A ~ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and an FF of 0.72, under the condition of $0 . 5 \%$ CN additive and $1 3 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ annealing temperature. More information about device perfor mances under different conditions can be found in Figure S7 in the Supporting Information.

Charge transport properties of PBDB-T:DF-PCIC blended films are investigated by the space charge limited current method (Figure S10, Supporting Information). The structures of the hole-only devices and electron-only devices are ITO/poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS)/PBDB-T:DF-PCIC/ $\mathrm { M o O } _ { 3 } / \mathrm { A g }$ and $\mathrm { I T O } / \mathrm { Z n O } /$ PFN/PBDB-T:DF-PCIC/PFN/Al, respectively. We find that thermal annealing at $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ can greatly improve both hole and electron mobilities. The hole mobility is improved from $0 . 1 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ (the as-cast device) to $1 . 1 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ while the electron mobility is improved from $0 . 1 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ (the as-cast device) to $1 . 0 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ However, CN additive has completely different effects on the hole and electron mobility. For the hole mobility, CN additive can enhance the hole mobility to $0 . 8 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } .$ But for the electron mobility, CN additive reduces the electron mobility to $0 . 0 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ This agrees well with the observation that thermal annealing can improve the current, and CN additive will reduce the current. When both thermal annealing and CN additive are applied, the hole mobilities are measured as $2 . 0 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for the $\mathrm { C N } + 1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ device and $1 . 2 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for the $\mathrm { C N } + 1 3 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ device, the electron mobilities are measured as $1 . 0 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for the $\mathrm { C N } + 1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ device and $0 . 2 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for the $\mathrm { C N } + 1 3 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ device. The changes of hole and electron mobilities are in accord with the variation tendency of the currents observed in $J { - } V$ curves.

The self-assembly of pure materials and bulk heterojunction (BHJ) blends treated under different processing conditions are studied by grazing incidence X-ray diffraction (GIXD). Presented in Figure 4 are the diffraction patterns and line-cut profiles. PBDB-T polymer shows a (100) lamellae stacking at $0 . 3 0 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ both in in-plane and out-of-plane directions. A $\pi - \pi$ stacking at $1 . 6 6 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ is seen in out-of-plane direction, giving a stacking distance of $3 . 7 8 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } .$ DF-PCIC exhibits low crystallinity, however, a $\pi - \pi$ stacking in out-of-plane direction is found to locate at $1 . 7 3 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ corresponding to a distance of $3 . 6 3 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ These structure features remain in BHJ blends. As seen from the as-cast thin film, the polymer lamellae diffraction is observed in both in-plane and outof-plane patterns with a broad azimuthal angle spreading. The inplane crystal coherence length (CCL) is estimated to be 115.2 A. The $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak area is dominated by that of DF-PCIC, centered at $1 . 7 3 \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 }$ . Thermal annealing can effectively change the structure order of the BHJ thin film, leading to great changes of scattering features. As seen from the corresponding image and profiles, $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ annealing enhances the crystallization of both donor and acceptor materials. The in-plane CCL for PBDB-T is enhanced to be $1 5 2 . 1 \mathring \mathrm { A }$ with the pronounced highly ordered reflections. A new peak at $0 . 5 0 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ in both in-plane and out-of-plane profiles is seen, which comes from the lamellae stacking of DF-PCIC, and their corresponding $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak can also be allocated. Quite sharp $\pi - \pi$ stacking is recorded for DF-PCIC in the $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ annealed BHJ film, with the peak centered at $1 . 7 0 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ (3.69 A), which is slightly away from that of the neat thin film. The CCL of $\pi - \pi$ stacking in out-of-plane direction is estimated to be ${ 5 8 . 7 \mathrm { ~ \AA } } ,$ which is much larger than that in the neat film (14.6 A). This obvious crystallinity enhancement for both donor and acceptor leads to a morphology change of forming bicontinuous crystalline networks, thus electron and hole mobilities undergo an order of magnitude improvement. In a consequence, $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF increase dramatically for the related OsC. On the other hand, the crystallinity improvement narrows the bandgap of DF-PCIC, thereby, its LUMO energy level would become lower, which may be responsible for the reduced $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of the device annealed at $1 8 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$

Blends processed from CN additive shows a polymer in-plane (100) CCL of $1 3 3 . 3 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ with highly ordered reflections. The $\pi - \pi$ stacking is located at $1 . 7 0 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ , with good intensities in both inplane and out-of-plane profiles. However, the peak from the lamellae stacking of DF-PCIC is not observed, implying that CN additive can only improve the crystallization of the donor. Moreover, CN can induce edge-on orientation of the materials, which is proved by (200) lamellae stacking at $0 . 6 0 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ for PBDB-T. These findings can account for the enhanced hole transport and the reduced electron transport. When thermally annealed at $1 3 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ the PBDB-T (100) in-plane CCL is calculated to be $1 3 4 . 8 \mathring \mathrm { A }$ which is similar as that of nonannealed thin film. Yet the $\pi - \pi$ stacking of the sample shifts to $1 . 7 3 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ , with the enhanced intensity and a more preferred face-on orientation. The lower temperature treatment can thus fine-tune the material self-assembly to form a tighter $\pi - \pi$ stacking and more favorable orientation to boost transport properties. In the meanwhile, the crystalinity of DFPCIC is not improved greatly. Therefore, high $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ remains, and $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF increase for the related device, providing the best PCE of $1 0 . 1 4 \%$ When annealed at $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for CN processed thin films, similar results are obtained as those in $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ annealed thin film without CN. These results suggest that only thermal annealing at high temperatures, e.g., $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ can induce the obvious crystallization of DF-PCIC, which agrees well with the findings from DSC and absorption measurements (Figures S1b and S9, Supporting Information).

Thin film morphology is investigated by transmission electron microscopy (TEM). As seen in Figure S11 in the Supporting Information, the as-cast thin film shows a homogeneous phase image. Thermal annealing at $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ induces the crystallization of both components, and in the corresponding TEM picture, quite tiny fibers with the length of several tens of nanometer are observed, which gives rise to the enhanced $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF. When CN is used, BHJ film shows a large phase separation. Annealed at $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ the phase separation size of the thin film is reduced, which is attributed to the formation of material crystallites. The reduced phase separation domains and enhanced crystallization can reduce $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and improve $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF, and thus PCE is improved. A more balanced improvement is obtained through the annealing under $1 3 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ which leads to the finer phase separation domains. This morphology feature help balance $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } / \mathrm { F F }$ and the champion device performance is obtained.

The current material and processing recipe does not only enhance the device performance, but also improve the device stability. We investigate the morphological stability of PBDBT:DF-PCIC blended films, and compared them with those of PBDB- $\mathrm { \cdot T { : } P C _ { 7 1 } B M }$ blended films. Through thermal treatment at $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for various times (Figure 5a), PBDB-T:DF-PCIC blended films remains uniform even after $3 \mathrm { { h } }$ thermal treatment. In contrast, PBDB- $\mathrm { \cdot T } \mathrm { : P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ blended films under similar condition shows granular agglomerations, which is much severer with the extension of treatment time. Such different morphological changes may result in different device stability. We then fabricate PBDB-T:DF-PCIC and PBDB-T $\mathrm { \cdot P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ devices, and test the stability of PCEs after thermal treatment at 130, 150, or $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for various times (Figure 5b). It is found that PCEs of the devices based on PBDB-T:DF-PCIC blended films are insensitive to thermal treatment at high temperatures and $8 5 . 5 \%$ and $8 6 . 2 \%$ initial efficiencies can remain after thermal treatment at 130 and $1 5 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 2 \mathrm { { h } }$ , respectively. Even the temperature is increased to $1 8 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ $6 9 . 8 \%$ initial efficiency can still be kept after $1 2 \mathrm { { h } }$ thermal treatment. But for PBDB- $\mathrm { T } { : } \mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ -based devices, the situations are bad. After $1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ thermal treatment, only $5 2 . 5 \%$ and $5 0 . 8 \%$ initial efficiencies can remain for 130 and $1 5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ respectively. If the temperature increases to $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , the PCE quickly decreases to $1 1 . 9 \%$ of initial efficiency after $1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ . Furthermore, we also test the device stability of PBDB-T:ITIC-based OSCs at high temperatures and find that the devices based on PBDB-T:ITIC are defeated by PBDB-T:DF-PCIC-based ones in device stability, too (Figure S12, Supporting Information). Withoutdoubts, OSCs based on PBDB-T:DF-PCIC blended films can exhibit excellent thermal stability. As for the reasons of the excellent morphological stability for PBDB-T:DF-PCIC films and the related devices, we believe that high temperatures lead to the stronger aggregation of DF-PCIC. However, since the F..H noncovalent interactions between CPDT units and 2,5-difluorobenzene groups would be destroyed under thermal treatment at high temperatures, e.g., $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ DF-PCIC may adopt a twisted molecular geometry, and thus over aggregation might be avoided at high temperatures. Meanwhile, the twisted D core has minor effects on the intramolecular charge transfer from CPDT units to IC end-groups as well as on the $\pi - \pi$ stacking between IC terminals of DF-PCIC, which is proved by the improved crystallinity of the thermally annealed films. Therefore, good absorptions and efficient charge transport can remain even the blended films or devices are thermally treated at $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 2 \ \mathrm { h } .$ which demonstrates that our molecular designing strategy is successful for achieving thermally stable OsCs. What is more, we further test the device stability of PBDB-T:DF-PCIC-based OSCs under continuous AM 1.5 illumination at $1 0 0 \mathrm { ~ m W ~ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for various times (Figure S13, Supporting Information). It turns out that $8 8 \%$ initial PCE is remained after $^ \textrm { \scriptsize 3 h }$ continuous illumination, which is meaningful for the practical applications of OSCs.

In summary, we design and synthesize a new nonfullerene acceptor (DF-PCIC) in an easy-synthesis protocol. Through the utilization of F..H noncovalent interaction, nearly planar molecular geometry can be constructed for DF-PCIC without involving complicated ring-fusing reactions to IDT or IDTT units. Thus, DF-PCIC possesses similar absorption bands and energy levels as ITIC. After proper optimizations, OSCs with PBDB-T as the donor and DF-PCIC as the acceptor provide the best PCE of $1 0 . 1 4 \%$ Furthermore, due to the unique D core of DF-PCIC built via noncovalent interactions, both PBDB-T:DFPCIC blended films and PBDB-T:DF-PCIC-based devices can show excellent morphological and device stability, even being tortured at $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ , which beat those of $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ or ITIC-based counterparts. In a word, good photovoltaic performance and excellent device stability are achieved for DF-PCIC. Such molecular design strategy would help obtain new nonfullerene acceptors practically applied in OSCs.
