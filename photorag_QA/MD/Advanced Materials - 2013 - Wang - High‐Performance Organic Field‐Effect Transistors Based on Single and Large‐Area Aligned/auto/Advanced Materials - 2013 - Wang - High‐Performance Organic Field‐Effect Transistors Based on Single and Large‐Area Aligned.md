# High-Performance Organic Field-Effect Transistors Based on Single and Large-Area Aligned Crystalline Microribbons of 6,13-Dichloropentacene

Mao Wang, Jie Li, Guangyao Zhao, Qinghe Wu, Yangguang Huang, Wenping Hu, Xike Gao, Hongxiang Li,\* and Daoben Zhu

Organic semiconductors are of great interest for electronics applications, as they have many advantages over their inorganic counterparts.1l To achieve practical applications with organic semiconductors, they should have high performance (high mobility, low threshold voltage and high current on/off ratio), stability and reproducibility, and should be easy to synthesize and purify. Recently, great progress has been made in the exploration of organic semiconductors,2] but organic semiconductors with mobility higher than $5 . 0 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { \bar { s } } ^ { - 1 }$ are still rare (see Figure S1 and Table S1).3] And most still are difficult to synthesize and purify and/or display low stability in ambient conditions.

The charge-transport properties of organic semiconductors critically depends on the inherent arrangement of molecules in the solid state.4l Theoretical calculations revealed that the cofacial $\pi - \pi$ stacking configuration of organic molecules facilitates charge transport.[5] Bao and co-workers found that the mobility of 5,11-dichlorotetracene, which packs with extensive long-range $\pi - \pi$ stacking, was three orders of magnitude higher than that of 5-chlorotetracene, which exhibits a sandwich-herringbone arrangement.[6] Anthony and co-workers synthesized triisopropylsilylethynyl substituted pentacene (TIPs-Pentacene) and revealed that TIPS-pentacene adopts a 2D "bricklayer" packing model with strong face-to-face $\pi - \pi$ interactions in the solid state.l7l Field-effect transistors made from TIPS-pentacene displayed a high mobility of up to $1 . 4 2 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ based on the self-assembled ribbons from solutions. Although great efforts have been carried out, until now it is still a great challenge to control the molecules' packing in the solid state with strong - interactions.[9]

6,13-dichloropentacene (DCp, chemical structure see Figure 1a), as a derivative of pentacene, could be easily synthesized and purified, and displayed good stability.[10] Single crystals revealed, due to the introduction of chlorine substituents, DCP adopted slipped $\pi - \pi$ stacking model with large intermolecular overlap, and CHCl interactions between neighboring DCP molecules were observed, suggesting the potential applications of DCP as high performance organic semiconductor. Herein, crystalline ribbons of DCP were prepared. XRD results showed DCP stacked to the same packing arrangement in the crystalline ribbons as that of single crystals. The single crystalline ribbon transistors displayed extremely high mobility up to $9 . 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ . As far as we know, this is one of the highest mobility reported for pentacene derivatives. The transistors exhibited good stability and showed no obvious degradation after storing in ambient conditions for two months. Moreover, unlike pentacene and most of the organic semiconductors, DCP adopted the same molecular packing upon the change of deposition conditions, which further demonstrated the potential applications of DCP in high performance transistors.

Crystalline ribbons of DCP were grown on octadecyltrichlorosilane (OTS)-treated $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { S i }$ substrate by physical vapor transport technique (PVT). OTS was used to form a siloxane self-assembled monolayer (SAM) on the $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ layer, which could promote and facilitate molecular self-organization during the growth of ribbons.[11] AFM images showed DCP ribbons displayed a faceted shape with smooth surface (Figure 1a), indicating the high quality of the ribbons. The shape of the yellow shining crystalline ribbons were several micrometers in width, several tens to hundreds micrometers in length. Figure 1b illustrated the out-of-plane XRD diffraction results of DCP crystalline ribbons. Two obvious diffraction peaks at $2 \theta =$ 9.40 and 18.99 degrees were observed, these diffraction peaks could be assigned as (020) and (040) respectively, referred to the single crystal structure of DCP. The $d$ spacing determined from these peaks were $1 8 . 6 4 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ This value is almost the same. as the $b$ axis of the unit cell (18.718 A). Thus, we inferred the b axis of unit cell was perpendicular to the substrate surface in ribbons (Figure 1c). With this orientation, the. $\pi - \pi$ stacking direction was parallel to the substrate surface according to the single crystal structure, we presumed this long-range ordered stacking with large $\pi - \pi$ overlap contributed to the high mobility of the ribbon transistors. The crystalline ribbons grown on the substrates were fabricated into top-contact, bottom-gate configuration transistors (Figure 2a). The gold source and drain electrodes were deposited through Au stripe mask".[12] The following was the procedure: firstly, ${ \sim } 1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ Au was deposited on bare $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { S i }$ substrate; then, the Au film was cut in to small stripes by mechanical tips, the size of the Au stripes depended on the ribbon and channel size, the stripe was then transferred on the ribbon as a mask; next, $3 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ Au was deposited on the ribbons; and finally, the stripes were removed from the ribbons and the surrounding Au films were used as source and drain electrodes. The electrical characteristics of the devices were measured at ambient conditions by a Keithley 4200-sCS semiconductor parameter analyzer. The transfer and output curves were displayed in Figure 2b and 2c. The field-effect mobility $( \mu )$ was extracted from the saturation regime, and the mobility was calculated by linear fitting of $( \mathrm { I _ { D S } } ) ^ { 1 / 2 }$ vs $\mathrm { v _ { G } }$ curves. All devices exhibited good gate modulation, and the mobilities of all crystalline ribbon transistors were above $3 . 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ (Figure 2d). The highest mobility could reach $9 . 0 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , and the averaged mobility of 14 independent devices was $5 . 7 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ with threshold voltage $( \mathrm { V _ { T } } )$ between $^ { - 8 }$ to $_ { 1 \textrm { V } }$ and on-to-off current ratios $\left( \mathrm { I } _ { \mathrm { o n } } / \right.$ $\mathrm { I _ { o f f } } ) > 1 0 ^ { 4 }$ . The hole mobility of pentacene in ultrapure singlecrystal transistors which employed perylene as gate dielectric had been reported as $2 . 2 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ [13] And very recently, Tahsin J. Chow group reported the single crystal transistors of pentacene using $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ as dielectric layer displayed a mobility up to $1 . 2 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ [14] The high mobility of DCP proved that the $\pi - \pi$ stacking with large intermolecular overlap facilitated charge transport and demonstrated the strong influence of chlorine substituents to the performance of materials. The DCP ribbon transistors exhibited good environmental stability and showed no apparently decline after storing in ambient conditions for two months.

A key factor for organic semiconductors in low-cost largearea applications is solution processibility.[15] Because of the introduction of chlorine substituents, DCP exhibited decent solubility in chlorinated solvents such as chlorobenzene or o-dichlorobenzene upon heating. When a $1 5 \mu \mathrm { L }$ solution of DCP $\mathrm { 1 . 0 { - } 1 . 5 \ m g / m L }$ in o-dichlorobenzene) was drop-cast on bare $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { S i }$ substrates, large-area well-aligned microribbons which were several micrometers in width and hundreds micrometers in length were obtained (Figure 3a). XRD characterization showed these ribbons displayed the same diffraction peaks as that of vacuum-deposited film and PVT grown crystalline ribbons (Figure 1b). This demonstrated DCP molecules had a strong tendency to adopt slipped. $\pi - \pi$ stacking regardless of the changes of substrates (OTS-modified or bare $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { S i }$ and deposition conditions. Most of the organic semiconductors, such as pentacene and rubrene, exhibited multiple crystal phases (different molecular packing) upon the changes of substrates and deposition conditions, and the performance of organic semiconductors dramatically depended on the crystal phases. For example, the single crystal of rubrene displayed excellent mobility $( 2 0 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } / \mathrm { V s } )$ [3a] while the mobility of thin film transistors of rubrene was reported as less than $1 0 ^ { - 3 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } / \mathrm { V s }$ [16] The low dependence on substrates and deposition conditions guaranteed the practical applications of DCP.

Well-aligned organic mirco/nano-sized structures have great advantages in fabricating transistor arrays which are the necessary steps towards circuits.[15a,17] However, till now it is still a great challenge to prepare large area and high orientated organic mico/nano-sized structures.18] The well-aligned DCP microribbon transistors were fabricated in a top contact structure. Au source and drain electrodes were patterned on the aligned ribbons through a shadow mask method. Due to the incomplete coverage of the channel (Figure 3a, 3b), the mobility of transistors was corrected by measuring the effective channel width W through optical microscope and AFM images. The transfer and output characteristics were shown in Figure 3c and 3d. The transistors displayed a mobility of $0 . 1 9 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , threshold voltage $( \mathrm { V _ { T } } )$ of $^ { - 2 3 }$ to $- 5 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ and on-to-off current ratios $ { ( \mathrm { I _ { o n } / I _ { o f f } ) } }$ $> 1 0 ^ { 4 }$ . This performance was at the same level as that of DCP thin films. However, comparing with the crystalline ribbons prepared from PVT technique, the mobility of ribbons grown from solution was much poorer. We reckoned this was caused by the following reasons: first, the aligned ribbons were grown too fast from solution, and displayed lower crystalline quality and shape (Figure 3b); second, more traps were exist in these ribbons which was proved by larger $\mathrm { V _ { T } }$ comparing with that of ribbons grown by PVT method; third, the purity of the DCP ribbons grown from solution was lower than that prepared by PVT method (During the physical vapor transport process, the DCP was further purified, and the purity of organic semiconductors strongly affected the device performance).[13] In light of the high performance of crystalline ribbons obtained by PVT technique, the performance of the aligned microribbons should be greatly improved through materials purification and optimization of the solution processing conditions.

In summary, crystalline ribbons of DCP were grown by way of the PVT method. The ribbons displayed a high mobility of up to $9 . 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , one of the highest values reported for organic semiconducting materials. Due to the existence of strong $\pi - \pi$ stacking and C-H-Cl interactions between neighboring DCP molecules, the DCP molecules had a strong tendency to adopt a slipped $\pi - \pi$ stacking mode regardless of the choice of substrate (OTS-modified or bare $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { S i }$ substrate) or deposition conditions. The $\pi - \pi$ stacking with large intermolecular overlap is responsible for the high mobility of DCP ribbons. Moreover, large-area uniaxially oriented DCP microribbons have been prepared through solution processing. These well-aligned organic crystals have great advantages in fabricating transistor arrays which are the necessary steps towards electronic circuits. These properties, combined with the high stability, demonstrate the potential application of DCP in organic electronics.

# Experimental Section

Synthese of DCP ribbons: The $\mathsf { S i } / \mathsf { S i O } _ { 2 }$ substrates were cleaned in deionized water and then immerged into hot sulphuric acid $( 9 8 \% )$ and hydrogen peroxide $( \mathsf { v } / \mathsf { v } = 2 { : } \mathsf { l } )$ mixed solution for 15 minutes to get rid of organic compounds physically absorbed on the substrate and successively cleaned with deionized water, isopropyl alcohol and acetone, at last dried with quick purged $\mathsf { N } _ { 2 }$ . Before any modification, the substrates were cleaned by UVO process for $1 5 \mathsf { m i n }$ , octadecyltrichlorosilane (OTS) modification was carried out for about $^ { 4 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ by vapor-deposition method. Then the substrates were rinsed with chloroform, n-hexane, isopropyl alcohol and acetone by ultrasonic cleaning. At last, the substrates were dried with quick purged $\mathsf { N } _ { 2 }$ . For ribbons obtained by PVT technique, the. DCP materials were kept at $1 7 5 ~ ^ { \circ } C$ for sublimation. The OTS-modified substrates were placed at a distance of around $9 \ c m$ from the stuff, the speed of carrier gas (high-purity argon) was around $5 0 0 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n }$ . For microribbons obtained by solution processing, several drops of DCP solution in o-dichlorobenzene (around $1 5 ~ \mu \ L )$ were dropped cast onto an bare $\mathrm { S i } / \mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ substrate $( 1 ~ { \mathsf { c m } } \times 1 ~ { \mathsf { c m } } )$ , the substrate with the droplet was placed on a Petri dish with a cover. Uniaxially orientated crystals were formed on the substrate after the substrate was put on a hot plate at $1 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathsf C$ for several minutes.

Device Fabrication: All devices were fabricated as bottom-gate top contact configuration, and Au was used as source and drain electrodes. For the devices based on the microribbons grown through PVT technique, the Au source and drain electrodes were fabricated through "Au stripe mask". The channel width (W) was several micrometers, the channel length (L) was 30-100 micrometers, the ${ \sf W } / { \sf L }$ was between $1 / 5 -$ $1 / 6 0$ which was measured through optical microscope and AFM images. For the devices based on large-area aligned microribbons, Au source and drain electrodes were patterned on the aligned ribbons through a shadow mask method. The channel length (L) was $3 7 \ \mu \mathrm { m }$ , the channel width (W) was measured through optical microscope and AFM images.

Characterizations: The single crystals were characterized with SMART APEX CCD $\mathsf { X }$ ray single-crystal diffractometer. X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were carried out in the reflection mode using a 2-kW Rigaku X-ray diffraction system. AFM was recorded on a Nanoscope IIIa atomic force microscopy in tapping mode. All optical microscope images were taken by an OLYMPUS PX-51-P microscope. Electric characteristics of all devices were measured by a Keithley 4200-scS semiconductor analyzer.
