# Recent Progress on New Charge Transfer Pathways in Organic Solar Cells

Tian-Fu Xiang, Aijun Li,\* and Xiao-Feng Wang\*

In the rapidly evolving field of organic solar cells (OsCs), the charge transfer (CT) process at the photoactive layer interface is pivotal for device performance. This article focuses on recent progress of Z-type CT pathway, an unconventional process in OsCs that differs significantly from traditional donor-acceptor systems. The Z-type process shows the potential for high open-circuit voltage devices. Herein, some applications in solar cells that are inspired by the Z-scheme in natural photosynthesis are first introduced. Subsequently, studies on mechanisms of charge generation and transfer in bilayer OsCs with Z-type characteristics are presented. In analogy to the devices in OsCs, other types of optoelectronic devices with similar electron transfer pathways are discussed. Lastly, challenges and potential opportunities are addressed, and the need for further research to harness the potential of Z-type devices in OsCs is emphasized.

# 1. Introduction

Organic solar cells (OsCs) have attracted significant attention in the realm of renewable energy due to their numerous advantages. OsCs can be manufactured using printing techniques, which offer the benefits of cost-effectiveness and simplified fabrication processes. Moreover, OsCs predominantly employ organic molecules, resulting in reduced pollution and enhanced environmental friendliness compared with conventional siliconbased devices. The spectral response of organic materials can be modified through chemical alterations, enabling efficient utilization of visible and near-infrared light for solar energy harvesting. Additionally, organic thin films not only demonstrate biocompatibility but also facilitate the production of flexible devices. Continuous research efforts are steadily improving the efficiency of OsCs, bringing them closer to commercial viability.

The most common OsCs is the donor-acceptor (D-A) device.1-4] Upon absorption of a photon, an electron situated on the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOmO) of the donor was excited to the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO), with an exciton generated. The exciton then diffuses to the interface, where it dissociates in the presence of the built-in electric field, resulting in the electron transitioning from the donor's LUMO to the acceptor's LUMO. Subsequently, the electron is captured by the electrode, providing energy to the external circuit. The open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ of a D-A device is determined by the HOMO-LUMO difference between the donor and the acceptor.

In this study, we present recent progress on new charge transfer (CT) pathways in OSCs. The unconventional energy-level alignment devices, called Z-type devices, provide a new CT pathways distinct from the aforementioned traditional D-A device.15 The Z-type device is characterized by a CT mechanism where the trajectory of

CT pathway at the interface of the photoactive layer resembles the shape of letter Z. Figure 1a,b shows the operational mechanisms and theoretical maximum $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $\textsf { Z }$ -type and D-A devices, respectively.5] This Z-type device exhibits a different CT pathway compared with D-A devices and holds higher theoretical maximum Vc.6]

Comparing the operating mechanisms of traditional D-A device with Z-type device, the difference lies in how they combine the energy generated from the two light-active layers. In D-A device, the currents from both layers are superimposed, while in Z-type device, the voltages from the two light-active layers are superimposed. On one hand, Z-type devices are expected to possess optimization potential similar to D-A device, while offering different optimization directions, thereby providing new insights for the development of OsCs. On the other hand, the loss in current transmission might be larger than the loss in voltage transmission in OSCs, due to the relatively low electrical conductivity of organic semiconductor materials. Consequently, Z-type devices might have higher development potential compared with D-A device.

A common concern is expressed regarding the recombination process in Z-type device, given that the CT is mainly the transition from excited-state electrons to excited-state holes. The concern is whether this recombination process could impact the theoretical efficiency limits and limit their development compared to D-A device.

When analyzing the theoretical maximum efficiency limits of bilayer solar cells, two primary factors need to be considered. First, thermodynamic detailed balance effect governs the equilibrium processes involving the photons, electrons, and phonons related to the absorption of light within the two photoactive layers. Second, the CT processes at the semiconductor heterojunction interface are subject to the dominion of quantum transport theory.

Given that the difference between these two devices lies in the CT mechanisms at the interface. When comparing their development potential, it is sufficient to focus on quantum transport theory at the interface without delving into issues related to the film.

Analyzing the CT processes of heterojunction interface, it is essential to consider D-A and Z-type devices at the same time. In qualitative analysis, we observe that D-A device primarily superimposes the current attributes of two active layers, with voltage being independent. And Z-type device mainly superimposes the voltage attributes of two layers. $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ corresponds to the energy of electrons, while current corresponds to the quantity. For the whole process, an increase in current leads to a decrease in voltage, and an increase in voltage results in a decrease in current. These two aspects, with their symmetric impact on overall conversion efficiency, render the maximum theoretical conversion efficiency independent of the interface transmission mode.

Furthermore, the energy and quantities of electrons are interconnected through the chemical potential or Fermi level, in condensed matter system. Hence, issues that may arise in D-A device could similarly manifest in Z-type device. Therefore, the developmental potential inherent in D-A device is also applicable to Z-type device.

What we would like to emphasize is that the significance of studying the Z-type CT process is not limited in promoting the acceptance of Z-type device. Rather, it should involve a comparison between Z-type and D-A CT pathways to explore how organic heterojunctions operate under nonequilibrium conditions such as illumination considering not only the structural constraints like energy-level alignment but also the dynamic limitations related to electron excitations.

First, we will introduce some devices exhibiting Z-type characteristics that are inspired by the Z-scheme process of photosynthesis in plants, which also give rise to the naming convention of Z-type.7.8] The main process of photosynthesis is summarized by the Z-scheme: the reaction is a photochemical reaction between photosystem I and photosystem II. In photosystem II, light is absorbed and used to excite a pair of electrons, which are subsequently transferred to photosystem I. In photosystem I, these electrons are excited again and transferred to an electron transport chain composed of multiple cytochromes and carotenoids.[9-13]

Certainly, it is important to note that distinctions exist between the mechanisms underlying the Z-scheme and those Z-type thinfilm solar cells. In the Z-scheme of photosynthesis, electron transfer primarily occurs at the chemical reaction process, involving the electrons transfer between different reactions.14-16] In thin-film solar cells, electron transfer relies predominantly on the electronic structure of the interface and is more characterized by properties of physical process.17] while there are similarities between them, the differences are also evident. The question of whether the Z-scheme of chemical reaction process can be applied to explain the physical process will be addressed in the subsequent section about the mechanism of Z-type device.

In the following sections, we first introduce devices with Z-type characteristics in OSCs as well as other types of solar cells. We then review recent progress of investigating about operating mechanism of Z-type device in OsCs, which provide a semiquantitative understanding of the CT process between active layers. In the third section, we compare Z-type device in OSCs with devices in organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs) and other organic optoelectronic fields, analyzing both their similarities and differences. The applications discussed in this section about other organic optoelectronics, which also exhibit the Z-type CT process, serve as evidence that the capability of Z-type CT is not exclusive to Chl and its derivatives.

Some reviewers are concerned about the universality of Z-type devices: besides Chl, are there any other molecules that can be used in Z-type devices? Our viewpoint is that it is possible. On one hand, in our unpublished work, we have successfully created Z-type devices. On the other hand, organic thin-film solar cells are condensed matter system, and the CT at the interface is determined by band theory and quantum transport theory. Different molecules should follow the same physical laws.

# 3. Investigation of Z-Type Charge Transfer Process

In previous studies, the devices drew inspiration from natural photosynthesis to mimic its operational principles. While these devices demonstrated functional operation, the exact mechanism of CT at the interface had not been definitively established. In this section, we further explore the establishment of the builtin electric field to investigate the specific CT mechanisms. Xiang et al. conducted an investigation to semi-quantitatively analyze the Z-type CT mode by studying the micro-level charge movement within the macroscopic electric field distribution. They reported an unconventional energy-level alignment structure for OsCs, wherein the generation of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ cannot be explained by traditional D-A OsCs theory.16] Their study entailed a semi-quantitative analysis of the charge transport mechanism within this unconventional energy-level alignment devices.

The most representative cells are based on D-A system, in which the photoactive layer is composed of a phased-separated mixture of two organic semiconductors, a donor and an acceptor, separated by potential gradient to convert the excitons into free carriers.2931 Based on general consensus, the theoretical $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of D-A OsCs is generally smaller than the difference between the HOMO of the donor and the LUMO of the acceptor and follows the linear relationship $q V _ { \mathrm { O C } } { \approx } E _ { \mathrm { C T } } { - } 0 . 6 \mathrm { e V }$ [17,31] where $E _ { \mathrm { C T } }$ is the energy of the CT states. However, they discovered device with an unconventional energy-level alignment. If it were possible to explain the device by the D-A system theory, the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ would paradoxically be greater than the difference between the HOmO of the donor and the LUmO of the acceptor. It can be inferred that this type of device has a new operating mechanism, which may provide guidance for the further investigation of Z-type electron transfer process.

They employed X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPs) to investigate the built-in electric fields between two active layers. As shown in Figure 6b, by analyzing the change of the binding energy of inner-shell electrons in Chl-A upon deposition of $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ they found that the electrons in Chl-A flow toward the ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ , forming an electric dipole layer pointing from Chl-A toward ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ between Chl-A and $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ (Figure 6c). In the dark state, there is an intrinsic electric field pointing from Chl-A toward $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$

Next, they introduced the small injection hypothesis to discuss the distribution of charge carriers under illumination.[28] The small injection hypothesis is widely used in the study of semiconductor heterojunctions. It assumes that the number of nonequilibrium carriers can be neglected compared to the number of majority carriers in equilibrium state. Therefore, the study of CT process in the photoactive layer is focused on the influence of nonequilibrium carriers on minority carriers. Considering that ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ is often used as an n-type semiconductor, electrons in $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ could be treated as majority carriers.[32] Similarly, holes in Chl-A could be considered as majority carriers.26 When the device is irradiated with light, it reaches a quasi-thermal equilibrium state, and the Fermi level of the two optically active materials is divided into the quasi-Fermi level of holes and the quasi-Fermi level of electrons. As shown in Figure 6d, since electrons in ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ are considered as majority carriers, the electron concentration under illumination remains almost unchanged, resulting in an unchanged electron quasi-Fermi level. Meanwhile, holes in Chl-A are treated as minority carriers, and the hole concentration under illumination also remains almost constant, leading to an unchanged hole quasi-Fermi level.

At the same time, although the electron concentration in this region is low, its relative change in concentration is large. Therefore, the electron quasi-Fermi level could be intuitively described as from the $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ region to the heterojunction interface, and the electron quasi-Fermi level remains unchanged (Figure 6d). From the interface to the ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ region, the electron quasi-Fermi level first changes abruptly, then gradually increases, and finally approaches stability (Figure 6d). It is known that the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ is determined by the difference between the quasiFermi level of the electrons in Chl-A and that of the holes in ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ when the heterojunction formed a complete circuit with an external circuit,33] as shown in Equation (1)

Based on the laws of charge conservation and continuity equations, it can be inferred that as the photogenerated carriers are transported to the external circuit, there should be a recombination at the $\mathrm { C h l - A / C _ { 6 0 } }$ interface between the photogenerated holes provided by Chl-A and the photogenerated electrons by ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ That is the Z-type CT process.

Since the small-injection assumption is consistently used in above analysis, where the influence of the photo-generated nonequilibrium carriers on the equilibrium majority carriers is neglected, Xiang et al. also reanalyzed the impact of nonequilibrium carriers on the majority carriers at different light intensities through the JV curves of the devices.l As shown in Figure 6e,f, as light intensity decreases, the fill factor of the device gradually decreases, and the $J { - } V$ curve tends toward linearity, indicating the device displayed resistive behavior. This confirms that the Z-type CT mode dominates at the photoactive interface of this device.

On the other hand, after the formation of the heterojunction between the two semiconductors, the process of charge flow between the four energy levels of the two semiconductors is a dynamic equilibrium process. We refer to the current formed by the movement of holes from the higher HOMO energy level to the lower HOMO energy level as $I _ { 1 }$ and the current formed by the movement of electrons at the light-active layer interface from the lower LUMO energy level to the higher HOMO energy level as $I _ { 2 }$ . By comparing these two currents, we can further discuss the issue. In the operation of D-A type devices, $I _ { 1 }$ plays a crucial role in generating the main current of the heterojunction, while $I _ { 2 }$ serves as the leakage current. On the other hand, in the operation of Z-type devices, $I _ { 1 }$ acts as the leakage current, while $I _ { 2 }$ becomes the primary current generator. This fundamental difference distinguishes the two device types. D-A type devices have undergone comprehensive optimization in practice to enlarge $I _ { 1 }$ and suppress $I _ { 2 }$ . In contrast, in Z-type devices, it is necessary to enhance $I _ { 2 }$ while suppressing $I _ { 1 }$ . This requires further optimization in various aspects, including material selection, for Z-type devices.

The introduction of the small injection assumption in this study aims to analyze the impact of nonequilibrium charge carriers on the equilibrium carrier concentration. The analysis sequence is determined by the small injection assumption, which first examines the influence of nonequilibrium carriers on the minority carrier concentration and then analyzes the impact on the majority carrier concentration through $J { - } V$ curves under different illumination conditions. In a semiconductor material with $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ mainly used as an ETL, emphasizing the evaluation of how nonequilibrium carriers impact its holes, as opposed to the effect on electrons, is a more crucial aspect in the semi-quantitative analysis. To consider the impact of nonequilibrium carriers on the two different charge carriers in two separate steps may lead to some loss of correlation from the perspective of many-body quantum mechanics. However, from a semiclassical perspective, it is reasonable.

In above work, the electric dipole layer at the photoactive interface plays a crucial role. Based on the model mentioned above, Xiang et al. adopted a strategy of treating the Chl-A film with ethylenediamine (EDA) to raise its Fermi level, increased the Fermi level difference between Chl-A and $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 } .$ enhanced the built-in electric field at the interface, and improved device efficiency.!l Moreover, a Z-characteristic phenomenon is directly observed in the IPCE measurement.

As depicted in Figure 7a, the treatment of EDA results in a decrease in the work function of Chl-A from 4.73 to $4 . 6 5 \mathrm { e V }$ which indicates that the EDA treatment enhances the electron-donating ability of Chl-A. From changes in the Chl-A absorption spectra as shown in Figure 7b, it can be concluded that the EDA treatment does not further affect the bandgap of Chl-A. After the treatment with EDA, the efficiency of the device significantly increases (Figure 7c). The $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ raises from 0.9 to $1 . 9 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ raises from 0.6 to $0 . 7 7 \mathrm { V }$ . Also, the overall efficiency of the device increases by $2 8 0 \%$

With EDA treatment, the binding energy of the zinc element inner $2 \mathrm { P } _ { 3 / 2 }$ electron in Chl-A is reduced to $1 0 2 1 . 1 5 \mathrm { e V }$ and then increases to $1 0 2 1 . 3 9 \mathrm { e V }$ after $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ deposition as shown in Figure 7d. It indicates that after EDA treatment, the deposition of $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ causes a change of $0 . 2 4 \mathrm { e V }$ in the binding energy of the zinc element inner $2 \mathrm { P } _ { 3 / 2 }$ electron in Chl-A.

Figure 7e shows that there is a large peak change in normalized incident photon-to-current efficiency (NIPCE) after doping EDA. In the short-wavelength region, the relative intensity of two peak positions at around 450 and $3 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ changes greatly. The peak of about $3 6 0 \mathrm { n m }$ is moved to $3 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ , and the peak of about $4 7 5 \mathrm { n m }$ is moved to $4 2 5 \mathrm { n m }$ . In the long-wavelength region, the peak at $7 3 0 \mathrm { n m }$ has a large change to $6 1 0 \mathrm { n m }$ . The phenomenon observed in the aforementioned NIPCE image, although difficult to be explained by traditional device theory, can be easily understood by the operating mechanism of Z-type CT devices.

The mechanism of the Z-characteristic device is explained as two types of photoactive materials are separately excited by photons. Holes in the HOMO of Chl-A and electrons in the LUMO of ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ recombine at the interface, while electrons in the LUmO of Chl-A and holes in the HOMO of ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ are transmitted outward to the external circuit. Thus, for each electron-hole pair supplied by the device to the external circuit, a pair of excited state carriers is recombined at the interface. If only one of the materials has excited carriers while the other does not, the interfacial CT process would be limited. Therefore, as depicted in Figure 7f, in the IPCE test of the Z-characteristic device, the efficiency of charge generation would be highest when there is a greatest overlap of the absorption spectra of two photoactive materials. This work provides further experimental support for the model established by previous mechanism analyses and directly observes the phenomenon of Z-characteristics.

It is important to emphasize that the aforementioned direct observation of the Z-characteristic is based on monochromatic light excitation. However, during the actual operation of the devices, they are exposed to sunlight with a broad spectrum. Under such conditions, both photoactive materials can absorb photons of different frequencies, leading to current generation. While we can infer the existence of the Z-type CT mode from the IPCE test, we cannot deduce that both photoactive materials must absorb photons with the same energy to enable the Z-type CT pathway. The reviewers have expressed a particular interest in optical field simulations and thickness absorption tests. As previously mentioned, the absorption spectra of the two photoactive materials should complement each other to maximize the efficiency of solar spectrum utilization. Therefore, optical field simulations and thickness absorption tests for Z-type devices are similar to those for D-A devices.

Though previous studies introduce operating mechanisms of Z-type CT devices in the photoactive layer, there are still some limitations to the applicability of these models. The small injection assumption has been widely used in the study of semiconductor heterojunctions. The main idea of small injection assumption is to assume that nonequilibrium carriers do not affect the built-in electric field generated by equilibrium carriers. Thus, the steps taken are to first analyze the distribution of equilibrium carriers in the equilibrium state to form the built-in electric field and then analyze the effect of the built-in electric field on the nonequilibrium carriers. However, the applicability of small injection assumption is limited for organic semiconductors. It is mainly because organic semiconductors have a lower equilibrium carrier concentration than inorganic semiconductors, and light-generated nonequilibrium carriers cause a significant influence to the equilibrium carriers. Xiang et al.'s work also supports this view experimentally.[6] The different J-V characteristics of the heterojunction at different light intensities are determined by the influence of nonequilibrium carriers on equilibrium carriers. Although the applicability of the small injection assumption is limited in organic semiconductor heterojunctions, it is still of great significance for qualitative analysis of CT at the heterojunction interface.

The small injection assumption considers the effect of majority carriers of two semiconductor materials in a structured way by taking into account the band bending. However, to model the performance of organic semiconductor heterojunctions, a dynamic perspective is needed to take into account the impact of photogenerated carriers on the band bending formed by majority carriers in equilibrium. This requires further develop. ment of quantitative models.

# 4. Applications of Z-Type Charge Transfer Process in Other Optoelectronic Devices

From the perspective of semiconductor heterojunctions, though the Z-type devices inspired by natural photosynthesis have particularities, they have university with the tandem devices in OSCs and other organic electroluminescent devices.34] In this section, we will focus on the similarities and differences of these characteristics.

The front subcell utilizes a wide bandgap active layer to reduce thermalization losses, and the back subcell utilizes a low bandgap active layer to reduce transmission losses. Tandem devices are a combination of subcells connected by ICL, describing a device composed of multiple subcells, while Z-type devices are entirety devices that reflect a combination of the photoactive layers. In tandem devices, two subcells are usually composed of traditional D-A structured OsCs. And their ICL forms the Z-type CT. This illustrates the difference between the Z-type CT process and the process of tandem devices, while emphasizing the equivalence of the Z-type transfer pathways and D-A CT pathways. According to the work of Xiang et al.,5] it was found that in Z-type devices, the stronger the Z-characteristics of the heterojunction, the higher the device performance.

OLEDs and OSCs share the same physical process, except that the former is electrically generated light and the latter is photogenerated electricity.36,40] Different from the recombination of two excited-state carriers in the Z-type CT process in OSCs, this process is reversed as charge generation in OLEDs. Tang jianxin's research group has done some distinctive work in this area.36] They studied the energy levels at various interfaces of Bphen/Mg:Bphen/ $\mathrm { \Delta M o O _ { 3 } / N P B }$ (Figure 9a) and found that this charge generation layer is a type of $\scriptstyle { \mathrm { n / n / p } }$ structure. Due to the small energy-level difference of only $0 . 8 \mathrm { e V }$ between NPB's HOMO and $\mathsf { M o O } _ { 3 }$ s conduction band, electrons are accumulated on the $\mathbf { M o O } _ { 3 }$ side and holes are accumulated on the NPB side. Under the influence of electric field, electrons can be injected into Mg:Bphen's LUMO via the conduction band of $\mathsf { M o O } _ { 3 }$ with a lower barrier and eventually into the emissive unit. It can be observed that, although in reverse process, the Z-type CT characteristics are also present in this device.

This report involves doping of inorganic semiconductors, which differs from the Z-type CT process in OsCs. However, in OLEDs, the charge-generating layer consisting of $\mathtt { p / n }$ organic semiconductors can also produce charges similar to other charge-generating layers. For example, the research group of Shu-Tang Li used a heterojunction of copper phthalocyanine and fluorinated copper phthalocyanine as a charge-generating layer to prepare a stack blue OLED, which achieved a significant increase in brightness and current eficiency.41] Similarly, Kang et al. determined the CT from Pentacene to ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ through UPS and XPs.42,43] Many research groups have also carefully studied the interface between ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ and pentacene and detailed the mechanism of dipole formation at the ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 0 } /$ Pentacene interface (Figure 9b).144) The formation process of the interface dipole layer in this organic semiconductor heterojunction is similar to that in the research of Xiang et al. on the operating mechanism of unconventional energy-level alignment devices.5,6] These heterojunctions have improved the power efficiency in OLEDs.

The Z-type CT process in OSCs is supported by corresponding studies on CT in OLED devices. Meanwhile, tandem devices in OSCs and Z-type CT devices have similar CT pathways, but with distinct properties. In tandem devices, the focus is on the connection between the two separate subcells, while in Z-type devices, two photoactive layers act as a entirety.

# 5. Conclusion

In summary, this work reviews the research process on the Z-type CT process in OsCs. Starting from the bio-solar cell inspired by natural photosynthesis, the further applications of OSCs are introduced. The application of Z-type CT process in other areas such as PSCs is also discussed. The starting point of research about Z-type device is the study of an unconventional energy-level alignment devices. If we attempt to explain the operation of this device with the theory of traditional D-A device, it would lead to a paradox that the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ exceeding its theoretical limit. Based on this observation, the model of the Z-type CT mechanism is studied. Moreover, the model established in this study is used for further research on Z-type device, in which the Z-type CT characteristics are enhanced by EDA. And Z-characteristic is directly observed in IPCE. Finally, considering the essential nature of Z-type CT in organic heterojunctions, similar processes in other optoelectronic devices such as tandem devices and OLEDs are also discussed.

Some reviewers have expressed interest in the optimization directions for Z-type device. The absorption spectra of the two photoactive materials in Z-type device should ideally be complementary. This not only promotes effective utilization of the solar spectrum but also ensures that both materials generate excitedstate charge carriers efficiently, which is a common feature with

D-A device. What sets Z-type device apart is the need for the excitation levels of charge carriers in both photoactive materials to be in harmony. If they are not well matched, it might lead to energy wastage in the conversion process.

In a word, this work describes recent progress of the Z-type CT pathways in OSCs, which is similar to the Z-scheme in photosynthesis. The Z-type CT process in OSCs could have the same potential for development as the traditional D-A CT process and can also provide important reference for the development of organic optoelectronic devices.
