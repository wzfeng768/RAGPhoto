# 18.66% Efficiency of Polymer Solar Cells Employing Two Nonfullerene Acceptors with Fluorine or Chlorine Substitution

Shuping Zhang, Xiaoling Ma, Lianbin Niu,\* Sang Young Jeong, Han Young Woo,\* Zhengji Zhou,\* and Fujun Zhang\*

Organic semiconducting materials with fluorine or chlorine substitution are commonly designed to prepare efficient polymer solar cells (PsCs) through finely modulating the energy levels and absorption spectra. Herein, two small molecular acceptors with fluorine substitution L8-BO or chlorine substitution eC9-2Cl are selected to prepare ternary PSCs with PM6 as polymer donor. The optimal ternary PSCs exhibit a power conversion efficiency of $1 8 . 6 6 \%$ , benefiting from the simultaneously increased open-circuit voltage $( \boldsymbol { v _ { \infty } } )$ of 0.89 V, short-circuit current density $( J { \boldsymbol { s } } { \boldsymbol { c } } )$ of $2 6 . 6 3 \ : \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and fill factor of $78 . 7 4 \%$ when the content of chlorine substitution eC9-2Cl is about $20 \%$ in acceptors. Fluorinated and chlorinated substitutions can improve the $\pmb { V } _ { \mathsf { O C } }$ or $J _ { s c }$ of the corresponding binary PSCs, which can be recombined into efficient ternary PSCs through optimizing their content in acceptors. Chlorinated materials have special crystalline conditions due to the specific features of the chlorine atoms with large atomic radius and the empty $3 d$ orbits compared to the corresponding fluorinated materials. Adding an appropriate amount of the chlorine substitution eC9-2Cl further enhances the crystallization and intermolecular interaction of the ternary PsCs, which is beneficial for charge transport in the active layer and for device performance improvement.

# 1. Introduction

Polymer solar cells (PSCs) have obtained huge achievement with power conversion efficiency (PCE) up to $1 9 \%$ , benefiting from some novel donor or acceptor materials, also from the device engineering with ternary strategy, layer-by-layer structure, or interfacial buffer layers.!1-51 Ternary strategy was first proposed to enhance photon harvesting of active layers containing two more materials with complement absorp tion spectra.[6-9] In addition to photon harvesting enhancement of active layers, the ordered molecular packing and phase separation degree of ternary active layers play a vital role in determining exciton dissociation and charge transport. The alloy model was proposed to optimize active layers with the excellent compatibility of three kinds of materials.[0,11] Ternary PSCs based on the alloy model also exhibit a gradually varied open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ dependence on the content of the third component, which may be attributed to the mixture of excited electrons on two well-compatible donors or acceptors.1213] The complementary photovoltaic parameters of binary PSCs are one of the materials selection basis for the preparation of efficient ternary PSCs.14,15] Fluorination and chlorination have been confirmed as efficient route to tune the absorption spectrum and the highest occupied molecular orbitals (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of donor and acceptor materials. Compared with the corresponding fluorinated molecules, the atomic size of Cl in chlorinated molecules is larger than that of F, and the enhanced $\pi$ electron overlap is conducive to increase the intermolecular packing and crystal properties, thus improving the charge transfer.16,17] The absorption spectra of chlorinated molecules are normally redshifted; the narrower bandgap acceptor materials can harvest more photons in long wavelength range, leading to the relatively large short-circuit current density $( J { \mathrm { s c } } )$ . The uplifted LUMO levels of the chlorinated acceptor eC9-2Cl are favorable to enhancing the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of psCs. The corresponding fuorinated or chlorinated acceptor materials may have great potential in achieving efficient ternary PSCs due to their excellent compatibility and complementary photovoltaic parameters of binary PSCs.

In this work, two Y6-derivations with different fluorine or chlorine substitutions, L8-BO and eC9-2Cl, were selected as acceptor and PM6 was selected as polymer donor; the full names of the materials used are described in the Supporting Information. The chemical structures of PM6, eC9-2Cl, and L8-BO are shown in Figure 1a. The energy levels and absorption spectra of materials used were measured and are displayed in Figure $^ { 1 \mathrm { b } , \mathsf { c } }$ respectively. The eC9-2Cl-based binary PSCs exhibit a PCE of $1 7 . 1 2 \%$ with $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 5 . 6 3 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 9 0 \mathrm { V }$ , and fill factor (FF) of $7 4 . 2 3 \%$ . The L8-BO-based binary PSCs display a PCE of $1 8 . 0 1 \%$ , with $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 6 . 3 7 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 8 \mathrm { V }$ and FF of $7 7 . 5 9 \%$ . The photovoltaic parameters $( J _ { \mathsf { S C } } , \mathrm { ~ } V _ { \mathsf { O C } } ,$ and FF) of two kinds of binary PSCs are complementary, providing a great opportunity to prepare ternary PSCs with increased PCEs. The optimal ternary PsCs were achieved by adjusting the content of the third component eC9-2Cl in active layers; the champion PCE of $1 8 . 6 6 \%$ is achieved with a $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 6 . 6 3 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , a $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 9 \mathrm { V }$ , and an FF of $7 8 . 7 4 \%$ when the weight ratio of PM6:L8-BO:eC9-2Cl is 1:0.8:0.2. This work indicates that employing one chlorinated acceptor and one fluorinated acceptor may be an excellent candidate for preparing efficient ternary PSCs.

To clarify the underlying reasons of eC9-2Cl incorporation the performance improvement of ternary PsCs, the photogenerated current density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ versus effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ curves of typical binary and ternary PSCs were measured and the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } - V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ curves are exhibited in Figure 3a. The $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ is defined as the $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ difference between under light illumination and in dark conditions. The $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ is expressed as the voltage difference between the applied voltage and the voltage at $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } = 0$ [20-22] The exciton dissociation $\left( \eta _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } \right)$ and charge collection efficiency $( \eta _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } )$ could be evaluated from $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \mathrm { \ddot { \Omega } } ^ { * } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \# / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } ,$ respectively. The $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \mathrm { ^ { * } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \#$ are the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ under short-circuit and maximal power output conditions, respectively.23-26] The detailed $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ values of typical PSCs under different conditions and their ratios are exhibited in Table 2. The relatively large $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ of ternary PSCs should be attributed to the enhanced photon harvesting induced by the addition of eC9-2Cl. The $\eta _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and the $\eta _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ of L8-BO-based device and eC9-2Cl-based device and L8-BO:eC9-2Cl-based device were $9 7 . 7 3 \% / 8 8 . 4 2 \%$ $9 6 . 4 8 \% / 8 7 . 6 9 \%$ , and $9 8 . 2 1 \% / 8 9 . 3 8 \%$ , respectively. The simultaneous improvement of $\eta _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $\eta _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ can well explain the increase of $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF in the optimal ternary PSCs.

According to the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } { - } V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ curves of typical PSCs, the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ of ternary PSCs can rapidly arrive into the saturated state under relatively low $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } ,$ indicating the more efficient charge transport in the active layer, resulting in the highest FF of $7 8 . 7 4 \%$

To explore the dynamic process of charge recombination in the active layer, the $J { - } V$ curves of all PSCs were measured under different incident light intensity illumination. The dependence relationship between $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ on incident light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ is plotted in Figure 3c. Through the correlation between $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } ,$ i.e., $\begin{array} { r } { J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \propto P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } \alpha } \end{array}$ the bimolecular recombination behavior can be identified.27-30] The closer the $\alpha$ value is to 1, the fewer the composition of bimolecular recombination is. When the eC9-2Cl content is $2 0 \mathrm { w t \% }$ in acceptors, the calculated $\alpha$ value is 0.975. The $\alpha$ values are 0.969 and 0.938 for the binary PSCs with L8-BO and eC9-2Cl as acceptor, respectively. Apparently, the $\alpha$ value of the optimal ternary device is closest to 1, indicating that the bimolecular recombination can be attenuated by the addition of appropriate eC9-2Cl. The relationship between $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ can be described as $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } \propto \beta k T / q \mathrm { l n } ( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ . The $\beta$ value represents the degree of trap-assisted recombination. Here, $k$ is the Boltzmann constant, $T$ is the temperature, and $q$ is the elementary charge.[31-34] When the value of $\beta$ is close to 1, the trap-assisted recombination could be negligible. The $\beta$ value of the optimal ternary blend system is 1.226, which is closest to 1 than 1.268 and 1.375 of PM6:L8-BO and PM6:eC9-2Cl binary PSCs. Trap-assisted recombination can be effectively inhibited in the ternary PSCs, which is conducive to elevating $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF.

The electrochemical impedance measurement was employed to gain deep insight into the effect of eC9-2Cl incorporation on charge transport and recombination in PSCs; alternating bias voltage at $V = V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ was applied with the frequency range $5 \mathrm { H z } \approx 5 \mathrm { M H z }$ [35-37] The Nyquist plots of all PSCs are plotted in Figure 3c, the relevant parameters are summarized in Table S1, Supporting Information, and the equivalent circuit diagram used to fit the Nyquist plots is inserted in Figure 3c. The $R _ { \mathrm { { O S } } }$ represents the series resistance generated by electrodes and the bulk resistance of active layers, while the $R _ { \mathrm { C T } }$ is the charge transfer resistance generated by the interfacial charge transfer process.[38-40] The values of $R _ { \mathrm { O S } }$ are 42.9, 37.4, and $5 4 . 6 \Omega$ in the L8-BO-based binary film, optimal ternary blended film, and eC9-2Cl-based binary film, respectively. The optimal ternary blend has a relatively small $R _ { \mathrm { O S } }$ It is shown that the introduction of an appropriate amount of eC9-2Cl favors the lessening of charge recombination loss, which can adequately account for the shift of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ in the corresponding device. Capacitor-like constant-phase element (CPE) is used for nonideal properties of capacitors. The CPE is contingent on the capacitance value $( \mathrm { C P E } _ { \mathrm { T } } )$ , the nonuniformity constant $( \mathrm { C P E _ { P } } )$ with the value of $( 0 \approx 1 )$ , and the angular frequency (@), which is expressed as $Z = \left( \mathrm { C P E } _ { \mathrm { T } } \right) ^ { - 1 }$ $\left( \mathrm { i } \omega \right) ^ { \mathrm { C P E _ { P } } }$ [41,42] The $\mathrm { C P E _ { P } }$ values for PM6:L8-BO, PM6:L8-BO:eC9-2Cl, and PM6:eC9-2Cl are 0.978, 0.984, and 0.962, respectively. The $\mathrm { C P E _ { P } }$ of the optimal ternary device is the closest to 1, indicating that the interface capacitance is more desirable by incorporating appropriate eC9-2Cl in active layers. The charge-transfer lifetime (t) in the blended film can be calculated according to the formula: ${ \boldsymbol { \tau } } = R _ { \mathrm { C T } } \times \mathrm { C P E _ { T } }$ , and the $\boldsymbol { \tau }$ values of PM6:L8-BO binary system, the PM6:L8-BO:eC9-2Cl optimal ternary blend film, and PM6: eC9-2Cl binary system are 118.14, 104.53, and 141.7 ns, respectively. The $\boldsymbol { \tau }$ of the optimal ternary PSCs is moderately short, indicating that charge recombination could be effectively suppressed in ternary active layers.

The space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method was adopted to investigate charge mobility in the binary and ternary blend flms.43 45] The device configuration of electron-only devices and hole-only devices are ITO/ZnO/active layer/PNDIT-F3N/Al and ITO/PEDOT:PSS/active layer/MoO3/Ag, respectively. The $\ln ( J d ^ { 3 } / V ^ { 2 } ) - ( V / d ) ^ { 0 . 5 }$ curves of the hole- or electron-only devices are shown in Figure S2, Supporting Information, and the relevant parameters are listed in Table S2, Supporting Information. The calculated hole mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } \right)$ and electron mobility $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ in the active layers are plotted in Figure 3d. The $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ in ternary active layers are slightly improved by adding $2 0 \mathrm { w t \% }$ eC9-2Cl. Normally, charge transport balance in devices can be evaluated from $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ [46-50] The $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ values are 0.79, 0.89, and 0.7 for devices with PM6:L8-BO, ternary, and PM6:eC9-2Cl as active layers, respectively. The $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of the optimal ternary devices is 0.89 and the closest to 1, indicating that adding an appropriate amount of eC9-2Cl can make the charge transport more balanced in active layers, leading to the relatively high FF of $7 8 . 7 4 \%$ for the optimal ternary PSCs.

The effect of eC9-2Cl on the crystallinity and molecular packing of the blend films was investigated by grazing-incidence wide-angle X-Ray scattering (GIwAxs).51-53) Figure 4a-f depicts the 2D-GIWAXS plots for the neat PM6, L8-BO, eC9-2Cl films, and the blend films. The corresponding in-plane (IP) and out-of-plane (OOp) line-cut profiles are shown in Figure 4g,h and the relevant parameters are listed in Table S3, Supporting Information. The neat PM6 films exhibit a strong $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak at $0 . 3 \mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 }$ in the OOP (100) direction, a relatively weak diffraction peak at $1 . 7 3 \mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 }$ in the OOP (010) direction, and an obvious lamellar stacking peak at $0 . 2 9 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ in the IP (100) direction. This indicates that PM6 molecules adopt both face-on and edge-on orientated molecular stacking in the neat films. Both the neat L8-BO and eC9-2Cl films feature a preferential face-on orientation as evidenced by an obvious $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak located at $1 . 7 3 \mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 }$ in the OOP (010) direction and the lamellar stacking peak $( 0 . 4 3 \mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 }$ vs $0 . 4 0 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 } \big ]$ in the IP (100) direction. According to the IP and OOP profiles of all blend films, a distinct $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking diffraction peak at $1 . 7 3 \mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 }$ in the direction of OOP (010) can be found, which is the same position as the diffraction peak of the neat acceptor films, indicating that the $\pi - \pi$ stacking in the blend films is dominated by acceptors. All the blend films exhibit a lamellar diffraction peak at $0 . 2 9 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ in the direction of IP (100), which reflects that the molecular arrangement and crystallinity of PM6 can be well reserved in the blend films. The crystalline coherence length (CCL) values related to $\pi - \pi$ stacking and lamellar stacking can be calculated (2.34 and $6 . 1 5 \mathrm { n m }$ for PM6:L8-BO, 2.36 and $7 . 5 3 \mathrm { n m }$ for PM6: L8-BO:eC9-2Cl, 2.25 and $9 . 5 8 \mathrm { n m }$ for PM6:eC9-2Cl) in the light of Scherrer formula: $\mathrm { C C L } = 2 \pi k / \Delta q$ [5456] The optimal ternary blended film exhibits larger CCL of both the $\pi - \pi$ stacking and lamellar stacking in comparison to the L8-BO-based binary blend film. These results indicate that the incorporation of appropriate eC9-2Cl is conducive to enhance the crystallinity and intermolecular interaction of the blend films. In addition, the lamellar stacking and $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking peak of ternary blended film can be simultaneously enhanced in comparison with that of the PM6:L8-BO binary blend film, illustrating more orderly face-on molecular arrangement and more efficient charge transport in the optimized ternary active layers.

# 3. Conclusions

In this work, two fluorinated and chlorinated acceptor materials (L8-BO and eC9-2Cl) with similar chemical structures were selected to prepare excellent ternary PSCs. The chlorinated material eC9-2Cl shows a broader absorption spectrum, elevated LUmO levels, and improved crystalline properties. The PM6:L8-BO:eC9-2Cl ternary blend film exhibited highly ordered face-on molecular arrangement and more balanced and stronger charge carrier mobility by adding $2 0 \mathrm { w t \% }$ chlorine substitution eC9-2Cl in acceptors. As a result, the optimal ternary PSCs achieve a champion PCE of $1 8 . 6 6 \%$ benefiting from the simultaneously enhanced $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 9 \mathrm { V }$ $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 6 . 6 3 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and FF of $7 8 . 7 4 \%$ . These results demonstrate that combining the advantages of fluorinated and chlorinated NFAs should be an effective approach for achieving highly efficient ternary PSCs.
