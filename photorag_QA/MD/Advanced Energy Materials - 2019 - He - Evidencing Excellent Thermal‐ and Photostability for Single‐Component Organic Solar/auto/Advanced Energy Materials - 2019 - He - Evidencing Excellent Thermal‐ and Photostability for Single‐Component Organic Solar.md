# Evidencing Excellent Thermal- and Photostability for Single-Component Organic Solar Cells with Inherently Built-In Microstructure

Yakun He, Thomas Heumuller, Wenbin Lai, Guitao Feng, Andrej Classen, Xiaoyan Du, Chao Liu, Weiwei Li, Ning Li,\* and Christoph J. Brabec\*

Solution-processed organic solar cells (OsCs) are promising low-cost, flexible, portable renewable sources for future energy supply. The state-of-theart OSCs are typically fabricated from a bulk-heterojunction (BHJ) active layer containing well-mixed donor and acceptor molecules in the nanometer regime. However, BHJ solar cells suffer from stability problems caused by the severe morphological changes upon thermal or illumination stress. In comparison, single-component organic solar cells (ScOsCs) based on a double-cable conjugated polymer with a covalently stabilized microstructure is suggested to be a key strategy for superior long-term stability. Here, the thermal- and photostability of scOsCs based on a model double-cable polymer is systematically investigated. It is encouraging to find that under $9 0 \textdegree$ & 1 sun illumination, the performance of ScOsCs remains substantially stable. Transport measurements show that charge generation and recombination (lifetime and recombination order) hardly change during the aging process. Particularly, the SCOsCs exhibit ultrahigh long-term thermal stability with $100 \%$ PCE remaining after heating at temperature up to $1 6 0 ^ { \circ } C$ for over $4 0 0 \ h$ , indicating an excellent candidate for extremely rugged applications.

As one of the most promising renewable energy sources, photovoltaics (PV) is widely deployed for daily use and for industrial applications. It is generally acknowledged that outdoor applications, such as building- and automotive-integrated photovoltaics are very attractive for a future renewable energy scenario. However, these scenarios require solar cells with an exceptional high temperature tolerance, since the surface of a dark-colored car or building wall can easily approach and surpass $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ in summer.1,2] To fulfill these requirements, mounted solar cells need to work sustainably and stably at $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ under illumination. Conventional inorganic solar cells, such as silicon or GaAs, can work properly at the required high temperatures.3] However, the traditional solar cells are not fully compatible with future building- and automotiveintegrated applications.

Presently, several research groups have reported power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of over $14 \%$ for lab-scale bulkheterojunction (BHJ) organic solar cells (OSCs), indicating a significant step toward commercialization.46] The BHJ concept involves interpenetrating networks of donor and acceptor materials forming domains of dimensions commensurate with the exciton diffusion length in the nanometer regime.

However, this requires careful and exquisite optimization of the multiple experimental variables involved in material design and device fabrication. For example, relative concentrations of donor and acceptor materials, ink design, film-processing conditions, or the introduction of additives have to be considered for device optimization, which are highly critical and troublesome. On the other hand, the optimized donor-acceptor (D-A) microstructure morphology is frequently thermodynamically unstable and evolves upon external thermal or illumination stress.7-9] This causes severe heat- or light-induced degradation, which restricts their photovoltaic performance and long-term stability[10-16] Under high thermal stress, especially under "accelerated conditions" with temperatures exceeding operating condition (between room temperature and $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ J17-21] many polymers or molecules tend to undergo a primary or secondary glass transition, triggering enhanced thin film diffusion, leading to unfavored morphological changes and a progressive loss of device performance.[22-25]

One of the elegant strategies to address the instability issues is the development of single-component OsCs employing doublecable polymers, which contain donor and acceptor blocks in one molecule. This ensures the functions of light absorption, exciton dissociation, and charge transport, which are otherwise achieved by two different materials-donor and acceptor--in the BHJ concept.26] The single-component OSCs present advantages such as considerable simplification of device fabrication and stabilization of the active layer morphology. Furthermore, the fact that light absorption and charge separation would occur at the same molecule should erase the problems related to the short exciton diffusion length in OSCs. One of the first single-component OSCs (SCOsCs) based on the double-cable approach was reported by Janssen and co-workers, in which a conjugated polymer with pendant fullerenes was adopted, showing a short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathsf { S C } } )$ of $0 . 4 2 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , an open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ of $0 . 8 3 \mathrm { V } ,$ a fill-factor (FF) of O.29 and a $0 . 1 0 \%$ efficiency under 1 sun irradiation.27] Despite an evident conceptual simplicity, the design of double-cable polymers is very complex, since the nanophase adjustment is directly related to the covalent linkage between the donor-type and acceptor-type groups, resulting in severe charge recombination and low photocurrent in solar cells. Therefore, the development is relatively slow. Zhang et al. synthesized a donor-acceptor double-cable polymer involving regio-regular poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT) and perylenediimide acrylate, and reported an improved efficiency of $0 . 4 9 \%$ [28] Geng and co-workers synthesized several extended D-A co-oligomers in which substituted fluorine-alt-bithiophene was selected as donor block and perylene-diimide as acceptor, and efficiencies of about $1 . 5 0 \%$ were reached after solvent annealing, which was among the highest efficiency at that time.29] $\mathrm { I n } ~ 2 0 1 7$ , Li et al. developed a double-cable polymer (called SCP3) using diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP)-based backbone and pendant perylene bisimide (PBI) as electron acceptor, connected with flexible alkyl side chains, which exhibited a PCE of $2 . 7 4 \%$ [30] Even higher efficiencies of $4 . 1 8 \%$ were achieved by a double-cable polymer containing electrondonating poly(benzodithiophene) (BDT) as linear conjugated backbone for hole transport and pendant electron-deficient PBI units for electron transport, connected via a dodecyl linker.[31] Besides, Pierini et al. reported a SCOsC based on electrospinning-fabricated 1D fullerene-grafted polythiophenes, in which a $5 . 5 8 \%$ efficiency was achieved.32] The significant progress during the last 2 years underlines the substantial potential of doublecable polymers for organic photovoltaic devices. Although one of the drives for developing double-cable polymers is to obtain highly stable OsCs, the stability of this class of materials, especially under high temperature, has not yet been investigated.

In this work, we explore the thermal stability of a doublecable polymer as a promising alternative to the blended BHJ microstructure. We systematically investigate the intrinsic stability of a model single-component polymer solar cell based on SCP3 (as shown in Figure 1) under both thermal stress and light illumination. With an inverted structure (Figure 1), the performance of SCP3-based OSCs was optimized by a thermal annealing step at $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ before evaporation of the top electrode. The differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) curves of SCP3 are shown in Figure S1, Supporting Information, which exhibited no melting peaks and no phase change between 50 and $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ . Besides, no clear glass transition was observed from the temperature modulated DSC (m-DSC) measurement, resulting from the high crystallinity nature of SCP3 material (Figure S2, Supporting Information). The current density-voltage $\left( J - W \right)$ curves for SCP3-based solar cells under both light and dark conditions are exhibited in Figure 1, showing an efficiency of around $2 . 6 \%$ which is in accordance with the values reported in literature.30] To exclusively study the stability under thermal and illumination conditions, the cells were tested in a home-built characterization setup in $\mathrm { N } _ { 2 }$ atmosphere, mimicking perfectly encapsulated conditions. The OSCs were aged under different conditions: 1) $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ~ \&$ dark, where the cells were kept at $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ without light exposure; 2) RT & illumination, the cells were kept at room temperature (RT) and under continuous 1 sun $( 1 0 0 \mathrm { m W } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ equivalent illumination provided by white light LEDs (Figure S3 in Supporting Information); 3) $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ~ \&$ illumination, the cells were kept at $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and under continuous 1 sun equivalent illumination.

Under both conditions, all the photovoltaic parameters remained almost unchanged, indicating their superior resistance to thermal stress and prolonged light exposure. The slightly increased $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and PCE could be attributed to the rearranged microstructure morphology under external stress. We consider the residual thermal stress from the ultrahigh temperature thermal annealing $( 2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ process could influence the creep and relaxation behavior of sCP3 under external stress, resulting in local rearrangement of chains or segments in the active layer, as demonstrated in Figures S5 and S6 in Supporting Information.33] Similar performance increase phenomena during degradation process were also reported in literatures.34-36] Particularly, SCP3-based solar cells exhibited almost $100 \%$ efficiency of the initial value, even after measurement up to $1 0 0 0 \ \mathrm { h }$ (as shown in Figure S7, Supporting Information). For comparison, we fabricated BHJ solar cells with the same donor and acceptor blocks as SCP3 material. Herein, PDPP2TBDT was used as donor, in which DPP is alternating with 2D BDT and PBI as acceptor material (chemical structures shown in Figure S8 in Supporting Information). The BHJ solar cells however showed efficiencies of only $1 . 4 \%$ , and performance loss of ${ \approx } 1 3 \%$ under $9 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } \ \&$ dark condition in $1 0 0 \ \mathrm { h }$ (Figure S8 and Table S4, Supporting Information). To put these values into perspective, we report typical stability findings for state-of-the-art BHJ systems based on fullerene and non-fullerene acceptors under even milder conditions: 1) $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ & dark, 2) RT & illumination. In stark comparison, systems like PBQ-QF: [7o]PCBM or PBQ-QF: ITIC (chemical structures shown in Figure S9, Supporting Information) with initial efficiencies of ${ \approx } 7 \%$ and ${ \approx } 9 \%$ both exhibited severe burn-in degradation with efficiency loss of $3 5 \%$ and $3 7 \%$ under $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ~ \&$ dark condition, and loss of $4 1 \%$ and $45 \%$ under RT & illumination condition after 300 hours' lifetime test (Figure S10, Supporting Information). Such findings point out a remarkable thermal stability of SCP3-based OSCs.

The excellent intrinsic thermal- and photostability of SCP3-based OSCs highlights the advantage of the strategy to covalently link the donor and acceptor in a double-cable architecture. Large scale de-mixing was prevented in such a design, and as a result we found an exceptional thermal stability for a solution-processed OSC.13,22,37,38] Moreover, the very impressive device stability data also highlight the excellent reliability of each component used in this inverted device architecture, including the electrontransporting layer $( \mathrm { Z n O } )$ and hole-transporting layer $( \mathrm { M o O } _ { x } )$

We carried out current-voltage and transient photovoltage (TPV) measurements to elucidate the transport properties. We first considered recombination dynamics by probing the current density-voltage behavior at different light intensities for fresh and aged solar cells. From the comparative, double logarithmic $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ versus light intensity plots represented in Figure 3A, it is apparent that the slope was unity for the fresh SCP3-based solar cells, i.e., charge carrier recombination at short-circuit condition was not limited by second-order recombination.39-41] After exposing SCP3-based solar cells to thermal or light stress, a slope of 0.98 or 0.99 was measured. $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ was still governed by second-order recombination. Considering now the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ versus light intensity plot in Figure 3B, the pristine solar cells featured a slope of $1 . 2 ~ k T / q$ , i.e., mostly second-order recombination,[42,43] and the slopes for aged SCP3-based solar cells under $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ~ \&$ dark and RT & illumination conditions were 1.2 and $1 . 3 ~ k T / q$ , respectively, which were almost the same as the value for the fresh ones. The same results were obtained for SCP3-based solar cells aged under harsh conditions $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ $\&$ illumination). The slightly increased recombination after degradation hardly influenced the solar cell performance.

To further examine the impact of thermal stress and light exposure on charge generation and charge transport, we measured the photocurrent density $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ as a function of the effective voltage $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ as well as the charge carrier mobility. The increase of $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ versus $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ in Figure 3C was almost the same for the SCP3-based solar cells before and after degradation. The photocurrent $\textstyle ( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \ @$ $- 5 . 0 \mathrm { V } )$ was $1 0 . 7 9 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ for fresh SCP3-based solar cell, and 11.40, 10.87, and $1 0 . 9 0 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for the ones aged under $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ & dark, $\scriptstyle \mathrm { { R T } } \&$ illumination and $9 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } \ \&$ illumination conditions. The enhanced $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ was consistent with the slightly improved $J _ { S C }$ values extracted after degradation, and could be attributed to the microscopic rearranged microstructure morphology upon external stress.44] We then focused on understanding the impact of thermal stress and light exposure on the charge transport behavior of SCP3-based solar cells. For this purpose, we investigated the charge carrier mobility $( \mu )$ of solar cells by employing the technique of charge carrier extraction by linearly increasing voltage (Photo-CELIV). Figure S11 (Supporting Information) displays the Photo-CELIV traces of fresh and degraded solar cells. Notably, the extraction peak $( \boldsymbol { t } _ { \mathrm { m a x } } )$ was hardly shifted and broadened after degradation under $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ~ \&$ dark and $\scriptstyle \mathrm { { R T } } \&$ illumination conditions. And the calculated mobility was almost the same, consistent with $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ -light intensity behavior in Figure 3B.

The recombination mechanism was further analyzed using TPV and charge extraction (CE) techniques in order to accurately calculate the charge carrier lifetime $\tau$ and charge carrier density n. Figure 3D displays $\tau$ versus $n$ for fresh and aged SCP3-based solar cells. We calculated a recombination order of 2.8 for fresh and 2.9, 3.0, and 2.9 for aged SCP3-based solar cells under $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ & dark, RT $\&$ illumination, and $9 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } \ \&$ illumination conditions, respectively. These values presented secondorder recombination $( R = 2 )$ behavior in the presence of significant energetic disorder. We attributed the slightly enhanced recombination order to the potential formation of trap states or further enhanced electronic disorder in the aged solar cells, although the trap density was not enough to influence the solar cell performance. According to literature, SCP3 is a kind of polymer with condensed crystal packing morphology. It is plausible that this peculiar morphology with high crystallinity is a chief aspect of its enhanced stability, which is consistent with earlier work, showing that crystalline polymers are less prone to thermal- or light-induced traps and associated loss in photovoltaic performance.30] As shown in Figures S12 and S13 (Supporting Information), the atom force microscopy (AFM) and photoluminescence (PL) and electroluminescence (EL) results also demonstrated the excellent morphology stability.

We also compared the charge carrier lifetimes at the same generation rates, i.e., same light intensities (Figure 3E), to see if the competition between generation and recombination changed, and also at the same charge carrier densities. Figure 3E evidenced that charge carrier lifetime of fresh and aged solar cells was almost identical. Overall, we did not find evidence of a significant change in recombination dynamics upon high temperature degradation. Besides, as shown in Figure 3F, the charge carrier density values of sCP3-based solar cells aged under $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ~ \&$ dark and RT & illumination conditions were also unchanged when compared with the value of fresh SCP3-based solar cells. A slightly higher charge carrier density at $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ was also observed in the SCP3-based solar cells aged under $9 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } \ \&$ illumination condition, which implied that the density of states (DOs) has increased in the aged solar cells. As calculated from the Photo-CELIV traces in Figure S11 (Supporting Information), the charge carrier mobility of aged SCP3-based solar cells under $9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ~ \&$ illumination condition was slightly decreased to $4 . 4 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ from $4 . 9 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ of the fresh sCp3-based solar cells. However, since the additional states observed in CE did not increase the recombination rate constant, and carriers were able to leave those states in charge extraction measurements, they might be shallow traps or increased energetic disorder.[45-47] To testify the speculation, Fourier transform photocurrent spectroscopy (FTPS) was also conducted, which showed no obvious deep trap states, that is, the trap states were under dark condition, which would not influence the photovoltaic performance (Figure S14, Supporting Information).

To further investigate the performance of SCP3-based solar cells under high thermal stress, we measured its temperaturedependent photovoltaic performance (Figure 4A-D) and compared it with conventional inorganic photovoltaics, such as Si, GaAs and Ge (values are taken from the literature3l). As shown in Figure 4B, all four kinds of solar cells showed a linear decrease in $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ with an increase in temperature (around $0 { - } 1 5 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ SCP3-based solar cells displayed a comparable temperature coefficient of $- 2 . 1 \ \mathrm { m V } \ \mathrm { K } ^ { - 1 }$ , which was comparable to Si and GaAs solar cells (around $- 2 . 1$ $- 1 . 7 \ \mathrm { m V \ K ^ { - 1 } }$ , and significantly better than the one of low bandgap Ge solar cells (around $- 4 . 7 \ \mathrm { m V \ K ^ { - 1 } }$ . On the other hand, the thermal coefficient of SCP3-based solar cells was directly comparable with values found for other organic polymer: fullerene solar cells, around $- 1 . 6 5 \mathrm { m V } \mathrm { K } ^ { - 1 }$ in the range of $3 0 { - } 6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ [48,49] However, generally speaking, organic solar cells could hardly tolerate temperatures up to $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ or even higher. As reported, PTB7:[70]PCBM devices showed rapidly decreased photovoltaic performance $( \approx 4 0 \%$ of initial performance) upon $8 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ annealing.[50] At these temperatures, SCP3-based solar cell remained unaffected (Figure 4D). The three inorganic types of solar cells showed a continuous decrease of PCE with the temperature coefficient of 0.0063, $- 0 . 0 0 3 3$ $- 0 . 0 0 2 1 \% \mathrm { K } ^ { - 1 }$ . For SCP3-based solar cells, the PCE value remained constant around $100 \%$ until about $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and then decreased slightly with higher temperatures. Such superior performance could be directly related to the smart design of the SCP3 double-cable structure.

To explore the temperature limitation of SCP3-based solar cells, we annealed the solar cells inside glove box under gradually increased temperature $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ $1 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ $1 6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ till $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ . The cells were kept at each temperature for 10 min and then cooled down to RT for $J { - } V$ measurement, and all the results were measured until the values came to equilibrium. It was found that the performance remained stable until around $1 6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and then decreased slightly at $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ when annealing for $1 \textrm { h }$ as shown in Figure S15 (Supporting Information). As indicated by DSC curves, SCP3 material should be fairly stable at $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ;$ while from the picture of silver electrodes after heating at $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 \textup { h }$ (Figure S16, Supporting Information), it is obvious that the top electrode became foggy, indicating silver penetrating into active layer and thus decreasing the performance. Consequently, we chose $1 6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ as the critical temperature for SCP3-based solar cells and investigated the long-term thermal stability at this harsh temperature in a $\mathrm { N } _ { 2 }$ -filled glovebox. As shown in Figure 4E, after thermal annealing at $1 6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $4 2 6 \ \mathrm { h }$ the performance of SCP3-based solar cells remained almost at the same level, which is among the best thermal-stability reported for OPV solar cells to date, as shown in Figure 4F. We believe that this new generation of double-cable polymers, showing superior device stability in parallel with respectable performance, could start a new venue in the design of organic semiconductors for extremely rugged applications.

In summary, SCP3, a representative of the most recent generation of double-cable polymers, exhibited excellent stability under thermal (up to $1 6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ and light stress, showing great resistance to microstructure instabilities. Our findings suggest that the double-cable design, relying on a covalently linked and therefore inherently fixed microstructure, is a generic way to overcome microstructure induced burn-in degradation. From the transport characterization, no evidence of significant changes in recombination dynamics was shown. The carrier lifetime for SCP3-based solar cells aged under different conditions was similar to that of fresh SCP3-based solar cells. Although the charge carrier lifetime was slightly increased, the slightly enhanced energetic disorder found in aged SCP3-based solar cells does not increase the recombination rate.

Double-cable polymers for SCOsCs have already been sporadically developed during the last two decades, because of their attractive advantages over multiple-component strategy: considerable simplification of device fabrication and a stabilization of the active layer morphology with a suppression of the large phase segregation.26,51] Indeed, the efficiency of SCOsCs is still highly desired to be improved. The reason for the low efficiency lies in: 1) the lack of the conjugated materials (mainly P3HT derivatives) for SCOsCs and the difficulty to synthesize these materials;[52,53] 2) the difficulty to form proper bicontinuous crystalline domains of donors and acceptors due to the chemical-bonded structure, which may cause mixed morphology and result in severe charge recombination and low photocurrent. Encouragingly, great efforts have been committed by many groups to pursue better performance of double-cable polymers.51,54] According to the latest update on NREL efficiency chart, the performance of organic solar cells was improved to a remarkably high level of $1 5 . 6 \%$ [55] With the development of synthesis methods and new material candidates, the further increase of SCOsC efficiency as BHJ solar cells could be envisaged and realized in the near future on a more solid basis. Even more, we believe that the double-cable approach can give valuable insight into the fundamental degradation mechanisms of OsCs in the absence of microstructure related burn-in processes. SCP3 opens novel long time degradation studies under harshest conditions, which are designed to investigate the integrity of interfaces and electrodes as well. In case of further efficiency enhancement, double-cable polymers may return back into the focus of research--almost 20 years after their first reports.
