# Asymmetric II-Bridge Engineering Enables High-Permittivity Benzo[1,2-B:4,5-b']Difuran-Conjugated Polymer for Efficient Organic Solar Cells

Yueyue Gao, Zuo Xiao, Minghuan Cui, Makhsud I. Saidaminov, Furui Tan,\* Luwen Shang, Wanpeng Li, Chaochao Qin,\* and Liming Ding\*

Organic solar cells (OsCs) exhibit complex charge dynamics, which are closely correlated with the dielectric constant (e,) of photovoltaic materials. In this work, a series of novel conjugated copolymers based on benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b'ldifuran (BDF) and benzotriazole $( { \tt B T z } )$ is designed and synthesized, which differ by the nature of $\pmb { \pi }$ -bridge from one another. The PBDF-TF-BTz with asymmetric furan and thiophene $\pmb { \pi }$ -bridge demonstrates a larger $\varepsilon _ { r }$ of 4.22 than PBDF-dT-BTz with symmetric thiophene $\pmb { \pi }$ -bridge (3.15) and PBDF-dF-BTz with symmetric furan $\pmb { \pi }$ -bridge (3.90). The PBDF-TF-BTz also offers more favorable molecular packing and appropriate miscibility with non-fullerene acceptor Y6 than its counterparts. The corresponding PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 OSCs display efficient exciton dissociation, fast charge transport and collection, and reduced charge recombination, eventually leading to a power conversion efficiency of $1 7 . 0 1 \%$ . When introducing a fullerene derivative (PCBO-12) as a third component, the PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6:PCBO-12 OSCs yield a remarkable FF of $8 0 . 1 1 \%$ with a high efficiency of $1 8 . 1 0 \%$ the highest value among all reported BDF-polymer-based OSCs. This work provides an effective approach to developing high-permittivity photovoltaic materials, showcasing PBDF-TF-BTz as a promising polymer donor for constructing high-performance OsCs.

# 1. Introduction

Solution-processable organic solar cells (OsCs) have recently drawn intense attention because they are light in weight, mechanically fexible, and made of earth-abundant elements.[1-3]

Significant efforts in developing active materials, controlling their morphology in film fashion, and innovating device configuration have enabled OsCs to surpass $1 9 \%$ power conversion efficiency (PCE) in single-junction architecture.!471 Nevertheless, this record efficiency of OsCs is still behind other photovoltaic technologies such as perovskite and silicon solar cells.8] One significant cause for the relatively low PCE of OsCs is their unsatisfactory charge dynamics process largely due to the low dielectric constant $( \varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } } )$ of corresponding photovoltaic materials $( \varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } } \approx$ 2-4).[9,10] It is therefore of crucial importance to develop high-permittivity photovoltaic materials to further advance OsCs.

The field has recently been focusing on enhancing the $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ of these materials.[11-14] Cho et al. replaced the alkyl side chain of polymer PIDT-DPP-Alkyl with polar nitrile to obtain polymer PIDT-DPP-CN, then increasing the $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ from 3.5 to 5.[15] The large $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ of PIDT-DPP-CN suppressed nongeminate recombinations in corresponding OSCs and increased their PCE by relatively

$100 \%$ over PIDT-DPP-Alkyl-based devices. Chen et al. introduced oligo(ethylene glycol) to reach a higher $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ of ${ \approx } 5 . 5$ in polymer PDPP3T-O16 than the control polymer PDPP3T-C20 with an alkyl side chain $( \approx 2 )$ [16] As a result, the PDPP3T-O16-based

Despite these excellent achievements, the PCE of OsCs remains low due to the unfavorable active layer morphology, which results from the large discrepancy of the surface energies between acceptor and polymer donors with polar side chains.17,18] Hence, highpermittivity polymer donors without impairing the active layer morphology need to be developed.

Different from the traditional strategy discussed above (i.e., inserting polar side chains into polymer structure), we propose to modify the polymer backbone via engineering the $\pi$ -bridge to enhance their $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ . We synthesized three conjugated copolymers based on benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b']difuran (BDF) and benzotriazole $( \mathrm { B T z } )$ , which differ by their $\pi$ -bridges from one another. The PBDF-TF-BTz with asymmetric furan and thiophene $\pi$ -bridges offers a larger $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ of 4.22 than PBDF-dT-BTz with symmetric thiophene $\pi$ -bridges (3.15) and PBDF-dF-BTz with symmetric furan $\pi$ -bridges (3.90). Besides, PBDF-TF-BTz exhibits more favorable molecular packing and appropriate miscibility with nonfullerene acceptor Y6 than its counterparts. Those superior merits of PBDF-TF-BTz lead to increased exciton dissociation, reduced trap-assisted recombination, and accelerated hole transport of PBDF-TF-BTz-based OSCs, which synergistically produce a PCE of $1 7 . 0 1 \%$ . Incorporation of a fullerene derivative (PCBO12) into PBDF-TF-BTz-based devices imparts an exceptional FF of $8 0 . 1 1 \%$ along with a remarkable efficiency of $1 8 . 1 0 \%$ , which is among the highly performing BDF polymer-based OSCs reported to date.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Materials Synthesis

The synthetic routes of the target copolymers are outlined in Scheme 1 and the detailed procedures are shown in the Supporting Information. Compounds 3 and 4 are readily prepared via Stille coupling reaction by fine-tuning the molar ratio between 4,7-dibromo-5,6-difluoro-2-(2-hexyldecyl)-2Hbenzo[d][1,2,3]triazole (1) and tributyl(furan-2-yl)stannane (2), which are both commercially available. Then, compound 3 is brominated by N-bromosuccinimide (NBS) to form the acceptor unit dF-BTz in $6 1 \%$ yield. The Stille coupling reaction between compound 4 and tributyl(thiophene-2-yl)stannane (5) produces an intermediate 6, which is then brominated by NBS to form an asymmetric acceptor unit TF-BTz in $86 \%$ yield. Finally, the target copolymers are synthesized via the typical Stille copolymerization between the acceptor units (dT-BTz or dF-BTz or TF-BTz) and donor unit (BDFP-T).[19]

# 2.2. Theoretical Properties

We first investigated molecular geometry and wave functions of the frontier molecular orbital of target copolymers by density functional theory (DFT) calculations at $8 3 \mathrm { L Y P } / 6 { - } 3 1 \mathrm { G } ^ { * }$ level. To simplify the computation, we used copolymer dimers with methyl side chains.

As displayed in Figure 1a, the dihedral angles between the BDF unit and $\pi$ bridge of all copolymers are less than $3 ^ { \circ }$ , and the dihedral angles between side chains and the backbone of copolymers are in the range of $1 . 1 { - } 1 . 8 ^ { \circ }$ . The small torsion of the molecular structure of copolymers testifies to their large conjugated plane, which is corroborated by their linear configuration (Figure S18, Supporting Information). The electron density at the HOMO/LUMO energy level of copolymers is similarly delocalized over the whole molecular backbone (Figure 1b,c), which is beneficial for charge transport.[20] The HOMO/LUMO energy level of copolymers is gradually deepened along with the thiophene $\pi$ -bridge being replaced by furan $\pi$ -bridge (Figure 1c), which we attribute to the stronger electronegativity of oxygen atom in furan unit than that of the sulfur atom in thiophene unit.21-23] Interestingly, despite displaying almost identical continuous positive electrostatic surface potential (ESP) of copolymers (Figure 1d), PBDF-TF-BTz presents a larger dipole moment (6.68 debye) than PBDF-dT-BTz (6.02 debye) and PBDF-dF-BTz (6.20 debye). The larger dipole moment of PBDF-TF-BTz can lead to higher $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ which is beneficial for ameliorating the charge dynamics of corresponding OSCs.[24]

# 2.4. Electrochemical Properties

The optical properties of target copolymers (Figure 2a) were studied through UV-vis absorption spectra. As shown in Figure 2c, the three copolymers in chloroform solution display wellresolved absorption profiles, in which the 0-0 and 0-1 peaks are located at $5 8 0 / 5 4 3 \mathrm { n m }$ , 593/552 nm, and $6 0 6 / 5 6 6 \ : \mathrm { n m }$ for PBDFdT-BTz, PBDF-TF-BTz, and PBDF-dF-BTz, respectively. The ratio of the intensity of $0 – 0 / 0 – 1$ peaks are 0.87, 0.89, and 1.02, respectively, demonstrating the enhanced aggregation behavior of copolymers in the order of PBDF-dT-BTz $<$ PBDF-TF-BTz $<$ PBDF-dF-BTz.[25] The absorption profiles of copolymers in the film state show a bathochromic shift compared to those in solution (Figure 2d). We also found that the intensity ratios of $0 -$ $0 / 0 – 1$ peaks are further increased in films, to 0.90, 0.95, and 1.05 for PBDF-dT-BTz, PBDF-TF-BTz, and PBDF-dF-BTz, respectively, which indicates their stronger intermolecular interactions accompanied with their increment of side chain ordering in film state.[26] PBDF-dT-BTz, PBDF-TF-BTz, and PBDF-dF-BTz display large optical bandgap $( E _ { \mathrm { o p t } } )$ of 2.04, 1.90, and $1 . 8 8 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ obtained from the equation, $E _ { \mathrm { o p t } } = \bar { 1 } 2 4 0 / \lambda _ { \mathrm { o n s e t } }$ where $\lambda _ { \mathrm { o n s e t } }$ denotes the absorption onset of copolymers in film state.127,28] Additionally, the three copolymers exhibit complementary light absorption with fullerene derivative PCBO-12 and non-fullerene small molecule Y6 (Figure 2b), which could lead to high photocurrent for derived OSCs.[29-31]

Electrochemical cyclic voltammetry (CV) was performed to determine the energy levels of copolymers following the following equations: i) $E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } } = - e ( V _ { \mathrm { o x } } + 4 . 8 0 - V _ { \mathrm { f e r r o } } )$ and ii) $E _ { \mathrm { L U M O } } =$ $- e ( V _ { \mathrm { r e d } } + 4 . 8 0 - V _ { \mathrm { f e r r o } } )$ where $^ { e }$ $V _ { \mathrm { o x } } / V _ { \mathrm { r e d } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { f e r r o } }$ represent the elementary charge, the oxidation/reduction onset potentials of target copolymers, and the potential of ferrocene as a reference, respectively. As shown in Figure 2e and Figure S19, Supporting Information, the $E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } } / E _ { \mathrm { L U M O } }$ of PBDF-dT-BTz, PBDF-TFBTz, and PBDF-dF-BTz are $- 5 . 5 0 / - 3 . 4 6 \mathrm { e V } , - 5 . 5 2 / - 3 . 6 2 \mathrm { e V } ,$ and $- 5 . 5 7 / - 3 . 6 9 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively, agreeing well with the trend observed in DFT calculations. A cascade energy level alignment among copolymers, PCBO-12 and Y6 is formed, resulting in reduced energy loss of charge transfer.[32] Compared to PBDF-dTBTz, the down-shifted $E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } }$ of PBDF-TF-BTz and PBDF-dF-BTz benefits the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of OSCs.[33]

# 2.6. Photovoltaic Properties

To investigate the photovoltaic performance of the three copolymers, we fabricated OSCs with ITO/PEDOT:PSS/Active layer/PNDIT-F3N/Ag device configuration (Figure 3a), where copolymers serve as donor materials and Y6 acts as an acceptor material in the active layer, respectively. To optimize the photovoltaic performance of devices, fabrication parameters were finely tuned including the concentration of additive, thermal annealing, and solvent annealing. The current density-voltage $\left( J - W \right)$ characteristics of devices are plotted in Figure ${ 3 \mathrm { b } , \mathsf { c } }$ and Figures S21-s29, Supporting Information, and the detailed photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table 1 and Tables S2-S10, Supporting Information.

Optimized PBDF-dT-BTz-based devices show a modest PCE of $1 6 . 0 3 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 8 5 2 \mathrm { V } ,$ $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 5 . 0 1 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and an FF of $7 5 . 2 1 \%$ (Figure 3b). The $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } , J _ { \mathrm { s c } } .$ and FF of PBDF-TF-BTzbased devices increased to 0.857 V, $2 5 . 5 2 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and $7 7 . 7 8 \%$ respectively, leading to a higher PCE of $1 7 . 0 1 \%$ . The enhanced $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of PBDF-TF-BTz-based devices originate from the relatively deep-lying $E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } }$ of PBDF-TF-BTz than that of PBDF-dT-BTz. Considering that PBDF-dT-BTz and PBDF-TF-BTz exhibit similar optical absorption bandwidth as well as molar absorption coefficient values (Figure 2d and Figure S30, Supporting Information), the higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of PBDF-TF-BTz-based devices than PBDFdT-BTz-based ones is ascribed to their improved charge dynamics due to the large value of $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ of PBDF-TF-BTz. Although the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of PBDF-dF-BTz-based devices further increased to $0 . 8 6 3 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for the deepest-lying $E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } }$ of PBDF-dF-BTz, the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF of corresponding devices decreased to $2 4 . 2 7 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and $7 4 . 4 2 \%$ respectively, which yields an inferior PCE of $1 5 . 5 9 \%$ . The low performance of PBDF-dF-BTz-based devices is because of their poor charge dynamics (vide infra).

Given the superior photovoltaic performance of PBDF-TFBTz, ternary devices with PCBO-12 featuring low-lying $E _ { \mathrm { L U M O } }$ and high charge mobility as the third component were constructed.129,30] As displayed in Figure 3c and Table S11, Supporting Information, the PCE of PBDF-TF-BTz-based ternary devices is further enhanced along with the increment of the amount of PCBO-12. When the weight ratio of PBDF-TFBTz:Y6:PCBO-12 is 1:1.2:0.2, the devices produced a champion PCE of $1 8 . 1 0 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of 0.881 V, a $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 5 . 6 4 \mathrm { m A c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , and a remarkable FF of $8 0 . 1 1 \%$ It is noteworthy that the champion PCE of PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6:PCBO-12 devices is not only higher than its counterpart (Figure S31 and Table S12, Supporting Information), but also is among the best result reported for BDF polymer-based OSCs (Figure 3f and Table S13, Supporting Information). The highest FF of the PBDF-TF-BTz-based ternary devices is indicative of their further improved active layer morphology and more proficient charge dynamics in light of the high $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ of PBDF-TF-BTz and PCBO-12 (Figures S32 and S33, Supporting Information).[37]

The derived $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ and electron mobility $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ of PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 devices are $2 . 2 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ and $2 . 4 8 \times$ $1 0 ^ { - 4 } \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \thinspace \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively, featuring a small $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ ratio of 1.09 (Figure 5b and Figure S36, Supporting Information).[44] In contrast, the $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ are $2 . 2 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } / 1 . 8 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and 2.19 $\times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } / 0 . 9 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for PBDF-dT-BTz:Y6 and PBDFdF-BTz:Y6 devices, respectively, featuring large $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ ratio of 1.20 and 2.33. On one hand, the desirable charge transport of PBDF-TFBTz:Y6 devices can facilitate charge collection, which is evidenced by their shortened photocurrent decay time of $1 . 7 5 \mu \mathrm { s }$ (Figure 5c) versus that of PBDF-dT-BTz:Y6 $( 2 . 1 8 ~ \mu \mathrm { s } )$ and PBDF-dF-BTz:Y6 devices $( 4 . 1 1 ~ \mu \mathrm { s } )$ [45] On the other hand, the preferable charge transport of PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 devices could suppress charge recombination, which is corroborated by the larger charge recombination resistance $( R _ { \mathrm { r e c } } )$ of PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 devices $( 1 . 1 9 \times$ $1 0 ^ { 4 } ~ \Omega$ Figure 5a) than that of PBDF-dT-BTz:Y6 $( 1 . 0 1 \times 1 0 ^ { 4 } ~ \Omega )$ and PBDF-dF-BTz:Y6 devices $( 0 . 8 8 \times 1 0 ^ { 4 } \Omega )$ . Besides, PBDFTF-BTz:Y6 devices present prolonged carrier lifetime of $1 . 2 5 ~ \mu \mathrm { s }$ (Figure 5d) in comparison with that of PBDF-dT-BTz:Y6 (0.93 s) and PBDF-dF-BTz:Y6 devices $( 0 . 5 6 \mu \mathrm { s } )$ , agreeing well with the larger $R _ { \mathrm { r e c } }$ of the former and confirming the suppressed charge recombination of PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 devices.

# 2.8. Morphological Properties

To gain a better understanding of the superior charge dynamics of PBDF-TF-BTz-based devices, a series of morphological characterizations were systematically conducted. The optimized PBDFdT-BTz:Y6 film displays homogeneous bulk phase morphology with a small domain size of less than $1 0 \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure 6a). In addition, nanofiber phase morphology with a small root-mean-square (RMS) roughness of $1 . 0 2 \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure 6d,g) is observed for PBDFdT-BTz:Y6 film. The highly uniform morphology of PBDF-dTBTz:Y6 film is attributed to the good miscibility between PBDFdT-BTz and Y6, which is confirmed by their small Flory-Huggins interaction parameter $\chi$ of 0.0064 (Figure S38 and Table S14, Supporting Information).[24]

In comparison, PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 film exhibits increased phase separation with obvious sharp white and black contrast in their bulk morphology (Figure 6b), which results from the slightly reduced miscibility between PBDF-TF-BTz and Y6 for their relatively enhanced Flory-Huggins interaction parameter $\chi$ of 0.0169 (Figure S38 and Table S14, Supporting Information). The increased molecular packing order with closer $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance $( d _ { \pi - \pi } )$ of $3 . 8 0 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ for PBDF-TF-BTz versus that of PBDF-dT-BTz (3.88 A, Figures S39 and S40, Supporting Information) should be the intrinsic origin for the abovementioned phenomenon.42] Moreover, a finely interpenetrating nanofiber network with an enhanced RMS of $1 . 3 5 \ \mathrm { n m }$ could be observed for PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 film (Figure 6e,h). The ideal phase morphology of PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 film facilitates exciton dissociation, and improves charge generation and transport, which is responsible for their superior charge dynamics and higher photovoltaic performance. Meanwhile, the fine morphology of PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 film results in better device stability (Figure S41, Supporting Information).

Comparatively, serious phase separation accompanied by large-scale aggregation could be found in PBDF-dF-BTz:Y6 film (Figure 6c). Meanwhile, the nanofiber clusters with a larger RMS of $1 . 7 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ are observed for PBDF-dF-BTz:Y6 film (Figure 6f,i). The poor morphology of PBDF-dF-BTz:Y6 film should result from the smallest miscibility between PBDF-dF-BTz and Y6 $( \chi =$ 0.04, Figure S38 and Table S14, Supporting Information) which is ascribed to the strongest crystallinity of PBDF-dF-BTz $( d _ { \pi - \pi }$ $= 3 . 7 2 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ , Figures S39 and S40, Supporting Information), and the poor morphology of PBDF-dF-BTz:Y6 film inevitably leads to fewer D/A interfaces, poor charge dynamics and inferior photovoltaic performance for derived OsCs.[49]

Besides, the 2D grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (2D-GIWAXS) was applied to investigate the molecular orientation in the optimized active layer film. As displayed in Figure 6j-l and Figure S42, Supporting Information, PBDF-dTBTz:Y6 film, PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 film, and PBDF-dF-BTz:Y6 film exhibit (100) diffraction peak at $q _ { x \gamma } = 2 . 5 8 4$ , 2.886, and 3.036 $\mathrm { n m } ^ { - 1 }$ in-plane direction, which corresponds to the lamellar distance $( d _ { \mathrm { l a m e l l a r } } )$ of 24.3, 21.8, and $2 0 . { \bar { 7 } } { \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ } }$ respectively, deduced from the equation of $d _ { \mathrm { l a m e l l a r } } = 2 \pi / q _ { x \gamma }$ [50] Meanwhile, those abovementioned optimized films display strong (010) peak at $q _ { z } = 1 7 . 7 2 3$ , 17.936, and $1 8 . 0 0 1 \ \mathrm { n m } ^ { - 1 }$ in out-of-plane direction with $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance of 3.54, 3.50, and $3 . 4 8 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ respectively, revealing the preferential face-on molecular orientation of those optimized films.51] Additionally, the crystal coherence length (CCL) in those optimized films can be calculated according to the Scherrer equation: $\mathrm { C C L } = 2 \pi k / \Delta q$ where $k$ denotes the Scherrer constant of 0.9 and $\Delta q$ is the full width at half maximum of the diffraction peak. The (010) CCL of PBDF-dT-BTz:Y6 film, PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 film, and PBDFdF-BTz:Y6 film is 20.61, 27.20, and $2 1 . 4 0 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ , respectively. The relatively large (010) CCL value of PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 film suggests their more favorable molecular packing order, being responsible for the improved charge dynamics of PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 devices.[25]

# 3. Conclusions

Three novel conjugated copolymers based on BDF and $\mathrm { B T z }$ namely, PBDF-dT-BTz, PBDF-TF-BTz, and PBDF-dF-BTz were developed. PBDF-TF-BTz with asymmetric furan and thiophene $\pi$ -bridges demonstrates larger $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ smaller $E _ { \mathrm { B } }$ , more favorable molecular packing, and appropriate miscibility with nonfullerene acceptor Y6 than its counterparts with symmetric $\pi$ bridges. Benefiting from those superior merits, more efficient exciton dissociation, faster charge transport and collection, and fewer charge recombination are realized for PBDF-TF-BTz:Y6 devices, which deliver an efficiency of $1 7 . 0 1 \%$ . Moreover, PBDFTF-BTz-based devices with PCBO-12 as a third component yield a high efficiency of $1 8 . 1 0 \%$ with a remarkable FF of $8 0 . 1 1 \%$ among the best results reported for BDF polymer-based OSCs. Our work provides an effective approach to developing highpermittivity polymer donor materials without sacrificing their miscibility with high-performance non-fullerene acceptor materials, which is highly potential for constructing efficient OSCs.
