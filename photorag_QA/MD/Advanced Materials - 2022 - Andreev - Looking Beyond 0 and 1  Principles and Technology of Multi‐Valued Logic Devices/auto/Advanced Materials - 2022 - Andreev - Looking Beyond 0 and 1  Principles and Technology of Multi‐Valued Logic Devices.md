# Looking Beyond 0 and 1: Principles and Technology of Multi-Valued Logic Devices

Maksim Andreev, Seunghwan Seo, Kil-Su Jung, and Jin-Hong Park\*

Ever since the invention of solid-state transistors, binary devices have dominated the electronics industry. Although the binary technology links the natural property of devices to be in the ON or OFF state with two logic levels, it provides the least possible information content per interconnect. Multi-valued logic (MvL) has long been considered as a means of improving the computation efficiency and reducing the power consumption of modern chips. In view of the power density limits of the conventional complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor technology, MVL technologies have recently gained even more attention, and various MVL unit devices based on conventional and emerging materials have been proposed. Herein, the recent achievements toward the development of compact MVL unit devices are reviewed. First, basic principles of MVL technologies are introduced by describing methods of obtaining multiple logic states and discussing radixrelated aspects of MVL computation. Next, MVL unit devices are classified and overviewed with emphasis on principles of operation, technologies, and applications. Finally, a comparative discussion of strengths and weaknesses is provided for each class of MVL devices, and the review concludes with the outlook for the MVL field.

# 1. Introduction

Modern very-large-scale integration technology relies entirely on classic binary logic and Boolean algebra with the complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOs) transistor as a core unit device. CmOs scaling has provided a cost-effective development route for the electronics industry and enabled unprecedented bit density and performance that have met consumer demands for several decades.1l In contrast to CMOs devices, interconnects do not benefit from scaling down and were eventually recognized as a "real limiter to high performance."[2] Specifically, increased wiring density, increased total interconnect length, and reduced interconnect dimensions have led to larger parasitic capacitances and resistances, leading to longer signal delays and higher power consumption. The problem of interconnects has become more severe with each next technological node; in fact, the interconnects of modern chips consume over $7 0 \%$ of the total dynamic power and occupy a significant portion of chip area.3] Furthermore, although the current binary technology reflects the natural property of devices to be in the ON or OFF state,[4] it provides the least possible information content per interconnect.[5]

The number of interconnects and logic gates can be sig. nificantly reduced if conventional binary logic is replaced with ternary or higher-radix logics, generally referred to as multior multiple- or many-valued logics (all abbreviated as MVL). MVLs enable higher information density and, therefore, logic functions can be performed with fewer logic gates and interconnects. It solves the issues of enormous wiring length and density and consequently reduces chip power consumption and RC delays. For example, transitions from binary logic to ternary, quaternary, and quinary logic are estimated to decrease the system complexity to $6 3 . 1 \%$ $5 0 \%$ , and $4 3 . 1 \%$ of its original level, respectively.[5] The earliest theoretical works that built a foundation for multi-valued algebras were published in the 1920s,[6,7] followed by the first works related to nonelectronic ternary systems in the 1960s.[8,9] However, the rapid and successful development of binary solid-state electronic devices, such as bipolar junction transistors and metal-oxide-- semiconductor field-effect transistors (MOSFETs), established the binary approach as dominant and shadowed the development of MVL technologies. Although various MVL circuits based on the binary CMOS technology were shown as proofof-concept MVL systems, their realizations were not compact and required a large number of CMOS devices, which negated the key advantages of the MVL concept. In 1988, Hurst summarized that "a truly multiple-valued switching device, comparable in size and switching efficiency to today's bipolar and MOs transistors" was needed for switching from binary to higher-radix digital systems.[10] Despite this conclusion was stated more than 30 years ago, the search for conceptually new compact unit devices suitable for MVL systems is still ongoing (Figure 1).

Lately, the advancements in synthesis and technologies of advanced materials have driven a significant progress in the MVL field. Indeed, a vast library of emerging materials, including van der Waals (vdW), organic, oxide materials, and others has significantly expanded the capabilities in device engineering enabling smart selection of materials with desired optical and electronic properties.[11,12] In view of the recent advancements in the MVL field, several attempts have been made to systemize the new data: Yoo and Kim provided a compact overview of representative examples of MVL systems with a strong emphasis on materials,[13] and Jo et al. presented a material perspective on the MVL gates.[14] However, there are no comprehensive reviews that classify compact MVL unit devices by operating principles and provide a comparative discussion of strengths and weaknesses for each class of MVL devices. Here, we review the achievements toward the development of compact MVL unit devices for logic and memory applications. We classify and overview MVL devices with focus on operating principles, technologies, and MVL applications to identify advantages and limitations of each class of MVL devices for a fair comparative assessment. Finally, we evaluate the current state of MVL technologies and conclude with the outlook for the MVL field.

# 2. Basics of MVL Technology

# 2.1. Optimal Radix for MVL Computation

The conventional binary logic uses two logic levels typically denoted as 0 and 1 (or false and true). Since two digits are used to represent data, it is said that the radix (R) of such systems is two. Logics with $R > 2$ , where $R$ takes integer values, are considered multi-valued. Hurst analyzed the correlation between the system complexity (cost) and $R$ and estimated the optimal $R$ as $e = 2 . 7 1 8$ , which becomes 3 when rounded to the nearest integer.l Each next increase of $R$ yields smaller complexity reduction and as a rule introduces additional sophistication into design of MVL devices; therefore, many researchers target ternary systems as those with the optimum trade-off between the complexity reduction and device sophistication. On the contrary, some believe that the adoption of MVL systems may start from the integration of MVL computing modules into binary chips to replace their most critical parts. In this scenario, efficient communication between binary and MVL modules within the same chip will be prioritized. In this regard, quaternary systems with $R = 4$ have advantages over ternary systems in terms of simpler signal conversion from or to binary systems, since $R = 4$ can be represented as a power of 2.24,25] As for higherradix systems $( R > 4 )$ , practical and theoretical works are rare due to the unavailability of appropriate technologies and complexity of algebraic problems. Indeed, the number of possible logic functions in MVLs dramatically increases a double exponential function of $R$ and hence the algebraic aspects become increasingly complex.[26,27] At present, there are no generally accepted algebras for MVLs. Proposed MVL algebras and corresponding universal sets of logic gates were summarized by Miller and Thornton,27] and additional considerations were described by Hurst.] Nevertheless, certain logic gates such as NOT, NAND, and NOR are common in many MVL algebras and therefore often considered basic and demonstrated as applications of proposed MVL unit devices.

# 2.2. Realization of Multiple Logic States

In this section, we introduce major phenomena used in the design of MVL devices, demonstrate how these phenomena enable multiple logic states, and set a terminology for the following discussions. Negative differential transconductance (NDT) is a phenomenon of decreasing drain current $\left( I _ { \mathrm { D } } \right)$ during the gate voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { G } } )$ sweep in three-terminal devices (Figure 2a). NDT devices connected with a load in a voltage divider configuration can be used to realize multiple logic states (Figure 2b). When the input gate voltage $\mathrm { \Delta } V _ { \mathrm { I N } }$ or $V _ { \mathrm { G } } )$ is low, the conductance of the NDT device is significantly lower than that of the load $G _ { \mathrm { N D T } } \ll G _ { \mathrm { L } } )$ , hence the supply voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { D D } } )$

drops across the NDT device and the output voltage is high $( V _ { \mathrm { O U T } } = \ : V _ { \mathrm { D D } }$ , Logic 2), and vice versa; when $V _ { \mathrm { I N } }$ is high, then $G _ { \mathrm { N D T } } \gg G _ { \mathrm { L } }$ $V _ { \mathrm { O U T } } = 0$ , Logic 0). In the NDT region, the differential transconductances $\mathit { \Omega } ( \mathrm { g } _ { \mathrm { m } } \ = \ d I _ { \mathrm { D } } / d V _ { \mathrm { G } } )$ of the NDT device and load are matched and also $G _ { \mathrm { N D T } } \approx G _ { \mathrm { L } } ;$ therefore, $V _ { \mathrm { D D } }$ is equally divided between two devices $( V _ { \mathrm { O U T } } = ~ V _ { \mathrm { D D } } / 2 )$ within the NDT range forming an additional logic state (Logic 1). This approach will be referred to as ${ } ^ { \ast } \mathrm { g } _ { \mathrm { m } }$ matching' throughout the manuscript. Thus, three logic states are formed, which corresponds to the operation of a standard ternary inverter (STI). Transfer $I { - } V$ characteristics of NDT devices come in various shapes: $N$ (Figure 2b),[20,28,29] w,[30-32] A (Figure 2c),33,34] etc. Regardless the shape, the principle of $\mathrm { { \dot { g } _ { m } } }$ matching can be applied in a similar manner to realize multiple logic states. We note that there is no agreed-upon notation system for logic states in MVL circuits and different notations such as $\{ 0 , 1 , 2 \}$ and {0,1/2,1} have been used in the case of ternary logic gates.[28,29]

By analogy with the NDT, we will use the term "zero differential transconductance" (ZDT) to refer to a phenomenon of constant or nearly constant $I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ during the $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ sweep in threeterminal devices, i.e., $g _ { \mathrm { m } } \approx 0$ (Figure 2d). ZDT devices can also be used to realize multiple logic states by $g _ { \mathrm { m } }$ matching, e.g., by using $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ independent resistive loads (Figure 2e).[21,23,35] Another approach suggests utilizations of two complementary devices with n- and p-type transfer $I { - } V$ curves, where $g _ { \mathrm { m } }$ matching occurs in the off-current regions (Figure 2f).22] By analogy with the NDT devices, three logic states are formed corresponding to $V _ { \mathrm { O U T } } = 0$ $V _ { \mathrm { D D } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { D D } } / 2$ . ZDT devices share many similarities with NDT devices since the key feature enabling multiple logic states is in their transfer $I { - } V$ characteristics $( \mathrm { g } _ { \mathrm { m } } < 0$ or $g _ { \mathrm { m } } \approx 0 )$ ; however, the physical mechanisms inducing ZDT phenomena are quite distinct, as it will be shown later.

Negative differential resistance (NDR) is a phenomenon of decreasing drain current $\left( I _ { \mathrm { D } } \right)$ during the drain voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { D } } )$ sweep (Figure 2g). Multiple states can be realized using NDR devices in a form multiple quiescent (operating) points (QPs), as shown with the load line analysis of the voltage divider in Figure 2h. The QPs in the positive differential resistance regions can be used directly for multi-valued memory applications.l36-38] More QPs can be realized if another NDR device is also used as a load.39] For detailed information on the load strategies, see this work.[40] As for logic applications, the use of QPs is less straightforward. For example, the monostable-to-multistable transition logic (MML) was developed and used to implement MVL gates using NDR devices.[41] Another type of MVL inverters composed of an NDR device and a $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ -dependent load has been recently investigated (Figure 2i).19,424) In this configuration, the load line can intersect the NDR region at some intermediate $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ values, which results in the QP shift in the opposite direction during the $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ $\displaystyle V _ { \mathrm { I N } } )$ sweep and the appearance of an additional logic state (Logic 1).

# 3.1. NDT Devices

The NDT phenomenon has been known for a long time and observed in various structures.45-52] In 2016, Nourbakhsh et al. first applied a heterojunction NDT device for ternary logic.[28] The NDT device channel was composed of two thin ${ \tt W S e } _ { 2 }$ and ${ \mathrm { M o S } } _ { 2 }$ flakes exfoliated from their crystals and mechanically transferred in a way that the $\mathrm { { W S e } } _ { 2 }$ flake was partially overlapped with the ${ \mathrm { M o S } } _ { 2 }$ flake (Figure 3a). The energy band alignment between ${ \mathrm { M o S } } _ { 2 }$ and $\mathrm { W S e } _ { 2 }$ in the overlapped region caused electron accumulation in ${ \mathrm { M o S } } _ { 2 }$ and hole accumulation in $\mathrm { W S e } _ { 2 }$ which effectively created a $\mathfrak { p } ^ { + } / \mathfrak { p } ^ { - }$ multicharged density profile in ${ \tt W S e } _ { 2 }$ and induced the multi- $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ transfer I-V curve ("Hetero-FET" in Figure 3b). A part of the same ${ \tt W S e } _ { 2 }$ fake with a matched transfer $I { - } V$ curve was used as a load to implement an STI, as shown in Figure 3b; the STI exhibited a remarkably narrow operational voltage range in its voltage transfer characteristic (VTC) owing to the high. $\kappa \ \mathrm { H f @ } _ { 2 }$ gate dielectric (Figure 3c). This load strategy when the same material is used as the load and as a part of the NDT device channel has been further adopted in many works (Table 1) because it can provide naturally matched $g _ { \mathrm { m } }$ between the NDT device and the load (Section 2.2).

NDT devices have also been employed in multifunctional logic gates that can be reconfigured for ternary or binary logic.20,53,54] For example, Huang et al. showed a logic gate based on a ${ \mathrm { B P } } / { \mathrm { M o S } } _ { 2 }$ heterojunction NDT device (Figure 3d), where the selection between binary and ternary inverter functions was controlled by $V _ { \mathrm { D D } }$ [20] Since the ambipolar BP and n-type ${ \mathrm { M o S } } _ { 2 }$ were arranged in series, $I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ was dominated by the channel part with the highest resistance (lowest $G$ ) during the $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ sweep. Thus, different channel regions (BP or $\mathrm { M o S } _ { 2 } )$ dominated $I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ during the $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ sweep, resulting in the N-shaped multi- $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ I-V curve ("HJFET" in Figure 3e). The middle logic obtained by $g _ { \mathrm { m } }$ matching between the HJFET and BP FET (Figure 3e, right panel) vanished with decreasing $V _ { \mathrm { D D } }$ due to the disappearance of the region of matched $g _ { \mathrm { m } }$ at lower $V _ { \mathrm { D D } }$ values (Figure 3e).

A potential route for large-scale fabrication was suggested by Yoo et al. on the basis of organic materials. The authors demonstrated a heterojunction NDT device consisting of organic p-type dinaphtho $[ 2 , 3 \mathrm { - b } ; 2 ^ { \prime } , 3 ^ { \prime }$ -f]thieno[3,2-b]thiophene (DNTT) and n-type $N , N ^ { \prime }$ -ditridecylperylenediimide (PTCDI-C13) fabricated by thermal evaporation.[29] Only the DNTT layer was in direct contact with the electrodes, whereas the PTCDI-C13 layer was partially overlapped with the DNTT layer (Figure 3g). An array of 36 NDT devices with a good yield and uniformity of electrical parameters was demonstrated (Figure 3h). When a negative gate-to-source voltage was applied to the n-type/ptype PTCDI-C13/DNTT stack and a positive gate-to-drain voltage was applied to the p-type DNTT side, charges of opposite polarities were accumulated at different electrodes giving rise to an additional band-to-band tunneling (BTBT) current between the DNTT and PTCDI-C13 layers through the lateral edge junction. At certain gate-to-source voltage values approaching the drain-to-source voltage, this additional BTBT current no longer contributed to $I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ resulting in the $I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ drop (NDT region, Figure 3i). Notably, the STI based on this NDT device exhibited a full-swing operation, while a hydrophobic poly-(perfluorobutenylvinylether) gate dielectric and encapsulating Parylene-C layers provided hysteresis-free performance.

Homojunctions have also been used to demonstrate NDT phenomena and MVL applications. To design homojunction MVL devices, materials with ambipolar conductance such as graphene, $\mathrm { W S e } _ { 2 } ,$ or $\mathrm { M o T e } _ { 2 }$ are typically used as channels, and then a part of the channel is doped or treated by various means to induce a $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ change and the corresponding W-shaped transfer $I { - } V$ curve.30-32,55-57] For example, Kim et al. demonstrated an NDT device based on a graphene/organic dye hybrid structure (Figure 4a).[30] A part of the graphene channel was covered with photosensitive organic dye molecules (rhodamine 6G) to realize optical gating and induce a p-n junction leading to multi- $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ transfer I-V curve (Figure 4b, black curve). By utilizing the doped n-type region of the graphene channel as a load, an STI was implemented (Figure 4c). Besides, multi$V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ transfer $I { - } V$ characteristics and corresponding MVL applications have been reported as a result of partial chemical doping,[31] ferroelectric doping,[58] doping by deposition of metal strips on graphene,[32] partial electrical gating,[57] and gate dielectric engineering.55,56]

Various MVL-related functionalities have been revealed through light-matter interactions. It has been shown that the light illumination can be used to induce,[30,59,60] tune the NDT phenomena,[54,57,59,61] or realize the switching function between binary and ternary regimes of operation.19,62,63] For example, Shim et al. showed a graphene $/ \mathsf { W S e } _ { 2 }$ heterojunction NDT device, where light could modulate the variable potential barrier at the graphene. $\mathrm { \sf W S e } _ { 2 }$ interface resulting in the NDT phenomenon (Figure 4d).[59] The authors demonstrated the STI operation (Figure 4e,f) and light-induced switching between the binary and ternary regimes of operation. The opposite effect was revealed by Duong et al. in an STI based on the $\mathrm { M o S } _ { 2 } /$ $\mathrm { M o T e } _ { 2 }$ heterojunction NDT device, where the light illumination forced the transition from the ternary to binary inverter regime of operation.[62]

There are very recent advancements in the development of flexible MVL devices.[60,67,68] Paul Inbaraj et al. demonstrated a $\mathrm { I n S e } / \mathrm { W S e } _ { 2 }$ heterojunction NDT device on a flexible polyethylene terephthalate (PET) substrate (Figure 4g).[60] Interestingly, the applied strain supported by laser illumination could tune the number and positions of current peaks in transfer $I { - } V$ curves (Figure 4h). In another work, Panigrahi et al. investigated the effect of strain in flexible organic NDT devices composed of $\mathrm { P h C } _ { 2 } \mathrm { H } _ { 4 }$ benzo[de]isoquinolino[1,8-gh]quinolone diimide (PhC2-BQQDI) and 2,7-dioctyl[1]benzothieno[3,2-b][1] benzothiophene (C8-BTBT).[67] The fabricated STIs tolerated tensile strains up to $1 . 2 5 \%$ and remained stable after multiple bending cycles (Figure 4i).

Discussion: NDT devices have become the most widely explored class of MVL devices coming in a variety of structures and shapes of transfer $I { - } V$ characteristics (Table 1). Many STIs based on NDT devices benefit from a relatively simple load strategy, because part of the transistor channel with readily matched $g _ { \mathrm { m } }$ can be used as a load. MVL applications based on NDT devices other than STIs are rare and most of the higher-radix MVL inverters have been demonstrated via simulations. Although, NDT devices have mostly been used for logic applications, there are examples when NDT characteristics were transformed into NDR characteristics via correlated gate and drain biasing to induce a latching function for memory applications.[69,70] Only few array-level demonstrations of NDT devices are currently available, and more complex MVL gates remain to be explored. This situation is partly due to the fact that NDT devices have mostly been based on vdW and organic materials which are convenient for realizing various types of heterojunctions under laboratory conditions, but do not always have sufficiently mature technologies to fabricate complex MVL gates and enable wafer-scale demonstrations. Another challenge is the instability of the middle logic, which is extremely sensitive to any mismatch between $G$ and $g _ { \mathrm { m } }$ . It has resulted in sizable variations of the $V _ { \mathrm { O U T } }$ in many works; however, this issue can be resolved if a mature fabrication process is developed and applied. The concept of NDT devices does not impose strict limitations on materials, and if high-quality hetero- or homojunctions can be formed, other materials can be utilized in NDT devices. Notably, several works provided a generalized theoretical background for organic antiambipolar NDT devices with A-shaped transfer $I { - } V$ curves, which can play an important role for a further development of MVL technologies.[71,72]

# 3.2. Ternary CMOS (T-CMOs) Devices

In contrast to the other MVL technologies based on the $g _ { \mathrm { m } }$ matching, T-cMos employs complementary single- $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ devices to realize multiple logic states. The transfer $I { - } V$ curves of T-CMOs devices are typically matched in the off-current region (Figure 2f). Unlike conventional CMOs devices, transfer I-V curves of T-CMOs devices consist of a constant $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ -independent off-current region and a $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ -dependent exponential current region. The $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ independent off-current region was implemented as the BTBT current by introducing highly doped body-to-drain tunnel junctions into both p- and n-type T-CMOS devices.22,80,81] The structure, energy band diagrams, and circuit configuration of the T-CMOS-based STI are shown in Figure 5a. The wafer-scale T-CMOS was processed with commercial 130 and $9 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ CMOs foundries on 8 in. wafers (Figure 5b). The processing compatibility of the T-CMOS technology with the conventional CMOs technology enabled integration with CMOS through the front-end-of-line process. The left panel of Figure 5c compares transfer $I { - } V$ characteristics of conventional CMOS and T-CMOS complementary devices at various drainto-source voltages $( V _ { \mathrm { D S } } )$ and demonstrate the matched transfer $I { - } V$ characteristics of T-CMOS devices in the off-current region, which enables the STI operation (Figure 5c, right panel). Along with STI operation, the authors showed the basic functions of ternary logic and memory systems, such as ternary quantization in two-stage T-CMOS inverters and memory latching based on cross-coupled T-CMOS inverters.

Ko et al. further explored the potential of the T-CMOs technology for the implementation of various logic nodes using only T-CMOS devices.[2] By performing T-CAD simulations, the authors demonstrated the operation of 22 ternary logic cells and proposed the design of a balanced ternary adder with a substantial decrease in the device count. Kim et al. addressed the $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ fuctuation issue originating from high-dose ion implantation used to form a BTBT layer.22,83] They proposed a vertically stacked nanosheet gate-all-around (GAA) FET as a T-CMOS device to suppress the interference effects between the BTBT dopants and the channel and to enhance the electrostatic gate controllability (Figure 5d). Kim and Kwon proposed a silicongermanium (SiGe) vertical TFET-based T-CMOS device with a vertical fin structure (Figure 5e).[84] In contrast to previous T-CMOS devices, the BTBT mechanism was responsible not only for the intermediate state, but for all three logic states, which is expected to provide a lower $V _ { \mathrm { D D } }$ and steeper subthreshold swing than other devices utilizing thermionic emission as a carrier injection mechanism. Gupta and Saurabh proposed a SiGe-based TFET with dual pockets where the $V _ { \mathrm { G } } .$ independent tunneling current responsible for the middle logic occurred within the channel (Figure 5f).[85] The STI operated at $V _ { \mathrm { D D } } = 0 . 8 \mathrm { \ V } ,$ and this value could be further reduced by controlling pocket doping concentrations. Kim et al. showed that oncurrent regions also can produce the intermediate logic state, which can lead to reduced $V _ { \mathrm { D D } }$ [86] They demonstrated SiGe vertical TFET operating at $V _ { \mathrm { D D } } < 0 . 6 ~ \mathrm { V } _ { \mathrm { i } }$ which had a gate underlap and additional $\mathrm { n ^ { + } }$ pocket for tunneling current enhancement.

Owing to the compatibility with conventional CMOs technologies, T-CMOS readily benefits from mature CMOS foundry technologies enabling wafer-scale demonstrations and studies of parameter variations that are currently unavailable for most of the discussed MVL concepts. # 3.3. Devices with Embedded Quantum Dots (QDs)

Materials in the form of QDs experience quantum confinement effects leading to quantization of their energy levels. When these quantized electronic systems are embedded into conventional FETs, resonant interactions between QDs and other materials can lead to current saturation effects useful for the implementation of multi- $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ MVL devices. Lee et al. revealed quantum-confined transport in ${ \mathrm { Z n O } }$ composite nanolayers, which represent heterogenous electronic systems of $\mathrm { Z n O }$ QDs embedded in amorphous ${ \mathrm { Z n O } }$ (a-ZnO).[23,87,88] They found a resonant energy matching between the localized states of a-ZnO and the quantized discrete states of ${ \mathrm { Z n O } }$ QDs which lead to generation of conducting states with a low density of states (DOS) below the mobility edge of $\mathsf { a } { - } \mathsf { Z n O }$ (Figure 6a). The low-DOS conducting states naturally limit the current and result in the current saturation effect. The proposed MVL devices were based on a hybrid organic-inorganic superlattice composed of alternating $\mathrm { Z n O }$ composite nanolayers (channels) and organic layers of Al4MP (4-mercaptophenol molecular layers with Al linkers) separating the $\mathrm { Z n O }$ channels (Figure 6b). The superlattice was fabricated by repeating two deposition techniques: molecular layer deposition for the Al4mP layers and atomic layer deposition for the $\mathrm { Z n O }$ nanolayers. The transfer $I { - } V$ characteristic of the ternary transistor has three distinct regions (Figure 6c). In Region I, electrons are injected from the source into the first ${ \mathrm { Z n O } }$ channel (first $I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ increase). However, due to the low finite DOs of the conducting states, the charge accumulation in the first ${ \mathrm { Z n O } }$ channel is limited, which results in the current saturation in Region II. At higher gate biases, the electric field from the gate penetrates through the first $\mathrm { Z n O }$ channel making the second $\mathrm { Z n O }$ channel conductive (Region III; second $I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ increase). The proposed technology enabled wafer-scale fabrication of 284 ternary MVL devices on a 4 in. Si wafer with good uniformity of transfer $I { - } V$ characteristics (Figure 6d). By using a resistive load, an STI (Figure 6e), NMIN, and NMAX gates were demonstrated. Furthermore, a transfer $I { - } V$ curve with two saturation current regions suitable for quaternary logic was obtained by increasing the number of ${ \mathrm { Z n O } }$ channels.

Embedment of QDs into the gate regions of transistors was also proposed for producing multiple logic states. An FET with $\mathrm { S i O } _ { x } .$ cladded Si QDs embedded into the gate region (QDGFET, Figure 6g) showed an intermediate current saturation region in the transfer $I { - } V$ characteristic (Figure 6h).[17,89] The QDGFET was processed via the conventional CMOS process followed by the deposition of $\mathrm { S i O } _ { x } .$ cladded Si QDs with a site-specific selfassembly method. During the $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ sweep, the charge carriers could move from the inversion channel into the QDs in the gate dielectric region by resonant tunneling. It caused a continuous increase of $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ making the drain current $I _ { \mathrm { D } } \propto \left( V _ { \mathrm { G } } - V _ { \mathrm { T H } } \right)$ effectively constant within a range of $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ . An STI was demonstrated by connecting complementary QDGFETs of n- and p-type in series in inverter configuration (Figure 6i). Various MVL gates and circuits were further demonstrated through simulations including NTI, PTI, NOR, NAND gates, multivalued SRAM cell,1,90] and more complex circuits such as a ternary multiplier, ternary half-adder, and ternary full-adder.

Discussion: Both concepts described in this section revealed current saturation mechanisms based on resonant-type interactions involving QDs. Such ZDT devices benefit from simple resistive loads for implementation of ternary and quaternary MVL gates. The use of $\mathrm { Z n O }$ nanolayers as transistor channels generates nearly ideal ZDT regions in contrast to other types of ZDT devices, since the current is naturally limited by the low DOs. The proposed technology has already enabled waferscale fabrication, however because costly and time-consuming molecular and atomic layer deposition techniques are used to grow the fine multilayer structures of ZnO/Al4MP, further development of this technology may meet difficulties analogous to those of molecular-beam epitaxy (MBE)-grown RT devices in the past (see discussion in Section 5). Thus, expansion of the library of materials with low-DOS conducting states and simplification of the fabrication process are desirable for future development. Meanwhile, a strong side of the QDGFET is the availability of circuit models, which allowed extensive analysis of complex MVL gates via simulations. The QDGFET technology partly benefits from mature CMOS processing but still needs a reliable method for QD integration into the gate dielectric region. Despite the numerous simulation works showing diverse MVL gates, practical research of the proposed mechanism and wafer-scale demonstrations can further confirm the viability of this approach.

Another class of recently emerged MVL devices makes use of channels composed of semiconductor materials of the same conductance types electrically arranged in series or in parallel. Such devices can have multi- $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ transfer $I { - } V$ characteristics with intermediate ZDT regions.[21,35,91] For example, Lim et al. demonstrated a multistate heterojunction transistor (MOSSFET) with the channel composed of two n-type semiconductors, $N , N ^ { \prime }$ dioctyl-3,4,9,10-perylenedicarboximide(PTCDI$\mathrm { C } _ { 8 } )$ and solution-processed indium-gallium-zinc oxide (IGZO; Figure 7a).[21] The IGZO layer was deposited over the $\mathrm { S i } / \mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ substrate by spin coating followed by thermal deposition of the PTCDI- $C _ { 8 }$ interlayer. For this class of devices, it is important to suppress the saturation current in the intermediate ZDT region to reduce $V _ { \mathrm { O U T } }$ variations of the middle logic state; however, achieving full current saturation could be problematic given that $I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ tends to continuously increase under increasing $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ even in saturation. The concepts described in this section do not impose strict limitations on material choice and will rather be based on the type of conductance, the value of field-effect conductance in the saturation regime, and $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ of composing materials. # 3.5. Single-Electron Transistors (SETs)

In 1986, Averin and Likharev proposed the concept of SETs based on the Coulomb blockade effect,[92] realized experimentally later by Fulton and Dolan.[93] A typical SET is a threeterminal device that consists of a nanoscale island with the discrete energy spectrum, source and drain electrodes separated from the island with tunnel barriers, and a gate electrode (Figure 8a). SETs can uniquely generate multi- $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ transfer I-V characteristics with periodic current oscillations corresponding to tunnel events of single electrons to the island (Figure 8b),[94] which can be linked with multiple logic levels. In a typical SET, the $I { - } V$ curve oscillates between two current levels, however Bitton et al. have recently shown that multiple well distinguished current levels can be achieved between peaks and valleys in the case of the strong coupling between the island and source/drain leads; this can further ease the implementation of MVL functions.[95]

One of the first practical examples of SETs for MVL applications was enabled by pattern-dependent oxidation technology (PADOX), in which a 1D Si wire is transformed into a nanoscale Si island with tunnel barriers at its ends.18] Takahashi et al. proposed a concept of multigate SETs and, using PADOX, fabricated a two-gate SET (Figure 8c). Two independent gates served as inputs, and the two-gate SET alone could perform the binary XOR function (Figure 8d). Taking the XOR gate as a unit device, a mod-sum circuit for MVLs of any radices and a quaternary multivalued multiplexer (T-gate) were proposed. Although a few more works suggested and simulated MVL gates composed of only SETs,96,97] such SET-only solutions suffered from practical constraints including very low applicable drain voltages and a heavy dependence of $I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ on the drain voltage.[98] To overcome these issues, Inokawa et al. suggested hybrid elements combining SETs and conventional FETs.[98,99] For example, a hybrid MOSFET-SET device (Figure 8e) showed the oscillating inputoutput characteristic, enabling a literal function essential for MVL applications.[98] Such hybrid MOSFET-SET devices could be used as NDR devices with multiple NDR regions, if the periodic three-terminal transfer I-V characteristic is converted into a periodic two-terminal output I-V characteristic.98-100] For example, by connecting the SET gate and MOSFET drain terminals, an NDR device with multiple NDR regions was developed and applied for a multivalued memory with six states.[100]

Degawa et al. proposed a family of MVL gates composed of SETs and MOsFETs,[101] experimentally investigated in followup works.102,103] For example, Kim et al. implemented logic gates transforming multi-valued inputs into a binary output using hybrid MOSFET-SET devices.[103] The Si-based SET formed by PADOX had two symmetrical sidewall gates serving as inputs (Figure 8f). The output voltage plotted as a function of two input gate voltages alternated between low and high values in the diagonal direction, corresponding to the conduction and Coulomb blockage regimes of the SET, respectively (Figure 8g). This plot was used to select the input voltages enabling desired MVL functions, such as the XOR gate with two ternary inputs and one binary output.

Room-temperature operation is considered one of the most critical challenges for SETs,[104] and most of the discussed SETs showed current oscillations only under very low temperatures. Lee et al. proposed a Si SET operating at room temperature. It was enabled by utilizing Si ellipsoidal QD systems (EQD) with large spacings between quantum levels.105] The Si EQD SET was self-formed by using subsequent isotropic wet etching and thermal oxidation of the Si nanowire. The tunnel barriers were implemented as very thin areas (much less than $5 \mathrm { n m }$ ) adjacent to the central Si ellipsoid (Figure 8h). A typical transfer $I { - } V$ characteristic of the Si EQD SET is a two-peak curve (Figure 8i). By plotting the contour plot of $I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ as a function of $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { D S } }$ (Figure 8j), ternary and quaternary operational regimes could be selected.

Discussion: SETs are unique and naturally multi- $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ devices promising for future MVL applications. Currently, the practical development of SETs remains in its infancy because unrealistic conditions are often required for correct and reliable operation. For example, since SETs deal with single electrons, their operation is extremely sensitive to random background charges and environmental conditions. Furthermore, achieving stable room-temperature operation remains a challenge and typically requires the island size to be less than $5 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , which imposes serious constraints on the available fabrication methods. Experimental SETs for MVL applications have been mostly based on Si due to the availability of reproducible fabrication methods and have often been implemented in combination with conventional FETs to reduce operational instabilities. Despite all the challenges, SETs are being actively developed and remain a promising MVL technology; for instance, decent performance has recently been exhibited by SETs based on mixed-dimensional ${ \sf A l } / { \sf M o S } _ { 2 }$ heterostructures,[106] nanochains of Ge nanoparticles,[107] Au nanoparticles,[95] ellipsoidal Si single QDs,[108] etc.

Choi et al. demonstrated a type III transitionmetal dichalcogenide (TMDC)/organic heterojunction based on $\mathrm { M o T e } _ { 2 }$ and dipyrazino[2,3-f:2' $3 ^ { \prime }$ -h|quinoxaline-2,3,6,7,10,11-hexacarbonitrile (HAT-CN, Figure 9i).[44] The HAT-CN molecules were thermally evaporated on the $\mathrm { M o T e } _ { 2 }$ surface forming a vdW interface. High PVCR of 137 and 4.48 were obtained at room temperature with and without applied bottom $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ respectively (Figure 9j). This device was connected to a $\mathrm { M o T e } _ { 2 }$ FET load to implement a multilevel inverter with three well-distinguished logic states and confirmed dynamic behavior (Figure 9k).

Discussion: Esaki diodes and TFETs have shown their conceptual feasibility for basic MVL applications. These devices have sufficiently high PVCRs, stable room-temperature operation, and the possibility to implement single- and multipeak output I-V curves. The interface quality is crucial for tunneling in Esaki diode-based devices. Therefore, III-V compound semiconductors grown by MBE and, more recently, vdW materials, both of which can provide high-quality interfaces, have primarily been used in MVL devices. Further exploration of various material pairs and development of scalable fabrication methods is desirable. As only multipeak I-V output characteristics were considered suitable for MVL applications for a long time, the demonstration of STIs using NDR devices with single-peak output $I { - } V$ characteristics is a major accomplishment confirmed in several works. This advance can significantly expand the library of available materials for MVL applications because the available Esaki diodes and TFETs with single-peak output characteristics significantly outnumber those with multipeak output characteristics and as a rule have simpler strictures. Thus, it is important to explore the limitations and applicability of this approach for more complex MVL gates.

# 4.2. Resonant-Tunneling (RT) Devices

RT and the related NDR phenomenon were theoretically pre dicted by Tsu and Esaki in 1973 and first experimentally demonstrated in a double-barrier structure (DBs) by Chang et al. in 1974.11,116] The extremely fast switching speed and output characteristics with multiple NDR regions made RT devices promising candidates for MVL applications.[117] The nature of RT requires a precise alignment of energy levels, which makes RT devices particularly susceptible to the quality of interfaces and dimension variations. Such high-quality multilayer structures were first enabled by the epitaxial growth of III-V semiconductors via MBE and its derivatives; it caused the rapid advent of RT devices in the 1990s and demonstrations of various multi-valued memory and logic applications.[39,117] Multipeak output characteristics have been realized, e.g., by using coupled quantum wells,118119] and integration of DBSs in parallel or in series [1,36,37,120] Figure 10a shows an example of the most widely used approach, where DBSs were integrated vertically and in series to obtain the I-V curve with multiple NDR regions and stable states for applications in multi-valued memories (Figure 10b).[120]

With the emergence of novel materials, RT and resulting multipeak $I { - } V$ curves have been reported in various structures based on graphene, TMDCs, organic materials, and perovskites.[121-129] Most of these works underline the application potential of the demonstrated RT devices in the MVL field, however, since they mainly represent early results, MVL applications are yet to be demonstrated. Several representative works will be briefly introduced in this section. Graphene/h-BN/graphene junctions have attracted attention as a platform to research the RT phenomenon.121,12] Kang et al. reported a vertical tunneling FET where RT occurred in the bilayer graphene/h-BN/bilayer graphene junction (Figure 10c).122) Two additional h-BN layers served as the capping layer and gate dielectric for the bottom gate. Two NDR peaks were shown at room temperature corresponding to two resonances between bilayer graphene layers (Figure 10d): the first resonance originated from the alignment of the charge neutrality points and the second resonance occurred when the lower conduction band of one layer aligned with the upper conduction band of the other layer. Takeyama et al. demonstrated multipeak output $I { - } V$ characteristics of $p ^ { + } { \cdot } \mathrm { M o S } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { h } { \cdot } \mathrm { B N } / \mathrm { W S e } _ { 2 }$ tunnel junctions as a result of subband quantization in few-layer ${ \tt W S e } _ { 2 }$ (Figure 10e).[123] The formation of such subbands in few-layer TMDCs was ascribed to the naturally occurring out-of-plane wave function confinement. Output characteristics with three and four current peaks at room temperature were demonstrated for three- and five-layer $\mathrm { W S e } _ { 2 } ,$ respectively. For example, each of the peaks $V _ { 1 } , \ V _ { 2 } ,$ and $V _ { 3 }$ in Figure 10f corresponds to RT to one of three subbands of the three-layer $\mathrm { W S e } _ { 2 }$ . The recent emergence of weak epitaxy growth (WEG) allowed the epitaxial growth of highly ordered multilayered organic structures with smooth interfaces. Zhu et al. applied WEG to grow organic quantum well of IDQA/PTCBI/ IDQA (Figure 10g).[129] By using low and high work functions contacts, the authors accessed the quantized electron and hole energy levels in PTCBI and demonstrated RT and NDR phenomena. In the case of high work function contacts, one, two, and three resonance peaks were demonstrated for different widths of PTCBI, which stemmed from the difference in the number of discrete quantized energy levels and their positions at different widths of PTCBI (Figure 10h).

Discussion: RT remains an attractive but challenging mechanism for practical MVL applications. The recent years have seen the appearance of various RT devices with multiple NDR regions potentially suitable for MVL applications. The issues with interfacial imperfections and realization of room-temperature operation typical for the previous generation of RT devices have been addressed by utilizing the pristine interfaces of vdW materials.130] While some of the recent RT devices resemble the conventional DBSs, several reports have demonstrated RT through a single barrier but to different subbands of the same material to generate multiple NDR regions. Such devices can potentially simplify the structure of RT devices as compared to the conventional RT devices, but at the same time a high gate voltage is required for their operation. High technological requirements for the quality of materials, interfaces, and dimensions of RT devices will likely to remain a challenge considering the insufficient technologic maturity of the recently emerged materials, which may encumber the systematic research of the novel RT mechanisms. Thus, it is difficult to conclude at this point whether any of the presented concepts will be able to emerge as a platform for the next-generation of MVL unit devices, therefore testing of newly reported concepts for applicability in MVL technologies is desirable.

# 5. Conclusions and Perspectives

The MVL field has entered a new development stage. The progress made in such a short period of time is remarkable; unique approaches and unit devices based on various materials have been proposed and extended to array- and wafer-scales, which have resulted in the increasing number of MVL-related publications in recent years. All types of devices covered in this review are listed and summarized in Table 2 with the description of their key advantages and limitations. New concepts continuously emerge and more MVL logic gates were demonstrated during the manuscript preparation, e.g., a quaternary NAND gate based on heterostack channel transistors,[131] and other MVL gates realized by using single- $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ negative capacitance FETs,132] by integration of memristors and CMOS in the back end of the line,133] by using $\mathrm { N b } { \sf O } _ { 2 }$ threshold switch devices,[134] and by utilizing stateful neural networks.[135]

NDR, NDT, and ZDT phenomena remain key enablers of multiple logic states (see Section 2.2). Practically, NDR devices have been more suitable for memory applications as their operation is based on multiple stable QPs, although logic applications have also been proven feasible using MML and a recently proposed method described in Section 4.1. NDT and ZDT devices have been found more suitable for logic applications implemented by $g _ { \mathrm { m } }$ matching in the intermediate region of transfer $I { - } V$ characteristics. In addition, recent reports indicate that not only multi- $V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ but also single- $\cdot V _ { \mathrm { T H } }$ devices, previously not considered suitable for MVL technology, can function as MVL unit devices.[15,64,116]

Most proposed MVL unit devices have been designed for ternary logic, and only a few works have demonstrated or considered the extensibility to MVLs with $R > 3$ [18,95] This is a predictable outcome as the design of ternary unit devices, not to mention devices for MVLs with $R > 3$ , often requires substantial complications of their structures compared to the binary counterparts. Hence, we anticipate that ternary logic will remain prioritized over other MVLs owing to the reasonable trade-off between device structure sophistication and benefits from the increased radix.

Many MVL unit devices have been enabled by advances in material science. Oxide, organic, and vdW layered materials have significantly expanded the library of available materials and enabled novel phenomena and functionalities. On the contrary, many of those materials still lack technological maturity and fail to provide sufficient yields and libraries for simulation tools, which can delay and hinder the future development. Sibased technologies remain strong and reliable, and Si-based MVL devices have regularly been published. Practically, any MVL technology that will utilize conventional mature technologies such as CMOs foundries will have an immediate superior advantage over other concepts based on less mature technologies. It is also expected that more universal concepts, that are less dependent on specific materials, ultimately will reveal their advantages over other concepts. The history shows that even significant progress might be not sufficient if material or technology has a critical drawback. A good example is the RT devices based on III-V compound semiconductors, whose development was very active in the 1990s and leads to significant advances in the MVL field. Despite strong theoretical foundation, reliable growth techniques, and the demonstrations of primitive and complex MVL gates, RT devices were not able to enter the stage of industrial adoption, to a great extent because of the unavailability of faster and less costly fabrication methods than MBE and its derivatives.

As for MVL applications, most recent works have reported single-input STI gates. Owing to the triviality and universality, the STI has become a figure of merit and criterion of unit device applicability to MVL technology. Significantly fewer works considered two-input MVL gates such as ternary NAND and NOR; two-input MVL gates have mostly been demonstrated based on ZDT devices as they can utilize simple resistive loads for implementation of logic gates.

Now, that we have reached the stage when there are a number of decent MVL concepts, it is also important to set adequate goals for further developments. Currently, the MVL field lacks roadmaps to help researchers concentrate their efforts on addressing most critical issues. From this prospective, conferences and other types of scientific meetings could play an important role in encouraging such roadmaps and promoting communications between researchers in the MVL field. Another issue is the gap in the literature between theoretical works on MVL algebras and experimental works that demonstrate devices and basic circuits. Therefore, deeper analysis of existing concepts and devices from prospective of computation and algebras is desirable.

It is almost certain that the MVL technologies will not be able to completely replace the mature conventional binary technologies in near future, however, there is a more realistic scenario that adoption of MVL technologies may start from the partial integration of MVL computational modules to replace most critical parts of the modern binary chips. For this purpose, MVL systems must have an ability to effectively communicate with binary systems within the same chip, and thus this research direction should not be undervalued. Finally, most demonstrated MVL concepts require further confirmations of their feasibility in terms of power consumption, scalability, reliability, and possibility to implement more complex logic gates.

Returning to the statement of Hurst mentioned in Introduction,10] we now must answer the question: has "a truly multiple-valued switching device, comparable in size and switching efficiency to today's bipolar and MOS transistors" been found? The answer is clearly "no," however, the future of MVL technologies certainly looks much brighter than 5 or 10 years ago. With the increasing number of MVL concepts and targeted efforts of researchers from different fields, the MVL technology has a sufficient potential to approach and eventually compete with the conventional binary technologies in mid- and longterm perspectives.
