ABSTRACT: Semitransparent organic solar cells (ST-OSCs) have been regarded as a promising candidate for building integrated photovoltaics. In general, most of the ST-OSCs are based on a bulk heterojunction (BHJ) structure in which the morphology of the BHJ flm must be delicately optimized. In this work, we introduce a sequentially deposited bilayer structure into STOSCs by using a PTB7-Th/IEICO-4F combination. The adoption of the bilayer structure not only simplifies the device optimization, but it is also found that, as the donor and the acceptor are separately deposited, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of bilayer ST-OSCs can be improved by simply increasing the thickness of IEICO-4F, which has strong near infrared absorption but weak visible light absorption, without significantly affecting the average visible light transmittance (AVT) of the device. However, in the BHJ structure, the increase in BHJ film thickness unavoidably enhances the donor absorption in the visible light region, leading to a tradeoff between the PCE and AVT in BHJ-structured

# INTRODUCTION

Organic solar cells (OsCs) have attracted extensive attention, owing to their benefts of being lightweight, mechanically flexible, and solution processable.-3 In addition, the high absorption coefficient and color tunability of organic semiconductors make them quite suitable to fabricate semitransparent OSCs (ST-OSCs), which shows great potential application in power-generating glasses in buildings or selfpowered greenhouses.-8 In the last several years, with the rapid development of nonfullerene acceptors (NFAs), the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of OsCs has been increased over $1 5 \%$ 9-14 Among these NFAs, great progress has been made in the narrow bandgap (NBG) acceptors with Strong absorption in the near infrared region.15-19 In the early efforts, Hou and co-workers designed a nonfullerene acceptor, IEICO-4F20 A high PCE of $1 0 . 0 \%$ was achieved when blending PTB7-Th with IEICO-4F as the active layer. Subsequently, many efforts had been made for this field. Ding and co-workers reported a highly efficient A-D-A nonfullerene acceptor, $\mathrm { C O _ { i } } 8 \mathrm { D F I C } . ^ { 2 }$ The OSCs utilizing PTB7-Th as the donor and $\mathrm { C O _ { i } 8 D F I C }$ as the acceptor displayed the best PCE at $1 2 . 1 \%$ These devices all exhibited strong external quantum efficiency at a wavelength of over 800 nm. The NBG acceptors thus provided striking advantages in achieving high-performance ST-OsCs.22-24

In some earlier works, a sequentially deposited bilayer structure has been utilized to fabricate donor and acceptor layers via thermal evaporation for small molecular ST-OSCs, which showed high AVT values (over $5 0 \%$ ) but a PCE of around $2 \% .$ This kind of bilayer structure may provide insufficient donor/acceptor (D/A) interfaces for exciton dissociation due to the formation of a planar heterojunction. Therefore, a bulk heterojunction (BHJ) structure of $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ has been widely used for ST-OSCs.27,28 The BHJ morphology is of great importance to the effciency of ST-OsCs.29,30 However, the phase separation and vertical distribution in the BHJ active layer are complicated to modulate. In order to obtain an optimal nanoscale interpenetrating network, it usually needs to try different blend ratios between the donor and the acceptor or different processing solvents and additives3132 Moreover, for a given active layer, since the donor and the acceptor are homogeneously blended with an optimal ratio, simply increasing the thickness of the active layer to improve the PCE will inevitably lead to enhanced absorption in the visible region, which finally reduces the visible light transmittance of ST-OSCs. Hence, the challenge in ST-OSCs is to simultaneously achieve both high PCE and AVT through materials design and device engineering. For comparison, the bilayerstructured OSCs fabricated by a sequentially deposited method in which the active layer is deposited by coating the solutions of a pure donor and acceptor, respectively, can result in highly efficient devices with greatly simplified processing.33, 33,34 Besides, the interpenetration between the polymeric donor and the small molecular acceptor during sequential deposition will lead to the formation of $\mathrm { D - A }$ interfaces for exciton dissociation. The enrichment of the donor and the acceptor at their respective interfaces helps the formation of a $\mathtt { p - i - n }$ -like junction, which facilitates the charge carrier transportation and extraction.3,36 These features offer the bilayer structure great potential for preparing high-performance ST-OSCs with easy processing. However, as far as we know, researches on preparing ST-OsCs employing a sequentially solutionprocessed bilayer structure are rarely seen.

In this work, we employed a sequentially deposited method to fabricate opaque and semitransparent OsCs with device architecture of indium tin oxide $\left( \mathrm { I T O } \right) / \mathrm { p o l y } ( 3 , 4 -$ ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS)/PTB7-Th/IEICO-4F/PNDIT- $\mathrm { F } _ { 3 } \mathrm { N } { \cdot } \mathrm { B r } / 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { ~ n m }$ Ag $\mathrm { ~ 1 ~ \ n m ~ \ A u / 1 2 ~ \ n m ~ \ A g } )$ . By using two semiorthogonal nonhalogen solvents ( $^ o$ -xylene, $^ o$ -xylene/ $\mathbf { \bar { \Phi } } _ { n }$ -butanol), we firstly obtained opaque OSCs with a PCE over $1 0 \%$ . After adding $2 \%$ CN in the acceptor solution, the performance of the opaque device could be further enhanced to $12 \%$ due to enhanced charge generation and reduced charge recombination. The opaque device recipe enabled the construction of highperformance ST-OSCs. When fabricating ST-OSCs using a bilayer structure, it was found that the visible light transmission spectra of devices show weak dependence on the thickness of the IEICO-4F layer, which has strong near infrared absorption but weak visible light absorption; thus, the PCE of bilayer STOSCs can be improved by simply increasing the thickness of IEICO-4F without significantly sacrificing the AVT of the device. However, in the BHJ structure, there was a tradeoff between the PCE and AVT in the BHJ-structured device. Eventually, the bilayer-structured device exhibited a better performance than the BHJ-structured device, e.g., a PCE of $8 . 5 \%$ for the bilayer structure versus a PCE of $8 . 1 \%$ for the BHJ structure with an AVT around $2 1 \%$ .

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The chemical structures and normalized absorption spectra of PTB7-Th and IEICO-4F are shown in Figure 1. It is obvious that the neat PTB7-Th film exhibits main absorption in $^ { 4 5 0 - }$ $7 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ while the neat IEICO-4F flm shows strong absorption in the near infrared region from 750 to $9 5 0 ~ \mathrm { \ n m }$ .The complementary absorption between PTB7-Th and IEICO-4F is beneficial to efficiently utilize the solar energy, which helps to obtain a large short-circuit current. Before the construction of ST-OSCs, opaque bilayer devices were first fabricated and optimized with a conventional structure consisting of ITO/ PEDOT:PSS/PTB7-Th/IEICO-4F/PNDIT- $\mathrm { . F } _ { 3 } \mathrm { N }$ Br (see the chemical structure in Figure 1a)/100 nm Ag (Figure 2a). In order to minimize the hazard on the environment, we utilized the nonhalogen solvent $^ o$ -xylene to dissolve the donor and the mixed solvent ( $^ o$ xylene/ $\overset { \cdot } { n }$ butanol) to dissolve the acceptor. As $n$ -butanol has poor solubility for PTB7-Th, it was added into the acceptor solution with a blend ratio of 3:1 ( $^ o$ -xylene/ $n$ -butanol, $\mathbf { v } / \mathbf { v }$ ) to avoid washing off the donor film during sequential deposition. Figure S1 displays that the film absorption of PTB7-Th shows a tiny difference before or after washing by the mixed solvent, indicating that the semiorthogonal solvent is suitable for sequential deposition. Notably, the bilayer devices do not need to optimize the $\mathrm { D } { - } \mathbf { A }$ weight ratio, which is supposed to be carefully optimized in BHJ devices, thus, the bilayer structure simplifies the device optimization procedure.

Opaque Organic Solar Cells. The optimization details of the bilayer thickness and CN content in the acceptor solution are summarized in Tables S1 and S2, respectively. Table 1 summarizes the open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ , short-circuit current density $\left( J _ { s c } \right)$ , fill factor (FF), and PCE of the opaque bilayer devices with $0 \%$ or $2 \%$ CN. It was found that after adding a $2 \%$ CN additive into the IEICO-4F solution, the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of the bilayer device was improved from 23.9 to $2 5 . 4 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ; meanwhile, the FF was also increased from 59.2 to $6 8 \%$ resulting in the best bilayer device with a PCE of $1 2 . 0 \%$ at a total thickness of $1 1 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ C $6 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ donor and $5 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ acceptor) (Figure 2b). The increased $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ in Table 1 was also verified by the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra presented in Figure 2c. By integrating the EQE curves, the calculated $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ values are also listed in Table 1. For bilayer OSCs with a $2 \%$ CN additive, the calculated $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ is $2 4 . 4 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ which is consistent with the measured $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ from the $J { - } V$ measurement. As shown in Figure 2c, the EQE in $6 5 0 { - } 1 0 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ was greatly enhanced in the CNtreated device, which indicates that the CN additive may increase the donor/acceptor interfaces in the acceptor layer and thus enhance exciton dissociation. For comparison, as summarized in Table S3, the opaque OSCs based on the BHJ structure using a halogen solvent (chlorobenzene) exhibited an optimal PCE of $1 1 . 6 \%$ with a $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 4 . 5 ~ \mathrm { m A ~ c m } ^ { - 2 }$

' Charge Generation, Transfer, and Recombination. Photoluminescence measurement was carried out to understand why these bilayer OSCs exhibited a high performance. As depicted in Figure S2, the pristine PTB7-Th film showed a strong emission peak when being excited at $5 6 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . However, the photoluminescence of bilayer films with $5 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ of IEICO4F was efficiently quenched, which confirms that there are adequate $\mathrm { D - A }$ interfaces for exciton dissociation. Therefore, the intermixed donor-acceptor phase may form between pure donor and acceptor phases during thermal annealing. To understand why CN induces such a dramatic improvement in device efficiency, we further investigated the charge generation and exciton dissociation behaviors for bilayer OsCs with or without the CN additive. We measured the photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ versus effective voltage $\left( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } \right)$ , as described in Figure 2d. The $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ is described as $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } = J _ { \mathrm { L } } - J _ { \mathrm { D } }$ where $J _ { \mathrm { L } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { D } }$ stand for the current density under illuminated and dark conditions, respectively. The $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ is defined as $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } = V _ { 0 } - V _ { a }$ where $V _ { 0 }$ is the voltage at $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } ~ = ~ 0 .$ and $V _ { \mathbf { a } }$ is the applied voltage.37 Generally, it is assumed that all photogenerated excitons are completely dissociated into free charge carriers and contributed to the saturation photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { { s a t } } } \right)$ at a high $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } .$ Thus, the saturation photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { { s a t } } } \right)$ is limited by the total amount of absorbed incident photons. Figure 2d shows that the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ of all opaque OSCs quickly reaches a saturation state at a $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ of $2 . 2 \ \mathrm { V } .$ As summarized in Table S4, the $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ for the as-cast device was $2 5 . 7 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ while the $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ for the CN-treated device was $2 7 . 2 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . The increased $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ suggested that charge generation was enhanced in the opaque OSCs with the CN additive. We also compared the exciton dissociation probabilities $\textstyle { \left[ P ( E , T ) \right] }$ of OSCs treated with or without the CN additive. The $P ( E , T )$ is determined by the value of $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } .$ 38 Under short-circuit conditions, a $P ( E , T )$ of $9 2 . 3 5 \%$ was achieved for the CN-treated device, which is higher than that $\left( 9 0 . 1 8 \% \right)$ of the as-cast device (Table S4). The results suggest that the addition of the CN additive makes the exciton generation and dissociation more efficient, implying that more $\mathrm { D - A }$ interfaces may form with the help of the CN additive.39,40

In order to figure out the influence of the CN additive on the charge transport and recombination process in the bilayerstructured OsCs, we measured $J { - } V$ curves at different illumination intensities varying from 3 to $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { \ m W ~ \ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ The recombination mechanism, which happened in the active layer, can be studied by calculating the slope of $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ versus the natural logarithm of $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ 7. Generally, the relationship between $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ can be described as $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } \propto \mathrm { l n } \left( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } \right) \mathrm { k _ { B } } T /$ $q$ $\cdot P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ is light intensity, $\bf k _ { \mathrm { B } }$ is Boltzmann's constant, $T$ is the absolute temperature, and $q$ is the elementary charge). If bimolecular recombination dominates the recombination process of charge carriers, then the slope is close to $\mathbf { k _ { B } } T / q$ If trap-assisted recombination or monomolecular recombination dominates, then $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ exhibits enhanced dependence on $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } \left( 2 \mathrm { k _ { B } } T / q \right)$ . As illustrated in Figure 2e, the CN-treated and as-cast devices based on the PTB7-Th/IEICO-4F bilayer structure displayed slopes of 1.27 and $1 . 3 9 \mathrm { k _ { B } } T / q ,$ respectively, manifesting that there is less trap-assisted recombination or monomolecular recombination in the CN-treated device. In general, a power law dependence of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ upon light intensity is described as $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \propto ( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } ^ { \mathrm { ^ { - } } } ) ^ { S }$ 42 Figure 2f depicts the linear relationship between logarithmic values of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ . The S values of fitted lines are 0.969 and 0.893 for the CN-treated and as-cast device, respectively, revealing that the bimolecular recombination is weaker in the former device. Combining the results from $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } { - } P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ curves, we can conclude that both the bimolecular recombination and trap-assisted recombination are really weakened in the CNtreated device.

Semitransparent Organic Solar Cells. Since the donor and the acceptor were separately deposited, simply increasing the thickness of IEICO-4F should have a tiny influence on visible light transmission spectra. To investigate the impact of $\mathrm { D } { - } \mathbf { A }$ thickness on the absorption spectrum of bilayer films, we measured two characteristic bilayer films with the same total thickness. As described in Figure 4a, two bilayer films with the same thickness of $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ exhibit different absorption spectra. The bilayer film with a thicker acceptor layer $( 4 0 ~ \mathrm { n m } / 6 0 ~ \mathrm { n m } )$ exhibits increased photon harvesting in the near infrared region and decreased absorption in the visible light range, which is in line with the requirement of ST-OSCs.

As high-performance opaque OSCs were obtained by using a bilayer structure already, a series of ST-OSCs were constructed by using $1 \ \mathrm { n m \ A u } / 1 2 \ \mathrm { n m \ A g }$ as the top transparent electrode to replace $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ Ag. First, we investigated the effect of acceptor thickness on the performance of bilayer ST-OsCs. The current density-voltage $\left( J - V \right)$ curves of ST-OSCs based on bilayer and BHJ structures are illustrated in Figure S3. Also, the photovoltaic parameters and AVT of corresponding devices are summed in Table 2. Keeping a thickness of 60 nm for PTB7-Th, the PCE of bilayer ST-OSCs was increased from 7.9 to $8 . 4 \%$ along with the thickness of IEICO-4F increasing from 50 to $7 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ , corresponding to over $6 . 3 \%$ PCE improvement but very tiny AVT loss $\left( 2 . 8 \% \right)$ . As displayed in Figure $^ { 4 \mathrm { b } , \mathrm { c } , }$ it is worthwhile to highlight that increasing the thickness of IEICO-4F enhances the utilization of the NIR light for charge generation, but it has nearly no influence on the visible light transmittance spectra. Owing to the near infrared absorption of IEICO-4F, the EQE in $7 0 0 { - } 1 0 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ is increased (Figure 4c). As a result, a thicker acceptor layer can improve the PCE while having no significant influence on the AVT of bilayer ST-OSCs. Furthermore, we investigated the effect of donor thickness on the performance of bilayer STOSCs. The device performance of bilayer ST-OsCs with different thicknesses of the donor is summarized in Table S5, and corresponding transmittance spectra, EQE spectra, and $J -$ $V$ curves are depicted in Figure S4. As shown in Table 2 and Table S5, the semitransparent device of $6 0 ~ \mathrm { n m } / 8 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ (or 50 $\mathrm { n m } / 7 0 ~ \mathrm { n m } )$ has a comparable PCE but better AVT than the device of $7 0 \ \mathrm { n m } / 7 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ (or $6 0 ~ \mathrm { n m } / 6 0 ~ \mathrm { n m } ,$ , which further indicates that applying a thicker acceptor layer is conducive to get a balanced PCE and AVT for ST-OSCs.

As for ST-OSCs based on the BHJ structure, the PCE was improved from 7.6 to $8 . 3 \%$ along with the thickness of blend films increasing from 95 to $1 4 0 \ \mathrm { n m } ,$ corresponding to $9 . 2 \%$ PCE improvement but significant AVT loss $\left( 1 6 . 2 \% \right)$ (Table 2). As depicted in Figure $^ { 4 \mathrm { d } , \mathrm { e } }$ when increasing the thickness of the active layer, the transmittance spectra of BHJ ST-OSCs had an overall decline in $4 0 0 { - } 1 0 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ while the EQE spectra displayed an overall improvement. This is because increasing the thickness of PTB7-Th/IEICO-4F blending films to improve the PCE inevitably leads to enhanced absorption of PTB7-Th in the visible region, which finally reduces the AVT of the active layer. Compared with bilayer ST-OSCs, the BHJ ST-OSCs show an apparent contradiction between the PCE and AVT. Therefore, the bilayer structure is an effective approach to balance the AVT and PCE of ST-OSCs.

Since the semitransparent device of $6 0 ~ \mathrm { n m } / 7 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ exhibited a better overall performance than other devices (in Table 2 and Table S5), a series of semitransparent devices with an acceptor layer $1 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ thicker than the donor layer were fabricated to improve device efficiency and transmittance. Table 3 summarizes the parameters of these ST-OSCs. All the devices exhibited a high FF over $6 4 \%$ and AVT over $2 1 \%$ .By increasing the thickness of the bilayer film, the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ is gradually enhanced while the FF is nearly unchanged (Figure S5). Among them, we obtained the best PCE at $8 . 5 \%$ and AVT at $2 1 . 5 \%$ (Table 3) for bilayer ST-OsCs while the bestperforming BHJ ST-OSCs exhibited a PCE of $8 . 1 \%$ and AVT of $2 1 . 1 \%$ (Table 2). The transmittance spectra and corresponding EQE of devices $_ { \mathrm { A - D } }$ are illustrated in Figure 5a,b. Comparing the BHJ data in Table 2 with the bilayer data in Table 3, bilayer ST-OSCs exhibited a comparable PCE but higher AVT than corresponding BHJ devices when having similar thickness of the active layer. The increased AVT values are attributed to the controllable thickness of the donor/ acceptor. Table S6 summarizes the previous work about STOSCs utilizing different device structures and materials as compared to this work. As shown in Table S6, our device is one of the best-performing ST-OSCs with an IEICO-4F acceptor. Besides, we also used visible light transmittance (VLT) to evaluate the transparency of ST-OSCs. VLT represents the integration of the transmission spectrum and the AM 1.5 photon flux weighted against the spectral response of the typical human eye.45 As summarized in Table S7, all devices (A-D) display a VLT over $2 3 . 2 \%$

Moreover, the transparency color perception of ST-OSCs is also an important evaluation indicator for power-generating windows. The color coordinates were calculated from the transmittance spectra of ST-OSCs and displayed in the CIE 1931 chromaticity diagram in Figure 5c. In addition to color coordinates, other important parameters for evaluating the optical properties of ST-OsCs are the correlated color temperature (CCT) and color rendering index (CRI), which correspond to the temperature of the blackbody radiator and the capability to show true colors of viewed objects, respectively. The CIE XYZ color coordinates of devices A, B, $\mathrm { C } ,$ and D are located at (0.261, 0.299), (0.260, 0.301), (0.258, 0.305), and (0.259, 0.312), respectively, corresponding to the CCT of 11,264, 11,310, 11,161, and $1 0 , 7 6 4 \ \mathrm { K } ,$ respectively (Table S7). The color coordinates of devices A-D are away from the white point, and the CRIs of devices A-D are below 80 (Table S7), which should be due to the intrinsic absorption properties of selected active layers. The photographs of the bilayer semitransparent devices, which were put in front of a scene, are presented in Figure 5d. Also, the photo images of devices A, B, C, and D are provided in Figure S6. Comparing with a reference photograph, the objects can be clearly seen from the bilayer ST-OSCs, indicating the aesthetics and the good transparency property of ST-OSCs.

Besides, we explored the storage and thermal stability for bilayer ST-OSCs by measuring the PCE and transparency variation as a function of time. As illustrated in Figures $S 7 a , c$ and $\mathtt { S 8 a } , \mathtt { c }$ in the Supporting Information, the transmittance spectra and color of devices showed a tiny difference after a few days of storage and thermal stress $( 8 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ . The devices retained $8 5 \%$ of its initial PCE after being aged in the dark for $^ { 1 9 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ (Figure S7b) and $67 \%$ of its initial PCE after being thermally stressed at $8 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for 67 h (Figure S8b).

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we have developed high-performance OSCs based on PTB7-Th/IEICO-4F using a sequentially deposited bilayer structure. Two semiorthogonal nonhalogen solvents ( $^ o$ -xylene, $^ o$ -xylene/ $\mathbf { \bar { \Phi } } _ { n }$ -butanol) were used to process the donor and acceptor layers. After adding the CN additive into the acceptor solution, the PCE of opaque device can be enhanced from 10 to $1 2 . 0 \%$ . It was found that the CN additive effectively enhances the charge generation and dissociation and suppresses charge recombination. Then, ST-OSCs were fabricated according to the recipe in the opaque device. It is worth mentioning that simply increasing the thickness of IEICO-4F will enhance the photovoltaic performance of STOSCs while having a weak influence on the visible light transmittance spectra of devices. However, the BHJ ST-OSCs show an apparent contradiction between the PCE and AVT. By modulating the donor/acceptor thickness, the sequentially deposited bilayer ST-OSCs exhibited a comparable PCE but higher AVT than corresponding BHJ devices (Tables 2 and 3), which indicates the better transparency of bilayer devices. Among which, the best-performing bilayer ST-OSCs exhibited a PCE of $8 . 5 \%$ with AVT of $2 1 . 5 \%$ while the best-performing BHJ ST-OSCs showed a relatively low PCE of $8 . 1 \%$ with AVT of $2 1 . 1 \%$ which suggests that the sequentially deposited bilayer structure is favorable to achieve both high AVT and PCE of ST-OSCs.
