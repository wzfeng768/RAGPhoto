ABSTRACT: Interfacial charge-transfer is the critical process during the operation of dye-sensitized solar cells, determining the photovoltaic performance of the cell. Interfacial charge-transfer processes are affected by many factors, such as the energy level and side chain. According to our previous work, the planarization of the carbazole segment can significantly improve the interfacial chargetransfer performance. However, whether the planarization strategy is universally effective in various dye systems is still uncertain. Moreover, the planarization of the molecule also leads to unfavorable $\pi \cdot$ aggregation. Thus, herein, the planarization strategy is further applied on indoline dyes while the alkyl chain is also introduced to investigate the effect of the above two factors on interfacial charge-transfer proceses. With the improvement f the planarit and the introduction of an alkyl chain, interfacial charge. transfer processes, including charge injection, dye regeneration, and charge recombination, are eficiently optimized, sugesting a certain universlt of our planarizatio strategy. It i noteworthy that the overlong alkyl chain stillnegatively affects these processes. Accordingly, the improvement of donor planarity as well as the introduction of a suitable alkyl chain can finely optimize interfacial charge-transfer processes, providing a promising strategy for the development of an eficient organic sensitizer.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

During the last decade, organic dyes have attracted considerable attention as powerful candidates of sensitizers in dye-sensitized solar cells (DssCs), due to their relatively high power conversion efficiency, easy synthesis and purification, and more importantly, tunable properties.1-4 Most properties of organic dyes, such as optical and electrochemical properties, can be easily regulated by the modification of their molecular structure, including the donor,5-11 $\pi$ linker,12-14 and acceptor segments.15-18 Besides, interfacial charge-transfer processes during the operation of DssCs can also be affected by molecular engineering of the dye to a considerable extent.

In our previous work, planarity regulation was applied on the carbazole donor through simple cyclization between carbazole and phenyl groups, leading to a significant improvement of interfacial charge-transfer performance.19 However, whether the planarization strategy is universally effective in various dye systems is still uncertain. Moreover, with planarization of the molecule, the trend of $\pi$ -aggregation becomes more and more severe. Thus, alkyl chains should also be introduced to prevent unfavorable aggregation, n,20-23 which will certainly change the set of conditions between interfaces in DssCs. However, the effect of an alkyl chain in the planar donor segment on interfacial charge-transfer processes in DSSCs is still barely investigated.

With these thoughts in mind, based on the reference indoline dye CS-31, CS-32-34 were designed and synthesized by the cyclization of a phenyl group with the indoline unit,24,25 while the $n$ -butyl/ $\mathbf { \bar { \Phi } } _ { n }$ -octyl groups were also introduced into the planar indoline unit to further evaluate their anti-aggregation capability. With this molecular engineering, the HOMO levels of CS-32-34 were gradually optimized, while their LUMO levels were almost unaffected. Besides the selective optimization of the HOMO levels, more importantly, the interfacial charge-transfer processes in four dye-based DSsCs were also effectively regulated by the planarization and extension of the alkyl chains, suggesting the generality of the planarization strategy.

# 2. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

As shown in Scheme 1, the reference dye CS-31 presented a quite large dihedral angle value of $5 5 . 9 ^ { \circ }$ between indoline and phenyl planes, suggesting poor conjugation between these two segments. Through the cyclization of these two segments, the dihedral angle of CS-32 was sharply reduced to $2 3 . 3 ^ { \circ } $ improving the conjugation of the donor unit. Moreover, along with the extension of the alkyl chains from methyl to butyl and octyl groups, the dihedral angles of CS-33 and CS34 were further reduced to 5.5 and $4 . 5 ^ { \circ }$ ,respectively, indicating a gradual improvement in the planarity (Figure S1). Thus, in our cases, besides the prevention of $\pi \cdot$ aggregation, the extension of the alkyl chains could also improve $\pi \cdot$ -conjugation, which would have an obvious influence on optical, electrochemical, and interfacial charge-transfer properties.

2.1. Optical and Electrochemical Properties. Figure 1a presents the normalized absorption and photoluminescence (PL) spectra of CS-31-34 in chloroform. All spectra exhibit strong absorption in the visible light region, ascribed to the intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) from the indoline unit to the benzothiadiazole. The absorption maxima $\left( \lambda _ { \operatorname* { m a x } } \right)$ of CS-32 was determined to be $5 0 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ presenting a $1 4 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ red shift compared with CS-31 (Table 1), which could only be attributed to the decrease in the dihedral angle between the phenyl and indoline groups, arising from the planarization of the indoline donor. Furthermore, when the dihedral angle was reduced from $2 3 . 3 ^ { \circ }$ of CS-32 to $5 . 5 ^ { \circ }$ of CS-33 and $4 . 5 ^ { \circ }$ of CS34, the $\lambda _ { \operatorname* { m a x } }$ values were further red-shifted to 519 and ${ 5 2 0 } \mathrm { n m } ,$ respectively. When the dyes were loaded on a $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ film, all ICT bands showed hypsochromic shifts (Figure 1b). As a result of $H .$ aggregation and deprotonation effect, CS-31 presented the longest blue shift of $3 1 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ among the four dyes (Table 1). Owing to the aggregation prevention effect of long alkyl chains, the blue shifts of CS-33 and 34 were decreased to 17 and $1 5 \mathrm { n m }$ respectively, mainly ascribed to the deprotonation effect. Moreover, the molar extinction coefficients of CS-32-34 also slightly increased compared with that of CS-31. Therefore, with broader absorption bands and higher molar extinction coefficients, dyes with a planar indoline donor presented strong light-harvesting capability, which would favor the photocurrent generation in solar cell devices.

The electrochemical properties of CS-31-34 were investigated by cyclic voltammetry to estimate the effects of molecular planarity and alkyl chain on the energy level (Figure S2). According to our previous experience in the carbazole system, with the improvement of the planarity in the donor segment, the HOMO level could be selectively upshifted by the planarization of the donor part, while the LUMO level. remained unchanged.1' In the case of indoline dyes, as shown in Figure 2, the HOMO level was upshifted from $0 . 9 9 \mathrm { V }$ of CS31 to $0 . 8 7 ~ \mathrm { V }$ of CS-32 along with the planarization of the donor part, when their LUMO levels stayed at around $- 1 . 1 7$ to $- 1 . 1 8 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } .$ This indicates that the selective shift of the HOMO level can also be achieved in the indoline dye system by adjusting the planarity of the donor segment, suggesting the certain generality of the regulation strategy. When the alkyl chain was extended from the methyl to the butyl group, the HOMO value of CS-33 was further upshifted to $0 . 8 3 \mathrm { V }$ and its LUMO level was slightly shifted to $- 1 . 1 9 \mathrm { V }$ This means that the variation of the alkyl chain would also slightly influence the orbital energy of the dye. Considering the similar electrodonating capability between methyl and butyl groups, the upshift of the HOMO level of CS-33 might be caused by the suppression of aggregation, which was also found in other dye systems.?7 Due to the similar anti-aggregation capability between the butyl and octyl group, further extension of the alkyl chain did not affect the energy levels anymore, leading to the same HOMO levels for CS-33 and CS-34.

Through the transient PL measurements, the PL lifetimes of CS-31-34 in the solvent $( \tau _ { \mathrm { { s o l u t i o n } } } )$ were obtained as 6.54, 3.58, 3.74, and 3.62 ns, respectively (Figure 3a). When the dyes were adsorbed on $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ electrodes, all four PL decay signals were obviously quenched to 1.68, 0.47, 0.46, and 0.45 ns, respectively, simulating the charge injection process. According to eq 1, the $\eta _ { \mathrm { i n j } }$ values for CS-31-34 were calculated to be 74.27, 86.81, 87.73, and $8 7 . 6 2 \%$ respectively. Therefore, although the PL lifetimes of CS-32-34 were shorter than that of CS-31, the improvement of the planarity in the donor segment still obviously favored the higher $\eta _ { \mathrm { i n j } }$ values, which lead to a more efficient charge injection process. Furthermore, due to the slight enhancement of charge lifetime in the solvent, the introduction of alkyl chains could further improve the charge injection process.

The $\eta _ { \mathrm { r e g } }$ values of all four dye-based DSsCs were also estimated by transient PL measurements. During these measurements, nanocrystalline $\mathrm { Z r O } _ { 2 }$ electrodes were utilized instead of $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ ones to avoid the PL quenching by charge injection. When the $\mathrm { I } ^ { - } / \mathrm { I } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ redox couple was introduced, the PL signals were effectively quenched due to the charge transfer from the redox couple to the dye, simulating the dye regeneration process. Actually, in the measurements, it was. found that the PL of the dye-loaded $\mathrm { Z r O } _ { 2 }$ film was still quenched to some extent compared with these values obtained in the solvent, which might be caused by the inevitable surface defects of the prepared $\mathrm { Z r O } _ { 2 }$ film. However, under the same conditions, we can still investigate the structure-activity relationship qualitatively. As shown in Figure 3b, without the redox couple, the CS-31-34-based $\mathrm { Z r O } _ { 2 }$ DSSCs presented PL lifetimes $( \tau _ { \mathrm { b l a n k } } )$ of 2.87, 3.10, 3.23, and 3.39 ns, respectively, while these values were effectively quenched to 1.10, 0.63, 0.66, and 0.81 ns, respectively, after the introduction of the $\mathrm { I } ^ { - } /$ ${ \mathrm { I } } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ redox couple $( \tau _ { \mathrm { I } } ^ { - } / \mathrm { I } 3 ^ { - } )$ . As a result, the planarization of the donor segment obviously enhanced the $\eta _ { \mathrm { r e g } }$ value. Meanwhile, it could also be found that the extension of the alkyl chain would lead to a slight reduction in the efficiency of dye regeneration, which might arise from the shielding effect of long alkyl chains on the redox couple.

Finally, the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were applied on CS-31-34-based DSSCs in the dark to investigate the influence of the donor segment on $\eta _ { \mathrm { c o l } } ,$ which was estimated according to eq 3, where $R _ { \mathrm { C T } }$ is the charge-transfer resistance and $R _ { \mathrm { T } }$ is the transport resistance (Figure S3).

$$
\eta _ { \mathrm { c o l } } = \frac { R _ { \mathrm { C T } } } { R _ { \mathrm { C T } } + R _ { \mathrm { T } } }
$$

By employing the transmission line model,28 the linear region with a $\overline { { 4 5 ^ { \circ } } }$ slope in the medium-frequency region around $1 0 \ \mathrm { k H z }$ of the Nyquist plot was fitted to obtain the $R _ { \mathrm { T } } ,$ which were determined as small values of 2.36, 2.02, 2.14, and $2 . 2 0 \ \Omega$ for CS-31-34-based DSSCs, respectively, indicating a quite smooth charge transport. In the other case, CS-32-34 presented large $R _ { \mathrm { C T } }$ values of 33.38, 37.25, and $3 3 . 6 3 \ \Omega$ respectively, which were much higher than that of CS-31 $\left( 2 \dot { 6 } . 6 9 ~ \Omega \right)$ . This means that the planarization of the donor segment led to effective suppression of charge recombination. Moreover, the extension of alkyl chains from methyl to butyl groups could further separate the ${ \mathrm { I } } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ species far away from the $\mathrm { \bar { T i O } } _ { 2 }$ surface, thus resulting in further improvement of this suppression effect. However, when the butyl groups were replaced by octyl groups, the $R _ { \mathrm { C T } }$ value of the CS-34-based device declined to some extent. In our opinion, with the very large steric hindrance of octyl groups, the distance between CS-34 molecules was enhanced, which would supply enough space for ${ \mathrm { I } } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ species to get close to the $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ surface, leading to the fast charge recombination. As a result, the $\eta _ { \mathrm { c o l } }$ values of CS31-34-based DSsCs were obtained as high values of 91.88, 94.29, 94.58, and $9 3 . 8 6 \%$ respectively.

2.3. Photovoltaic Performance of DssCs. As discussed above, when the planar indoline donor was utilized, the interfacial charge-transfer processes of the CS-32-based device were obviously improved. Consistent with these results, the incident photon-to-current conversion efficiency (IPCE) action spectrum of CS-32-sensitized DSSC was much broader than that of the CS-31-sensitized one. When the alkyl chain was extended from the methyl to butyl group, the IPCE action spectrum of CS-33-sensitized DSSC was further broadened. The onset wavelength in the action spectrum of CS-33- sensitized DssC was determined to be around $7 7 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , which was red-shifted by around $2 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ compared with that of CS-32 (Figure 4a). When the alkyl chain was extended by introducing an octyl group, the onset wavelength of the CS-34-sensitized DSSC was further red-shifted to around $7 9 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . However, its IPCE values were slightly lower than those of the CS-33- sensitized DssC almost throughout the whole action region, due to its slightly lower charge-transfer efficiencies as discussed above. Besides the relatively lower charge-transfer efficiencies of CS-34, in our opinion, the overlarge alkyl chains would increase the molecular volume, thus resulting in its lower dyeloaded amount $( 0 . 6 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 7 } \mathrm { ~ m o l ~ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ listed in Table 2), which would also decrease the photocurrent generation. As a result, the CS-33-based cell device presented the best IPCE performance among the four dye-sensitized cell devices, as well as the highest $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $1 5 . 7 9 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . Moreover, the integrated short-circuit photocurrents $\left( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \right)$ were also calculated and are presented in Figure 4a. Similar values of integrated $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ were found for all four DssCs with respect to their experimental data obtained through a solar simulator (Table 2), confirming the reliability of the results.

Furthermore, the open-circuit photovoltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ values of the DssCs were also greatly affected by the planarity of the indoline donor and the alkyl chains. The $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the CS-32 sensitized device was obtained as $7 4 3 ~ \mathrm { m V } _ { \mathrm { \Omega } }$ which was $1 0 ~ \mathrm { m V }$ larger than that of CS-31. When the alkyl chains were extended from methyl to butyl groups, the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the CS-33-sensitized device was further enhanced to $7 5 5 ~ \mathrm { m V }$ . Similar to the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } ,$ when the octyl groups were introduced, the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ value of the CS-34-based device was slightly decreased to $7 5 1 ~ \mathrm { m V }$

As is well known, the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the DSSC is mainly affected by the CB edge shift $\left( \Delta E _ { \mathrm { C B } } \right)$ of $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 } ,$ as well as the charge recombination process. Thus, to gain insight into the photovoltage variation, the transient photovoltaic measurements of four DssCs were further characterized. As shown in. Figure 5a, the plots of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ against charge density were examined to evaluate the $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { C B } }$ . At a fixed charge density, CS31 obviously showed the largest $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { C B } }$ among the four dyes, which was contrary to the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ results. Actually, the $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { C B } }$ can be determined according to eq $4 ^ { 2 4 }$

$$
\Delta E _ { \mathrm { C B } } = - \frac { q \mu _ { \mathrm { n o r m a l } } \gamma } { \varepsilon \varepsilon _ { 0 } }
$$

where $q$ is the electron charge, $\mu _ { \mathrm { n o r m a l } }$ is the vertical dipole moment, $\gamma$ is the dye-loaded concentration, and $\varepsilon _ { 0 }$ and $\varepsilon$ are the permittivity values of the vacuum and organic monolayer, respectively. Therefore, to reveal the essential reason for the high $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { C B } }$ of the CS-31-sensitized $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ film, the $\mu _ { \mathrm { n o r m a l } }$ values of all four dyes were extracted from their Gaussian calculation results,26 which were in the order of $4 . 8 7 > 4 . 3 3 > 4 . 2 9 > 4 . 1 1$ D for CS-31, CS-34, CS-33, and CS-32, respectively (Figure S4). As a result, considering the high vertical dipole moment and dye-loaded amount, the high $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { C B } }$ of CS-31 could be easily understood.

Thus, the relatively low $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the CS-31-based DSSC could only be attributed to its fast charge recombination rate, which was also examined by transient photovoltaic measurements. As shown in Figure 5b, the CS-31-based device obviously showed the shortest charge lifetime among the four DSSCs. At a certain charge density, taking $7 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 7 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 3 }$ as an example, the charge lifetime in the cell device sensitized by CS31 was found to be $2 6 ~ \mathrm { m } s _ { \mathrm { ; } }$ which was less than half of that of CS-32 $\mathbf { \hat { \phi } } _ { 6 0 \mathrm { ~ \ m s } } )$ .Therefore, due to the fastest charge recombination process among the four dyes, CS-31 presented the lowest $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ value of $7 3 3 ~ \mathrm { m V }$ although it showed the largest $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { C B } }$ . When the alkyl chains were extended to butyl groups, the lifetime of CS-33 was significantly enhanced to around 161 ms, which was over five times higher than that of CS-31, being consistent with their EIS results. It is noteworthy that the transient photovoltage and photocurrent characterizations of four DSsCs based on CS-31-34 were also carried out by EIS measurements at different bias voltages (as shown in Figure S5). As is well known, the upshift of the $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ CB edge will cause the decrease of the density of occupied states (DOSs), corresponding to a reduction of chemical capacitance $C _ { \mu } ,$ due to the direct proportional relation between DOs and $\dot { C } _ { \mu }$ 29,30 Thus, the evaluation of $C _ { \mu }$ allows us to gain insight into the $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ CB shift. At the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of four DSSCs, the $C _ { \mu }$ values were determined to be 4.44, 7.46, 6.63, and $5 . 5 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { ~ F ~ c m } ^ { - 2 } ,$ respectively. Apparently, with the lower $C _ { \mu }$ value, CS-31 would cause the larger upshift of the $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ CB, with respect to the other three devices, with values in the same order as the results obtained in Figure Sa. Similarly, the charge lifetimes for all four DSSCs obtained by EIS measurements were also the same as the results presented in Figure $5 \mathbf { b } _ { \cdot }$ suggesting the reliability of our measurements.

# 3. CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, through the molecular engineering of planarity and alkyl chain in the donor segment, three planar indoline dyes with various alkyl chains were designed and developed. Along with the planarization of the indoline donor, the lightharvesting capability of planar dye CS-32 was obviously improved. When the alkyl chains were extended, the lightharvesting capability of CS-33 and 34 was further enhanced due to the anti-aggregation effect of butyl and octyl groups. Moreover, besides the effective and selective regulation of HOMO levels of the planar dyes, their interfacial chargetransfer processes were also efficiently improved. Combining the previous results, the effective regulation of the HOMO level and interfacial charge-transfer processes through the planarization strategy seems to have a certain universality in various dye systems. Meanwhile, the interfacial charge-transfer processes could be further improved by the extension of the alkyl chain, while overlong alkyl chain had a negative influence on these processes instead. As a result, both the planarization of the donor segment and the extension of the alkyl chain can significantly optimize the energy levels and multiple interfacial charge-transfer processes, which greatly affect the photovoltaic performance. These findings provide us with a meaningful and universal strategy to design and optimize the donor segment for further development of organic sensitizers.
