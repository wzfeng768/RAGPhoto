# Supporting Information

ABSTRACT: Organic photovoltaic (OPV) cells have shown effectiveness as off-grid power entities to drive the low power consumption electronics among the Internet of Things. The trap states and the induced recombination in OPV cells are critically relevant to the photovoltaic performance but remain ambiguous in OPV cells for indoor application. Here, we investigate the trap effects on the indoor photovoltaic performance by employing PBBD-T series donors and wide bandgap acceptor BTA3. It is revealed that the discrete density of state in OPV cells introduces low-lying trap states and further aggravates the trap-induced recombination. Instead of the domination of bimolecular recombination under solar radiation, trap-induced recombination prevails under indoor scenarios because of the low level of carrier densities under indoor weak illuminations. This work illustrates the details of charge carrier recombination behavior in OPV cells for indoor application and points out the importance of trap controlling in achieving high-performance indoor OPV cells.

W consumption. electronics.- Since most electronic devices employed in indoor IoTs generally work under the low power supply in the range of $1 { - } 1 0 0 \ \mu \mathrm { W } _ { ; }$ photovoltaics-based indoor light energy conversion is adequate for semipermanent off-grid energy sources.' Compared with other photovoltaic techniques, organic photovoltaics (OPVs) possess the advantages of low-cost, environmentally friendly manufacturing, tunable light-absorption property, and solution-based printing fabrication, which qualifies OPV cells as the most promising photovoltaic devices for indoor photon harvest.-s

To convert indoor light energy to electricity efficiently, the construction of indoor OPV cells should concern the following rules. First, the indoor OPV devices should have high external quantum efficiency (EQE), and the absorption spectra should match the emission spectra of the indoor light sources covering 400 to $7 5 0 ~ \mathrm { { \ n m } }$ so that a high-level photocurrent can be maintained.,10 Second, energy loss $\left( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } \right)$ of the OPV cells should be suppressed as much as possible to acquire a maximum open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ 11,12 For the limitation of both restrained wavelength range and reduced light intensity, there is an extra ${ \sim } 0 . 1 6 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ voltage loss of OPV cells applied under indoor iluminations.13,14 Therefore, the state-of-the-art OPV systems that exhibit excellent performances under solar radiation can only obtain $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ lower than $0 . 8 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ when applied under indoor ambient. Third, since the charge carrier density is proportional to the incident light intensity, the decreased carrier density can enlarge the percentage of trap-induced recombination, so that it is critical to suppress trap states in the indoor OPV cells. The majority of the reported indoor OPV works in the community recently have focused on the first two principles through molecule design and device construction. For instance, the indoor OPV materials toward high $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ were developed, such as nonfullerene acceptors (NFAs) ITCC, IO4Cl, and FCC-Cl, and the polymer acceptor PBN-10.10,15-17 Indoor OPv devices constructed by these molecules can realize $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ beyond $1 . 0 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ under 1000 lux LED illumination. The increased material bandgaps also lead to the blue shift of the absorption spectra. When fabricating OPV cells with widebandgap donor/acceptor (D/A) systems, the match between emission and absorption spectra can achieve maximal indoor photon utilization. Although the current indoor OPV cells have yielded high power-conversion efficiencies (PCEs) near $\dot { 3 } 0 \% , \ L ^ { 1 5 , 1 8 , 1 9 }$ there are bare reports that focus on the trap effect on OPV cells for indoor application. The trap states in bulk heterojunction (BHJ) flms, whose influence might be covered by the high-level charge density under solar radiation, are critical on the recombination kinetics when it comes to the indoor scenario. However, its detailed origination and quantitative impacts on indoor OPV performance are still blurry.

To clarify the differences in current density, we applied transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy to trace the dynamics of the charge carrier. The blends of the three systems are pumped at $6 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ with a low-level fluence of about $1 \mu \mathrm { J } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ to avoid the exciton-exciton annihilation. Figure 3 and Figure S9 summarize the TA results probed in the NIR wavelength region from 800 to $1 3 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . All three blends have the broad absorption (PIA) bands centered at around $1 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . These PIA signals originated from the polaron generated after the excitation8, 1 illustrate the deay dynamics we applied the model involving monomolecular and bimolecular recombination to fit the polaron dynamics.30-32 Previous studies have revealed that charge density is way lower than that under solar light to trigger significant bimolecular recombination under weak incident light intensity, so that the charge carrier mainly decay through the trap-induced recombination.13,14 Therefore, here we assume that trap-induced recombination is the only carrier decay channel under extremely low light fluence:

$$
\frac { \mathrm { d } n ( t ) } { \mathrm { d } t } = - k _ { 1 } n ( t )
$$

where $n ( t )$ is the charge carrier density as the function of time and $k _ { 1 }$ is the rate of trap-induced recombination. The solution of the equation is a single exponential decay function. When the excitation light is sufficiently high to generate enough charge to raise the bimolecular recombination, the charge density decays as follows

$$
- \frac { \mathrm { d } n ( t ) } { \mathrm { d } t } = k _ { 1 } n ( t ) - \frac { 1 } { 2 } k _ { 2 } n ( t ) ^ { 2 }
$$

with the solution of

$$
n ( t ) = \frac { n ( 0 ) \mathrm { e } ^ { ( - k _ { 1 } t ) } } { 1 + \frac { k _ { 2 } } { 2 k _ { 1 } } n ( 0 ) [ 1 - \mathrm { e } ^ { ( - k _ { 1 } t ) } ] }
$$

where $k _ { 2 }$ is the bimolecular decay rate. To determine the recombination rates, pump-fluence dependent TA spectra were measured, and the dynamics at $1 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ were shown in Figure $3 c - 3 e$ The decay dynamics pumped with low fluence were fitted by single exponential function with $k _ { 1 }$ values of $4 . 6 3 ~ \times$ $1 0 ^ { 9 }$ $5 . { \dot { 4 } } 3 \ \times \ 1 0 ^ { 9 }$ , and $1 9 . 4 \times 1 0 ^ { 9 } \ \mathrm { p s ^ { - 1 } }$ for PBDB-T:BTA3, PBDB-TF:BTA3, and PBDB-TCl:BTA3 blends, respectively. As the pump fluence increases to excite $n ( 0 )$ exceeding $1 \times$ $1 0 ^ { 1 7 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 3 }$ , the polaron dynamics are fitted well using eq 3 with the increased $k _ { 2 }$ values. The fitting details were collected in Table S7. Under the pump fluence of $1 6 . 1 ~ \mu \mathrm { J ~ c m } ^ { - 2 } .$ the $k _ { 2 }$ of PBDB-T:BTA3, PBDB-TF:BTA3, and PBDB-TCl:BTA3 blends are $7 . 0 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 9 }$ $7 . 8 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 9 }$ , and $5 . 7 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 9 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 3 }$ $\mathrm { p s } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively. Based on the results, it indicates that the density or energetic distribution of trap states are different in the three blends. The charge carriers in PBDB-T:BTA3 blend are least affected by the trap states under low light fluence, while the blends of PBDB-TF:BTA3 and PBDB-TCl:BTA3 exhibits exacerbated trap-induced recombination, leading to the reduced device current densities under indoor light source. With the rising of light intensity, the bimolecular recombination takes the upper hand since the model assumes that the trap-induced recombination rate is free from the influence of light fluence. The variations in recombination behavior can be attributed to the differences in energy disorder among the three photovoltaic blends as the subsequent discussion.

Temperature-related photoluminescence (PL) and electroluminescence (EL) spectra were also measured to support the intensive energy disorders in PBDB-TF and PBDB-TCl based blends (Figure S10).38 At low temperatures, the thermally activated energy disorder can be suppressed. Thus, the emission spectra exhibit separated spectral peaks with narrow line-width. Gaussian fittings of the long-wavelength located peaks show that PBDBT has the narrowest edge emission feature while PBDB-TCl has the widest in both PL and EL spectra. This can be the quantitative evidence to support the various DoS among the applied $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ blends.

As discussed in $J { - } V$ and TA characteristics, the influence of the trap states in BHJ film holds different percentages under solar radiation and indoor illumination. To directly detect the difference, the dependence of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and light intensity under $\mathrm { A M } ~ 1 . 5 ~ \mathrm { G }$ and $2 7 0 0 ~ \mathrm { K }$ LED lamp were measured and plotted in Figure 5d. Generally, the slope of the linear fit will be close to $k \bar { T } / e$ when the trap-induced recombination is suppressed.39 The calculated slopes under AM $1 . 5 \ : \mathrm { G }$ are 1.13, 1.24, and 1.33 $k T / e$ for PBDB-T, PBDB-TF, and PBDB-TCl-based devices, respectively. When it comes to the LED light, the corresponding slopes increase to 2.75, 4.16, and $3 . { \overset { \cdot } { 8 } } 9 ~ k T / e$

The significantly enlarged values imply that trap-induced recombination is prominent under weak incident light intensity, while bimolecular carrier recombination is the primary loss channel under solar radiation to degrade the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values in PBDB-TF and PBDB-TCl devices. Under both scenes, PBDB-T-based cells exhibit the most suppressed trap-induced recombination, while the trap impacts are severely increased in PBDB-TF and PBDB-TCl-based blends. The charge carrier densities in the BHJ films under LED light were calculated to verify the recombination effect. The measure was based on the capacitance-frequency dependence under changing applied bias (details in Supporting Information) with results exhibited in Figure S13.40,41 The PBDB-Tbased device has the highest capacitance level under $1 0 0 0 \ \mathrm { l u x }$ LED illumination and the PBDB-TF and PBDB-TCl exhibit reduced capacitance successively. At zero bias, the charge carrier densities were integrated with $5 . 0 7 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 5 }$ $2 . 7 9 \times 1 0 ^ { \mathrm { { 1 5 } } }$ and $1 . 7 1 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 5 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 3 }$ for PBDB-T:BTA3, PBDB-TF:BTA3, and PBDB-TCl:BTA3, respectively. In comparison with the reported results measured under solar irradiation, the densities are $_ { 2 - 3 }$ orders lower.42 The decreasing carrier densities further prove that trap-induced recombination is the dominant factor in photocurrent generation under indoor weak illumination.

Based on the results illustrated above, the photovoltaic performance of indoor OPV cells is sensitive to the trap states through influencing the charge transport and recombination dynamics under low illumination. According to the GDM, the wider DoS of charge hopping sites can induce charge recombination and works as trap states. The enlarged energetic disorder (PBDB-TF:BTA3 and PBDB-TCl:BTA3 blends in this work) can introduce deeper trap states that intensify the trap-induced recombination during the charge transport. Under indoor weak illuminations, the trap-induced recombination is "amplified' since the bimolecular recombination is naturally suppressed. As a result, the photovoltaic blends with significant energy disorders can only acquire degraded $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF and enlarged nonradiative energy loss under indoor illuminations. We also blended the PBDB-T series donors with two other nonfullerene acceptors, O-IDTBR and ITIC, to investigate the indoor photovoltaic performances (Figure S14 and Table S9). The results manifest a similar trend with the BTA3 based devices, indicating a widespread impact of the trap states on indoor OPV cells. The origins of trap state in concern of molecular structure can be ascribed to the following: 1) Rigidity and planarity of donor and acceptor molecules. The covalent bonds connecting molecule backbones can be twisted or broken during the formation of D:A blends, which can induce energy disorders as charge traps in effecting the charge recombination dynamics.43 2) Electrostatic potential distribution of the molecules. The electrostatic hole-- electron interaction can arise significant static energy disorder, so that donor-acceptor binding configurations should be manipulated, for instance, introducing electron-withdrawing component, to reduce the negative effects on the nonradiative recombination. Recent works have verified that molecules with improved planarity can effectively suppress the nonradiative energy loss and recombination rate to support the high performance of OPV devices.44 The reported designs of the electrostatic potential distributions have also proven that the intermolecular interaction is critical in turning the chargetransfer state in highly effcient OPV cells.46,47 The torsional flexibility and electrostatic potential distributions should be taken into consideration in the further design of OPV molecules for indoor applications. Furthermore, this work solely focuses on the trap at the shallow tail states rather than the midgap trap states. Recent research has proposed that the recombination from the trap state located $0 . 3 5 { - } 0 . 6 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ below the transport edges can be switched off at low incident light intensities.8 Further efforts are required to separate and clarify the effects from different types of trap states to assist future research in the indoor OPV community.

In summary, by combining photovoltaic characterization, TA spectroscopy, energy loss, and energetic distribution measurements, we clarify the influences of the trap states and the related recombination kinetics in a series of widebandgap D/A systems for indoor application. The results show that the PBDB-T series polymer donor blended with BTA3 acceptor have different scales of energy disorder that induce various distributed trap states. These differences lead to the changes in the trap-induced recombination rate and further result in the significantly changed Enon-rad and charge carrier density. In contrast with the OPV cells working under sun light where bimolecular recombination has the dominating position, trap-induced recombination under indoor weak illumination is severely enlarged that limits the photo current generation. Besides the principles of energy loss restriction and optical spectra overlapping, the findings of this work complement the trap effect of OPV cells working under indoor low light fluence and provide new insights about trap state control in material design and device construction toward efficient indoor OPV cells.
