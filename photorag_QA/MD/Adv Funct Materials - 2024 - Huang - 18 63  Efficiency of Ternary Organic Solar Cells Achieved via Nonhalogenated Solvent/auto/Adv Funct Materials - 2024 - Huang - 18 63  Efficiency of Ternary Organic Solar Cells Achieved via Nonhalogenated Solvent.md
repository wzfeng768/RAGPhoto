# 18.63% Efficiency of Ternary Organic Solar Cells Achieved via Nonhalogenated Solvent and Hot Spin-Coating Process

Tianhuan Huang, Shuang Geng, Dongjie Wang, Yang Zhang, Nan Weng, Xiao Li, Qiaogan Liao, Zheling Zhang, Jiangrong Lu,\* and Jian Zhang

The solubility problem of conjugated polymers in nonhalogenated solvents limits the application of nonhalogenated solvents in Organic solar cells (OSCs). By combining with a hot spin-coating process, efficient PM6:BTP-BO-4F:GS-ISO ternary OSCs are prepared by employing o-xylene as a solvent. After adding $10 \%$ content of GS-ISO to PM6:BTP-BO-4F, the surface morphology in the active layers is improved and the charge extraction, transport, and collection in OsCs are enhanced. Compared with the efficiency of PM6:BTP-BO-4F OSCs $( 1 6 . 2 5 \% )$ , an efficiency of $1 8 . 6 3 \%$ is achieved for PM6:BTP-BO-4F:GS-ISO ternary OSCs. Moreover, PM6:BTP-BO-4F:GS-ISO ternary OsCs with an efficiency of $1 4 . 1 3 \%$ are prepared in air. The work provides a new strategy for preparing efficient and environmentally friendly OSCs using green solvents.

# 1. Introduction

Organic solar cells (OsCs) have attracted special attention from researchers thanks to their solution processability, color, semitransparency, light weight, and flexibility.[1-5] In recent years, with significant progress in material synthesis, interface engineering, and device engineering, the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of OsCs based on halogenated solvent treatment has been over $1 9 \%$ [6-11] However, most highly efficient OSCs are prepared using halogenated solvents such as chlorobenzene and chloroform at this stage.14,12,13] However, such solvents not only cause harm to human health and the environment, but also increase the fabrication cost of OsCs due to their complicated post-processing procedures. Meanwhile, the use of these toxic solvents will affect the preparation of large-area devices.[14,15] Therefore, the use of halogenated solvents has undermined the advantages of OsCs and hindered their commercial application. Hence, developing a process based on environmentally friendly green solvents is crucial for the development of OSCs.[16-19]

In general, green solvents are nonhalogenated, high-boiling solvents such as toluene (TOL), o-xylene (O-XY), and tetrahydrofuran. However, the solubility of organic photovoltaic materials in nonhalogenated solvents is low, which can easily lead to poor film microstructure, resulting in lower PCE of OSCs prepared with nonhalogenated solvents compared to those prepared with halogenated solvents. This has seriously limited the application of these solvents in OSCs.[14,20] Therefore, the potential for commercialization of OsCs can be further exploited by investigating a preparation process that allows for the use of green solvents for treatment and achieving a high PCE. In addition, it has been shown that the solubility of nonhalogenated solvents for organic materials can be significantly enhanced by simply increasing the stirring and spin-coating temperatures of the solution.121-23] For instance, Peng et al. blended PM6 and BTPBO-4Cl in TOL and improved the solubility of the two materials by increasing the stirring and spin-coating temperature. Meanwhile, the active layer nanofiber morphology is optimized by delaying the treatment time to improve the charge dissociation and transport with an efficiency of $1 7 . 3 3 \%$ [24]

Herein, highly efficient ternary OsCs are prepared in the PM6:BTP-BO-4F binary host system with the addition of the non-fullerene acceptor GS-ISO and the use of a green solvent (O-XY) as the primary solvent. The chemical structures for the three materials are illustrated in Figure 1a. The device structure in this work is shown in Figure 1b. Meanwhile, in order to increase the solubility of O-XY to organic materials and to improve the morphology of the active layer, hot spin-coating is performed at $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ in this work.[21,25] After relevant characterization, it is found that there is good compatibility between GS-ISO and BTP-BO-4F, which is conducive to the formation of an alloy model between the two acceptor materials.[26-28] Meanwhile, improving the morphology of the active layer of the ternary OSCs is favorable for the enhancement of the light capture capability and the improvement of the charge transport and collec tion. As a result, when the content of GS-ISO in the ternary film is $1 0 ~ \mathrm { w t \% }$ , the open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ is $0 . 8 6 5 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ the shortcircuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } )$ is $2 7 . 5 5 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ and the fill factor (FF) is $7 8 . 1 9 \%$ , which ultimately achieves a PCE of up to $1 8 . 6 3 \%$

# 2. Results and Discussion

The energy level diagrams of PM6, BTP-BO-4F, and GS-ISO are illustrated in Figure S1 (Supporting Information), and the corresponding highest occupied molecular orbitals (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LUMO) can be obtained from the relevant literature.29-31] It can be seen that after adding GS-ISO a cascade structure of energy levels can be formed between the three materials, which facilitates exciton dissociation and charge transport to obtain a higher $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ . Figure S2 (Supporting Information) has illustrated the normalized UV-vis absorption spectra of PM6, BTP-BO-4F, and GS-ISO films. The main absorption peak and shoulder peak of PM6 are located at 621 and $5 7 8 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively, while the main absorption range is $4 0 0 { - } 6 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ and the band gap $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } )$ is $1 . 8 0 \mathrm { e V } .$ The maximum absorption peak of GS-ISO is located at $6 3 0 \mathrm { n m }$ , and the absorption range and $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ are similar to that of PM6, which are $4 1 0 { - } 6 6 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ and 1.77 eV, respectively. There is a significant increase in the absorption coefficient of the ternary film in the wavelength range of $5 0 0 { - } 8 4 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . This should be caused by the improved molecular arrangement in the active layer after the addition of GS-ISO.32,33] It is worth noting that the ternary film has stronger absorption properties than the binary film, which helps ternary OSCs to obtain higher photocurrents.[33,34]

The compatibility between the two acceptor materials is investigated by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) testing of BTP-BO-4F, GS-ISO, and BTP-BO-4F:GS-SIO as shown in Figure 2a. The surface energies of PM6, BTP-BO-4F, and GS-ISO are 26.77, 28.37, and $2 8 . 2 3 ~ \mathrm { m N ~ m ^ { - 1 } }$ , respectively. Meanwhile, thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) of BTP-BO4F, GS-ISO, and BTP-BO-4F:GS-SIO showed good thermal stability above $3 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (Figure S4, Supporting Information). In ternary OSCs, the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ will vary with the content of the third component. As can be seen from Figure 3a, the relationship between the change in $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and GS-ISO content shows a linear increase, indicating the formation of an alloy acceptor between BTP-BO-4F and GS-ISO. Due to the small intermolecular distances between organic materials, good compatibility between acceptors is a prerequisite for the formation of alloy acceptors.[41,42] The alloy state can be thought of as an excited state shared by the two materials, determined by the relative content between them. Among OSCs with the same device structure and electrodes, $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ has an important correlation with the difference between the HOMO energy level of the donor and the LUMO energy level of the acceptor.41,43] The LUMO energy level of GS-ISO is higher than that of BTP-BO-4F, leading to an increase in LUMO in the alloying state of the acceptor, and thus the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ increases with increasing GS-ISO content.

The molecular dynamics process between BTP-BO-4F and GSISO can be obtained by investigating the photoluminescence (PL) of BTP-BO-4F film, GS-ISO film, and BTP-BO-4F:GS-ISO film under $6 8 0 \mathrm { n m }$ light excitation, and the test results are illustrated in Figure 3b. The GS-ISO exhibits a strong PL emission peak at $7 0 5 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , while the PL emission peak of BTP-BO-4F is relatively weak. In the blended film, the emission peak of GS-ISO is fully quenched at $7 0 5 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , and the relevant PL emission peak shows a slight blue shift. The PL emission peak of the blended film shows a blue shift, which indicates that a common excited state is formed between GS-ISO and BTP-BO-4F. Meanwhile, the PL emission peaks of the blended film increased with the increase of Gs-IsO content, indicating the existence of energy transfer from GS-ISO to BTP-BO-4F in the active layer.[28] In addition, the absorption spectra of BTP-BO-4F overlap with the PL spectra of GS-IsO, which can effectively support the energy transfer between GS-ISO and BTP-BO-4F,[39] as shown in Figure 3c. To further investigate the molecular dynamics process between BTP-BO-4F and GS-ISO, the $J { - } V$ curves of the pure acceptor devices were tested, as shown in Figure S6 (Supporting Information). Because there is no donor (PM6) in the active layer, the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of the device is relatively low.128,44] The $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of the device with BTP-BO-4F and GS-ISO blending is $0 . 1 3 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , located between the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of the device with only BTP-BO-4F or GS-ISO (0.23 and $0 . 0 3 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , respectively). Therefore, it can be assumed that there is no charge transfer between GS-ISO and BTP-BO4F. Based on the above analysis, there is an energy transfer from GS-ISO to BTP-BO-4F in the active layer. The exciton utiliza tion of ternary OSCs can be further improved by this energy transfer.

By studying the exciton dissociation and charge recombination in the blended film, we can further understand the reasons for the differences of photoelectric properties in different OSCs. The relationship between the photocurrent density $( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } )$ and the effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ was tested. And the obtained curves are shown in Figure 3d. In the low effective region, $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ will increase with the increase of $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ It is assumed that $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ reaches saturation current density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { { s a t } } } \right)$ when $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } \geq 3 . 0 \mathrm { V } _ { \mathrm { i } }$ which indicates that the excitons can be fully dissociated into free carriers and collected by the electrodes at this time. The exciton dissociation efficiency $\left( \eta _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } \right)$ and charge collection efficiency $( \eta _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } )$ can be evaluated by $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ under short-circuit and maximum output power conditions, respectively. For binary, and ternary OsCs, the $\eta _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ values obtained after the calculation are $9 7 . 0 9 \%$ $9 5 . 6 3 \%$ , and $9 7 . 7 9 \%$ , respectively. The higher $\eta _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ value shows that the exciton dissociation effect of ternary OSCs is better.[45-47] In addition, the $\eta _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ value of ternary OSCs is $8 9 . 5 0 \%$ , higher than that of the two binary OSCs $( 8 8 . 1 5 \%$ and $7 3 . 8 0 \% $ . This is similar to the trend of FF changes in OSCs. The detailed calculation results are displayed in Table S3 (Supporting Information). The higher $\eta _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $\eta _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ values indicate that the photoinduced exciton dissociation and charge collection are more efficient in the optimized ternary OsCs, thus contributing to higher $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF.[45-47]

Transient photocurrent (TPC) and transient photovoltage (TPV) are used to further investigate the correlation with charge extraction and complexation in the active layer of OsCs. The correlation decay curves are illustrated in Figure 4a,b. In the TPC decay curves, the charge extraction times $\left( \mathrm { t } _ { \mathrm { r e c } } \right)$ based on PM6:BTPBO-4F and PM6:GS-ISO are 2.128 and 2.997 us, respectively, which are higher than the value of the $\mathrm { t } _ { \mathrm { r e c } }$ based on PM6:BTPBO-4F:GS-ISO (1.873 us). The results show that faster and more efficient charge extraction can be achieved in ternary OsCs, thus higher EQE correspondingly and FF can be obtained.48-50] Meanwhile, the carrier lifetime $( \mathrm { t _ { p h o } } )$ of 11.596 us for the ternary OSCs can be observed through the decay curves in the TPV, and it is higher than the $\mathrm { t _ { p h o } }$ of the two binary OSCs (2.315 and 1.708 us). The higher $\mathrm { t _ { p h o } }$ value indicates that the inhibition effect of charge recombination is more effective in ternary OSCs, which is helpful to improve $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF.[48-50] As a result of the above analysis, the addition of GS-ISO to PM6:BTP-BO-4F can effectively inhibit the charge recombination of OsCs and effectively improve the charge transport and collection efficiency. This is consistent with the above $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } { - } V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ results.

Furthermore, the recombination dynamics of OsCs are investigated by testing the relationship between different light intensities $( { \mathrm { P _ { l i g h t } } } )$ and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } \mathrm { s }$ The $J { - } V$ curves under different $\mathrm { P _ { l i g h t } }$ are displayed in Figure S7 (Supporting Information), and the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } { \cdot } \mathrm { P } _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ curves are shown in Figure 4c. The dependence between $\bar { V } _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and $\mathrm { P _ { l i g h t } }$ can be evaluated by $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } { \propto } \mathrm { n ( k T / q ) } \mathrm { l n } \ \mathrm { ( P _ { l i g h t } ) }$ In the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } { \cdot } \mathrm { P } _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ relationship curves, the slopes (n) based on PM6:BTP-BO-4F and PM6:GS-ISO are 1.24 and 1.39, respectively, whereas the n value for ternary OsCs is 1.11. The lower n values (more closer to 1) indicate that trap-assisted recombination is effectively suppressed in ternary OsCs.51-53] It suggests that the addition of GS-ISO to PM6:BTP-BO-4F can reduce the probability of trap-assisted recombination of OsCs. Meanwhile, the space charge limiting current (SCLC) method is utilized to further study the charge transport characteristics in OSCs. The $J ^ { 1 / 2 } – V$ curves for the pure electron/hole devices are shown in Figure S8 (Supporting Information). The curves and detailed data on the variation of electron mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } \right)$ and hole mobility $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } )$ versus GS-ISO content are shown in Figure 4d and Table S4 (Supporting Information), respectively. This shows that when $1 0 \ \mathrm { w t } \%$ GS-ISO is added to PM6:BTPBO-4F, electrons, and holes are transported more effectively and more balanced, which is helpful for OsCs to obtain higher FF.[35,54]

The morphology of the active layer has a significant impact on the photoelectric performance of OsCs. In this work, atomic force microscopy (AFM) is used to investigate the surface morphology of the blended films. The height and phase images of the blended films are given in Figure 5, while the 3D height image of the surface morphology of the blended film is given in Figure S9 (Supporting Information). The root-mean-square roughness (RMS) of each blended film is labeled on the graph, where the RMS of PM6:BTP-BO-4F, PM6:BTP-BO-4F:GS-ISO, and PM6:GS-ISO films are 1.25, 1.10, and $1 . 5 8 \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. In general, the smoother the surface of the blended film and the smaller the RMS value, the more favorable it is to form a good ohmic contact between the active layer and the electron transport layer. The phase image from AFM shows that the surface of the ternary film exhibits finer nanoscale fibers compared to the two binary films. These results are favorable for the charge transfer efficiency.[55,56] The effect of Gs-1sO on the morphology of the films can be studied by transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Because different substances have different electron densities, they are shown as bright and dark regions in the TEM image.

As seen in Figure 5c, the ternary film exhibits more uniformly distributed light and dark regions compared to the two binary films. This indicates the presence of uniform phase separation and bicontinuous interpenetrating structures, resulting in more efficient charge extraction, transport, and collection.[45,57,58] These results are conducive to improving the FF of OsCs.

It is well known that most of the highly efficient OSCs are currently prepared in a glove box in an $\Nu _ { 2 }$ environment and using halogenated solvents. However, this type of preparation process is not suitable for large-scale production because of the specific processing requirements for its fabrication. Therefore, in order to further develop the application potential of OsCs, this article uses a nonhalogenated solvent (O-XY) and prepares the OSCs in air $5 0 \pm 5 \%$ RH and $2 3 \pm 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ . Figure 6a,b shows the $J -$ $V$ curves and the corresponding EQE curves of OSCs prepared in air, respectively. The detailed parameters can be viewed in Table 2. The $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } , \ J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ , FF, and PCE of binary OSCs based on PM6:BTP-BO-4F are 0.799 V, $2 3 . 6 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ $7 6 . 2 9 \%$ , and $1 2 . 6 9 \%$ , respectively. The efficiency of PM6:GS-ISO-based binary OSCs is very low at $2 . 8 5 \%$ . In contrast, the ternary OSCs with 10 $\mathrm { w t \% }$ GS-ISO content had a $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 2 3 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 4 . 4 9 ~ \mathrm { m A }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and an FF of $7 0 . 0 9 \%$ , resulting in a final efficiency of $1 4 . 1 3 \%$ . This efficiency is more than $7 5 \%$ of the efficiency of ternary OSCs $( \Nu _ { 2 } )$ . Here, there is a significant decrease in $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ for ternary OSCs (air) compared to ternary OSCs $( \Nu _ { 2 } )$ . In order to better understand the reason for the decrease in $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ , the nonradiative voltage loss $( \Delta \mathsf { V } _ { \mathrm { n r } } )$ is further investigated by measuring the electroluminescence quantum efficiency $( \mathrm { E Q E _ { E L } } )$ for the two preparation conditions. Where $\Delta \mathsf { V } _ { \mathrm { n r } }$ can be determined by the equation $\Delta \mathrm { V } _ { \mathrm { n r } } = \mathrm { ~ \Omega ~ } - \mathrm { k T / q l n }$ $( \mathrm { E Q E _ { \mathrm { E L } } } )$ [60,61 The $\mathrm { E Q E } _ { \mathrm { E L } }$ value of ternary OSCs $( \Nu _ { 2 } )$ is $1 0 . 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 }$ , which is higher than that of ternary OSCs (air) $( 6 . 6 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } )$ , as shown in Figure S12 (Supporting Information). Their corresponding $\Delta \mathsf { V } _ { \mathrm { n r } }$ values are 0.236 and $0 . 2 4 6 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ respectively. The $\Delta \mathsf { V } _ { \mathrm { n r } }$ of the ternary OSCs $( \Nu _ { 2 } )$ is $1 0 \mathrm { m V }$ less than that of the ternary (air). In addition, the total energy loss $( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } )$ of OsCs can be evaluated by the equation $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } = E _ { \mathrm { g } } \ - \ - \ q V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ [45] Here, $\mathrm { E _ { g } }$ can be obtained from the relevant EQE curves.[62] The $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the ternary OSCs $( \Nu _ { 2 } )$ are $1 . 3 4 1 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ and $0 . 8 6 3 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ while the corresponding values for the ternary OsCs (air) are $1 . 3 4 8 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and $0 . 8 2 6 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ respectively. It is found that the $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of ternary OSCs $( \Nu _ { 2 } )$ $( 0 . 4 7 8 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ is 44 meV less than that of ternary OsCs (air) $( 0 . 5 2 2 \ : \mathrm { e V } )$ . Meanwhile, the integrated currents of PM6:BTP-BO-4F and PM6:BTP-BO4F:GS-ISO obtained from EQE are 21.68 and $2 3 . 6 4 ~ \mathrm { m A ~ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ respectively. These values are within $5 \%$ error of the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ obtained from the $J { - } V$ curves. This result indicates that the PM6:BTP-BO4F:GS-ISO system can prepare highly efficient OSCs in air as well.

# 3. Conclusion

In this work, a nonhalogenated solvent (O-XY) is used as a solvent, and a hot spin-coating $( 1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ process is used to prepare a highly efficient ternary OSCs. In order to enhance the photovoltaic properties of PM6:BTP-BO-4F binary OSCs, the acceptor material Gs-IsO is added as the third component to this host binary OSCs. It is found that good compatibility between BTPBO-4F and GS-IsO facilitates the formation of alloy acceptors in the active layer, optimizes the surface morphology and molecular stacking of the active layer. At the same time, there is an energy transfer from GS-ISO to BTP-BO-4F in the active layer. These results induced a significant enhancement of charge ex traction, transport, and collection in ternary OsCs, as well as a more balanced charge transport, resulting in higher $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } , J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ , and FF. Compared with the host binary OSCs, when adding $1 0 \mathrm { w t \% }$ of GS-ISO content, the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of ternary OSCs is enhanced from 0.840 to $0 . 8 6 5 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ from 25.41 to $2 7 . 5 5 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , the FF from $7 6 . 0 7 \%$ to $7 8 . 1 9 \%$ , and the highest PCE from $1 6 . 2 5 \%$ to $1 8 . 6 3 \%$ In addition, an efficiency of $1 4 . 1 3 \%$ is obtained by applying this ternary system and preparation process to OsCs prepared in air. The result of this study provides new paths for the preparation of highly efficient and environmentally friendly OsCs with nonhalogenated solvents.
