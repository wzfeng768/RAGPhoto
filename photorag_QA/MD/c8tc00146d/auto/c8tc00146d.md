# Modification of acenes for n-channel OFET materials

Arun Naibi Lakshminarayana, $\textcircled{1}$ Albert Ong and Chunyan Chi

With their electron-rich skeletons, acenes have traditionally been used as hole transporting materials in organic electronic applications. However, prudent structural modifications and revision of the structure of devices can yield good electron mobilities from acene-based organic semiconductors (OSCs). The majority of OSCs predominantly exhibit hole transporting behaviour and there is a scarcity of efficient and operationally stable n-type materials. Organic field effect transistors (OFETs) have been proved to be commercially viable alternatives to their inorganic counterparts, and there is increased need for efficient electron transporting materials in n-channel OFETs to build complementary circuit elements. This has boosted thorough investigation into acene-based n-type materials. In this review, various strategies are discussed for chemical modification of acenes for n-channel OFETs.

# General design strategies for n-channel OFETs

Unlike inorganic semiconductors, in which the n-type and p-type materials are differentiated essentially based on the external dopants, organic semiconductors (OSCs) are classified as n-type or p-type depending on the accumulation of charges (electrons or holes) when a gate voltage (positive or negative) is applied.17 Despite most OSCs being intrinsically ambipolar (capable of transporting both electrons and holes),18 they exhibit predominantly p-type behavior, primarily because of higher electron injection barrier than hole injection barrier from commonly used metal electrodes. Therefore, to design n-channel OFET materials the following issues must be considered.

# (a) Frontier energies and technical aspects

Even though the nature and working principle of OSCs are unique and fundamentally different from those of inorganic semiconductors, their fabrication processes are similar to those used for silicon-based materials.17 To maximize efficiency from OSCs, the entire device processing has to be re-examined and optimized. This requires thorough fundamental study of organic material-based device physics. Principles of charge transportation in OSCs can be understood by looking at their frontier energies and the Fermi energy $\left( E _ { \mathrm { F } } \right)$ of the source electrode (Fig. 1).19,20 When the HOMO energy is situated closer to the $\mathbf { E } _ { \mathrm { F } }$ of the electrode (Fig. 1a), hole injection is facilitated because of the small hole injection barrier $\left( \phi _ { \mathrm { h } } \right)$ , resulting in p-type behavior. For OSCs with smaller band gaps, both hole and electron injection barriers are small or within the limits of operating voltage, resulting in the ambipolar behavior of such materials (Fig. 1b). When the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy of OSCs is near to the $E _ { \mathrm { F } }$ of source electrode, injection of electrons into the LUMO of the semiconductor is facilitated because of the small electron injection barrier height $\left( \phi _ { \mathrm { e } } \right)$ (Fig. 1c), resulting in n-type behavior. Therefore, molecular frontier energies play a major role in determining the n-channel FET activity of a material.

From a technical point of view, interfaces between different adjoining substructures in a device are of great importance in determining device behavior and performance.21 Therefore, the nature of the interaction in such interfaces plays an important role in determining various parameters such as mobility $( \mu )$ , on/off current ratio $\left( I _ { \mathrm { o n / o f f } } \right)$ and threshold voltage $\left( V _ { \mathrm { T h } } \right)$ . For instance, charge accumulation and transport take place close to the interface between the gate dielectric and the semiconductor. Ideal gate dielectric materials are expected to have high dielectric breakdown strength and high dielectric constant e (which determines the capacitance $C _ { \mathrm { i } }$ ). Residual impurities and other charge traps can hinder mobility. A commonly used dielectric is $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ $\mathit { \Omega } _ { \mathcal { E } } = 3 . 9$ ), which is readily available and is of high quality with good reproducibility. However, active hydroxyl groups on the surface (silanol) irreversibly trap electrons accumulated on the interface, severely hampering the electron mobility and increasing the threshold voltage beyond an operationally accessible range. An efficient way to solve this problem is to alter the surface properties by coating the dielectric surface with a modifying agent to passivate silanol groups. A lot of work has been done on developing various self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) of organosilanes and phosphonic acids.17,21,22 Surface energy and roughness of dielectric layer determine the molecular ordering, orientation and morphology of organic thin films in bottom gate devices where organic semiconductors are deposited. Apart from surface passivation, SAMs also serve to facilitate custom-modification of the dielectric surface by controlling structure and dielectric properties such as surface dipole and trap states, thereby significantly improving device performance.

# (b) Molecular aspects

Acenes are well-known hole transporting materials, with electron rich aromatic cores. Members of this family have been used as p-type semiconductors in OFETs, with incredible device performance. Theoretically, organic semiconductors cannot be considered as strictly n-type or p-type materials. Common p-type polymers such as poly $( p$ -phenylenevinylene) (PPV) and poly(3- hexylthiophene) (P3HT), have shown n-type behavior when fabricated using contact electrodes with low work functions and surface passivated dielectrics.18

The frontier molecular orbital, especially the LUMO energy of the acene moiety plays a crucial role in determining the performance of n-channel OFETs. The electron injection barrier between the source electrode and OSC should be small, to allow efficient injection of electrons into the semiconductor from an environmentally stable electrode such as gold (with high work function). Secondly, anions generated after electron injection into the semiconducting layer are highly susceptible to oxygen and water, leading to severe decline in device performance.17 A LUMO energy of ca. $- 4 \ \mathrm { e V }$ or lower is required to realize stable electron transport performance for an OSC, which has been theoretically proven and empirically demonstrated.17,23,24 In the following section, many such acene based n-type semiconductors are detailed to display improved stability.

Another important factor that has a significant influence on the stability and field effect mobility is the molecular packing of the organic semiconductor in the solid state. Innately, most of the neat acenes, in their solid state, arrange themselves in a herringbone motif, which is dominated by edge-to-face interaction.4 However, studies show that face-to-face $\pi$ -stacking with shorter intermolecular distances improves charge migration and yields better field effect mobility. Again, depending on the degree of face-to-face and edge-to-face interaction along with other electrostatic and weak intermolecular bonding, a variety of molecular packing motifs can be seen in single crystals. Fig. 2 depicts four common classifications of molecular packing in the solid state of a typical conjugated aromatic organic semiconductor.25 Among these, two-dimensional $\pi - \pi$ stacking is known to facilitate easier charge migration, resulting in good device performance. There have been several reports that incorporation of fluoroalkyl chains also makes the molecules more compact, increasing ambient stability by creating a kinetic barrier against moisture and air diffusing into the bulk of the material.26 However, favorable molecular packing does not guarantee high mobility of that molecule. It is necessary to have strong electronic coupling between adjacent molecules to show efficient charge transport. This, in turn, depends on the relative position, phase and nodal properties of frontier molecular orbitals. All these parameters should be taken into account when designing a strategy to engineer a molecule as an n-type material for field effect transistor applications.

# Modifications of acenes for n-channel field effect transistors

Acenes are basically electron-rich systems exhibiting global aromaticity along their perimeters. Their high electron density means that these molecules have been used traditionally as hole transporting materials. Pentacene, 2 (Chart 1) is the most widely studied organic semiconductor for FET applications. High order acenes (acenes longer than pentacene) are highly susceptible to photo-oxidation, and, hence, are used less frequently in

OFET studies. Pentacene is also highly sensitive to oxygen in the presence of light. Photodegradation of pentacene is particularly severe in solution.27 Therefore, many research groups have attempted to stabilize acene moieties by various functionalization methodologies. The present review summarizes design strategies for n-type acenes. As discussed in the previous section, the general idea is to induce electron deficiency to develop an acene-based n-type material by adding electron withdrawing functionalities. Careful tuning of LUMO energy level below $- 4 . 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ improves the electron injection efficiency of the system and also increases the ambient stability against atmospheric oxygen and moisture. Some of the recent reviews on molecular design for a wide range of n-type semiconductors offer thoughtful insights on nature and structure–property relationship of OSCs.28–33 It is also important to incorporate solubilizing groups to improve the solution processability of the material, as the industrial interest lies in development of molecules compatible with solution processing techniques for large-scale manufacturing. Additionally, factors affecting film morphology and molecular packing must be considered to achieve better efficiency. Major molecular modification strategies are discussed in the following section, with appropriate examples.

# (a) Halogenated acenes

Addition of halogens such as fluorine and chlorine to acenes results in deepening of the LUMO energy level.34 By controlling the number of halogens added and the positions at which these are incorporated, electron affinity can be substantially increased, facilitating the electron injection. Fluorine, being highly electronegative with less steric demand, aids in decreasing the LUMO, thereby stabilizing anions generated during electron injection.

Perfluorination is a strategy commonly used to increase the electron affinities of simple aromatic systems. Perfluorotetracene (5) and perfluoropentacene (6) were synthesized, with LUMO energy levels around $- 3 . 3 1 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ and $- 3 . 6 7 ~ \mathrm { { \ e V } }$ , respectively (Chart 2 and Table 1).35,36 Their crystal structures exhibit the common feature of close cofacial packing. The minimum interplanar $\pi - \pi$ distances in perfluorotetracene (3.27 Å) and perfluoropentacene (3.21 Å) are shorter than the layer separation of graphite (3.35 Å), probably because of electrostatic interaction between electropositive acene moieties and electronegative fluorine atoms.36 Top-contact devices of 6 fabricated on octadecyltrimethoxysilane (OTS)-treated dielectric $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ exhibited an electron mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } \right)$ of $0 . 1 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ under vacuum and $I _ { \mathrm { o n / o f f } }$ of ${ { 1 0 } ^ { 5 } }$ .35 The device performance was further improved after optimizing the fabrication conditions, achieving a mobility of $0 . 2 2 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ . 36 However, perfluorotetracene 5 showed a much lower electron mobility of $2 . 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ because of the poor quality of the perfluorotetracene thin films.

TIPS-pentacene, 3 (Chart 1) introduced a new outlook in acene chemistry. Various silylethynyl substituted acenes and heteroacenes were synthesized following Anthony’s work.37 Insertion of the silylethynyl group enhances the stability of the electron-rich acene skeleton by reducing the LUMO energy level as sp carbons are more electronegative than $\mathsf { s p } ^ { 2 }$ carbons. Appropriate silylethynyl functionalization has been shown to reduce the triplet energy, thereby preventing singlet oxygen sensitization and, in turn, improving the photooxidation resistance.38 The alkyl groups of TIPS improve the solubility of the parent acenes considerably. Furthermore, spherical trialkylsilyl groups arrange themselves in such a way to permit exquisite control over molecular packing by forcing the acenes to assume face-to-face $\pi$ -stacking and keeping the acene skeleton sufficiently away from insulating alkyl groups because of the presence of the ethynyl group.4 A series of halogenated triisopropylsilyl (TIPS) incorporated acenes (7a–7c) and thienoacenes (8a–8d) were studied by Bao et al., in an attempt to understand the effect of halogenation on FET behavior of the synthesized molecules.34,39–42 Valuable results were obtained from this work, displaying a clear degree correlation of halogenation between LUMO energy level and FET behavior. For OFETs with OTStreated substrates and top contact gold electrodes, studies revealed that molecules with higher LUMO (higher than $- 3 . 1 5 \ \mathrm { e V }$ ) exhibited p-type behavior because of large barriers to electron injection. Once the LUMO dropped below $- 3 . 1 5 \ \mathrm { e V }$ , both p-type and n-type (i.e., ambipolar) operations could be observed. When the LUMO was below $- 3 . 1 5 \ \mathrm { ~ e V }$ and the HOMO was below $- 5 . 6 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ , the materials exhibited strictly n-type behavior, which was attributed to large hole injection barriers.42 A number of derivatives, such as $\mathbf { 8 b , ^ { 4 0 } \ 8 d } ,$ , and $\mathbf { 7 c ^ { 4 1 } }$ showed electron mobility in excess of $0 . 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ with good $I _ { \mathrm { o n / o f f } }$ $\left( \geq 1 0 ^ { 5 } \right)$ , although threshold voltages were relatively high (50–90 V).41 It was noted that the quality of the film arising from OTS-treated $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ dielectric was critical to device performance.

# (b) Cyanated acenes

The cyano group, being highly electron withdrawing, significantly lowers the LUMO of aromatic systems such as acenes 11–15 (Chart 3). Additionally, it favors dense, face-to-face molecular packing,46–50 an important factor for efficient charge transport in organic materials. Moreover, cyanation imparts high thermal stability of materials, which enables processing at elevated temperatures. A common problem is insolubility of the cyanated acenes, which limits the possibility of using the molecules for solution-processible applications. The 5-cyanotetracene (11) and 5,11-dicyanotetracene (12) were first synthesized in 2009 by A. Li et al.46 They have good air stability. The reorganization energy of 12 predicted by UB3LYP/6-311g(d,p) is 0.0881 eV, which is the lowest among the existing compounds. Based on the theoretical model,51 the calculated hole mobilities $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } \right)$ of these air-stable cyanated tetracene derivatives are $2 . 9 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \thinspace \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for 11 and $2 . 2 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for 12. In 2011, Uno et al. reported synthesis of dicyano tetracene, 13 and symmetrically cyanated pentacene, 14 and studied their electronic properties.47 13 was obtained as a red needle-shaped crystal and displayed face-to-face slipped $\pi$ -stacking in the solid state. As expected, both 13 and 14 exhibited deep LUMO of $- 4 . 0 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and $- 4 . 1 4 \ \mathrm { { \ e V } _ { \mathrm { { ; } } } }$ , respectively. Vapor-deposited thin films of 13 and 14 in top contact devices on hexamethyldisilazane (HMDS)-treated $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ layer exhibited ambipolar transport, with $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ of $1 . 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of $1 . 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 }$ for 13, and $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ of $3 . 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of $3 . 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for 14 (Table 1). Glo¨cklhofer et al. further improved the synthesis, reporting one-pot synthesis of 14 and 15 from corresponding acenequinones.48 While the LUMO $\left( - 4 . 1 6 \mathrm { e V } \right)$ of 14 remained similar to that of the previous report, the LUMO energy level of 15 was found to be as deep as $- 4 . 5 4 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ , indicating the significant impact of addition of four cyano groups onto the pentacene core. Crystallographic investigations indicate that the bulk assemblies of both cyanated pentacenes are dominated by non-covalent interactions, resulting in dense, stable, face-to-face packing. Similar to the previous report, 14 exhibited ambipolar charge transport. Newly synthesized 15 behaved as an n-type semiconductor, with electron mobility around $2 . 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , illustrating the gradual progression from p-type to ambipolar to n-type behavior with subsequent addition of –CN groups onto peri positions of acenes.

# (c) N-Heteroacenes

Heteroacenes are a type of acenes with one or more carbon atoms of their skeleton replaced with hetero atoms such as N, O, B, S, P. Among them, N-heteroacenes (or aza-acenes) have been widely studied, and are highly promising n-type acenebased semiconductors. The synthesis, chemistry, and transistor performance of electron-deficient aza-acenes have been well summarized in the literature.52–58 Herein, the present authors would like to highlight a number of aza-acenes which showed n-type FET behavior with good performance. Again, poor solubility and stability limit the solution-based fabrication of these aza-acenes. An interesting aspect is that the molecular packing and electronic behavior of N-heteroacenes can be altered and fine-tuned by changing the position, number and valency of nitrogen atoms incorporated in them. The idea here is to replace carbon with electron-deficient pyrazine-type nitrogen to increase their electron affinity.

Isoda et al. synthesized tetraaza-tetracene and pentacene (16 and 17, Chart 4, respectively). The LUMO energy level is $- 4 . 0 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for 16 and $- 4 . 1 4 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for 17. 16 adopts a herringbone packing structure with a face-to-edge arrangement, and 17 forms a one-dimensional columnar structure. Both behaved as n-type materials, albeit with very low electron mobilities of ${ \bf 8 . 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } c m ^ { 2 } V ^ { - 1 } } { \bf s ^ { - 1 } }$ for 16 and $3 . 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 }$ for 17 in vapor-deposited thin films with top contact geometry (Table 2).59 The $I _ { \mathrm { o n / o f f } }$ was extremely low, in the range of 90–800. Silylethynylated N-heteropentacenes 18 and 19 were prepared by Miao’s group.60 18 has LUMO energy level at $- 4 . 0 1 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and forms a 2D brickwork $\pi$ -stacking in the solid state. Interestingly, its constitutional isomer, 19 showed a distinctly different LUMO of $- 3 . 9 8 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ with molecules arranged in unique bilayer $\pi$ -stacks (with $\mathrm { C - H \cdot \cdot \cdot N }$ hydrogen bonds), which are further arranged in a herringbone motif. Vacuum-deposited films of 18 on octadecyltrimethoxysilane (OTMS)-treated $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ exhibited n-type FET behavior with a maximum electron mobility of $3 . 3 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ in vacuum. Under the same fabrication conditions, 19 showed ambipolar behavior, with hole mobility of $0 . 2 2 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ in ambient air and slightly higher electron mobility of $1 . 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ under vacuum. This clearly suggests that the position of nitrogen in the acene backbone plays an important role in determining molecular packing, electronic behavior and charge carrier mobility. Later, 18 was reported to show improved performance by interface engineering.61 Solution-processed OFETs fabricated on 12-cyclohexyldodecylphosphonic acid (CDPA)-treated $\mathbf { A l O x } / \mathbf { S i O } _ { 2 }$ exhibited an average electron mobility of $7 . 6 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ with the maximum up to $1 1 ~ \mathrm { { c m } } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { { V } } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { { s } } ^ { - 1 }$ under vacuum. They displayed very good operational stability in ambient conditions, with a mobility of $2 . 5 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \thinspace \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace s ^ { - 1 }$ when measured in air.61 The enhanced mobility of the OTFTs on CDPA-modified $\mathbf { A l O x } / \mathbf { S i O } _ { 2 }$ can be attributed to reduction of hydroxyl groups and nitrate anions in the dielectric,62 which are known as electron traps to depress electron mobility.63 Li et al. grew high-quality single crystals of 18 directly on divinyltetramethyldisiloxane–bis(benzocyclobutene) (BCB)-covered $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { S i }$ substrates,64 where the BCB layer $\sim 1 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ thick) was found to efficiently eliminate the electron traps on $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ . 65 The highest electron mobility of $1 3 . 3 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ was achieved when the devices were tested in a nitrogen atmosphere. The high mobility mainly resulted from reducing the amount of the polar solvent residues in the crystals by heat treatment, as trace amounts of polar solvent residues in the crystals ruined the electron conductance.

Tetrachlorodiazapentacene, 23 synthesized by Tao et al., is another air stable n-channel semiconductor with slipped $\pi - \pi$ stacking in the solid state and a LUMO of $- 3 . 7 9 ~ \mathrm { e V } . ^ { 6 \mathfrak { c } }$ Highly flexible and bendable needle-like single crystals of 23 exhibited good electron mobility up to $3 . 3 9 ~ { \mathrm { c m } } ^ { 2 } ~ { \mathrm { V } } ^ { - 1 } ~ { \mathrm { s } } ^ { - 1 }$ under ambient conditions when fabricated on parylene dielectric with colloidal graphite as electrode contacts and gate. Chlorination was employed to reduce the LUMO of heteroacenes by synthesizing several cyclobutadiquinoxalines, 24a–f, which are structurally similar to tetraaza-pentacene but are much more stable.70 Single crystals of all the molecules were grown from either solution or physical vapor transport, except for 24c, 24d and 24e which formed mixed crystals. Obtained crystals displayed varying degrees of $\pi - \pi$ cofacial stacking and all the synthesized molecules possessed low-lying LUMO in the range of $- 3 . 9 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ to $- 4 . 1 4 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ (Table 2), making those ideal candidates for n-type semiconducting materials. FETs were fabricated by thermal evaporation of synthesized molecules onto CDPA-treated dielectric of aluminum oxide and titanium oxide (AlOx/TiOx), using gold as contact electrodes. ${ \bf 2 4 d / e }$ mixture and 24e showed good electron mobilities of $0 . 2 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $0 . 4 2 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ under vacuum, respectively. Miao’s group recently reported synthesis of extended N-heteroacenes with four-membered rings, 25a and 25b, which exhibited deep LUMO of $- 4 . 3 3 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and $- 4 . 4 2 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ , respectively.71 Single crystals of 25b were obtained, and XRD analysis identified the presence of one-dimensional face-to-face $\pi$ -stacking with intermolecular distance of about $3 . 3 1 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ , making this a good system for n-channel FET study. Devices were fabricated using dip-coated 25a and 25b films onto a layer of aluminum oxide and silicon oxide passivated with CDPA using gold as contact electrode. 25a exhibited electron mobility around $1 . 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , while 25b displayed a better performance one order of magnitude higher than the former $\left( 1 . 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 2 } \mathrm { ~ c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { ~ V ~ } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { ~ s } ^ { - 1 } \right)$

Among other heteroacenes, electron-deficient derivatives of triphenodioxazine (TPDO) have been reported to show good electron transport behavior. 26a with ${ \bf - C F } _ { 3 }$ groups para to the nitrogen atoms and 26b with $- \mathrm { C F } _ { 3 }$ groups situated para to the oxygen atoms were synthesized by Di et al. in 2008.72 Vacuumdeposited thin film transistors of 26a and 26b exhibited mobilities up to $0 . 0 7 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $0 . 0 3 \ : \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ : \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ : \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively. Both molecules have identical LUMO of about $- 3 . 6 7 \ \mathrm { e V }$ , and display air stability with negligible loss of electron mobility after 30 days. Inspired by TIPS-pentacene, Nicolas et al., synthesized silylethynyl substituted TPDO, 27a and found it to be a n-type material with lower performance in the range of $1 0 ^ { - 3 } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ .73 The same group later reported cyanated counterparts of the previous moiety, 27b, 27c and 27d with their LUMO situated at $- 3 . 7 9$ , $- 3 . 8 1$ and $- 4 . 1 8 \ \mathrm { ~ e V }$ , respectively.74 All of these derivatives exhibited n-type FET behavior from the thin film devices with top contact Ag electrodes under ${ \bf N } _ { 2 }$ atmosphere. Among them, 27d showed maximum electron mobility up to $0 . 1 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ under ${ \bf N } _ { 2 }$ , maintaining the same stability in air.

Although the n-channel semiconducting properties of N-heteroacenes have been studied for only last two decades, the members of this class have been known to synthetic chemists for over a century. N-Heteroacenes presented an ideal opportunity to study structure–property relationships and to experiment with various aspects of molecular design, device morphology, fabrication technique and conditions to enhance device performance. This is evident from the elaborate and systematic studies carried out.52–74 Perhaps this is the reason that N-heteroacenes are reported to exhibit excellent electron mobilities.

# (d) Acenequinones

Although pentacenequinone, 28 (Chart 5) has been used as an insulator,75–77 there is a lack of studies of acenequinones as OSCs for OFET applications. Only recently have material chemists realized the possibility of using acenequinones as electron transport materials. Quinones are well-known oxidizers and are generally electron-deficient. Acenequinones are very stable and adopt favorable $\pi$ -stacking with few distortions. Thus, they can be incorporated as n-type materials for OFET applications. The general design strategy is to introduce electron-deficient groups to the acenequinone core.

In 2009, Mamada et al. synthesized derivatives of anthracenequinone by introducing electron-deficient trifluoromethylphenyl groups, 29a–29c.78 Single crystal data indicated that 29b and 29c were almost planar, while 29a was more twisted. All showed $\pi - \pi$ stacking, and the intermolecular distances in the columns were ca. $3 . 5 8 \mathring \mathrm { A }$ between the anthraquinone rings in 29a, ca. 3.45 Å between the thiophene and anthraquinone rings in 29b, and ca. 3.39 $\mathring \mathbf { A }$ between the thiazole and anthraquinone rings in 29c. These results suggested that the intermolecular interactions along the stacking direction were smaller in 29a than in 29b and 29c. Both top contact and bottom contact FET devices were fabricated by depositing thin films on HMDStreated $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ and all the three derivatives exhibited n-type behavior. Bottom contact devices showed better performance with electron mobilities up to ${ 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } }$ , $0 . 0 2 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ s ^ { - 1 }$ and $0 . 0 7 4 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for 29a, 29b and 29c, respectively (Table 3). $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of 29c was further improved to $0 . 1 8 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ by applying higher drain voltage when the $V _ { \mathrm { D } } = 1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { V }$ . Miao’s group synthesized a series of electron-deficient acenequinones 30–32 by fluorination or introducing pyrazine-type nitrogen atoms.79 These have LUMO energy levels in the range of $- 3 . 7 8$ to -3.41 eV. Single crystals of 30b, 31b and 32b were obtained and formed closed p–p stacking with short distance between 3.32 and 3.39 Å.79,80 All of the synthesized molecules were thermally deposited on OTMS-treated $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ surface and devices were fabricated in top contact (Au) and bottom gate (Si) geometry. Except for 32a, all others show n-type behavior (Table 3). Compounds 30b and 31b exhibited relatively high electron mobility up to 0.18 and $0 . 1 2 { \mathrm { ~ c m } } ^ { 2 } { \mathrm { ~ V } } ^ { - 1 } { \mathrm { ~ s } } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively. Synthesis and FET performance of an elongated N-heteroacenequinone, 33 was reported by Zhang’s group.81 This molecule is slightly twisted and adopts a face-to-face two-dimensional (2D) ‘‘bricklayer’’ arrangement. Its LUMO energy level is located at $- 4 . 3 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ , indicating its electron-deficient property. Single crystals of 33 obtained by a drop-casting method were used to fabricate FET devices with $\mathbf { A } \mathbf { g }$ as source and drain electrodes. These exhibited n-type FET behavior with electron mobility up to $0 . 2 \ \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { \bf ~ V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { \bf ~ s } ^ { - 1 }$ in ambient air and $I _ { \mathrm { o n / o f f } }$ of about ${ { 1 0 } ^ { 5 } }$ . 33 exhibited excellent air stability, with no obvious degradation observed even after one month under ambient conditions.

# (e) Introducing TCNQ into the acene backbone

7,7,8,8-Tetracyanoquinodimethane (TCNQ) is a well-known electron acceptor and has been widely used for preparation of organic conductors and charge transfer complexes.85 By incorporating TCNQ into the acene backbone, the strong electron withdrawing nature of this quinoidal unit results in lowering of the LUMO energy levels. Additionally, these TCNQ-embedded molecules can be efficiently synthesized from acenequinones, which are easily available and inexpensive.

Chi’s group reported introduction of the TCNQ unit into a thienoheptacene backbone, 37 (Chart 6) in 2012.86 High electron affinity of TCNQ stabilizes the parental core and decreases the LUMO of the molecule to $- 3 . 6 7 \ \mathrm { e V } .$ Bottom gate top contact FETs were fabricated by spin-coating 37 on OTMS-treated $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ .

![](images/fbc042d6d4d67a01d19a4c12d9f595887de7e0e14161026c3073480e89bbbdb6.jpg)

Au was used as contact electrode. These exhibited ambipolar behavior in ${ \bf N } _ { 2 }$ , revealing an average mobility of up to $0 . 0 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathbf { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathbf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $I _ { \mathrm { o n / o f f } }$ of $1 0 ^ { 4 }$ for n-type behavior with a low FET hole mobility of about $1 0 ^ { - 5 } \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \thinspace \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace s ^ { - 1 }$ .

Later, the same group introduced one or two TCNQ units into heptacene and nonacene backbones, and accordingly compounds 38–40 were synthesized.87 This offered an opportunity to study the effect of the presence of more than one TCNQ unit in the longer acenes on their structural, physical and chemical properties. Single crystals of 38 and 39 were obtained and the XRD analysis showed a butterfly-like bent structure for 38 because of strong steric congestion between the cyano groups and the hydrogen atoms at the peri-position. In the solid state, compound 38 adopted a herringbone-type packing motif. In the crystal of compound 39, the molecule was found to have a zigzag conformation with two TCNQ moieties pointing towards different directions, i.e., trans to each other. In the crystal phase, compound 39 adopted a ‘‘slipped’’ face-to-face $\pi$ -stacking structure. The LUMO energy levels of 38, 39 and 40 were found to be $- 3 . 6 4 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ , $- 3 . 8 4 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ and $- 3 . 6 0 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ , respectively. However, 38 and 40 did not show FET activity and the compound 39, with its favorable close packing and lower LUMO, exhibited n-type behavior when fabricated in top contact geometry by a spin-coating method. It showed an average mobility of $1 . 2 \ \times \ 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $I _ { \mathrm { o n / o f f } }$ of ${ 1 0 ^ { 4 } } { - 1 0 ^ { 5 } }$ . This mobility value is much lower than that of 37, mainly caused by the poor film quality of compound 39 in the solid state. Several structurally similar moieties 41a–c were synthesized as electron acceptors in solar cell applications.88 These heteroacene-based molecules with LUMO energy level around $- 4 . 1 \ \mathrm { e V }$ behaved as n-type materials. Their electron mobilities were measured to be $2 . 7 5 \times$ $1 0 ^ { - 5 } \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \thinspace \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace s ^ { - 1 }$ for 41a, $3 . 0 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for 41b and $1 . 1 4 \ \times \ 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for 41c, by a space charge-limited current (SCLC) method.

# (f) Acene diimides

Imide groups are known for inducing high electron affinity and excellent thermal stability. They are very versatile moieties, allowing functionalization on the imide nitrogen to tune the physical properties of molecules including solubility without disturbing the electronic properties of the aromatic core.89 There are two types of acene diimides (Chart 7): one has imide groups fused onto central peri positions of acenes through six-membered rings; the other has imide groups fused laterally on the acene terminals through five-membered rings.

![](images/27c073b4dde292bbd426cbc7c01afaee898f56e89c116779f370d49d4fee5432.jpg)

Naphthalene diimide (NDI) is a highly studied acene diimide because of its excellent electron mobilities.28 For example, 42a has two cyclohexyl groups at the imide nitrogen positions. This showed good electron mobility of up to $6 . 2 \ : \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ : \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ : \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ under an argon atmosphere when it was vapor-deposited on OTS-modified $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ (Table 4).90 The cyclohexyl groups adopt an energetically favorable chair conformation, which aids ordered molecular packing. The mobility of 42a was significantly decreased to $0 . 4 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ when devices were operated in air, which was probably a result of electron trapping by $\mathbf { O } _ { 2 }$ and/or ${ \bf H } _ { 2 } { \bf O }$ at grain boundries.90 To improve the ambient device stability, chemical modifications could be made by substitution with strong electron-withdrawing groups, such as F, Cl, CN, and fluoroalkyl groups, either at the imide positions or at the NDI core. This yields air-stable n-channel operation by significantly lowering the LUMO of the resulting molecule, which aids in resisting ambient oxidation. Fluoroalkyl chains are also highly waterrepellent, and they serve as a kinetic barrier against the diffusion of ${ \bf O } _ { 2 }$ and $_ { \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } }$ by creating dense packing. For example, compound 42b has fluorinated alkyl chains at the imide positions.91 It showed a LUMO energy level of around $- 3 . 7 2 \mathrm { ~ \ e V } .$ OFET devices with bottom gate top-contact configuration were prepared by vacuum-deposition techniques. The highest mobility of $0 . 3 8 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ was obtained for 42b on OTS-treated $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { S i }$ substrate under a nitrogen atmosphere. Chlorination of 42b results in 42c, with a decreased LUMO energy level at around $- 4 . 0 1$ eV. Under similar fabrication conditions, it exhibited higher electron mobility up to $0 . 9 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ on a bare $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { S i }$ substrate in air.91 42c crystals with ribbon-shape could be grown on $n$ -octadecyl triethoxysilane (OTES)-modified substrate from $\mathrm { C H C l } _ { 3 }$ solution, on which gold electrodes were subsequently deposited by a gold wire shadow mask method to give single crystal FETs.92 The devices afforded unprecedented n-channel mobility of up to $8 . 6 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \thinspace \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ in air, with a threshold voltage of about $_ { 9 \mathrm { ~ v ~ } }$ and on/off current ratio of $7 \times 1 0 ^ { 7 }$ . 42d with slightly longer fluoroalkyl chains $\left( \mathrm { - } \mathrm { - } \mathrm { C H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { C } _ { 4 } \mathrm { F } _ { 9 } \right)$ showed the same LUMO energy level as 42c. Using a vacuumdeposition technique, it showed good electron mobilities both in ${ \bf N } _ { 2 }$ $( 1 . 2 6 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ and in air $\left( 1 . 4 3 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } \right)$ , with an $I _ { \mathrm { o n / o f f } }$ ratio as high as $1 0 ^ { 7 }$ , and a threshold voltage of about $2 3 \ \mathrm { V } . ^ { 9 1 }$ All devices based on dichloro-NDIs (42c–d) exhibited excellent operational stability in air because of the dense packing of molecules with strong intermolecular interactions. The tetrachlorinated counterpart 42e, despite having a lower LUMO $\left( - 4 . 1 4 \ \mathrm { \ e V } \right)$ , exhibited an average electron mobility of $0 . 3 4 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ under nitrogen, which decreased to about $0 . 0 3 6 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { \mathbf { V } } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { \mathbf { s } } ^ { - 1 }$ when measured in air.91 The poor performance of 42e is attributed to less dense molecular packing compared with its dichlorinated counterpart, 42d. Similarly, upon fluorination, 42b resulted in 42f, which has a LUMO energy level of $- 3 . 9 7 \ \mathrm { e V } .$ .93 OFETs with bottom contact top gate geometry were fabricated by vacuum deposition of 42f on tetradecylphosphonic acid (TDPA)-modified $\mathbf { A l } _ { 2 } \mathbf { O } _ { 3 }$ , which exhibited a maximum electron mobility of $0 . 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { \thinspace s } ^ { - 1 }$ , an on/off ratio of close to $1 0 ^ { 4 }$ , and a threshold voltage of $- 9 \mathrm { \bf ~ V }$ . Dicyano-NDI (42g) has considerably lower LUMO of $- 4 . 5 \ \mathrm { e V }$ , hence is stable at ambient operating conditions.94 Top contact devices of $\mathbf { 4 2 g }$ displayed an average mobility of $0 . 1 5 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ in vacuum and $0 . 1 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ in air by a vacuum-deposition technique.

Wudl and his co-workers first synthesized anthracene diimides (ADIs) (43b–e) containing six-membered imide rings in 2011.95 Later, Yamada’s group reported synthesis of a series of acene diimides, 43a, 43c, 44 and 45.96 The LUMO energy level is located at -3.86, $- 4 . 1 3$ and $- 4 . 1 7 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for 43a, 44a and 45, respectively. Single crystals of only 44a and 44b were obtained which showed 1D slipped face-to-face $\pi$ -stacking. Top contact FET devices were fabricated by vapor deposition of 44b on HMDStreated $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ surface using gold as contact electrodes. This exhibited n-type behavior with mobility of $3 . 3 \ \times \ 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $I _ { \mathrm { o n / o f f } }$ of $5 . 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 3 }$ .

The second class of acene diimides incorporates those with laterally fused five-membered imide rings. Marks and his co-workers synthesized a series of ADIs, 46a–d and 47a–d functionalized with $n$ -octyl $\left( - n \mathbf { - } \mathbf { C } _ { 8 } \mathbf { H } _ { 1 7 } \right)$ , cyclohexyl, phenyl, $^ { 1 H , 1 H }$ -perfluorobutyl $\left( - n { \cdot } \mathbf { C } \mathbf { H } _ { 2 } \mathbf { C } _ { 3 } \mathbf { F } _ { 7 } \right)$ , cyano $\left( - \mathbf { C N } \right)$ , and bromo $\left( - \mathbf { B } \mathbf { r } \right)$ substituents.97,98 The LUMO energy level of 46a–d and $\bf { 4 7 a - b }$ is between $- 3 . 2 2$ and $- 3 . 7 5 \ \mathrm { e V }$ (Table 4). Introduction of a cyano group to the anthracene backbone decreases the LUMO energy level to $- 4 . 0 7 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for 47c and $- 4 . 2 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for 47d. Singlecrystal structures of 47b and 47d exhibit slipped-stack cofacial crystal packing with close $\pi - \pi$ distance of $\sim 3 . 2 \mathring \mathbf { A } .$ . Additionally, their relatively large fluoroalkyl chains adopt a trans configuration, which enables close packing with neighboring chains, resulting in relatively short intermolecular $\mathrm { F / F }$ contacts (2.8–3.4 Å). Top contact/bottom gate OFETs were fabricated by vapor depositing these organic semiconductors on HMDS-treated $\mathrm { S i } / \mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ substrates, and the resulting devices exhibited exclusively electron transport with good mobilities of ${ { 1 0 } ^ { - 3 } }$ to $0 . 0 6 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \thinspace \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace s ^ { - 1 }$ . Among them, core-cyanated ADIs, 47c and 47d showed airstable FET device operation with electron mobilities up to $0 . 0 4 ~ { \mathrm { c m } } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { \mathbf { V } } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { \mathbf { \Lambda } } \mathbf { S } ^ { - 1 }$ in air.98 The extended pentaene diimide with cyano groups (48) was synthesized by Chi et al.99,100 Bottom gate top contact devices of spin-coated 48 on OTS-modified $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ exhibited n-type behavior with mobility up to $0 . 0 8 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $I _ { \mathrm { o n / o f f } }$ of $1 0 ^ { 7 }$ in ${ \bf N } _ { 2 }$ . As the LUMO energy level is situated at around $- 4 . 1 5 ~ \mathrm { { \ e V } }$ , the fabricated device exhibited good air stability with similar performance.100

# Conclusion

One of the greatest advantages of organic materials is their infinite tunability to customize their properties for a specific application. Acene-based materials are known to be excellent p-type semiconductors. However, by meticulously engineering the molecules and device structures to facilitate electron injection and to improve the charge transport as well as overall stability, decent n-channel mobility can be extracted. Various synthetic strategies are now available to induce n-type behavior of acenes by chemical modifications. In general, molecular modifications to develop new acene-based n-type materials are aimed at (i) increasing their electron affinity; (ii) improving their solubility; (iii) engineering their molecular interactions to achieve favorable close packing; (iv) enhancing the synthetic yield and purity of the materials and (v) improving the thermal, chemical and operational stability by considering various aspects discussed in this article. The above discussed modification strategies have resulted in several promising electron transport materials, some of which showed electron mobility surpassing $1 0 ~ \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ . Such strategies could also be useful for design of efficient n-type OSCs based on other extended $\pi$ -systems.
