# Ethylenedioxythiophene incorporated diketopyrrolopyrrole conjugated polymers for high-performance organic electrochemical transistors†

Naixiang Wang, ‡a Liuping Xie, ‡bc Haifeng Ling, $\textcircled{1}$ a Venkatesh Piradi,c Li Li,d Xingzhu Wang, \*b Xunjin Zhu $\textcircled { 1 0 } \star \textcircled { 1 }$ and Feng Yan a

The synthesis of conjugated polymers specifically for organic electrochemical transistors (OECTs) has attracted much attention recently for their great potential in biological applications. In this case, not only the carrier mobility but also the ionic conduction capability and redox stability of the polymers in an aqueous environment are critical to the device performance. Here, we report a series of dithienyldiketopyrrolopyrrole (DTDPP) based polymers used in organic electrochemical transistors (OECTs). With the combination of ethylenedioxythiophene (EDOT) and glycolated side chains, the optimized polymers present low ionization potential and ultrahigh volumetric capacitance. The resultant OECTs show high transconductance, a high on/off ratio, low threshold voltage, fast response time and remarkably high stability in aqueous solutions. Furthermore, various short-term synaptic functions are successfully mimicked through the optimization of electrolytes and operation modes, indicating that the DPP-based polymer is promising for broad applications in bioelectronics.

# Introduction

Organic electrochemical transistors (OECTs) have attracted much attention recently for their great potential in practical applications.1–3 OECTs are mainly based on conjugated polymer (CP) channel materials due to the mixed ionic and electronic conduction capability of $\mathrm { C P S } . ^ { 4 , 5 }$ In the OECTs, ionic species can migrate between the electrolyte and the bulk of CPs at low gate voltages (typically $< 0 . 5 { \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ ), leading to the modulation of the redox state, conductivity and current of the channels.6,7 This unique feature renders OECTs an ideal platform to interface with biological molecules and tissues in a physiological environment.8,9 Over the past decade, OECTs have been successfully used in logic circuits,10,11 neuromorphic computing,12–14 and highly sensitive biosensors suitable for wearable healthcare devices.15–18 Hence, it is necessary to develop novel CPs to realize high-performance OECTs for applications.19,20

The most widely employed active material in OECTs is the commercialized dispersion poly(3,4-ethylenedioxy-thiophene): polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS).21 Though PEDOT:PSS shows mixed ionic/electronic transport and redox stability in an aqueous environment, the aqueous dispersion in an acidic environment makes it very challenging to carry out further chemical modification. Besides, the highly doped nature makes PEDOT:PSS intrinsically conductive, leading to a depletion mode operation of OECTs. The high channel current of the depletion mode will cause higher power consumption than that of an OECT with an accumulation mode. Therefore, much more effort is focused on various synthetic approaches to obtaining more suitable CPs. Berggren’s group reported a strategy to replace alkyl side chains of CPs with carboxyl groups, demonstrating an enhanced electrochemical doping effect and channel currents.22,23 A similar approach was adopted to realize high transconductance OECTs by functionalizing polythiophene with sulfonate tetrabutylammonium end groups.24 Then Giovannitti et al. reported glycolated side chain engineering to enable OECTs with fast response, high transconductance and steep subthreshold.25 Since then, a series of CP backbones, including benzodithiophene,26,27 dioxythiophene,28 naphthalene tetracarboxylic diimide,29,30 and isoindigo,31 were functionalized with glycol side chains to enhance the bulk electrochemical doping and consequently the

OECT performance. The effect of the length and ratio of the glycolated side chains was also systematically investigated.32,33 Recently Nicolini et al. reported an alternative side chain option through hydroxyl-alkyl functionalization to realize both low film swelling and high transistor performance.34 Notably, the strategy of side chain engineering can effectively enhance ion transport between the polymer film and the aqueous electrolyte, leading to high volumetric capacitance and efficient channel current modulation of OECTs.35

Dithienyldiketopyrrolopyrrole (DTDPP)-based polymers have been popularly used in organic field-effect transistors due to their ultrahigh carrier mobility.36 Recent efforts put into chemical structure and processing technique optimization lead to a higher mobility of DTDPP based polymers than 1 cm2 V-1 s -1 . 37,38 However, the well performed conventional DTDPP-based polymers in field effect transistors showed poor performance in OECTs.39,40 The remarkable aggregation and planarity properties of DPP endow the polymers with efficient charge transport capability.41 However, these features are not favorable for ion penetration during operations in aqueous electrolytes, which leads to less efficient ion and carrier transport in DTDPP-based OECTs. Very recently, several attempts to design DTDPP based polymers suitable for OECTs have been reported. Giovannitti et al. first investigated the energetic control of redox active polymers by combining pyridine flanked DPP with bithiophene or methoxybithiophene.42 Wu et al. reported the integration of the polar glycol side chain on the DPP polymer and the device was operated in a tetrafluoroborate anion to enhance the electrochemical doping efficiency.43 Moser et al. further investigated the polaron delocalization effect in DPP based donor–acceptor polymers by replacing the donor block with thienothiophene, thiophene and methoxybithiophene, indicating that by careful selection of the building blocks of the conjugated backbone, the DPP polymers are capable of being employed in high performance OECTs.44

In this work, in order to realize both efficient ion transport of polymers and operation stability of OECTs, triethylene glycol (TEG) chains were grafted onto DPP to form a gDTDPP monomer, and bis(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) (bis-EDOT) was selected as another monomer for co-block polymers (CPs) owing to its high redox stability and capacity as well as the low ionization potential.28 For comparison, an alkyl chain substituted monomer, aDTDPP, and $^ { 4 , 4 ^ { \prime } }$ -didodecyl- $^ { \cdot 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -bithiophene (aBT) were used as monomers for the synthesis of the respective polymers. As shown in Fig. 1, three polymers, denoted as P(aDTDPP-bis-EDOT) (P1), P(gDTDPP-bis-EDOT) (P2) and P(gDTDPP-aBT) (P3), were prepared and characterized unambiguously. Among them, P2 shows the highest volumetric capacitance of $1 8 9 . 6 \ \mathrm { F } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 3 }$ . The resultant OECTs demonstrate significantly low threshold voltage $\left( - 0 . 0 6 \ : \mathrm { V } \right)$ , a high on/off ratio $( \sim 1 0 ^ { 4 } )$ , fast response time $\left( 2 2 . 4 ~ \mathrm { m s } \right)$ and ultrahigh operation stability over an 8 hour switching test. Furthermore, the OECTs exhibit the basic synaptic functions essential to the neuromorphic platform. Various types of shortterm synaptic plasticity are realized, which opens a window for using the OECTs as neuromorphic devices.

# Results and discussion

As shown in Scheme S1 $\left( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \right)$ , the three polymers, P1, P2 and P3, were prepared through the Stille coupling polymerizations. The gel-permeation chromatography (GPC) measurements recorded a molecular weight $\left( M _ { \mathrm { w } } \right)$ of $1 2 0 \ \mathrm { k D a }$ for P1, 97 kDa for P2, and $1 9 0 ~ \mathrm { \ k D a }$ for P3, corresponding to the relatively narrow polydispersities (PDI) of 1.42. 1.22 and 1.65 (Table S1, $\mathbf { E S I \dagger }$ ), respectively. The biggest $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ for P3 is mainly due to its low solubility in THF solvent. At the same time, the characteristic phase transition properties of the polymers were investigated by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC). Although no obvious glass transition temperatures $\left( T _ { \mathrm { g } } \right)$ were observed for the three polymers, crystallization-temperatures/melting-temperatures $\left( T _ { \mathrm { c } } / T _ { \mathrm { m } } \right)$ were found to be 295/305, 217/300, and $3 0 2 / 3 3 2 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for P1, P2, and P3 (Fig. S1 and Table S1, $\mathbf { E S I \dagger }$ ), respectively. And thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) indicated that P1, P2, and P3 have high thermal stability with the decomposition temperatures $\left( T _ { \mathrm { d } } \right)$ of 383, 365 and $3 9 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (Fig. S2, $\mathrm { E S I \dag }$ ), respectively. Obviously, P2, P1 and P3 show a gradual increase of $M _ { \mathrm { w } } ,$ consistent with the tendency of $T _ { \mathrm { m } }$ and $T _ { \mathrm { { c } } } .$ Next, their UV-visible absorption spectra were recorded, as shown in Fig. S3 $\mathrm { ( E S I \dag ) }$ . Compared with P1 and P2, P3 in $\mathrm { C H C l } _ { 3 }$ shows a much red-shifted absorption, indicating a stronger $\pi - \pi$ conjugated polymer backbone upon the introduction of an EDOT unit and glycolated side chains. This was confirmed by the density functional theory (DFT) calculations on trimers of aDTDPP-bis-EDOT, gDTDPPbis-EDOT, and gDTDPP-aBT. Their ground-state geometries reveal that the gDTDPP-bis-EDOT trimer approaches complete planarity with the smallest inter-ring torsion (Fig. S4 and Table S2, ESI†). In addition, the calculated HOMO energy levels of P1 and P2 are the same due to their very similar backbone structures, but higher than that of P3 because of the stronger electron donating properties of bis-EDOT than bithiophene (Fig. S5–S7 and Table S3, $\mathrm { E S I \dag }$ ). The ionization potentials (IPs) of these polymers are determined by the cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements (Fig. S8, $\mathrm { E S I \dag }$ ), which are equal to the highest occupied molecular orbitals (HOMO), being -4.99, $- 4 . 9 9$ , and $- 5 . 1 1 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for P1, P2, and P3, respectively. Using Tauc plots extracted from their absorption spectra in $\mathrm { C H C l } _ { 3 }$ solution, the optical bandgaps for P1, P2 and P3 are evaluated to be 1.21, 1.18, and $1 . 3 1 \ \mathrm { { \ e V } _ { \Omega } }$ , respectively. As a result, the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LUMOs) were calculated to be $- 3 . 7 8$ for P1, $- 3 . 8 1$ for P2, and $- 3 . 8 0 \ : \mathrm { e V }$ for P3. Although simplifying the polymers into trimers led to the value deviation of the theoretical energy levels with the experimental ones, the tendency is consistent with each other. The UV-Vis spectroelectrochemistry characterization was further carried out, as shown in Fig. S9 $\bigl ( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \bigr )$ . With increasing negative bias voltages ( $_ { 0 . 2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ to $- 0 . 8 { \mathrm { ~ V } } )$ ) applied on the film, P1 and P2 present a more significant $\pi - \pi ^ { \ast }$ absorption decrease at the peak region and a polaronic absorption increase at higher wavelengths compared to P3. The spectroelectrochemical changes observed are in agreement with the CV behaviors of these three polymer films.

OECTs based on the three polymers with the device structure shown in Fig. 1e were fabricated by the method we reported before.45 The polymers were deposited and patterned by photolithography in the channel area between source and drain electrodes and the electrodes were packaged using a SU-8 insulating layer. The OECTs were then characterized in an aqueous electrolyte of $0 . 1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ N a C l }$ . The transfer characteristics $\mathrm { \Delta } [ I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ versus $V _ { \mathbf { G } } ]$ of the OECTs are displayed in Fig. 2. A fixed drain voltage $V _ { \mathrm { D } } = - 0 . 6 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ is applied between the source and drain electrodes while the gate voltage $V _ { \mathbf { G } }$ is applied on the platinum gate electrode. Although OECTs based on P1 and P3 (Fig. 2a and c) show on/off ratios up to three orders of magnitude, a relatively large hysteresis for forward and reverse scans can be observed. Fortunately, the OECT based on P2 (Fig. 2b) displays a much narrower hysteresis loop and an on/off ratio of $1 0 ^ { 4 }$ . The gate leakage curves $[ \boldsymbol { I _ { \mathrm { G } } }$ versus $V _ { \mathbf { G } }$ ) for the corresponding transfer characterization are illustrated in Fig. S10 $\left( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \right)$ , indicating the good insulation performance of the SU-8 photoresist to package the device. Besides, the threshold voltage of the P2-based OECT is only $- 0 . 0 6 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ , which is much higher than those for P1 $\left( - 0 . 5 3 \mathrm { ~ V } \right)$ and P3 $\left( - 0 . 5 9 \mathrm { ~ V } \right)$ -based devices, as demonstrated in Fig. S11 $\mathrm { ( E S I \dag ) }$ . Notably, the varying IP of the CP channel will affect the ambipolarity of these polymers, and further influence the shift of the threshold voltage.

Then we can compare the transconductance of the devices. All of the devices based on the three polymers present a similar nonmonotonic dependence of transconductance on $V _ { \mathbf { G } }$ . 4 6 As shown in Fig. S12 $\mathrm { ( E S I \dag ) }$ , the transconductance of the P2-based OECT is higher than $6 0 ~ \mu \mathrm { S }$ when $V _ { \mathbf { G } }$ is below $- 0 . 2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ and displays the maximum value of $4 2 0 ~ \mu \mathrm { S }$ at $V _ { \mathrm { G } } = - 0 . 7 8 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ while the transconductances of the OECTs based on P1 and P3 are much lower than $6 0 ~ \mu  { S }$ at any gate voltage. Notably, transconductance is an important parameter for an OECT-based sensor because the channel current response to the potential change at the gate or the channel is proportional to the transconductance of the device. Thus, P2 is the most suitable channel material for OECTs.

In sensing applications, OECTs should have fast device response and stable performance in aqueous electrolytes. The transient behavior of OECTs with different active layer materials was then characterized at pulsed gate voltages. Fig. 3a shows the channel current response of the P2-based OECT in response to gate pulses between 0 and $- 0 . 3 \mathrm { V }$ . By fitting the $I _ { \mathbf { D } }$ curve with an exponential decay,45 the response times $( \tau )$ of the OECT could be extracted, which are $\tau _ { \mathrm { o n } } = 2 2 . 4 \ : \mathrm { m s }$ and $\tau _ { \mathrm { o f f } } = 1 6 . 3 \mathrm { m s }$ . As shown in Fig. 3b, the OECT demonstrates stable performance (maintains $8 9 . 8 \%$ of the maximum drain current) under continuous gate voltage pulses $\left( \Delta V _ { \mathrm { G } } ~ = ~ 0 . 5 ~ \mathrm { ~ V ~ } \right.$ , pulse length $= ~ 6$ s) for $^ \mathrm { ~ 8 ~ h ~ }$ , corresponding to a switch time of $\sim 5 0 0 0$ . So P2 has remarkable redox stability during the operation in aqueous electrolytes. The transfer curves of the device before (black) and after (red) the switching test are presented in Fig. 3c, demonstrating little degradation in the device performance. The small threshold voltage shift to the positive direction is in good agreement with the trend of the off-current increase in the initial period of the switching test in Fig. 3b, which may be ascribed to the retention of a few anions in the channel layer during the doping/dedoping processes.

The transient response and long-term stability were also characterized on P1 and P3-based OECTs. As shown in Fig. S13 $\left( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \right)$ , both devices show relatively slow response speeds with the response times longer than 2 s for switching on, which are two orders of magnitude longer than that of P2-based OECTs. The extracted $\tau _ { \mathrm { o n } }$ and $\tau _ { \mathrm { o f f } }$ are summarized in Table 1. Besides, P1 and P3 based OECTs show poor stability under gate pulses. P1 based devices retained only $3 7 \%$ of the initial current after the $^ \mathrm { ~ 1 ~ h ~ }$ test, corresponding to 360 switching cycles, while P3 based devices retained less than $1 0 \%$ of the initial current only after the 15 minute test, as illustrated in Fig. S14 $\left( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \right)$ . The slow response of the device can be attributed to the difficult diffusion of ions into the CPs, which is also consistent with the large hysteresis in the transfer curves shown in Fig. 2. In order to further investigate the ion diffusion into the CPs, volumetric capacitances of the polymer (P1, P2 and P3) films were characterized by electrical impedance spectroscopy. As seen in Fig. S15 (ESI†), the effective areal capacitance was plotted versus frequency ranging from $0 . 5 ~ \mathrm { { \ H z } }$ to $1 0 ^ { 6 } ~ \mathrm { \ H z }$ . When the applied bias voltage $V _ { \mathrm { b i a s } }$ is increased from $- 0 . 2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ to $_ { 1 . 0 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ , slight changes could be observed in the curves of P1 and P3 films (Fig. S15a and c, ESI†). However, for the P2 film, a substantial increase of the capacitance with increasing $V _ { \mathrm { b i a s } }$ can be observed at frequencies below $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { H z }$ , as shown in Fig. S15b $\mathrm { ( E S I \dag ) }$ . The effective capacitance values at $0 . 5 \ \mathrm { H z }$ are

extracted from each curve and summarized in Fig. S15d $\left( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \right)$ . With the increasing $V _ { \mathrm { b i a s } }$ from $- 0 . 2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ to $_ { 1 . 0 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ , the capacitance of the P2 film increases for about two orders of magnitude, while the capacitances of P1 and P3 remain stable when $V _ { \mathrm { b i a s } } <$ $_ { 0 . 8 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ . This result indicates that P2 is more favorable for ion penetration from aqueous electrolytes at certain bias voltages, which is in good agreement with high channel current and transconductance observed in P2-based OECTs. Compared to P1, the replacement of alkyl side chains (grafted onto the DPP moiety) with glycolated ones in P2 leads to an eightfold increase of volumetric capacitance. Additionally, the performance of P3 indicates that only employing the glycolated side chains without the EDOT moiety in the backbone could not present such high capacitance due to a high ionization potential for redox activities. Therefore, we conclude that the combination of both the EDOT moiety in the backbone and glycolate functional groups in the side chains of DPP based polymers can not only lower the oxidation potential but also form a hydrophilic local environment due to abundant oxygen atoms present in the side chain, which can facilitate the swelling of the film, and therefore easier ion penetration into the polymers, resulting in the highest $C ^ { \star }$ in the three polymers.

Based on the above measurements, the material properties and corresponding device performance are summarized in Table 1. P2 presents the highest volumetric capacitance $C ^ { \star } =$ $1 8 9 . 6 \mathrm { F } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 3 } )$ , which is among the highest values for CPs, while the mobility $\left( 0 . 0 8 7 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } \right)$ is similar to those of other DPP-based CPs in OECTs.39,40 The figure of merit $\left( \mu C ^ { * } \right)$ of P2 reaches $1 6 . 5 \ \mathrm { F \thinspace c m ^ { - 1 } \thinspace V ^ { - 1 } \thinspace s ^ { - 1 } }$ , which is comparable to the stateof-the-art materials reported in the literature.6 More importantly, through meticulous structure design, the polymer shows excellent stability in OECTs upon continuous switching operations in aqueous electrolytes for 5000 cycles in 8 hours, which is more stable than any OECT based on other CPs (Table S4, ESI†).

P2-based OECTs are further employed to mimic the behavior of biological synapse, including excitatory and inhibitory modes. As shown in Fig. 4a, with the baseline of $V _ { \mathbf { G } } = \mathbf { 0 } \ : \mathbf { V }$ , both positive and negative gate pulses as the excitation spikes with a fixed duration of $1 0 ~ \mathrm { { m s } }$ are applied on the device operated in $0 . 1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ N a C l }$ solution. When a positive excitation spike $\big ( V _ { \mathbf { G } } = 0 . 1 \ : \mathbf { V } \big )$ is applied, the channel current presents a sudden decay of $0 . 3 7 \ \mu \mathrm { A } .$ . Once the spike is removed, the current gradually recovered back, reproducing a biological inhibitory synaptic behavior. With the increase of the excitation amplitude from $_ { 0 . 1 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ to $_ { 0 . 4 \mathrm { V } }$ , the device shows a corresponding enhanced decay, from 0.37 to $0 . 8 2 ~ \mu \mathrm { A }$ . Meanwhile, when the negative excitation spikes with increasing amplitudes $- 0 . 1 \mathrm { V }$ to $- 0 . 4 { \mathrm { V } } )$ are applied, significant larger responses (positive current change from 0.58 to $5 . 8 7 ~ \mu \mathrm { A } )$ can be observed, which reproduces a biological excitatory synaptic behavior. So the device response can successfully mimic the post-synaptic current behavior in both inhibitory and excitatory synaptic modes. The large amplitude difference in channel current response of the two modes could be attributed to the p-type nature of P2. When negative $V _ { \mathbf { G } }$ is applied, anions in the electrolytes are driven to penetrate into the polymer, and a large number of compensated holes are generated to form the high current flow in the channel. Then the device is biased with $V _ { \mathbf { G } }$ of $- 0 . 3 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ and applied with pulses at $_ { 0 . 4 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ with increasing number from 2 to 20 times (Fig. 4b and c). The channel currents recover to their initial values quickly after the spikes are removed, demonstrating a stable short-term synaptic response with nearly no memory effect. This result indicates that the anions (majorly $\mathbf { C l } ^ { - }$ ) can diffuse efficiently between the P2 channel layer and the aqueous solution.

As previously reported,7 the crystallographic radius and hydration shells of the anions in the electrolyte will affect the doping/dedoping process with the CPs, and thus affect the operation of devices. To investigate this effect, NaCl electrolyte was replaced by a polyelectrolyte solution of sodium polystyrene sulfonate (NaPSS) with a larger size of anions in device characterization. As shown in Fig. 4d, similar inhibitory and excitatory synaptic behaviors of the OECTs operated in NaPSS are observed. As shown in Fig. 4e and f, this effect is more pronounced, when twenty pulses are applied, that the amplitude ratio $A 2 0 / A 1$ reaches $1 8 9 \%$ , compared to the value of $A 2 / A 1$ of $1 1 8 \%$ . Meanwhile, the relaxation time for the triggered current pulse is much longer compared to that operated in NaCl solutions. The phenomenon indicates that anions with a larger size $\left( \mathbf { P S S } ^ { - } \right)$ need more time to migrate between the polymer channel and the aqueous electrolyte. When the relaxation time is longer than the pulse interval time, the anions penetrating into the channel cannot diffuse back to the aqueous electrolyte on time and will accumulate in the channel to further contribute to the doping of the channel during the following pulses. As a proof-of-concept, we demonstrate that various kinds of short-term synaptic behaviors could be mimicked by P2-based OECTs by using a suitable electrolyte.

# Conclusions

In conclusion, we demonstrate that DPP-based p-type semiconducting polymers are promising active materials of OECTs. Due to the high mobility from DPP and superior redox stability and capacity from EDOT units, the optimized polymer P(gDTDPP-bis-EDOT)-based OECTs can operate with high current modulation in aqueous electrolyte with low threshold voltage and fast response. The ultrahigh volumetric capacitance $C ^ { \star }$ of P(gDTDPP-bis-EDOT) of $\sim 2 0 0 \mathrm { F c m } ^ { - 3 }$ is among the highest values reported previously. Notably, the material shows a remarkably high stability during continuous switching operations up to 5000 times in $^ { 8 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ . In addition, synaptic OECTs based on P(gDTDPP-bis-EDOT) successfully mimic the excitatory and inhibitory behaviors based on electrochemical doping processes and various kinds of short-term synaptic plasticity are realized. This work opens a window of using DPP-based polymers as the active materials of OECTs for further applications of bioelectronics.
