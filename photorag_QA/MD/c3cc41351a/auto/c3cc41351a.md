# Solution-processed bulk heterojunction solar cells based on $\mathsf { B F } _ { 2 } .$ hydroxychalcone complexest

Received 21st February 2013, Accepted 11th March 2013

Sylvain Chambon,a Anthony D'Aleo,b Carole Baffert, Guillaume Wantza and Frederic Fages\*b

DOI: 10.1039/c3cc41351a www.rsc.org/chemcomm

$\mathbf { 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -Hydroxychalcone derivatives featuring a triphenylamine terminal unit were synthesized in one step and behaved as effective ligands for borondifluoride coordination, enabling the straightforward generation of D-A molecules with strong absorption. Solution-processed solar cells based on these complexes and $\mathsf { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathsf { B M }$ showed a PCE of $1 . 1 3 \%$

Small molecules are receiving a tremendous amount of interest as alternatives to polymers for the generation of bulk heterojunction photovoltaic solar cells.,2 Discrete molecular semiconductors have well-defined chemical structures and their synthesis is well reproducible. They can be either vacuum deposited' or solution processed,' offering thin films with long-range order and high charge carrier mobility. Generally, low band gap materials based on electron donor (D) and acceptor (A) moieties display improved light-harvesting property, red-shifted absorption, and high opencircuit voltage, resulting in high values of the power conversion efficiency (PCE).1,4-6 Solution-processed solar cells based on small molecules containing D and A groups connected to a $\pi$ conjugated molecular backbone were shown to reach up to over $7 \%$ efficiency.a,5b,7 Despite the huge amount of small molecular structures published over the last five years,' there is still a critical need for the design of such semiconductors in order to further explore structure-property correlations and overcome the technological barriers that concern large-area device efficiency, processability, and cost-effective materials and device mass production.

Recently, some of us reported a family of organic dyes based on borondifluoride complexes of $2 ^ { \prime }$ -hydroxychalcone derivatives in which the terminal aromatic part of the arylpropenone backbone plays the role of the electron donor group, while the difluorodioxaborine ring acts as a good electron acceptor. Compounds that specifically contain a triphenylamine (TPA) unit as a donor end-unit were shown to exhibit an intense and broad absorption band that extended from UV to the far-red end of the visible spectrum. we present herein preliminary results obtained for molecules 1-3 (Scheme 1) that illustrate the potentialities of this family of dyes as donor materials in organic solar cells blended with [6,6]phenyl- $\mathbf { \cdot C _ { 6 1 } }$ -butyric acid methyl ester $\mathbf { \left( P C _ { 6 1 } B M \right) }$ as an electron accepting material. Compared to a number of $\mathbf { D - A }$ structures reported in the literature. $^ { 1 , 3 - 7 }$ and especially those based on $\mathrm { B F } _ { 2 }$ complexes,' such as BODIPY derivatives,1o it is remarkable that the chalcone- ${ \bf \cdot B F } _ { 2 }$ species can be obtained straightforwardly involving cheap starting materials in a very simple two-step synthetic route.

Dye 3 has been reported previously,, while the new dyes 1 and 2 served to evaluate the effect of substitution at the phenyl ring bearing the dioxaborine chelate. The boron difluoride complexes were obtained as deep-blue solids in two synthetic steps (see Table of content graphic) from commercially available cheap starting materials, following a previously published procedure. They were characterized using $^ 1 \mathrm { H } \cdot$ $^ { 1 3 } \mathrm { C } \mathrm { - }$ and ${ } ^ { 1 9 } \mathrm { F } \cdot$ NMR spectroscopies, mass spectrometry and elemental analysis (see Section 2 in Esit). The introduction of the TPA unit endowed the three compounds with both good solubility in organic solvents and increased chemical stability vs. hydrolytic boron decomplexation.8,11 Compound 2, however, lacking a side group on the phenolate part, was found to be less soluble as compared to the two other analogues. Solutions prepared from carefully dried solvents exhibited no changes in optical properties over a period of several weeks, which allowed performing easily spectroscopic measurements and fabrication of solution-deposited thin films.

The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectrum of the bestperforming device (device 1(c), Table 2) is given in Fig. 3 and compared to the UV-vis absorption spectrum of the active layer. It is interesting to note that between 350 and $6 2 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , dye 1 can convert more than $1 5 \%$ of the incident photons into electrons. The EQE spectrum presents three bands centred at 337, 400 and $5 7 5 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . The first band corresponds to the contribution of the $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ while the last one is associated with the lowestenergy transition band of compound 1. The fact that an important contribution arises from photons absorbed at $4 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ remains yet unclear.

In conclusion, we have demonstrated the use of coordination of borondifluoride, a strong Lewis acid, to produce very simply D-A molecules with enhanced push-pull character. When combined with the TPA unit, these dyes exhibit strong absorption in the visible spectrum, high oxidation potentials, high stability and good solubility in common organic solvents. Given the extreme simplicity of synthesis of these dyes, involving commercially available cheap chemicals, this work demonstrates that borondifluoride complexes of $2 ^ { \prime }$ -hydroxychalcones may be promising donor materials for solution-processed organic solar cell technology. We believe that further optimization of both dye structure and device architecture, including thin film morphology, will greatly improve the performance of devices.
