# Modulation of Vertical Component Distribution for Large-Area Thick-Film Organic Solar Cells

Lin Zhang,\* Shuzhi Yang, Bocheng Ning, Fang Yang, Wen Deng, Zhi Xing, Zhaozhao Bi, Ke Zhou, Yong Zhang, Xiaotian Hu,\* Bin Yang, Junliang Yang, Yingping Zou, Wei Ma, and Yongbo Yuan

Thick active layers in organic solar cells (Oscs) have a great promise of enhancing light absorption and providing pinhole-free films for large-scale fab. rication. Since charge carriers in thick films need a longer transporting path in the vertical direction to the electrode than in thin films, modulation of the active layer morphology in thick films is highly required for effective charge transport. Herein, thin-film $\left( \approx \mathsf { I } \mathsf { I } 0 \mathsf { n m } \right)$ and thick-film $( \approx 3 0 0 \ : \mathrm { n m } )$ OSCs based on a PM6:IT-4 F film are fabricated by blade coating with various additive contents. It is found that the optimized thick-film device needs more additives than the optimized thin-flm device. The addition of more additives in thick-films promotes vertical component distribution and enhances the crystallization, resulting in efficient charge transport with reduced charge recombination and electron (or hole) accumulation within the thick active layer. These results are also confirmed by PM6:Y6-based devices, in which optimized thin-flm and thick-film devices exhibit power conversion efficiency (PCE) of $1 6 . 6 9 \%$ and $1 4 . 9 1 \%$ at the additive contents of $0 . 3 \%$ and $0 . 6 \%$ , respectively. Encouragingly, thick-film device with $0 . 6 \%$ additive has a narrow distribution of PCE values, and high PCEs of $1 3 . 9 4 \%$ and $1 3 . 0 5 \%$ are obtained for the large-area $( 1 < m ^ { 2 } )$ rigid and flexible thick-film OsCs, showing great application prospect.

# 1. Introduction

Bulk heterojunction (BHJ) organic solar cells (OSCs) have drawn great attention due to the unique advantages in preparing largearea and flexible solar cells by solution processing coating technology. Considerable research efforts have been dedicated to improve the photovoltaic efficiency, especially for the successful breakthroughs in ITIC and Y6 series nonfullerene acceptors, boosting the power conversion efficiency (PCE) to over $1 8 \%$ [1-7] However, most devices with state-of-the-art performance are prepared by spin coating in the glovebox, which is not conducive to large-scale production of OsCs. As a large-area filmforming technology, blade coating has been successfully used to fabricate OSCs and has made great progresses.[8-21] Even so, there is an important challenge to achieve high performance for large-area OsCs toward commercial application. In general, most high-performance organic materials present excellent photovoltaic performance at the optimized thickness of active layer ${ \approx } 1 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ [19] It is difficult to ensure that the film is always formed without pin-holes during the fast and large-area production. Otherwise, the pin-hole in active layer will cause the irreversible short circuit of devices, which in turn will damage the entire device.22] Increasing the thickness of active layer film can eliminate the pin-hole phenomenon and contribute to the formation of homogeneous large-area film, whereas it will lose device performance. Many methods were explored to fabricate efficient thick-film devices, such as ternary strategy.[23-25] However, the photovoltaic performance of thick-film devices still fall behinds the thin-film devices significantly, which restricts the wide application of OsCs. Therefore, improving the photovoltaic performance of printed large-area thick-film $( \geq 3 0 0 \mathrm { n m } )$ OSCs is of great importance.

Generally, due to the limited exciton diffusion length and different carrier mobility for BHJ film, a bicontinuous interpenetrating network structure of donor (D) and acceptor (A) is needed to boost exciton dissociation and charge transport in BHJ OSCs.[26-29] Thus, the active layer requires not only a large interface area between donor and acceptor materials, but also the continuous channels for effective charge transport to the electrode. Since charge carriers in thick film need to a longer transporting path in vertical direction to the electrode than in thin film,30-32] effective charge transport is highly required to suppress charge recombination and reduce space charge accumulation within the active layer, so as to improve photovoltaic performance. In OsCs, charge transport is positively correlated with carrier mobility and carrier lifetime, which is not only related to the inherent property of organic materials, but also closely related to the microscopic morphology of active layer, such as the vertical component distribution and crystallization. Therefore, the vertical component distribution and crystallization in thick-film active layer should be finely controlled for the effective charge transport.

Proverbially, many methods have been successfully employed to modulate the vertical component distribution and crystallization in thin-film devices, such as the addition of additive, thermal annealing, solvent annealing, and so on.33-37] For example, the addition of 1,8-diiodooctane (DIO) additive is a widely used method in OsCs, and can enhance crystallization for improving charge transport.38] Due to the good solubility of DIO in methanol as well as the good solubility of $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 0 } \mathrm { B M }$ in DIO, the spin-coated methanol on PBDTTT-CT: $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 0 } \mathrm { B M }$ film leads to a graded vertical component distribution in BHJ active layer.39] Besides, it was reported that the vertical segregation effect is enhanced by DIO additive in BHJ flm.140] Consequently, the addition of additive is an effective method in morphology optimization for thin-film OsCs. However, the effect of additive content on the vertical component distribution and crystallization of thick-film is indistinct. Moreover, with the development of thin-film OsCs toward thick-film OsCs, the morphology optimization by adding additive in printed thick film may be different to the thin film, whereas the differences on the specific optimization mechanism and process are unclear.

In this work, in order to study the difference of morphology optimization by adding additive between printed thin film and thick film, PM6:IT-4 F-based thin-film and thick-film OSCs were fabricated by blade coating with various additive contents in ambient environment. It was found that the optimized thick-film device needs more additive than the optimized thin-film device. These results were further confirmed by blade-coated PM6:Y6 devices. Moreover, large-area $( 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ rigid and flexible thick-film OSCs were also prepared and exhibit high PCEs of $1 3 . 9 4 \%$ and $1 3 . 0 5 \%$ , respectively.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The chemical structures of PM6, IT-4 F, DIO, and the diagrammatic drawing of blade coating are shown in Figure 1.[41] In the normalized absorption spectra (Figure S1, Supporting Information), PM6 and IT-4 F possess strong light absorption at the range of 500-650 and $6 0 0 { - } 8 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively, exhibiting excellent complementary light absorption, where it can be confirmed by the absorption curve of PM6:IT-4 F blend film. Thus, conventional thin-film OsCs with the device configuration of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/PM6:IT-4 F/PDINO/Al were fabricated by blade-coating without any other treatment in ambient environment. Before the deposition of active layer, various contents (0, 0.3, 0.6, and $0 . 9 \%$ of additive (DIO) were added into the chlorobenzene solution with PM6 and IT-4 F materials. The optimized thickness of active layer for the thin-film devices is ${ \approx } 1 1 0 \mathrm { n m }$ . As displayed in Figure 1b and Table 1, the device without DIO exhibits a highest PCE of $1 1 . 3 6 \%$ with an open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ .of $0 . 8 5 \mathrm { V }$ a short-circuit current $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ of $1 9 . 3 6 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and a fill factor (FF) of $6 9 . 0 8 \%$ . Upon adding $0 . 3 \%$ DIO into the blend solution, the PCE value increases to $1 3 . 0 6 \%$ ,accompanied by the significantly improved $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ $( 2 0 . 5 7 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ and FF $( 7 3 . 6 5 \% )$ . For the device with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO, a decline of device performance was achieved with the PCE of $1 2 . 7 5 \%$ $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 8 6 \mathrm { V }$ $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 0 . 4 3 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and FF of $7 2 . 6 4 \%$ The further increased DIO content $( 0 . 9 \% )$ leads to a remarkable decrease in photovoltaic performance with the PCE of $1 1 . 9 2 \%$ . Consequently, the optimized thin-film device needs only $0 . 3 \%$ DIO additive. The integrated current density $( J _ { \mathrm { i n t } } )$ calculated from external quantum efficiency (EQE) curves (Figure 1c) are 18.62, 19.96, 19.68, and $1 9 . 2 7 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for the thin-film devices with 0, 0.3, 0.6, and $0 . 9 \%$ DIO, respectively, which match well with the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ measured from the $J { - } V$ curves. The carrier mobility measured by space-charge-limited-current method[42,43] are enumerated in Figure 1d, Figure s2 and Table S1, Supporting Information. With the DIO content increasing from 0 to $0 . 9 \%$ both electron and hole mobilities increase gradually. Notably, the relatively high and balanced electron mobility and hole mobility were obtained for the device with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO, resulting in less charge recombination and charge accumulation, which coincide with the high FF and PCE.

To understand the variation of device performance, morphology characterization of grazing incidence wide angle X-ray scattering[50 (GIwAxs) and resonant soft X-ray scattering[51] (RSoXS) were performed. As show in Figure S4, Supporting Information, remarkable scattering signals were observed at $q = 0 . 2 8$ and $q = 1 . 6 7 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ for pure PM6 film, which are corresponding to the (100) and (010) scattering peaks, respectively. Meanwhile, the IT-4 F film shows broad (100) and (010) scattering peaks at $q = 0 . 3 6$ and $q = 1 . 4 5 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ . For the PM6:IT-4 F blend films, all the GIWAXS data (Figure 2a,b) present pronounced (010) scattering peaks at $q \approx \mathrm { i } . 7 \mathrm { 0 } \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ in the out-of-plane direction, suggesting the preferential face-on orientation, which is beneficial to the charge transport of OsCs. The calculated crystallization parameters are summarized in Table S2 and Table 2. With the increase in DIO content, the interplanar distance (d) decreases gradually, and the crystallization coherence length[52] (CCL) increases, indicating that the addition of DIO could enhance crystallinity. The RSoXS measurement is an effective method to analyze the phase separation of active layer, where an X-ray with the energy of $2 4 8 . 4 8 \mathrm { e V }$ was chosen in this project due to the high contrast between donor and acceptor. As displayed in Figure 2c, S5, Supporting Information, and Table 2, both domain size and relative purity are enlarged with the increased addition of DIO. Overall, although the film exhibits enhanced crystallinity and purity with the increased DIO content, the corresponding domain size is also increased. The excessively large domain is not conducive to the improvement of photovoltaic performance, due to the less interface area of donor and acceptor. Therefore, the active layer with appropriate crystallinity, purity, and domain size is of great importance, which is the reason why the device with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO has the best performance.

Unlike the thin-film device with the best performance at $0 . 3 \%$ DIO content, the thick-film device exhibits the highest PCE value at the DIO content of $0 . 6 \%$ Thus, the optimized thick-film device needs more additive than the thin-film device.

Considering the abovementioned different requirements of additive content, the carrier dynamics and recombination behavior for the thick-film devices were investigated. As shown in Figure 3b, the exciton dissociation probabilities[44,45] of thick-film devices with $0 . 3 \%$ and $0 . 6 \%$ DIO are $9 4 . 6 3 \%$ and $9 4 . 9 2 \%$ respectively, suggesting similar exciton dissociation and charge generation efficiencies. However, according to the dependence of $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ on light intensity[53] (Figure 3c), the device with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO exhibits less trap-assisted recombination than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO distinctly, due to a smaller value of $s { = } 1 . 4 2 \mathrm { k T / q }$ than that of $s { = } 1 . 6 5 k \mathrm { T } / \mathrm { q }$ . Similarly, the dependence of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ on light intensityl45] (Figure 3d) indicates the less bimolecular recombination for the device with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO, on account of the higher value of $\alpha = 0 . 9 5 9$ than that of $\alpha = 0 . 9 2 2$ . To directly determine the carrier lifetime $( \tau )$ of thick-film OSCs, transient photovoltage[12] (TPV) measurements were conducted. As shown in Figure 3e, the carrier lifetime obtained by fitting TPV profile for the device with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO $( \tau = 1 . 9 5 \mu \mathrm { s } )$ is longer than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO $( \tau = 1 . 6 2 \mu \mathrm { s } )$ . Furthermore, the charge extraction property was Studied by' transient photocurrent&,54) (TPC) measurement (Figure 3f). The photocurrent of the device with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO decays faster than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO, suggesting the more effective charge extraction at the electrodes for the device with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO. Consequently, these results imply that the thick-film device with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO exhibits similar exciton dissociation and charge generation efficiencies, but the less trap-assisted recombination, less bimolecular recombination, longer carrier lifetime, and more effective charge extraction than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO.

To clarify the above performance differences of thick-film devices, the vertical morphology of active layer was studied by the angle-dependent GIwAxs, where the molecular packing and crystallinity of blend films in the vertical direction can be obtained.16,,so] The grazing incidence angles $( \alpha )$ should match the related depth regions of the film. Due to the influence of surface roughness, the total reflection of film will be disturbed with the small incidence angle of $0 . 1 1 ^ { \circ }$ . When $\alpha < 0 . 1 5 ^ { \circ }$ $( 0 . 1 5 ^ { \circ }$ is the critical angle of Si substrate), the signal intensity reflects the scattering of X-ray beam by blend film through the depth of film. If $\alpha > 0 . 1 5 ^ { \circ }$ , the X-ray beam will penetrate the entire film and enter the $\mathrm { S i } / \mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ substrate, showing the average structure information of whole film. As shown in Figure 4a,b, S8 and Table S4, Supporting Information, the scattering intensity of (010) peak at $q \approx 1 . 7 2 \bar { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ (assigned to the $\pi - \pi$ stacking of PM6) increases with the increase in incidence angle, suggesting the gradually enhanced crystallinity of PM6 from top to bottom in the active layer. It can be confirmed by the variation of CCL values (Figure 4c), which are obtained by fitting the (010) peaks of PM6 in the out-of-plane direction. Notably, the CCL values for the film with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO are almost larger than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO in the whole film, indicating the higher crystallinity of the film with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO, which is consist with the previous results (Figure 2). Meanwhile, with the increase in incidence angle, the variation of CCL values for the film with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO is more dramatic than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO, indicating that the addition of more DIO can facilitate the vertical distribution with PM6 crystals enriched at the bottom. In addition, time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry[57] (TOF-sIMs) was also employed to study the difference in vertical component distribution of active layer films. $\mathrm { C N ^ { - } }$ is assigned to represent the IT-4 F molecule, since there is no $\mathrm { C N ^ { - } }$ in the PM6 structure. As shown in Figure 4d, both films exhibit strong intensity signals of $\mathrm { C N ^ { - } }$ near the surface, suggesting the effective vertical component distribution. However, the intensity of $\mathrm { C N ^ { - } }$ signal for the film with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO near the surface is stronger than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO, suggesting more $\Pi { - } 4 \operatorname { F }$ enriched on the surface. On the contrary, the intensity of $\mathrm { C N ^ { - } }$ signal for the film with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO near the substrate is weaker than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO. It indicates that the vertical component distribution for the film with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO is stronger than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO, which matches well with the angle-dependent GIWAXS results. For the thick film with stronger vertical distribution $( 0 . 6 \%$ DIO), the $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ interfaces for exciton dissociation are closer to the middle of active layer, resulting in similar transport distances for electrons and holes in the vertical direction to the electrode. Thus, the stronger vertical distribution leads to more effective charge transport for the thick-film device, which can reduced charge recombination and electron (or hole) accumulation within the thick active layer. That is why the thick-film device with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO has higher photovoltaic performance than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO. Moreover, the surface morphology of thick films was measured by atomic force microscope (AFM). As shown in Figure S9, Supporting Information, the film with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO shows a rougher surface with root-mean-square (RMS) value of $4 . 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO $( 2 . 9 4 \mathrm { n m } )$ , which should be attributed to the high crystallinity and effective vertical component distribution. The schematic diagrams of morphology for the thick films with $0 . 3 \%$ and $0 . 6 \%$ DIO are shown in Figure 4e,f, respectively.

# 3. Conclusions

In summary, OSCs based on PM6:IT-4 F blend with various additive contents were fabricated by blade coating in ambient environment. It was found that the best photovoltaic performance occurs at the DIO content of $0 . 3 \%$ for the thin-film $( \approx 1 0 0 \mathrm { n m } )$ device. However, for the thick-film $( \approx 3 0 0 \mathrm { n m } )$ OSCs, the device with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO exhibits better performance than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO. The big difference is attributed to the relation between photovoltaic conversion process and the morphology of active layer. Since the thickness of active layer for thin-film device is only ${ \approx } 1 1 0 \mathrm { n m }$ , the carrier mobility is large enough for most carriers to be extracted and collected to the electrode. Due to the small domain and balanced carrier mobility for the thin-film device with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO, large numbers of carriers could be generated and then be effectively collected, resulting in highest device performance. When the thickness increases to ${ \approx } 3 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ , charge carriers need a longer transporting path in the vertical direction to the corresponding electrode. Therefore, the photovoltaic performance is dominated by charge transport. Due to the higher carrier mobility and longer carrier lifetime as well as the more effective vertical component distribution, the thick-film device with $0 . 6 \%$ DIO shows higher photovoltaic performance than that with $0 . 3 \%$ DIO. Thus, the optimized thick-film device needs more additive than thin-film device. In addition, we fabricated PM6:Y6-based OSCs with the additive of CN by similar method. The corresponding thin-film device with $0 . 3 \%$ CN has better performance than that with $0 . 6 \%$ CN. On the contrary, thick-film device with $0 . 6 \%$ CN achieves a higher PCE than that with $0 . 3 \%$ CN, further confirming the above result. Furthermore, the thick-film device with $0 . 6 \%$ CN has a narrow distribution of PCE values, and the PCEs of $1 3 . 9 4 \%$ and $1 3 . 0 5 \%$ were obtained for the large-area $( 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ rigid and flexible thick-film $( 3 0 0 \mathrm { n m } )$ OSCs, respectively, showing a great potential for the large-area printing of efficient OSCs.
