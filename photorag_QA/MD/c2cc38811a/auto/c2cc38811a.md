# A diketopyrrolopyrrole-thiazolothiazole copolymer for high performance organic field-effect transistorst

A diketopyrrolopyrrole-thiazolothiazole copolymer with a short $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking distance (3.52 A), due to the introduction of heteroaromatic rings, exhibits a high charge mobility above $3 . 4 0 \ c m ^ { 2 } \lor ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 }$ at a relatively gentle annealing temperature.

In their most conspicuous forms, large area flexible organic electronics, such as organic radio frequency identity tags, sensor arrays and flexible displays, address a range of applications outside those where Si integration is used, or they approach those conventional applications with a uniquely advantageous performance benefit.' Organic field-effect transistors (OFETs) that use solutionprocessed organic semiconductors to replace amorphous silicon as the active layer have attracted much interest in recent years, owing to the advantages of low cost, light-weight, and potential use of flexible substrates.? Compared with small-molecule OFETs, solution processed conjugated polymers could offer better film forming and mechanical properties, such as flexibility and large-area uniformity.' However, solution-processed polymer thin films always comprise amorphous regions along with crystalline domains, which is not conducive to the charge carrier transport.4 Thermal annealing is a commonly used practice to improve the molecular organization of the polymer thin films to obtain higher charge carrier mobility. High field effect mobility has been achieved for a few polymers with the aid of thermal annealing, but the annealing temperature is somewhat high, most are higher than.. $1 8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and some even more than $2 5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ 5 These thermal annealing steps are energy-consuming and usually need to be conducted under vacuum or in an inert atmosphere. This poses a challenge in the cost-effective production of printed OFETs. Changing the original structure of the polymer by introducing heteroaromatic rings could enhance the accumulation of the molecule. Heteroaromatic ring structures have a strong tendency to form $\pi - \pi$ stacks with a large overlapping area that is. favourable for charge-carrier transport. In addition, introduction of the electron deficient heteroaromatic rings into the polythiophene system is a promising approach to increase the ionization potential and thereby improve the stability.7.

Here we report the design and synthesis of a solution-processable polymer semiconductor PDPPTzBT (Scheme 1), which shows a high hole mobility of $3 . 4 3 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ in OFET devices. In this polymer, we deliberately combined the diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP) moiety with a donor-acceptor-donor (D-A-D) building block, namely, thiophene-thiazolothiazole-thiophene (TzBT), to form an A-D-A-D polymer with a repeat unit of DPPTzBT. These two electron deficient cores, DPP and TzBT, have been demonstrated to be significant building blocks for high performance OFETs.' In this system, the long alkyl and heteroaromatic rings in TzBT blocks could induce a high order molecular organization through alkyl-assembling, $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking and heteroatom contact.

The polymer was soluble in most common organic solvents such as chloroform and toluene. The number average molecular weight was $2 3 . 9 \mathrm { \ k D a }$ , with a polydispersity index (PDI) of 1.81, which was determined by using gel permeation chromatography (GPC) against polystyrene standards $\mathrm { ( E S I , \dagger }$ S2 and Fig. S4). The thermal properties of PDPPTzBT were examined by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DsC) techniques. TGA showed a decomposition temperature of $3 7 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ in air, indicating a high thermal stability of the polymer (Fig. S2, $\mathrm { E S I \dagger }$ J. DSC characterization was carried out up to $3 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ under nitrogen (which is $5 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ lower than the decomposition temperature) to study the thermal transitions of the polymer. During the first heating scan, two endothermic transition temperatures were observed at $1 7 0 . 2 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and $2 8 6 . 4 \ ^ { \circ } \mathbf { C } ,$ whereas in the second heating scan the endothermic peaks were not observed, so it is probably due to the melting of the bulk (Fig. S3, ESI). The optical properties of PDPPTzBT in chloroform and as a thin film were characterized by using UV-vis-NIR spectroscopy. In chloroform, PDPPrzBT exhibited a maximum absorption $\left( \lambda _ { \mathrm { m a x } } \right)$ at $7 7 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ (Fig. 1). The polymer thin film showed a slight red shift of. $3 4 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ in $\lambda _ { \mathrm { m a x } }$ $\left( 8 0 6 \ \mathrm { n m } \right)$ . A vibronic absorption shoulder at $7 1 8 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ was observed, suggesting that the. molecule in the thin film underwent molecular organization to form a more ordered structure. The absorption cut-off of $9 6 5 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ in the solid state corresponds to an optical band-gap $\left( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } } \right)$ of $1 . 2 8 \ \mathrm { \ e V }$ indicating that PDPPTzBT is a small band-gap semiconductor. The electrochemical property of the PDPPTzBT thin film was investigated by cyclic voltammetry (Cv) in order to determine the HOMO/LUMO energy levels (Fig. S1, $\mathrm { E S I \dagger }$ J. The HOMO value of PDPPTzBT was calculated from the onset oxidation potential, which was $- 5 . 2 8 \ \mathrm { e V }$ according to the equation $E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } } = - \mathrm { e } ( E _ { \mathrm { o x } } ^ { \mathrm { o n s e t } } + 4 . 4 ) \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ whereas the LUMO value of $- 4 . 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ was determined from the difference between the HOmO and the optical band-gap. We could note that the HOmO energy level of PDPPrzBT was obviously lower than that of regioregular poly(3-hexylthiophene) $( - 4 . 7 6 ~ \mathrm { e V } )$ 10 The decreased HOMO energy level is expected to be beneficial for increasing the stability of the polymer against oxidation as well as enhancing the hole injection from a gold source electrode into p-type thin film.

To confirm the diffraction pattern and molecular ordering, the microstructure of the polymer thin films was studied using grazing incidence X-ray scattering (GIxs). For the GIXS measurements, the polymer thin films were prepared by drop-casting 1,2-dichlorobenzene (DCB) solution of the polymers on silicon wafers with the native oxide layer. As shown in Fig. 2, when the films were subjected to thermal annealing, the diffraction patterns became sharper and multiple diffraction peaks were observed for the films, which demonstrated that the highly crystalline films were formed in the annealing process. After annealing at $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , the thin film showed a strong primary peak at $3 . 5 4 ^ { \circ }$ (100), which corresponds to a $d$ spacing of $2 4 . 9 \mathring { \mathbf { A } } ,$ and a peak at $2 5 . 2 ^ { \circ }$ (010), which corresponds to a $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance of $3 . 5 2 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ The small $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance implies the stronger intermolecular interactions between the fused aromatic DPP unit and the TzBT planar segment comprising the PDPPTzBT polymer backbone.1 Based on the $d$ spacing results, there is no significant difference when dodecyl side chains on the thiophene rings of PDPPTzBT were replaced by octyloxy side chains, indicating that longer side chains while increasing solubility and alkyl-assembling do not impede the accumulation of this copolymer.

Bottom-contact, bottom gate OFET devices were fabricated using solution processed PDPPTzBT thin films as the semiconducting layer on the OTS treated $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ substrate $\mathrm { ( E S I , \dagger }$ S3). The organic semiconductor layer was deposited by spin-casting for $_ { 6 0 \mathrm { ~ 5 ~ } }$ at $2 0 0 0 ~ \mathrm { r p m }$ from a $3 \mathrm { \ m g \mathbf { m } L ^ { - 1 } }$ DCB solution in the air. OFET device characteristics were measured under ambient conditions. Typical transfer and output curves for PDPPTzBT based FET devices are shown in Fig. 3. Initially, the field-effect mobility of the as-spun film of PDPPTzBT was $0 . 4 1 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ $\left( I _ { \mathrm { o n } } / I _ { \mathrm { o f f } } = 7 . 1 \times 1 0 ^ { 6 } \right)$ in the saturation regime. Interestingly, the hole mobility was increased to $3 . 4 3 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { \mathbf { V } } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { \mathbf { s } } ^ { - 1 }$ - $I _ { \mathrm { o n } } / I _ { \mathrm { o f f } } = 7 . 2 2 \times$ $1 0 ^ { 6 } )$ as the annealing temperature increased to $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $5 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ in air (Table 1). Moreover, when the annealing temperature increased to $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , the hole mobility was already increased to. $2 . 8 6 ~ \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { \bf ~ V } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { \bf ~ s } ^ { - 1 }$ promising a relatively gentle annealing temperature for high performance polymers.' The high hole mobility observed for PDPPTzBT was thought to arise from the strong intermolecular interactions of this polymer. First, the fused DPP and TzBT moieties have a tendency to form strong intermolecular overlapping through enhanced stacking, resulting in efficient channels for charge carrier transport.11 Second, the donor moieties (thiophene units) and the acceptor moieties (DPP and thiazolothiazole units) in the nearest neighbouring molecules would interact strongly to bring the polymer chains closer as is evidenced by an observed short. $\pi - \pi$ distance of $3 . 5 2 \mathring \mathrm { A } .$ The enhanced intermolecular interactions through donor-acceptor interaction were perhaps an important factor for achieving such high mobility of this polymer.12 The AFM phase images (Fig. 4) of the polymer thin films show the fibrillar intercalating networks in the thin films at room temperature. with increasing annealing temperature, crystal-like grains are observed at $1 0 0 \mathrm { ~ \Omega ~ } ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ or $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ which is in good agreement with the Gixs results. The strong crystallinity and ordered packing in PDPPTzBT would form highly efficient pathways for charge carrier transport throughout the polymer film.

In conclusion, the diketopyrrolopyrrole-thiazolothiazole copolymer, PDPPTzBT, with the combination of fused ring aromatic moieties of DPP and TzBT is a high mobility p-type polymer semiconductor for OFETs. The small $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance implies that heteroaromatic ring structures have a strong tendency to form $\pi - \pi$ stacks with a large overlapping area that is favourable for charge-carrier transport. The facile preparation and high mobility make such polymers very promising for application as solution-processable semiconductors for organic electronic devices.
