# Quasiplanar Heterojunction All-Polymer Solar Cells: A Dual Approach to Stability

Congcong Cao, Hengtao Wang, Dongsheng Qiu, Tingxing Zhao, Yulin Zhu, Xue Lai, Mingrui Pu, Yan Li, Heng Li, Hui Chen, and Feng He\*

In addition to efficiency, stability is another key factor in developing organic solar cells. The quasiplanar heterojunction (Q-PHJ) structure, combining two pure layers as major and tiny nanoscale bulk heterojunction (BHJ) at interface, demonstrates superior device stability compared with BHJ devices. In this contribution, the polymer donor, PBQx-H-TF, and configurationally defined polymeric acceptor, PBTIC- $\gamma$ TSe are synthesized and used to fabricate bilayer devices by orthogonal solvents, the corresponding Q-PHJ all-polymer solar cells (all-PsCs) deliver reliable stability with high efficiency. An encouraging PCE of $1 5 . 7 7 \%$ is achieved, which is the highest one among Q-PHJ all-PsCs with real-bilayer structure. There is a major improvement over the $1 3 . 9 1 \%$ PCE in the BHJ device, and the carrier transport performance is improved substantially following the reduction of recombination in the Q-PHJ all-PSCs. Benefiting from the bilayer morphology, the stability of Q-PHJ all-PSCs has been greatly enhanced over that of the BHJ devices. The charge recombination process is also more serious in the aging BHJ compared with the aging Q-PHJ all-PSCs. This work inspires the application of Q-PHJ in the preparation of high-efficient all-PsCs, but also provides guidance on the improvement of device stability from a dual approach of material and device engineering aspects.

# 1. Introduction

As the development of molecular structure design and device processing continues, all-polymer solar cells (all-PsCs) have attracted more and more attention, especially in the study of device stability.[1,2] Bulk heterojunction (BHJ) all-PSCs use polymers simultaneously as donors and acceptors, which produces some unique advantages, such as improved stability, flexibility, and processing performance.3-5] In 1995, the earliest polymer acceptor CN-PPV was used in OSCs by Yu et al., but it demonstrated an unsatisfactory efficiency.[6] The emergence of polymers constructed with the electron-withdrawing compound naphthalene diimide (NDI), in particular the polymer N2200, provided a major advance.7-8] In 2017, when a strategy of polymerizing acceptor-donor-acceptor structured small molecular acceptor was proposed by Zhang et al. who prepared a new polymer acceptor, PZ1 with a PCE of $9 . 1 9 \%$ [9] Subsequently, polymer acceptors constructed with IDIC, ITIC, and BTIC were designed, synthesized, and applied in OsCs.[10-13] The short-circuit current $( J _ { \mathsf { S C } } )$ of all-PSCs has also been greatly improved and all-PSCs based on BTIC have emerged gradually, with PCEs of $12 \% - 1 6 \%$ [14-17] All-PSCs usually have a more stable morphology than their small molecule analogs. However, in some all-polymer systems, the device photo and storage stability are less than satisfactory. To solve this problem, improvement in the molecular design and device engineering is necessary, especially for the control of the morphology of the blend active layer in BHJ devices. [18-21]

Bulk heterojunction (BHJ) structures with an abundant donor (D)/acceptor (A) interface are capable of achieving effective exciton dissociation, charge generation, and extraction. The substantial progress of the devices in OsCs has been made on the basis of BHJ.22-25] Nevertheless, production of ideal BHJ morphology by spin coating from a single solvent is still very difficult because the polymer donor and acceptor materials are randomly mixed in solution and the solubility of different polymer materials in different solvents varies greatly. The domain size in BHJ should be controlled precisely and an ideal bicontinuous interpenetrating network is required to realize the effective transfer of charge carriers. More importantly, the inevitable poor reproducibility and long-term stability of the BHJ structures also is a huge challenge when contemplating large scale production and various applications.[26-29] To optimize the active layer morphology and alleviate the reproducibility and long-term stability of the active layer, building a planar heterojunction (PHJ) structure through layer-by-layer solution processing[30,31] with two different solvents can be achieved in all-PsCs. The donor and acceptor are deposited sequentially from two orthogonal solvents, and the lower layer will not be damaged by the solvent used for the upper layer during its spin coating. Meanwhile, the nanoscale p-i-n structure will be constructed due to expansion in the solvent, intermolecular diffusion, and the interaction or entangling of sidechains. Such a structure was defined previously as a quasiplanar heterojunction (Q-PHJ).[32-35] In the Q-PHJ structure, each component of the bilayer can be separately optimized, which facilitates better control of the active layer morphology and targeted analysis. This kind of structure achieves efficient exciton dissociation without adversely affecting the transports of the carriers in either the polymer donor or acceptor layers, and the carrier recombination could be reduced significantly.[36,37] However, the Q-PHJ structure is rarely applied in all-PSCs due to the limited access to the appropriate materials and solvent systems.

In this work, the Q-PHJ structure has been used in allPSCs and encouraging results were obtained. In the Q-PHJ all-PsCs, the polymer donor will be enriched at the anode and the acceptor at the cathode. An effective D/A interface is realized by the contact of the two layers which ensures that effective exciton dissociation and charge transfer can be achieved in the Q-PHJ structure. The vertical phase separation of Q-PHJ is favorable for the transport of holes in the donor and electrons in the acceptor, which in turn relieves the charge recombination. A configurationally defined polymer acceptor PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { T S e }$ with a selenophene-containing building block was designed and synthesized. The configurational control of isomerism, widely used in our previous work, was confirmed to be very effective in fine-tuning the intermolecular packing and improving the charge transport performance.38] The polymer PBQx-H-TF was selected as the donor material in the fabrication of Q-PHJ allPSCs, due to its excellent optical and electrical properties.39] The molecular weight and solubility of these two polymer donors and acceptors are quite different, and it is therefore difficult to adjust their concentrations in the same solvent. Thus, the Q-PHJ all-PSCs were fabricated and optimized layer by layer from chlorobenzene (CB) and chloroform (CF) individually. Benefiting from the excellent photoelectric properties of the two polymers, PBQx-H-TF:PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { { T S e } }$ based Q-PHJ allPSCs achieved a much higher PCE of $1 5 . 7 7 \%$ with the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 9 1 \mathrm { V } ,$ $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 3 . 4 9 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and an FF of $7 3 . 7 6 \%$ . This is the highest PCE in all-PSCs reported with a bilayer structure in the active layer.4,41] More importantly, the photo and storage stability of Q-PHJ all-PSCs are greatly improved compared to the BHJ devices. The $T _ { 8 0 }$ $80 \%$ of the initial PCE) lifetime under illumination without encapsulation is about $^ { 8 6 0 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ for Q-PHJ all-PSCs, which is about a three-fold and 17-fold improvement compared with BHJ all-PSCs $T _ { 8 0 } = 2 7 0 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ ) and small molecular acceptor based BHJ devices (50 h). The Q-PHJ all-PSCs without encapsulation still maintains $86 \%$ of the initial PCE after $2 0 0 0 \mathrm { { h } }$ storage. For the Q-PHJ all-PSCs, the recombination caused by the defect state in the active layer is significantly reduced, and the stability of the morphology is enhanced. Along with the development of new polymers with enhanced exciton diffusion length and higher charge carrier mobility, the Q-PHJ all-PSCs would become more practical to built-up durable and efficient solar devices for next commercial applications.

# 2. Result and discussion

# 2.1. Design, Synthesis, and Characterization of Materials

The chemical structures of polymer acceptor PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { { T S e } }$ and polymer donor PBQx-H-TF are shown in Figure 1a. The configurationally defined polymer acceptor PBTIC. $\gamma \mathrm { T S e }$ was synthesized by Pd-catalyzed polymerization from configurationally defined monomer $\gamma \mathrm { B r }$ BTIC and 2, 5-bis (trimethylstannyl) selenophene (Figure 1b), and the details of the synthesis can be found in the Supporting Information. The polymer acceptor PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { T S e }$ shows significant thermal stability with only $5 \%$ weight-loss at over $3 5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ according to a thermogravimetric analysis test (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The synthesis of the polymer donor PBQx-H-TF is described in the Supporting Information.

The UV-Vis absorption spectra of PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { { T S e } }$ were measured in solution and as a film, and are shown in Figure 1c. The absorption spectrum of PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { { T S e } }$ in solution has an absorption peak at $7 9 5 \ \mathrm { n m }$ or $8 1 9 \ \mathrm { n m }$ in the film, and thus a redshift of $2 4 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . This redshift in the film illustrates the enhanced intermolecular $\pi - \pi$ stacking, which contributes to the improved $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ . The polymer donor PBQx-H-TF as a film exhibits an absorption spectrum between 300 and $6 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ with a peak at $5 7 0 \mathrm { n m }$ , which is complementary to the absorption of the polymer acceptor PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { T S e }$ . The energy levels of polymers were obtained by cyclic voltammetry, and are shown in Figure 1d and Figure S2, Supporting Information. The $_ \mathrm { H O M O } /$ LUMO energy levels of PBQx-H-TF and PBTIC $\gamma \mathrm { T S e }$ are $- 3 . 5 8 / - 5 . 5 2 \mathrm { e V }$ and $- 3 . 8 2 / - 5 . 5 7 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively. Density functional theory (DFT) was used with B3LYP/6-31G (d,p) to further study the molecular configuration and electronic distribution of the polymers, and these are shown in Figures S3 and S4, Supporting Information. Both polymers exhibit high planarity, which benefits the intermolecular interaction. As can be seen from the calculation, the HOmO orbitals are mainly distributed on the molecular skeleton and the LUMO orbitals are spread all over the molecular skeleton. The HOMO/LUMO energy levels calculated from the DFT are 2.35/-4.98 eV and -3.39/-5.44 eV for PBQx-H-TF and PBTIC- $\gamma$ I'Se, respectively.

# 2.2. Photovoltaic Performance

The Q-PHJ and BHJ devices were fabricated with the device ITO/PEDOT:PSS/activelayer/PNDIT-F3N/Ag (see the Supporting Information for fabrication details). The Q-PHJ active layer was sequentially deposited with a solution in CB of PBQxH-TF and a solution in CF of PBTIC $\gamma \mathrm { T S e }$ The PBQx-H-TF used in the Q-PHJ device was extracted with CB, and being insoluble in CF cannot be eroded by the upper CF solution, however, corresponding BHJ devices using CB as the solvent have a lower PCE (Table S1 and Figure S5, Supporting Information). The optimal BHJ active layer was cast from the low molecular weight PBQx-H-TF:PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { T S e }$ blend solution in CF. Figure 2a shows a schematic diagram of the structure of BHJ and Q-PHJ all-PSCs, respectively. In the BHJ structure, a random interpenetrating network structure between PBQx-HTF and PBTIC- $\gamma$ TSe fills the whole active layer, in which the two materials aggregate irregularly to form their own pure domain. For the Q-PHJ structure, the polymer donor PBQxH-TF and acceptor PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { { T S e } }$ will be enriched mainly at the anode and cathode, respectively. The nanoscale p-i-n structure is formed by the swelling of polymer side chains in solvent, intermolecular diffusion, and the entanglement of the polymer chains, finally developing a vertical phase separation structure.

The $J { - } V$ curves, external quantum efficiency (EQE) curves, and corresponding photovoltaic parameters of Q-PHJ and BHJ all-PSCs are shown in Figure 2b,c and in Table 1. An outstanding PCE of $1 5 . 7 7 \%$ was achieved in Q-PHJ all-PSCs with an enhanced $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 3 . 4 9 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and a FF of $7 3 . 7 6 \%$ . This is much higher than the PCE of $1 3 . 9 1 \%$ found in BHJ all-PSCs. The improvement in the device performance of Q-PHJ all-PSCs compared with the BHJ all-PSCs is mainly due to $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF. The same phenomenon can be seen in the EQE spectra: the slightly enhanced EQE spectra reflects the increased degree of light response in Q-PHJ all-PSCs, and this contributes to the

$J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of corresponding devices. The $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ derived from the $J { - } V$ curves matches well with the integrated values of $J _ { \mathrm { C a l } }$ from the EQE spectra.

The improved $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values indicate a more efficient exciton utilization process in Q-PHJ all-PSCs than in the BHJ devices.42] To support this, the corresponding characterizations were measured. The photoluminescence (PL) spectra were measured to study the dissociation and charge transfer process in the Q-PHJ and BHJ structures. (Figure 3a,b) The PL emission peaks of PBQx-H-TF in the range of $5 5 0 \mathrm { - } 8 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ and PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { T S e }$ in the range of $8 0 0 { - } 1 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ were all largely quenched in the Q-PHJ and BHJ blends, which confirms the efficient electron transfer from PBQx-H-TF to PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { { T S e } }$ in both the Q-PHJ and BHJ structures.[43] Then the recombination mechanisms in the Q-PHJ and BHJ structure were also studied by the light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ -dependence of $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ in both Q-PHJ and BHJ devices (Figure 3c,d). The relationship between $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ is expressed by the equation of $J _ { S C } \propto P _ { l i g h t } ^ { \alpha }$ [44] The $\alpha$ values of Q-PHJ and BHJ devices fitted from $J _ { \mathrm { S C } ^ { \mathrm { - } } } P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ curves are 0.980 and 0.926, respectively, which shows that the bimolecular recombination in PHJ all-PSCs is significantly restricted compared to the BHJ structure. The $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } { - } P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ curves were measured to analyze the trap-assisted recombination of devices, and the corresponding equation is: $V _ { O C } \propto n ( K T / q ) l n ( P _ { l i g h t } )$ in which, $k$ is the Boltzmann constant, $T$ is the temperature, and $q$ is the elementary charge. The slope values of PHJ and BHJ allPSCs are 1.19 and $1 . 3 8 ~ k T / q$ , which shows that the trap-assisted recombination in the PHJ structure is almost negligible. This illustrates that restricted bimolecular and monomolecular recombination in PHJ all-PSCs proceeds well compared with BHJ all-PSCs.

In order to further analyze the charging process, the timeresolved techniques of transient photocurrent (TPC) and transient photovoltage (TPV) measurement were carried out (Figure 3e,f). The TPC was adopted in an effort to understand the charge extracting capacity. The sweep-out time $( \tau _ { \mathrm { s } } )$ was 0.383 and $0 . 5 7 8 ~ \mu \mathrm { s }$ for Q-PHJ and BHJ all-PSCs, respectively. It was observed that a faster sweep-out process, or charge extraction capacity was obtained in the Q-PHJ structure. The TPV measurement under open-circuit conditions was carried out to measure the charge carrier lifetimes (), which were found to be 1.40 and $0 . 9 9 ~ \mu \mathrm { s }$ for Q-PHJ and BHJ all-PSCs, respectively. The prolonged lifetime and much faster charge extraction contribute to the increased $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF in the Q-PHJ all-PSCs. The charge carrier mobilities were determined by the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method, and are shown in Figure S6 and Table S2, Supporting Information, details could be found in the Supporting Information. Measured by SCLC, the hole mobilities $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } )$ and electron mobilities $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ were $6 . 0 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ and $5 . 6 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for the Q-PHJ structure and $3 . 3 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $2 . 8 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for the BHJ structure. Much higher and more balanced carrier mobilities were achieved in the Q-PHJ all-PSCs, and this is beneficial for the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF in devices. Through this characterization and analysis therefore, an enhanced charge transport and reduced recombination process was achieved in the Q-PHJ all-PSCs.

# 2.3. Morphology and Stability

Dynamic X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy was used to study the vertical distribution of the active layer. Figure 4a,b display the selenium (Se) 3d scanning signal images of the depth profile in the active layer region of Q-PHJ and BHJ. The binding energy of a selenium 3d electron is $5 6 . 2 \ \mathrm { e V } .$ The brightness of the images represents the magnitude of the maximum signal intensity, while the vertical axis represents the etching time along with the active layer. As a characteristic element of the polymer acceptor PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { T S e }$ scanning signals from the Se 3d electron can be used to characterize the content of PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { { T S e } }$ at different depths of the active layer. Correspondingly, scanning signals from the fluorine (F) 1s were probed as a characteristic element of the polymer donor PBQx-H-TF (Figure S7, Supporting Information), and the binding energy is about $6 8 5 . 9 \mathrm { e V } .$ On the surface of the Q-PHJ active layer, the Se 3d signal could be detected, but no F 1s signal. As the etching depth increases, the Se 3d electron scanning signals gradually weaken until they disappear and the F 1s signals gradually appeared and are enhanced after etching for ${ 1 5 0 \ \mathrm { s } } .$ Conversely, an even vertical distribution was found in the BHJ active layer. This indicates the enrichment in the upper layer of the polymer acceptor while the polymer donor is enriched in the lower layer, accompanied by the donor-acceptor blending region due to the solvent swelling, intermolecular diffusion, and intermolecular interactions. The cross-sectional scanning electron microscopy images of the Q-PHJ and BHJ all-PSCs are shown in Figure 4d,e, respectively. In the cross-sectional images, ITO, PEDOT:PSS, active layer, and Ag are distributed in sequence. In the Q-PHJ structure (Figure 4d), the PBQx-H-TF in the lower layer and PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { { T S e } }$ in the upper layer can be clearly distinguished and maintain close contact, so that effective exciton dissociation and carrier transport in the respective layers can be achieved. while for the BHJ film as shown in Figure 4e, only one blend layer can be found between the Ag electrode and PEDOT:PSS interlayer from its cross-section image. The surface morphology of the active layer was also studied by atomic force microscopy (AFM), as is shown in Figure S8, Supporting Information. The Q-PHJ and BHJ films both exhibit smooth and uniform surfaces with similar root mean square roughness values of 0.980 and $1 . 0 3 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , suggesting that the appropriate film surface morphology can be obtained in both structures. The molecular packing and orientation of active layer films in Q-PHJ and BHJ were analyzed by the GIWAXS analysis (Figure 4c,f), and the corresponding line-cut profiles were shown in Figure 4g,h. In the out-of-plane direction, similar $\pi \mathfrak { - } \pi$ stacking peaks at $1 . 6 4 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ were observed. Along with the in-plane direction, the lamellar (100) diffraction peaks were located at about $0 . 3 1 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ with the full width at half-maximum (FWHM) of 0.09 and 0.11 A for the Q-PHJ and BHJ films, respectively. Both of the Q-PHJ and BHJ films exhibit favorable face-on orientation and proper crystallinity, which is convenient to charge transport.

Device stability is a quite important indicator which can be used to evaluate the performance of OsCs. Polymers usually have better morphology stability than their small molecule analogs, but there are fewer random phase separations in Q-PHJ. Consequently, the morphology of the active layer is in theory, more stable. We took advantage of the light-soaking stability $( 1 0 0 \mathrm { m W } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ and the shelf-life stability of the Q-PHJ and BHJ all-PsCs in a glovebox under nitrogen without encapsulation. The PBQx-H-TF:Y6 BHJ devices were used as a reference. Benefiting from the stability of the material and the optimization of the device structure, the PBQx-H-TF/PBTIC $\gamma \mathrm { T S e }$ based Q-PHJ all-PsCs demonstrated superior light stability and shelf-life stability. After illumination for $9 0 0 \ \mathrm { { h } }$ (Figure 5a), the Q-PHJ allPSCs retain about $80 \%$ of the initial PCE $T _ { 8 0 } = 8 7 0 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ , and the BHJ all-PSCs can also retain a moderate PCE of about $70 \%$ of that of the initial PCE $T _ { 8 0 } = 2 6 0 \ \mathrm { h } )$ , much better than the small molecule acceptor based BHJ devices $[ T _ { 8 0 } = 5 0 \mathrm { h }$ ). Similar results can be seen in the shelf-life stability tests: Q-PHJ all-PSCs stored for $2 0 0 0 \ \mathrm { h }$ under nitrogen still retain over $86 \%$ of their initial efficiency and show extremely slow decay (Figure 5b). This indicates that Q-PHJ all-PSCs exhibit the best stability among the three device structures, and provides an effective strategy to improve the device stability in long-term use.

By analyzing the variation of FF and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ under illumination, FF was found to be the parameter with the largest attenuation in the light stability of the devices. The corresponding FF and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ are shown as a function of illumination time in Figure S9, Supporting Information. From the perspective of charge dynamics, the FF of devices is related to charge recombination. In order to study the recombination mechanisms, the charge carrier lifetime $( \tau )$ and the charge carrier density (n) were determined through the TPV and the charge extraction measurement under different light intensities.45,46] The normalized photovoltage under different illumination intensities is shown in Figure S10, Supporting Information. The charge density in the device increases with the light intensity, and this leads to an increased probability of charge recombination in the device. Thus, the lifetime $\tau$ of devices will exponentially decrease as a function of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ according to the formula: $\tau = \tau _ { 0 } e ^ { - \beta V _ { o c } }$ while the carrier density (n) rises exponentially as a function of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ according to the formula: $n = n _ { 0 } e ^ { \gamma V _ { O C } }$ [47,48] The $\tau$ values under 1 sun illumination of the aging Q-PHJ and BHJ all-PSCs after $9 0 0 \ \mathrm { h }$ illumination were 0.92 and $0 . 2 2 ~ { \mu \mathrm { s } }$ . The carrier lifetime of the aging BHJ all-PSC is extremely reduced compared to a fresh device, and this is due to serious recombination difficulties in the device. A double logarithmic plot of carrier lifetime versus carrier density is shown in Figure 5e,h for the Q-PHJ and BHJ devices, respectively, and the relationship between them is expressed by a recombination exponent $\lambda ,$ which can be calculated by the formula: $\tau = \tau _ { 0 } ( n _ { 0 } / n ) ^ { \lambda }$ [49] The equation $R = \lambda + 1$ represents the recombination orders of devices, which were 2.04, 2.28, 2.84, and 3.78 for the fresh Q-PHJ, BHJ and the aging Q-PHJ and BHJ all-PSC devices, respectively. Such a high recombination order was found in the aging BHJ device, which indicates the presence of a trap state caused by an energetic disorder in the device in the process of device aging with light radiation, which is less serious in the Q-PHJ all-PSCs. This trend is consistent with that in device parameters and morphology.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, the Q-PHJ structure used in all-PSCs achieved an encouraging result. By introducing the Q-PHJ structure into the all-PsCs, the efficiency and stability of the device have been significantly improved. A configurationally defined polymer acceptor PBTIC- $\gamma \mathrm { T S e }$ with a selenophene-containing building block was designed and synthesized as the polymer acceptor and the polymer PBQx-H-TF was selected as the donor material with which to fabricate Q-PHJ all-PSCs. In Q-PHJ devices, the polymer donor and acceptor were sequentially deposited from two orthogonal solvents, to form a vertical phase-separated structure in the active layer. The difference in solubility and compatibility between polymer donor and acceptor can be well resolved. An excellent PCE of $1 5 . 7 7 \%$ was achieved in the Q-PHJ all-PSCs, which is higher than the $1 3 . 9 1 \%$ found in the corresponding BHJ all-PSCs. The enhanced efficiency comes from the improvement of carrier transport performance and the reduction of recombination in the double-layer active materials. At the same time, the $T _ { 8 0 }$ lifetime of Q-PHJ all-PSCs is also greatly improved compared with BHJ devices due to its better morphological stability. Even after $9 0 0 \ \mathrm { h }$ of illumination, the Q-PHJ film with both polymer layers still maintains almost original microstructures while BHJ film suffers from severe morphology change. This research covers new ground in the application of quasiplanar heterojunction in the preparation of high-efficiency all-PSCs, but also achieves significant guidance on the improvement of device stability.
