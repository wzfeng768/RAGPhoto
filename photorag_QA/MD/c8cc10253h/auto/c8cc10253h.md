# Direct access to substituted benzo[b]carbazoles through cascade annulation of 2-vinylbenzaldehydes with indoles

Deng-Yuan Li, An Wang, Xiao-Ping Zhu, Wei Feng and Pei-Nian Liu

rsc.li/chemcomm

A highly efficient palladium-catalyzed cascade annulation of 2-vinylbenzaldehydes with indoles has been achieved to afford 6-(3-indolyl)- benzo[b]carbazoles under mild conditions in good yield and with excellent regioselectivity. Mechanistic investigations reveal that the reaction proceeds via double addition of indoles, unexpected intramolecular 1,4-aryl and 1,2-hydrogen migrations, and oxidative aromatization.

Benzo[b]carbazoles are an important class of $N \cdot$ -fused polyaromatic compounds found in many naturally occurring alkaloids that display a range of attractive biological activities.1,2 For instance, naturally occurring alkaloids such as ellipticine I are analogues of benzo[b]carbazoles and show promising antitumor activity ${ \bf \xi } ^ { 2 a - c } { \bf \xi } N .$ Methyl-substituted benzo $[ b ]$ carbazole II was discovered to possess anticancer activity in vitro.2d Recently, 5H-benzo[b]carbazole III with an indolyl group at the C-6 position has been found to inhibit the A1-40 aggregation, making it potentially useful for treating progressive neurodegenerative diseases2e (Fig. 1). On the other hand, planar benzo[b]carbazole scaffolds demonstrate promising optical and electronic properties, making them attractive as functional building blocks in optoelectronic devices, such as organic light-emitting diodes and organic field effect transistors., For example, benzo[b]carbazole end-capped oligothiophenes IV have been used as semiconductors in organic field effect transistors (Fig. 1).4a

In view of the great importance of benzo[b]carbazoles, various strategies have been derived for their synthesis in recent decades.1a,5 Most of the early strategies to construct benzo[b]carbazoles involve multiple steps, while more recent approaches involve intra- or intermolecular annulation of pre-functionalized precursors.6-10 The examples include the cycloaromatization of keteneimines,' intramolecular dehydro-Diels-Alder cycloaddition reactions using ynamides, and benzannulation reactions with indoles,' carbazoles, or naphthalenes,o etc. However, most of these methods have some disadvantages that limit their utility, such as harsh conditions, restriction to special substituted substrates, and low product yields. Therefore, highly efficient methods are still demanded to synthesize substituted benzo[b]carbazoles from easily accessible starting materials under mild conditions.

One promising starting material may be vinylaldehydes, which contain both carbonyl and alkenyl groups. They are versatile synthons because they can be trapped with other reagents in a cascade manner to construct various carbo- and heterocycles.11 For example, Yoshikai,12a,b Breit,12c and Stanley12d independently reported the enantio- and diastereoselective intramolecular hydroacylation of vinylaldehydes to indanones. Masson achieved a photoredox-catalyzed tandem reaction of vinylaldehydes involving azidation or cyanidation of carbonyl aldehyde and trifluoromethylation of alkenyl, affording trifluoromethylated phthalans and isoindolines.13a Zhu reported the remote trifluoromethylamidation and aryldifluoroalkylation of vinylaldehydes to afford $\varepsilon { \mathrm { - C F } } _ { 3 } { \mathrm { . } }$ substituted amides13b and 5-, 6-, or 7-difluoroalkylated ketones.13c More recently, Giri reported the Ni-catalyzed regioselective 1,2-dicarbofunctionalization of olefins, which involved transient metallacycles derived from vinylaldehydes.14 Despite the advances, most of the reactions of vinylaldehydes are intramolecular reactions or the intermolecular reactions only involve a single functionalization of the carbonyl group or alkenyl group, which could only afford rather simple scaffolds.

During our on-going interest in the construction of polycyclic structures,15 we envision to develop a new cascade reaction of vinylaldehydes, which combines the functionalization of both the carbonyl group and alkenyl group in vinylaldehydes. Such a reaction may greatly expand the synthetic applications of vinylaldehydes, especially for the construction of polycyclic skeletons. Herein, we report a Pd-catalyzed cascade annulation of 2-vinylbenzaldehydes with indoles to afford 6-(3-indolyl)benzo[b]carbazoles in good yield and with excellent regioselectivity. Mechanistic studies indicated that this reaction proceeds via double intermolecular addition of indoles to the carbonyl of 2-vinylbenzaldehyde and unexpected intramolecular 1,4-aryl and 1,2-hydrogen migrations, followed by oxidative aromatization. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first example of simultaneous functionalization of both the carbonyl and alkenyl groups in 2-vinylbenzaldehydes.

Initially, 2-vinylbenzaldehyde 1a and 1-methyl-1H-indole 2a were chosen as benchmark substrates to optimize the reaction conditions (Table 1). (entry 1). Based on the optimized reaction conditions (entry 14, Table 1), we explored the substrate scope of this transformation (Scheme 1). The reaction worked efficiently with 2-vinylbenzaldehydes bearing electron-neutral or -rich functional groups such as Me, MeO, F, Cl and $\mathrm { C F } _ { 3 }$ , producing compounds 3b-f in good to excellent yield. The reaction tolerated 2-vinylbenzaldehydes substituted with an alkyl group or halogen at the para position of the benzaldehyde (3g-k). Comparable yields were obtained of the methyl-substituted product $3 \mathbf { g } \left( 7 2 \% \right)$ as of the fluoro-substituted 3i $( 8 2 \% )$ one. Our method was compatible with most of the substrates bearing electron-donating or electronwithdrawing substituents such as MeO and $\mathrm { C F } _ { 3 }$ affording the desired products 3h and 3k in respective yields of $4 0 \%$ and $7 5 \%$ The reaction even tolerated 4,5-difluoro-substituted substrates, affording the product 3l in $6 0 \%$ yield. Notably, non-terminal 2-vinylbenzaldehydes such as 2-styrylbenzaldehyde and 2-(pent-1- en-1-yl)benzaldehyde only generated complicated mixtures in their reactions with 2a.

Next we investigated the compatibility of the method with different types of 1-methyl-1H-indoles. The reaction tolerated 1-methyl-1H-indoles which contain electron-donating or electronwithdrawing groups on the phenyl ring $\left( 3 \mathbf { m } \mathbf { - } \mathbf { v } \right)$ . For example, the 5-CN-substituted product 3q and the 6-Me-substituted product 3r were obtained in respective yields of $3 5 \%$ and $8 7 \%$ . The singlecrystal X-ray diffraction analysis of 3u further confirmed the structure of the products (see the $\mathrm { E S I \dag }$ J. The screening of polysubstituted 1-methyl-1H-indoles showed that the reaction could proceed in the presence of two electron-withdrawing groups on the 1-methyl-1H-indole unit, affording product 3w in $6 7 \%$ yield. The substrate 5,6-dihydro-4H-pyrrolo[3,2,1-ij]quinolone also worked in the reaction, affording product 3x in $9 1 \%$ yield. It is noteworthy that the free indole produced complicated products in this cascade reaction with 1a.

As shown in Scheme 2, control experiments were performed to investigate the reaction mechanism. First, 1a was replaced with compound 4 under standard conditions, and the desired product 3a was obtained in $8 2 \%$ isolated yield (eqn (1)). Then we tried to capture the reaction intermediates by simultaneously decreasing the reaction time to $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ and the temperature to $2 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } .$ The addition product 5 was isolated in $4 9 \%$ yield, along with a small amount of product 3a ( $1 1 \%$ yield, eqn (2)). Under similar reaction conditions, the addition product 5 could be converted smoothly to product 3a in $4 5 \%$ isolated yield (eqn (3)). These results clearly demonstrate that compounds 4 and 5 are intermediates in the cascade reactions.

To further understand the cascade pathway, deuterium experiments were performed (Scheme 3). Reacting 1a and 2a in the presence of ${ \bf D } _ { 2 } { \bf O }$ under standard conditions afforded the product 3a without deuterium incorporation (eqn (1)). This result indicates that the hydrogen transfer is not intermolecular. Reaction of [D]-1a and 2a under standard conditions also led to 3a without deuterium incorporation (eqn (2)). In contrast, reacting $[ \bf { D } ] { - } { \bf { 1 } } \mathbf { a } ^ { \prime }$ with 2a generated [D]-3a with approximately $9 4 \%$ of deuterium incorporation in the newly generated methyl (eqn (3)). The results demonstrate that the cascade annulation involves intramolecular 1,2-hydrogen migration rather than 1,5-hydrogen migration. In addition, a kinetic isotope effect of 1.1 was obtained from parallel reactions (see the $\mathrm { E S I \dagger }$ J, suggesting that the C-H bond cleavage is not the rate-determining step in the reaction.

These results, together with the literature,16 led us to propose a plausible mechanism for the reaction of 2-vinylbenzaldehydes with indoles (Scheme 4). Using 1a and 2a as reactants, the addition of 2a to the carbonyl of 1a generates intermediate 4, which undergoes a second addition of 2a to form the key intermediate 5.16a-c Intermediate 5 undergoes 1,4-aryl migration via intermediate A to form intermediate B through 5-ipso cyclization at the alkenyl double bond and the subsequent C-C bond cleavage. $1 6 d { - } f$ The unexpected 1,2-hydrogen migration may occur upon the $\beta \mathrm { - H }$ elimination to generate palladium-alkene complex C, which undergoes intramolecular cyclization via addition of Pd-H to $\mathrm { C } { = } \mathrm { C }$ to afford intermediate D. Finally, D undergoes oxidative aromatization in the presence of ${ \mathrm { K } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { S } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 8 }$ to produce 3a as the product.

In summary, we have developed a novel Pd-catalyzed cascade annulation of 2-vinylbenzaldehydes with indoles to afford 6-(3-indolyl)benzo[b]carbazoles in good yield and with excellent regioselectivity. Mechanistic studies show that this reaction begins with double intermolecular addition of indoles to the carbonyl in vinylbenzaldehydes, and the resulting intermediate undergoes unexpected intramolecular 1,4-aryl and 1,2-hydrogen migrations, as well as oxidative aromatization to afford the final polycyclic product. This one-step reaction is highly efficient to construct polycyclic carbon skeletons, which form three new C-C bonds via simultaneous functionalization of both the carbonyl and alkenyl groups in 2-vinylbenzaldehydes. This novel protocol may be applied to the synthesis of useful polycyclic compounds and optoelectronic materials and give insight to the development of cascade reactions.
