# Improved Performance of All-Polymer Solar Cells Enabled by Naphthodiperylenetetraimide-Based Polymer Acceptor

Yikun Guo, Yunke Li, Omar Awartani, Han Han, Jingbo Zhao, Harald Ade,\* He Yan,\* and Dahui Zhao\*

A new polymer acceptor, naphthodiperylenetetraimide-vinylene (NDP-V), featuring a backbone of altenating naphthodiperylenetetraimide and vinylene units is designed and applied in all-polymer solar cells (all-PsCs). With this polymer acceptor, a new record power-conversion efficiencies (PCE) of $8 . 5 9 \%$ has been achieved for all-PSCs. The design principle of NDP-V is to reduce the conformational disorder in the backbone of a previously developed high-performance acceptor, PDI-V, a perylenediimide-vinylene polymer. The chemical modifications result in favorable changes to the molecular packing behaviors of the acceptor and improved morphology of the donor-acceptor (PTB7-Th:NDP-V) blend, which is evidenced by the enhanced hole and electron transport abilities of the active layer. Moreover, the stronger absorption of NDP-V in the shorter-wavelength range offers a better complement to the donor. All these factors contribute to a short-circuit current density $\left( \boldsymbol { J } _ { s c } \right)$ of $\mathsf { 1 7 . 0 7 ~ m A ~ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ With a fill factor (FF) of 0.67, an average PCE of $8 . 4 8 \%$ is obtained, representing the highest value thus far reported for all-pSCs.

As an emerging technology for harnessing solar energy, polymer solar cells (PsCs) have received extensive attention during the past decade. With a binary active layer formed by interpenetrating electron donor and acceptor, the bulk-heterojunction (BHJ) devices facilitate efficient exciton diffusion, charge separation, and carrier collection, giving rise to high power-conversion efficiencies (PCE). The appealing properties of solution-processed BHJ PSCs include low cost, light weight, mechanical flexibility, and promise of facile large-area fabrications.14 Up to date, PSCs containing hole-transporting polymers as the donor, with fullerene or organic small molecules as the acceptor, have demonstrated impressive performance, with the state-ofthe-art PCE values reaching ${ > } 1 0 \%$ [5-12]

Compared to fullerene- and small molecule-based devices, all-psCs offer additional merits of high morphological stability, superior mechanical properties, and suitability for printing-related fabrication techniques.[13-20] The bottle-neck that limits the advancement of all-PSCs is currently the properties of polymer acceptors. With extensive research efforts spent on new acceptor designs and device optimizations,21-26] the record PCE of all-PSCs was pushed to $8 . 2 7 \%$ , as reported by Li and co-workers. This high PCE was accomplished by a widely studied low-bandgap naphthalenediimide (NDI) based polymer acceptor N2200, along with a medium-bandgap donor polymer. Due to their well-complemented absorption spectra, a high short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ of $1 4 . 1 8 ~ \mathrm { m A ~ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ was obtained.[23] Recently, we also reported that, by implementing an acceptor of perylenediimide (PDI) polymer with vinylene linkers (PDI-V, Figure 1), a high PCE of $7 . 5 7 \%$ can be obtained,[27] which was the best performance achieved for PDI-based polymer acceptors.

In the current contribution, we report our further endeavors at improving the PCE of all-PSCs to a new record of $8 . 5 9 \%$ This is realized by designing a new polymer acceptor, NDP-V, featuring a backbone of naphthodiperylenetetraimide and vinylene units. The design rationale is to reduce the conformational disorder of the previous PDI-V acceptor. By covalently fusing half of the vinylene linkers in PDI-V backbone to the bay region of adjacent PDI units on both sides, larger, shape-persistent NDP units are obtained, based on which a new polymer (NDP-V) featuring a poly(NDP-vinylene) backbone is designed and synthesized (Figure 1). The electrochemical characterizations conducted with cyclic voltammetry (CV) indicate that NDP-V possesses a lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of about $- 4 . 0 3 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ similar to that of PDI-V. Estimated from the optical bandgap and above LUMO level, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of NDP-V is lowered by more than $0 . 1 \ \mathrm { e V }$ relative to that of PDI-V. To understand the larger bandgap of NDP-V, density functional theory (DFT) calculation using B3LYP functional and a $6 { \cdot } 3 1 \mathrm { G } ^ { \ast }$ basis set were performed on both PDI-V and NDP-V (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The partially locked backbone of NDP-V reduced the density of vinylene donor units in the main chain, which originated the lower lying HOMO, thus a wider bandgap. DFT calculations confirm that such HOMO and LUMO energy levels are in agreement to the simulated frontier orbital features.[1] Using the spacecharge limited current (SCLC) method and a device configuration of indium tin oxide $\mathrm { ( I T O ) / Z n O / p o l y m e r / C a / A l }$ the electron mobility of NDP-V is calculated to be about $2 . 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , which is evidently higher than that of PDI-V.

Our experimental results confirm that such chemical modifications to the polymer backbone yield favorable changes to the BHJ morphology and enhanced device performance. The blend of PTB7-Th:NDP-V exhibits both increased and more balanced hole and electron mobilities compared to the film of PTB7-Th:PDI-V, which lead to an improved fill factor (FF) of 0.67. Moreover, the NDP chromophore has also promoted an absorption enhancement of the polymer and offered a better complement to the donor component. All these factors have contributed to the higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $1 7 . 0 7 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and a generally elevated external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectrum. Thus, a high PCE of $8 . 5 9 \%$ is recorded from a solar cell comprising the active layer of PTB7-Th:NDP-V.

The new NDp-V polymer is synthesized by polymer izing dibromo-NDP $( \mathrm { N D P - B r } _ { 2 } )$ with trans-1,2-bis(tri- $n$ -butylstannyl)ethylene under Stille cross-coupling conditions (Scheme 1). $\mathrm { N D P - B r } _ { 2 }$ is prepared using a reported procedure.[28] The polymer exhibits adequate solubility in common organic solvents, including $o$ dichlorobenzene, chlorobenzene (CB), and chloroform. Slightly higher molecular weight is determined for NDP-V than PDI-V (Iable 1), which is a reasonable result from the larger monomer unit of NDP-V.

The optical bandgap of NDp-V is slightly widened compared to that of PDI-V, which is typical for PDI derivatives with annulated bay region.[28-30] The extinction coefficients of the absorption peaks at 400 and $5 4 5 \ \mathrm { n m }$ assignable to different $\pi - \pi ^ { * }$ transitions of the aromatic backbone, are both substantially increased (Figure 2). The EQE was measured to examine the spectral responses of the new all-psC. As depicted in Figure 3b, compared to that of PTB7-Th:PDI-V, EQE enhancement is exhibited by PTB7-Th:NDP-V in a broad range of $3 7 0 { - } 7 5 0 \ \mathrm { ~ n m }$ . Particularly, a distinct peak arises at ${ \approx } 4 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , which corresponds to the absorption maximum of NDP-V. Another elevated region occurs between 450 and $6 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ which can be correlated to another absorption peak of NDP-V around $5 5 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ Besides these increments attributable to the new acceptor, the EQE values of PTB7-Th:NDP-V also slightly improve in the range of $6 5 0 { - } 7 5 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , where NDP-V does not absorb. Apparently, this part of the photoresponse arises from the donor polymer. These observations suggest that NDP-V has contributed to the higher PCE by both providing a more complemented absorption spec trum and improving the BHJ morphology.

The photoluminescence quenching of the active layer may help in assessing the efficiency of photo-induced carrier generation in all-PSCs. Upon blending with PTB7-Th, the fluorescence of NDP-V is quenched by over $9 5 \%$ , indicating highly efficient exciton collection at the donor/acceptor interfaces (Figure S2,

Supporting Information). The carrier transport properties of the PTB7-Th:NDP-V blend are characterized using the SCLC method (Table 2). Both the electron and hole mobilities $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } } )$ of PTB7-Th:NDP-V are increased about $5 0 \%$ compared to those of PTB7-Th:PDI-V. The smaller $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ value of the former is also beneficial for improving FF of the solar cell.[33]

Grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXs)34] is conducted to analyze the molecular packing and phase separation features in the neat acceptor films and active layers. From the 2D GIWAXS pattern of the neat NDP-V film (Figure S3, Supporting Information), a similar packing model is observed compared to that of PDI-v.[27] Nonetheless, the reduced $\pi { - } \pi$ and lamellar distance in the out-of-plane (OOP) direction (Table S2, Supporting Information) and lack of poly morphs in the in-plane (IP) direction indicate that NDP-V exhibits improved molecular packing, which is consistent with its higher SCLC electron mobility (Table 1). The 2D GIWAXS pattern of the PTB7-Th:NDP-V blend is displayed in Figure 4. The (100) and (010) peaks are observable preferentially in IP and OOP directions, respectively, indicating preferential face-on orientation is exhibited (Figure 4). The strong and well-defined (001) peak in the in-plane direction reflects a relatively regular polymer packing along the direction of polymer backbone and a preferential in-plane orientation of the backbone. These data suggest that the backbone modification did not cause any negative changes to the polymer packing of the blend film compared to that of PTB7-Th:PDI-V.[27]

The surface morphology of the active-layer is further characterized by the atomic force microscopy. The height and phase images reveal that the PTB7-Th:NDP-V blend forms relatively smooth and uniform surface with a root-mean-square (RMS) roughness of $0 . 8 6 \ \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure S4, Supporting Information). The RMS roughness is slightly larger than that of PTB7- Th:PDI-V. It has been reported in the literature,[35-37] that when the surface is sufficiently smooth higher RMS roughness may help improving the contact between the active layer and interfacial electrode, and thus favor the charge collection.

From the above results, it is clear that the chemical modification to the polymer acceptor, involving partially locking the rotational freedom and modulating the backbone rigidity of PDI-V, has brought about favorable effects on the all-PSC performance. As an additional step to optimize the polymer acceptor structure, the side chain-length effect on the photovoltaic performance is investigated. Namely, polymer analogs of NDP-V with different alkyl side-chain lengths are prepared and corresponding all-PsCs are fabricated (Chart S1, Supporting Information). When the side chain length is extended from 1-pentylhexyl to 1-octylnonyl, the solar cell performance exhibits only insignificant fuctuations, and all devices based on different NDP-V derived acceptors provided average PCEs of ${ > } 8 . 0 \%$ (Table S3, Supporting Information). Such observations are quite different from the results from other polymers.17,22,3840] We speculate that the relatively insensitive dependence of the polymer acceptor performance on the side-chain length is likely related to the unique geometry of NDP structure. Such large, shape-persistent yet nonplanar polycyclic scaffolds may be favorable for establishing a proper balance between self-aggregation and solute-solvent interactions. The material morphology is thus dominantly governed by the polymer backbone and less dependent on the side chains.

In summary, we report a new PCE record of $8 . 5 9 \%$ for allPSCs. This achievement is accomplished by developing a new polymer acceptor, NDP-V. The backbone of this polymer is composed of large electron-deficient naphthodiperylenetetraimide units joined by small vinylene linkers. Compared to a related polymer analog of PDI-V, this new acceptor provides enhanced and more complementary absorptions to the donor polymer PTB7-Th. As importantly, the solar cell characteristics suggest that more favorable morphology is shown by the polymer blend of PTB7-Th:NDP-V, as evidenced by both increased hole and electron mobilities, as well as an improved FF. Benefitting from the more favorable absorption and morphology, an impressive $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $1 7 . 0 7 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ is exhibited, along with a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 7 4 \ \mathrm { V } .$ Nice reproducibility of the cell performance is demonstrated by a high average PCE of $8 . 4 8 \%$ , and no additive or post-treatment is required during the device processing. All these properties suggest a promising future for the application of this new polymer acceptor material.

# Experimental Section

Device Fabrication: Prepatterned ITO-coated glasses with a sheet resistance of ${ \approx } 1 5 ~ \Omega ~ 5 { \sf q } ^ { - 1 }$ were used as the substrates. They were cleaned by sequential cycles of sonication in soap water, deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol for 15 min each. After UV/ozone treatment for $6 0 \mathsf { m i n }$ ${ \sf z n o }$ electron transport layers were prepared on top of the ITO by spincoating a ${ \sf Z n O }$ precursor solution (diethyl zinc) at 5o00 rpm. The activelayer solutions were prepared in CB (with a polymer concentration of $9 \mathrm { \ m g \ m L ^ { - 1 } }$ and a $\mathsf { D } / \mathsf { A }$ ratio at 1:1). To completely dissolve the polymers, the solutions were stirred on a hot plate at $1 1 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathsf { C }$ for at least $^ \textrm { \scriptsize 3 h }$ The active layers were spin-coated from the cooled polymer solution on the ${ \sf z n o }$ -coated substrates in a glove-box under $\mathsf { N } _ { 2 }$ atmosphere at 1700 rpm to obtain a thickness of ${ \approx } 1 2 0 ~ \mathsf { n m }$ The blend films were then transferred to the vacuum chamber of a thermal evaporator in the same glove-box. At a vacuum level of $\leq 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 6 }$ Torr, a thin layer $( \approx 7 ~ \mathsf { n m } )$ of ${ \mathsf V } _ { 2 } { \mathsf O } _ { 5 }$ was deposited as the anode interlayer, followed by the deposition of 100 nm of Al as the top electrode. All cells were encapsulated using epoxy inside the glove-box. The device $J { - } V$ characteristics were measured under AM1.5G $( 7 0 0 \ m \times 1 0 0 ^ { - 2 } )$ using a Newport solar simulator. The light intensity was calibrated using a standard Si diode (with a KG5 filter purchased from PV Measurement) to bring spectral mismatch to unity. The J-V characteristics were recorded using a Keithley 2400 source meter unit. Typical cells have a device area of $5 . 9 \ \mathsf { m m } ^ { 2 }$ , which is defined by a metal mask with an aperture aligned with the device area. EQEs were measured using a Newport EQE system equipped with a standard Si diode. Monochromatic light was generated from a Newport 300 W lamp source.
