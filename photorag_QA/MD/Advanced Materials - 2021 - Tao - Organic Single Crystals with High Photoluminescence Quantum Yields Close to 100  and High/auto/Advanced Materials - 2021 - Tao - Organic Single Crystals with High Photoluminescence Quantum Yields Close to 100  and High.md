# Organic Single Crystals with High Photoluminescence Quantum Yields Close to 1o0% and High Mobility for Optoelectronic Devices

Jingwei Tao, Dan Liu, Jiangbo Jing, Huanli Dong, Leijing Liu,\* Bin Xu, and Wenjing Tian\*

Organic single crystals with excellent optical and electrical properties are critical for the development of organic optoelectronics. Herein, two compounds 9,10-bis([N,N-diphenyl] $\mathbf { \mathcal { A } ^ { \prime } }$ -phenylethynyl)anthracene (TPA-An) and 9,10-bis([l', $3 ^ { \prime }$ -diphenyl]- $. 5 ^ { \prime }$ -phenylethynyl)anthracene (TBA-An) are synthesized by introducing two different luminescent groups, triphenylamine and 1,3-diphenylbenzene, at the 9,10 positions of anthracene via triple bond connection. Single crystals based on TPA-An and TBA-An with a ribbon morphology obtained through the slow solvent-evaporation method exhibit high photoluminescence quantum yields (PLQYs) of $98 \%$ and $9 9 \%$ at room temperature, and remarkable hole mobilities of 0.45 and $0 . 7 5 \mathsf { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathsf { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathsf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ in single-crystal organic field-effect transistors (SC-OFETs). Furthermore, UV phototransistors based on the two single crystals obtain photosensitivities of $1 . 0 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 3 }$ and $3 . 4 5 \times 7 0 ^ { 4 }$ , ultrahigh photoresponsivities of $7 . 1 9 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ and $1 . 5 0 \times$ $\mathsf { 1 0 ^ { 5 } A W ^ { - 1 } }$ , and the detectivities exceeding $1 . 4 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 6 }$ and $1 . 6 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 7 }$ Jones.

# 1. Introduction

Organic single crystals based on various organic $\pi$ conjugated semiconductor materials have been studied widely in recent years since they have potential applications in organic lightemitting diodes (OLEDs),1l organic field-effect transistors (OFETs),2] organic electrically pumped lasers (OEPLs),[3] organic light-emitting field-effect transistors (OLETs),(4] and organic phototransistors (OPTs).[5] OPTs as multifunctional optoelectronic devices represent an important emerging technology, since they can provide the optical sensing capability of electrical switches, making them an ideal choice for signal amplification electro-optic sensors. And this attractive characteristic is the key to overcome the noise in the conventional photodiode readout circuit, because the gate field-controlled tunable gain mechanism can achieve controllable sensitivity and low noise.[6] In recent years, some multifunctional organic semiconductors with strong fuorescence emission, high mobility, and light sensitivity have been designed and synthesized, and they have been applied in OPTs to obtain excellent photoresponse properties. In 2015, Hu and co-workers designed and synthesized 2,6-diphenylanthracene (DPA), whose crystal exhibited a mobility of up to $3 4 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and a photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) of $4 1 . 2 \%$ [7] Then, they reported DPA thin film as photoresponsive layers of OPTs, which showed the photosensitivity as high as $8 . 5 ~ \times$ $1 0 ^ { 7 }$ photoresponsivity as high as $1 . 3 4 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { A W ^ { - 1 } }$ , and the high detectivity reached $1 . 2 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 7 }$ Jones upon the illumination of $4 3 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ light.1] In 2018, our group designed and synthesized a butterfly molecule, $5 , 5 ^ { \prime }$ -bis(2,2-diphenylvinyl)-bithiophene (BDPV2T), and its single crystal exhibited an average mobility of $1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } .$ a PLQY of $30 \%$ , and a photoresponsivity of $2 . 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 3 }$ A $\mathbb { W } ^ { - 1 }$ [9] Then, our group reported 1,6-distriphenylamineethynylpyrene (1,6-DTEP) single crystal exhibiting a high mobility of up to $2 . 1 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \thinspace \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , a PLQY of $32 \%$ , and its single-crystal OPTs obtained a photosensitivity of $1 . 6 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ , a high photoresponsivity of $2 . 8 6 \times 1 0 ^ { 6 }$ A $\mathbb { W } ^ { - 1 }$ , and an ultrahigh detectivity reached $1 . 4 9 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ Jones upon an illumination of $3 7 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ light.[0] Although a few multifunctional organic semiconductors with good carrier transport and effective fluorescence emission have been used for high-performance OPTs, the design and synthesis of such materials are still a great challenge, because there is a contradiction between high mobility and strong fuorescence emission. The former requires strong intermolecular interaction, while the latter requires weak or even no intermolecular interaction.[11 To the best of our knowledge, organic semiconductors with photoluminescence quantum yields close to $100 \%$ have very low or even no mobility. Therefore, it is necessary to develop new multifunctional organic semiconductor materials with strong photoluminescence and high mobility for organic optoelectronics.

Because anthracene has a high PLQy, high-speed radiance, well-resolved absorption spectra, and excellent charge transportation, and triphenylamine and 1,3-diphenylbenzene are widely used as fuorescent groups, which can improve the luminescent properties of molecules, while triple bond can effectively expand the $\pi$ conjugate length of molecule and improve the mobility of the molecule,[12] herein, we synthesized two compounds by linking triphenylamine and 1,3-diphenylbenzene groups through a triple bond at 9,10 positions of anthracene, 9,10-bis([N, N-diphenyl] $4 ^ { \prime }$ -phenylethynyl)anthracene (TPA-An) and 9,10-bis $[ [ 1 ^ { \prime } , 3 ^ { \prime }$ -diphenyl]- $. 5 ^ { \prime }$ -phenylethynyl)anthracene (TBAAn), and obtained their single crystals via the solvent-evaporation method. The two ribbon crystals have high PLQYs of $9 8 \%$ and $9 9 \%$ at room temperature, high mobilities up to 0.45 and $0 . 1 5 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ respectively. Furthermore, the ultrasensitive UV phototransistors based on the two crystals have photosensitivities up to $1 . 0 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 3 }$ and $3 . 4 5 \times 1 0 ^ { 4 }$ ultrahigh photoresponsivities up to $7 . 1 9 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ and $1 . 5 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ A $\mathbb { W } ^ { - 1 }$ , and ultrahigh detectivities $( D ^ { * } )$ up to $1 . 4 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 6 }$ and $1 . 6 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 7 }$ Jones, respectively.

# 2. Results and Discussion

Compounds TPA-An and TBA-An (Figure 1a) were successfully synthesized via three-step high-efficiency and simple reactions (Scheme S1, Supporting Information), and their total yields exceeded $8 5 \%$ which are soluble in most common solvents. TPA-An and TBA-An show good stability sublimating at 489 and $5 2 2 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ from thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) data (corresponding to $5 \%$ weight loss; Figure S1, Supporting Information).

The two crystals of TPA-An and TBA-An are prepared by the slow solvent vapor-diffusion method in the ambient atmosphere. It can be seen from the fluorescence microscopic photographs (Figure 2a,c) that TPA-An and TBA-An crystals exhibited a regular ribbon shape with lengths of over $1 \mathrm { m m }$ . The TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals indicated that there are two dihedral angles between triple bond linked benzene rings and anthracene ring in two molecules (Figure 2b,d). The single crystal of TPA-An forms dihedral angles of $4 4 . 0 6 ^ { \circ }$ and $4 4 . 0 6 ^ { \circ }$ , and the single crystal of TBA-An forms dihedral angles of $6 . 7 9 ^ { \circ }$ and $3 . 2 5 ^ { \circ }$ , respectively. Figure 2e,f shows that TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals have compact and ordered stacking structure, and the crystal data of TPA-An and TBA-An are summarized in Table S1 (Supporting Information). In Figure S3 (Supporting Information), short interatomic $\mathrm { C } \cdot \cdot \cdot \mathrm { H } ^ { \prime }$ (2.833-3.216 A) and intermolecular $\mathrm { C - H } \cdot \cdot \cdot \pi$ (2.868-2.949 A) interactions are observed for TPA-An single crystal, and a few intermolecular $\mathrm { C - H } \cdot \cdot \cdot \pi$ (2.823-2.826 A) interactions as well as a few $\pi - \pi$ overlapping (3.373-3.376 A) interactions are observed for TBA-An single crystal. Although the dihedral angles of TBA-An molecule are smaller than that of TPA-An molecule, there are more intermolecular interactions in TPA-An single crystals than that in TBA-An single crystals, which are more favorable for charge transport in TPA-An single crystals.

The optical and electrochemical properties of TPA-An and TBA-An were carried out by UV-vis absorption, fluorescence spectra, and cyclic voltammetry (CV), as summarized in Tables S2 and S3 (Supporting Information). The UV-vis absorption of the solution-state, film-state, and crystal-state samples of TPA-An and TBA-An reveal strong absorption in UV region (Figure 1). The PLQYs of TPA-An and TBA-An solutions are $7 7 \%$ and $80 \%$ , and those of TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals are $9 8 \%$ and $9 9 \%$ measured by the integrating sphere at room temperature. The reason for higher PLQYs of TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals is that the intermolecular interactions in the single crystals restricted the vibration and rotation of benzene rings of TPA-An and TBA-An molecules, resulting in the enhancement of the fluorescence emission of their single crystals.9l The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOmO) levels for TPA-An and TBA-An were $- 5 . 3 2$ and $- 5 . 6 0 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively, as estimated from CV curve with an reference electrode $( \mathsf { A g } /$ AgCl electrode) in $\mathrm { C H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { C l } _ { 2 }$ solution (Figure S2, Supporting Information). The respective optical bandgaps for TPA-An and TBA-An calculated by the onset absorption in their films were 2.22 and $2 . 1 9 \ \mathrm { { \ e V } , }$ respectively. The lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) levels for TPA-An and TBA-An calculated from their HOMO levels, and optical bandgaps were $- 3 . 1 0$ and $- 3 . 4 1 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively. Figure S4 (Supporting Information) shows density functional theory (DFT) calculation results of the electron cloud distribution on TPA-An and TBA-An HOMO and LUMO energy levels, respectively. The electron cloud distribution of TPA-An HOMO energy level is more delocalized than that of TBA-An, indicating that TPA-An has better charge carrier mobility, and explaining that TPA-An has higher HOMO level.

Organic single crystals have ordered molecular packing, no grain boundary, and a few charge traps, which usually show inherent charge-transport characteristics and excellent performance. We fabricated single-crystal bottom-gate top-contact (BGTC) single-crystal OFETs (SC-OFETs) using TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals as active channels, and a $3 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ thick layer of thermally grown $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ as the gate dielectric. We then, respectively, deposited a $\mathrm { C H C l } _ { 3 } / n$ -hexane solution of TPA-An and a $\mathrm { C H C l _ { 3 } } / \mathrm { C H _ { 3 } O H }$ solution of TBA-An on octadecyltrichlorosilane (OTs)-modified silicon wafers through drop-casting, and successfully prepared Au source-drain electrodes by the "Au stripe mask" method.13 Optical microscopy (OM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) (Figure 3a,d) showed that the single crystals of TPA-An and TBA-An on OTS-modified Si substrates were ribbon, whose morphologies were keeping in with TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals prepared by solvent evaporation. Figure S5 (Supporting Information) shows that the X-ray diffraction (XRD) peak intensity of TPA-An single crystals on substrates was higher than that of TBA-An single crystals on substrates, demonstrating the higher quality of TPA-An single crystals than TBA-An single crystals.The diffraction peaks corresponding to $( 0 0 h )$ facet reflections for TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals on substrates were observed, in accord with those for TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals prepared by solvent evaporation. The interlayer distances in TPA-An and TBA-An crystals on substrates calculated from their (001) peaks were 14.38 and 17.44 A, respectively which were very close to those of TPA-An crystal (14.96 A) and TBA-An crystal (17.37 A) prepared by solvent evaporation. Furthermore, AFM images (Figure S6, Supporting Information) showed that ribbon crystals of TPA-An and TBA-An on substrates had different thicknesses (about $3 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ for TPA-An and about $1 7 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ for TBA-An) and smooth surfaces for two ribbon crystals on substrates with the rootmean-square (RMS) values of about $1 . 1 6 9 \ \mathrm { n m }$ (TPA-An) and $2 . 3 8 5 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ (TBA-An), which demonstrated that the thickness of the TPA-An crystal on substrates is thinner than that of TBAAn, and TPA-An on substrates forms higher-quality crystals than TBA-An. Figure 3 shows the transfer and output curves of TPA-An and TBA-An SC-OFETs. SC-OFETs based on TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals exhibited the highest mobilities up to 0.45 and $0 . 1 5 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \thinspace \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , and high on/off ratio up to $1 0 ^ { 6 }$ and $1 0 ^ { 5 }$ , respectively. To the best of our knowledge, the mobility of TPA-An single crystal is the highest mobility of organic fluorescent molecules with photoluminescence quantum yields close to $100 \%$ The mobilities of the reported organic semiconductors with photoluminescence quantum yields close to $100 \%$ are summarized in Table 1. These results indicated that the main reasons for the higher mobility of TPA-An single crystals than that of TPA-An single crystals are the higher quality and thinner crystals, longer conjugation length, and more intermolecular interactions for TPA-An single crystals.

The light-response characteristics of TPA-An and TBA-An SC-OFETs were further studied. Figure 4a illustrates the schematic diagram of TPA-An and TBA-An single-crystal phototransistors. To begin with, we probed the influence of the illumination intensity on the phototransistor performance for TPA-An by using several white lights with different irradiation intensities of $0 . 0 3 4 5 { - } 0 . 6 3 2 0 \mathrm { m W } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , where the source-- drain currents increased and the threshold voltage decreased with the increase in light intensity (Figures S7 and S8, Supporting Information). It can be understood that phototransistors under light irradiation will produce photogenerated charge carriers that decrease Schottky barriers and high density traps, resulting in the overall current enhancement. In addition, we further evaluate the performance of TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals as phototransistor by using the same intensities of 370, 400, 450, 500, 550, and $6 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . As the monochromatic wavelength decreased, the source-drain current increased and the threshold voltage obviously shifted to positive voltage for TPA-An and TBA-An phototransistors (Figure 4), which indicated that the short-wavelength light irradiation with higher energy is conducive to generate more photogenerated carriers and effectively reduce the trap density. Three important parameters to evaluate the performance of phototransistors are photosensitivity $( P )$ photoresponsivity $( R )$ , and detectivity $( D ^ { * } )$ , which can be calculated by Equations (S2), Equation (S3), and Equation (S4) (Supporting Information), respectively.[6a] Under $0 . 0 0 0 2 \mathrm { m W } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ $3 7 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ wavelength illumination, the similar ultrasensitive UV phototransistors for TPA-An and TBA-An achieved ultrahigh photosensitivities $1 . 0 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 3 }$ and $3 . 4 5 \times 1 0 ^ { 4 }$ ultrahigh photoresponsivities reached $7 . 1 9 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ and $1 . 5 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ A $\mathbb { W } ^ { - 1 }$ .. and ultrahigh detectivities reached $1 . 4 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 6 }$ and $1 . 6 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 7 }$ Jones, respectively, which are superior to the most reported UV OPTs. Moreover, the switching behavior of TPA-An and TBA-An single-crystal phototransistors in five cycles under ambient conditions showed good operating stability (Figure 4e,g; Figures S7d and S8d, Supporting Information, in the dark under $0 . 0 0 0 2 \mathrm { ~ m W ~ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ with $3 7 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ wavelength illumination or in the dark $ 0 . 6 3 2 0 \mathrm { m W } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ white light illumination). The effect of $V _ { \mathrm { G S } }$ modulation on R, P, and $D ^ { * }$ for TPA-An and TBA-An device performance are shown together in Figures S9-S12, S14, and S15 (Supporting Information), respectively.

In order to better explain the ultrahigh sensitivity of TPA-An and TBA-An single-crystal phototransistors to $3 7 0 \mathrm { n m }$ UV light, we measured the device noise of TPA-An and TBA-An singlecrystal phototransistors, and collected temperature-dependent PL spectra (98-298 K) of TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals (Figure S16a,b, Supporting Information). In Figure S16a,b (Supporting Information), we can clearly observe that the fluorescence emission intensities of TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals decrease with the increase of temperature, due to the temperature-activated exciton dissociation,[17] which is conducive to the generation of photogenerated carriers at room temperature. From the temperature-dependent fuorescence spectra of TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals, the maximum fuorescence intensity corresponding to each temperature was extracted, and the temperature-dependent free-exciton emission intensity of TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals was plotted (Figure S16c,d, Supporting Information). From fitting

Equation (S5) (Supporting Information), the lower binding energies, $E _ { \mathrm { B } }$ , of $5 3 . 5 7 \mathrm { m e V }$ (TPA-An) and $8 3 . 2 9 \ \mathrm { m e V }$ (TBA-An) were obtained.[10] Furthermore, TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals with photoluminescence quantum yields close to $100 \%$ reflected the high-quality single crystals grown by the solution method, which may effectively reduce the defect density of their phototransistors. At the same time, the high PLQYs of TPA-An and TBA-An single crystals may indicate the effective suppression of nonradiative recombination.18] As shown in Figure S17 (Supporting Information), TPA-An and TBA-An single-crystal phototransistors have low noise. According to Equation (S6) (Supporting Information), the specific detectivity of TPA-An and TBA-An single-crystal phototransistors is still higher than $1 0 ^ { 1 6 }$ under different frequency.10] We believe that the remarkable UV phototransistors' performance of TPA-An and TBA-An should be attributed to strong UV absorption, high mobility, small exciton binding energies, effective temperature-activated exciton dissociation, long exciton diffusion length, as well as low device noise.

# 3. Conclusion

We have developed two molecules with both efficient solidstate emissive and excellent charge-transporting properties by introducing different substituents at the 9,10 positions of anthracene, TPA-An and TBA-An, and applied them to SCOFETs and phototransistors. The ribbon crystals of TPA-An and TBA-An exhibit the PLQYs of $9 8 \%$ and $9 9 \%$ at room temperature and remarkable hole mobilities of 0.45 and $0 . 1 5 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ in SC-OFETs, respectively. Furthermore, the two crystals have been applied to UV phototransistors, and photosensitivities of $1 . 0 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 3 }$ and $3 . 4 5 \times 1 0 ^ { 4 }$ ultrahigh photoresponsivities of $7 . 1 9 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ and $1 . 5 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ A $\mathbb { W } ^ { - 1 }$ , and detectivities above $1 . 4 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 6 }$ and $1 . 6 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 7 }$ Jones were obtained. It demonstrated the potential applications of 9,10-position-substituted anthracene compounds as high-performance multifunctional photoelectric materials.
