# 1. Introduction

Organic solar cells (OsCs), as a next-generation photovoltaic technology, have gained much interest due to their impressive advantages, e.g., fast printing, low cost, visible light transparency, and mechanical flexibility.[1-5] Fullerenes and their derivatives and charge transfer process need ideal donor (D)/acceptor (A) interpenetrating network that forms independent hole and electron transport pathways. The bulk heterojunction (BHJ) structure meets the aforementioned requirement.24-26] Furthermore, OSCs generally have two architectures, namely conventional and inverted structures. The material surface energy and posttreatments affect the vertical phase separation of the active layer, and the different vertical phase distribution fits different device architecture.27-29] In FREA-based BHJ devices, inverted structure (Figure 1a) generally yields higher efficiency than the conventional one. Because polymer donors generally exhibit lower surface energy compared with FREA acceptors,30-34] polymer donors and FREA acceptors tend to enrich at the top and bottom surfaces of devices, respectively. However, this kind of inverted BHJ structure usually suffers from the following two problems: 1) Donors with low surface energy tend to excessively enrich in the top part,35,36] and the D/A interfacial area is largely reduced, which is unfavorable for exciton dissociation. Moreover, the excessive vertical phase separation leads to severe energetic disorder of electronic localized states, which may cause open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ loss.37l 2) Post-treatments such as thermal annealing, solvent annealing, and solvent additives are usually used to optimize the BHJ morphology. The extra post-treatments are unfavorable for device stability and reproducibility as well as large-scale fabrication.[38-41]

In this work, we reported the sequentially processed heterojunction (SHJ) with an unusual inverted structure (Figure 1b), where a polymer donor is spin-coated on the cathode as the bottom layer and a nonfullerene acceptor is spin-coated as the top layer to decrease the donor content and avoid excessive accumulation in the top part, and to balance the overall vertical phase separation. Most reported layer-by-layer (LBL) processed OSCs have a conventional structure (Figure 1c), where the bottom donor layer is spin-coated on the anode, whereas the top acceptor layer is next to the cathode.42-46] In typical LBL-processed OSCs with inverted structure (Figure 1d), the bottom acceptor layer is spin-coated on the cathode, whereas the top donor layer is next to the anode. This unusual inverted SHJ structure is different from the traditional BHJ and LBL structures. We designed and synthesized a new FREA named FOIC1 (Figure 2a), which exhibits good solubility in 2-methyltetrahydrofuran (2-MeTHF). The polymer donor PTB7 $\mathrm { \cdot } \mathrm { T h } ^ { [ 4 7 ] }$ (Figure 2a) has poor solubility in 2-MeTHF but relatively good solubility in $o$ -dichlorobenzene (o-DCB). The cosolvent of 2-MeTHF with $5 \mathrm { v o l \% }$ $o$ -DCB can dissolve the small-molecule acceptor FOiC1 (top layer) but cannot dissolve the polymer donor PTB7-Th (bottom layer). Because the top cosolvent can swell the bottom polymer network,[48] FO1C1 can penetrate into the swollen network, leading to FOIC1 /PTB7-Th intermixing across whole active layer without sharp donor/acceptor interface. Without any post-treatments, an optimal vertical composition distribution is formed in this unusual SHJ device, contributing to simultaneously improved $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } ,$ short-circuit current density $( J { \mathrm { s c } } )$ , fill factor (FF), and power conversion efficiency (PCE), relative to the traditional BHJ device.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Material Synthesis and Characterization

Our previously reported FOIC demonstrates a high electron mobility, intense absorption in visible-NIR region, and high photovoltaic performance.49l However, its solubility in 2-MeTHF is poor, and it is hard to find suitable orthogonal solvents to sequentially process PTB7-Th donor layer and FOIC acceptor layer. We introduced F atoms onto the side phenyl groups in FOIC and synthesized FOIC1 (Figure 2b). The solubility of FOIC1 in 2-MeTHF is improved due to enhanced solute-solvent interaction via F...O and F...H interactions. The improved solubility of FOIC1 in 2-MeTHF is beneficial to the sequential process.

FOIC1 in diluted chloroform solution shows an intense absorption ranging from 600 to $8 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ with a maximum molar extinction coefficient of $1 . 9 5 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ at $7 6 0 \mathrm { n m } ;$ the absorption peak in solid film exhibits a red shift of $5 8 \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels of FOIC1 were calculated to be $- 5 . 3 9$ and $- 3 . 9 9 \mathrm { e V }$ , respectively, in accordance to the cyclic voltammetry measurement (Figure S2, Supporting Information). The electron mobility $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ of FOIC1 is $1 { \dot { . } } { \dot { 8 } } \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ as measured by space charge limited current (SCLC) method (Figure S3, Supporting Information). The high $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of FOIC1 is related to its 3D conjugated framework.50] In the crystal structure of FOIC1, two types of $\pi - \pi$ stacking modes form the 3D conjugated framework, whereas the alkyl chains fulfill the free $\pi \cdot$ electron voids (Figure 3, Table S1, Supporting Information). As for the parallel $\pi - \pi$ stacking, the F and H atoms in the side and terminal groups form the H-bonding connection, leading to the enhanced overlap of intermolecular D-A fragments. Two terminal groups form $\pi - \pi$ stacking with a distance of $3 . 5 6 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ . As for the zig-zag $\pi - \pi$ stacking, the F-H interaction of side groups promotes the dense entanglement of alkyl chains, indicating the increased packing index of conjugated framework.

# 2.2. Device Fabrication and Performance

PTB7-Th was chosen as the electron donor for the following reasons: 1) PTB7-Th and FOIC1 have orthogonal solubility. The 2-MeTHF dissolves FOIC1 but hardly for the PTB7-Th (Figure S4, Supporting Information). 2) PTB7-Th and FOIC1 have complementary absorption spectra to achieve wide and intense light absorption (Figure 4a). 3) PTB7-Th and FOIC1 have matched energy levels (Figure 4b) and comparable charge mobilities favorable to the exciton dissociation and charge transport.

The steady photoluminescence (PL) measurement was adopted to study the exciton dissociation between the donor PTB7-Th and the acceptor FOIC1 (Figure S5a, Supporting

Information). Pure PTB7-Th film exhibits a broad emission in the range of $7 0 0 { - } 8 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ under an excitation at $5 4 4 \mathrm { n m }$ . For BHJ and SHJ films, the PL of PTB7-Th is efficiently quenched $( > 9 9 \% )$ , indicating an efficient exciton dissociation. From timeresolved PL (TRPL) measurements (Figure S5b, Supporting Information), the average PL lifetime $( \tau )$ of pure PTB7-Th, BHJ, and SHJ films are 652, 555, and 529ps, respectively. The $\tau$ of blend films is shorter than that of pure film; the $\tau$ of SHJ is slightly shorter than that of BHJ, suggesting a more efficient exciton dissociation in SHJ.

# 2.3. Morphology and Vertical Phase Separation

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) was used to image the surface morphology of the films (Figure S7, Supporting Information). Root-mean-square surface roughness $( R _ { \mathrm { q } } )$ of pure PTB7-Th and FOIC1 are 0.984 and $0 . 5 7 6 \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. For pure PTB7-Th film, after washing with pure 2-MeTHF or cosolvent of 2-MeTHF with $5 \mathrm { v o l \% }$ $o$ -DCB, $R _ { \mathfrak { q } }$ increases to 1.52 and $1 . 5 4 \mathrm { n m }$ respectively. The polymer donor is hardly dissolved by solvents used to dissolve the acceptor, which is consistent with the absorption data (Figure S4, Supporting Information). The $R _ { \mathrm { q } }$ of BHJ and SHJ films are 1.53 and $1 . 4 3 \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively.

Grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) and grazing-incidence small-angle X-ray scattering (GISAXs) characterizations were conducted to understand the morphological difference between BHJ and SHJ films.57l Both BHJ and SHJ films show preferential face-on orientation with the $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak locating in the out-of-plane direction at $q \approx 1 . 7 0 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ $( d = 3 . 7 0 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } )$ , and have similar crystallinity suggested by the similar overall scattering intensity (Figure 6a,b and Figure S8, Supporting Information). From the in-plane GISAXS profiles of the BHJ and SHJ film (Figure 6c,d), we can estimate the domain sizes of different phases.5s] The intermixing region $( \xi )$ and pure acceptor $( 2 R _ { \mathrm { g } } )$ domains (in the in-plane direction) in the BHJ are 40.4 and $2 7 . 5 \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively, whereas those of SHJ are 20.2 and $1 9 . 1 \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. The relatively smaller intermixing region for SHJ is beneficial to the charge collection from the excitons generated in intermixing region, in line with the observed higher $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF in devices.

The film-depth-dependent light absorption spectroscopy was measured using our previously reported method.35,59] The vertical segregation in BHJ and SHJ films is different as a result of different phase evolutions during film deposition. 1) The absorption peaks around $7 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ generally correspond to O-O transition of PTB7-Th. The vertical variation of 0-0 transition reflects the vertical variation of charge transport levels (HOMO/LUMO levels), which induces the film-depth-dependent energetic disorder of electronic localized states. Consequently, such variations of charge transport levels along charge transport direction (film-depth direction) could form local traps,[36,37] deteriorating charge transport, increasing charge recombination, and lowering photovoltaic performance. The BHJ film is featured with a more pronounced film-depth-dependent variation of PTB7-Th absorption peaks (Figure 7a,b) and thus an inferior photovoltaic performance compared with the SHJ film. 2) In the BHJ film, an over segregation of PTB7-Th with ${ > } 7 0 \%$ content at the top and ${ > } 6 0 \%$ at the bottom is unfavorable for the exciton dissociation and charge transport (Figure 7c,d). The SHJ film is featured with a well-distributed interpenetrated structure, which could benefit the exciton dissociation and charge transport, as well as reduce the charge recombination. 3) The exciton generation contours were calculated via a modified transfer-matrix method,[60] with input from the film-depth-dependent optical variations. Due to the light interference within the device, the excitons are located mainly at the bottom and middle parts of the film (Figure 7e,f). The acceptor exhibits a higher exciton generation rate than the donor, so a higher acceptor content at the bottom half of the SHJ facilitates the generation of excitons. This is consistent with the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra (Figure 5b), where in the range of $7 0 0 { - } 8 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ the EQE of the SHJ device is higher than that of the BHJ device. Moreover, because charge generation mainly occurs at the bottom part of the active layer, the holes need to transport across more than $5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ to reach the anode. Therefore, uniform charge transport levels of SHJ film along film-depth direction would provide a flat and smooth pathway, definitely contributing to the efficient charge transport of holes toward the anode.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we designed and synthesized a new nonfullerene acceptor, FOIC1, and fabricated OSCs with an unusual inverted structure, namely, the SHJ. By spin coating the donor PTB7-Th as the bottom layer and the acceptor FOiC1 as the top layer, a well-controlled vertical phase distribution is formed. A relatively lower content of PTB7-Th is located at the top surface and the rich FOIC1 aggregates at the bottom interface which is different from the BHJ film with higher contents of PTB7-Th at the top and bottom surfaces. The SHJ devices exhibit a more efficient exciton generation and dissociation as well as a more balanced charge transport compared with the BHJ counterpart. The SHJ cells yield a PCE of $1 2 . 0 \%$ , which surpasses the traditional

BHJ cells $( 1 1 . 0 \% )$ . This indicates that the unusual SHJ has great potential in realizing high-performance OSCs.
