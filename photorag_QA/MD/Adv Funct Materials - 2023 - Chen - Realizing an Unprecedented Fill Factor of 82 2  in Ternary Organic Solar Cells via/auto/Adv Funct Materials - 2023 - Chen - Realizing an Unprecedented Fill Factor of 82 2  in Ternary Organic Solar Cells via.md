# Realizing an Unprecedented Fill Factor of 82.2% in Ternary Organic Solar Cells via Co-Crystallization of Non-Fullerene Acceptors

Chen Chen, Liang Wang, Yuandong Sun, Yiwei Fu, Chuanhang Guo, Bojun Zhou, Zirui Gan, Dan Liu, Wei Li, and Tao Wang\*

Ternary strategy is demonstrated as an efficient approach to achieve high short-circuit current and open-circuit voltage to boost the performance of organic solar cells (OsCs), however, the realization of high fill-factor (FF) in ternary OsCs has been rare. In this study, three thiophene terminated non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs) with methyl or chlorine substitutions on their end-groups are designed and synthesized, and further incorporated into the state-of-the-art PM6:L8-BO system to construct ternary OSCs. Subtle changes in their chemical structures significantly modify the molecular packings of these thiophene terminated NFAs. While BTP-ThMe and BTP-ThCI have limited forms of dimer, versatile molecular dimers, including "Z" shaped D-D, "S" shaped A-A, and "F" shaped A-D packings exist in BTP-ThMeCl, which lead to the formation of compact 3D honey-comb network and this is analogous to the host acceptor L8-BO. This synergetic molecular packing between BTP-ThMeCl and L8-BO contributes to maintain the 3D charge transport network in the ternary system via the formation of NFA co-crystals at the molecular level, and consequently realizing a maximum power conversion efficiency of $1 9 . 1 \%$ with a superior FF of $8 2 . 2 \%$ , which is the highest FF reported so far for OSCs.

# 1. Introduction

During the past decades, organic solar cells (OsCs) have been attracting widespread attention as a promising photovoltaic technology due to their advantages including lightweight, flexibility, and solution-processability.[1-5] In the last couple of years, with the extensive endeavors including new material design (especially non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs)), morphology optimization, and interface engineering, the photovoltaic performance of OsCs has witnessed a rapid development, with power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) increased from $10 \%$ to over $1 9 \%$ [6-11] However, although encouraging progresses have been made to improve charge generation and suppress energy losses to boost the short-circuit current density $( J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } } )$ and open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ in OsCs, enhancement of the fill factor (FF) is still slow.[12-15,21,23] Currently, OSCs with FFs over $8 1 \%$ are rarely reported, lagging far behind other photovoltaics such as perovskite solar cells of which the FF of $86 \%$ has been reported.[16,17]

The FF of OsCs manifests the photon current density evolution of OsCs at different applied bias and can be recognized as the product of competition between charge extraction and recombination.[18] Historical experiences correlate the undesignable FFs in OSCs to non-geminated recombination at the interfaces of OsCs, as well as to both geminated and non-geminated recombinations in the active layer due to the disordered nature of organic semiconductors.[11-14] Regards that, it has been widely accepted that constructing favorable molecular packing to enable an efficient charge transport network is key to further enhance FFs to approach the Shockley-Queisser limit.[2430] As such, various molecular design strategies have been devoted to optimize the molecular packing of NFAs. For example, Wang et al. have modified the alkyl chain length of NFA (C5-16) and thus allowed stronger intermolecular stacking to improve FF,[30] while Sun and co-authors designed an NFA (L8-BO) with branched side chains which enabled the FF over $80 \%$ due to its compact 3D molecular packing network and fibrillar morphology in the photoactive layer.[10]

Incorporating a third component into the host binary blend to construct ternary solar cells has also been demonstrated to be an efficient strategy to further elevate the photovoltaic performance, mainly through the selection of components with appropriate absorption and energy levels to enhance light harvesting and suppress energy loss for improved $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ [19,31-35] but also via morphological optimization to promote charge transport for improved FF.[36] For example, by introducing a highly crystalline polymer D18 into PM6:L8-BO solar cells to realize double-fibril aggregates (D18 and PM6 fibrils), a superior PCE of $1 9 . 6 \%$ with the FF of $8 1 . 9 \%$ was achieved.1i However, due to the complex chemical and morphological structures in ternary systems, reports on promoting charge transport and FF via aggregation tuning have been rare, and the component selection and aggregation optimization principle still follows the trial-and-error protocols.

In this work, we designed and synthesized a series of thiophene terminated NFAs based upon thiophene based end-groups with varied substitutions, namely BTP-ThMe, BTP-ThCl, and BTP-ThMeCl. The thiophene terminated NFAs have been reported to possess high crystallinity and small non-radiative energy losses, benefiting from the stronger intermolecular interactions induced by sulphur-sulphur interaction, which makes them suitable third component for ternary OSCs.[37-39] PM6:L8- BO was selected to be the host system due to its remarkable photovoltaic performance in both binary and ternary single junction organic solar cell.[20,25,40 42] As a result, with the monosubstitution of methyl and chlorine, BTP-ThMe and BTP-ThCl only form limited dimers which deliver 2D or loose 3D charge transport networks. While the methyl and chlorine co-substituted BTP-ThMeCl demonstrates a compact 3D honey-comb charge transport network, analogous to the host acceptor L8-BO. This synergetic molecular packing between BTP-ThMeCl and L8-BO contributes to maintain the 3D charge transport network in the ternary system via the formation of NFA co-crystals at the molecular level, and finally translates to a champion PCE of $1 9 . 1 \%$ along with a superior FF of $8 2 . 2 \%$ , which is the highest FF reported to date for OSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The chemical structures and synthetic routes of BTP-ThMe, BTP-ThCl, and BTP-ThMeCl are shown in Figure 1a and Figure S1 (Supporting Information). These thiophene terminated NFAs share the same fused backbone and alkyl side-chains but different electron-withdrawing end-groups based upon 2-(6-oxo5,6-dihydro-4H-cyclopenta[c|thiophen-4-ylidene)malononitrile (CPTCN), in which BTP-ThMe and BTP-ThCl possess an electron-donating methyl and an electron-withdrawing chlorine at the $\alpha$ -position of CPTCN, while BTP-ThMeCl has both methyl and chlorine substituents on CPTCN. During their synthesis, Geward reactions were first applied to obtain multifunctional thiophene rings, and then the amino group at the $\alpha$ position was reduced to afford chlorine or hydrogen substituent, while the methylation was realized by changing the starting materials.1237,43] Finally, all NFAs were obtained via Knoevenagel condensation from the received end-groups and fused-core. The intermediate and target compounds were characterized by the NMR spectrometry (Supporting Information). All these NFAs have good solubility in chlorobenzene, dichlorobenzene, and chloroform.

Density functional theory (DFT) was first conducted to examine their molecular configurations. As shown in Figure S3 (Supporting Information), with the mono-substitution of methyl or chlorine, BTP-ThMe, and BTP-ThCl exhibit extradentary small dihedral angles of $3 . 7 ^ { \mathrm { o } } / 5 . 6 ^ { \mathrm { o } }$ and $3 . 9 ^ { \mathrm { o } } / 3 . 5 ^ { \mathrm { o } }$ respectively, and these values increased to $5 . 2 ^ { \mathrm { o } } / 9 . 2 ^ { \mathrm { o } }$ in the dual-substituted BTP-ThMeCl due to the increased steric hindrance between the substituents and cyano-group. Nevertheless, all these NFAs possess good planarity which could allow ordered molecular packing in their films and the larger dihedral angles of BTP-ThMeCl might also be helpful for molecular packings in three dimensions. The electrochemical properties and ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption spectra of those NFAs were characterized and shown in Figure 1b,c, Figure S2 (Supporting Information), and Table 1. As expected, due to the weaker intramolecular charge-transfer effect induced by the substitution of the electron-donating methyl, BTP-ThMe received the widest optical bandgap of $1 . 4 3 \mathrm { e V }$ and relatively elevated LUMO/HOMO values of $- 3 . 6 5 / - 5 . 5 2 \ : \mathrm { e V }$ When chlorine was introduced into the CPTcN group either with or without the presence of methyl, all films show a narrowed bandgap of ${ \approx } 1 . 3 9 \ \mathrm { { \ e V } } _ { \mathrm { { } i } }$ with almost the same LUMO/HOMO values of around $- 3 . 8 0 / - 5 . 7 0 ~ \mathrm { e V } ,$ suggesting that the chlorine substitution plays a determining role in their bandgap and energy levels. Besides, compared to BTP-ThMe and BTP-ThCl, BTP-ThMeCl presents stronger red-shift in its absorption spectra when changing from solution to solid states, implying BTPThMeCl might possess more compact aggregates in the film. The chemical structures and energy levels of L8-BO are also shown in Figure 1a,b. It is clear that all NFAs show a cascade energetic structure within the host blend, which might be beneficial for facilitating charge transport in the ternary blends.

To investigate the aggregation behaviors of these NFAs, their molecular dynamics simulations were first conducted and shown in Figure 2. For the methyl monosubstituted BTP-ThMe, it only shows an "F" shaped A-D packing with a short $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking distance of $3 . 5 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ as the $^ { \alpha } { \bf S } ^ { n }$ shaped "A-A" like packing has a large distance of $6 . 6 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } }$ , translating to an inefficient 2D charge transport network. The chlorine monosubstituted BTP-ThCl allows the formation of two kinds of $\pi \cdot \pi$ stacking dimers, including a $^ { \omega } Z ^ { n }$ shaped D-D packing with a distance of $\bar { 3 . 7 \mathrm { \AA } }$ and a $^ { \ d } \mathrm { S } ^ { \dag }$ shaped A-A packing with a distance of $3 . 6 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ This molecular configuration feature also enables a well-defined but loose 3D honey-comb network with a relatively low packing coefficient of $1 . 9 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 }$ $\mathring { \mathsf { A } } ^ { - 3 }$ only (Table S1, Supporting Information). In contrast, when chlorine and methyl were simultaneously substituted, the $^ { \prime \prime } Z ^ { \prime \prime }$ shaped D-D, "S" shaped A-A, and "F" shaped A-D packings are all observed in BTP-ThMeCl, and further contribute to a compact 3D honey-comb transport network with a larger packing coefficient of $3 . \dot { 2 } 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 3 }$ as shown in Figure 2c. Their different packing configurations can be well explained by their different electrostatic potential distributions shown in Figure S3 (Supporting information), as the positive electrostatic methyl tends to adsorb on the negative electrostatic benzothiadiazole core and enables the formation of A-D packing, while the negative electrostatic chlorine can polarize the end-groups and rebuilt strong $\pi \cdot \pi$ interaction to form both A-A and D-D molecular packings. More importantly, the molecular packing configuration of BTP-ThMeCl is highly analogous to that of the host acceptor L8-BO (Figure 2d), in which the compact 3D honey-comb network has been previously revealed to enhance the charge transport abilit.[1011,1] The specific electron mobilities $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ of neat BTP-ThMe, BTP-ThCl, and BTP-ThMeCl films, measured by space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method, are $6 . 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ $8 . 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ $8 . 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 }$ $\mathbf { S } ^ { - 1 }$ . The gradually elevated charge mobilities of these NFA further confirmed the contributions of their different 3D packing networks for charge transport (Figure S4, Supporting Information and Table 1).

To investigate the origin of different FF gains of these ternary OSCs, the structural order features of these thiophene terminated NFAs and L8-BO neat films were performed utilizing grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXs). As shown in Figure 4a and Figure S7 (Supporting Information), all these NFAs show dominated face-on orientation with strong $\pi$ $\pi$ stacking diffraction signals in the out-of-plane (OOP) direction and alkyl chain lamellar diffraction in the in-plane (IP) direction, in their neat and PM6 based blend films. It can be found that the $\pi \cdot \pi$ stacking peak of these thiophene terminated NFAs is sig. nificantly sharper than that of L8-BO, responding to coherence lengths of $2 0 . { \bar { 2 } } { \bar { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } }$ for BTP-ThMe, $2 0 . 2 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ for BTP-ThCl, 21.7 A for BTP-ThMeCl, and $1 6 . 6 \mathring \mathrm { ~ A ~ }$ for L8-BO, which proves that the stronger interaction between NFAs could enhance their structural order. L8-BO exhibits distinct (021) and (11-1) diffraction peaks at $q = 0 . 5 0 \ \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ and $0 . 4 5 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ in its neat and PM6 based blend film, which has been previously revealed to be the characteristic diffraction signal of L8-BO fibrils according to its single crystal structure.110,11] Interestingly, these (021) and (11-1) diffraction peaks are also observed in all these NFA neat films, especially for the BTP-ThMeCl film with pronounced (021) and (11-1) signal locatedng at $0 . 3 8 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ and $0 . 4 5 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively, suggesting all these NFAs might be able to involve in the molecular packing of L8-BO in the ternary blends,[8,44] especially BTP-ThMeCl. To validate that, GIWAXS was further performed for blend films. As depicted in Figure 4b, all ternary films show a decent lamellar diffraction peak in the IP direction and a strong $\pi \cdot \pi$ diffraction peak in the OOP direction, suggesting the introduction of third components could maintain the favorable face-on molecular orientation of the PM6:L8-BO host. Moreover, compared to BTP-ThMe and BTP-ThCl which only exhibit negligible impact on the (021) and (11-1) peaks of L8-BO in their ternary films, BTPThMeCl greatly elevates the diffraction intensity of the (021) and (11-1) peaks as shown in Figure 4b,f. According to the molecular packing discussion shown in Figure 2, we believe that the synergetic molecular packing of BTP-ThMeCl may trigger the cocrystallization of BTP-ThMeCl and L8-BO at the molecular level, and maintain the 3D network to facilitate charge transport.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, three thiophene terminated non-fullerene acceptors with methyl and chlorine mono- or dual- substitutions, named BTP-ThMe, BTP-ThCl, and BTP-ThMeCl were synthesized and introduced into a state-of-the-art PM6:L8-BO host system to fabricate ternary solar cells. Due to their different electrostatic potential distributions enabled by the end-group substitutions, those NFAs exhibit different molecular packings and charge transport networks. The coexistence of $\mathrm { F ^ { \prime \prime } }$ shaped A-D, "Z" shaped D-D, and $^ { \omega } { \bf S } ^ { \gamma }$ shaped A-A molecular packings in BTP-ThMeCl enables a compact 3D charge transport network, in line with the host acceptor L8-BO. This feature enables the formation of molecular co-crystals between BTP-ThMeCl and L8-BO, maintaining the 3D charge transport networks in the ternary blend and contributing to a maximum PCE over $1 9 \%$ with a superior FF of $8 2 . 2 \%$ which is the highest FF value reported to date. Our work provides a critical strategy to optimize the molecular packing among non-fullerene acceptors in ternary systems to reach high FF and efficiency.
