# Recent Developments of Polymer Solar Cells with Photovoltaic Performance over 17%

Jianghao Jin, Qiao Wang, Kaige Ma, Wenfei Shen,\* Laurence Alphonse Belfiore, Xichang Bao,\* and Jianguo Tang\*

With the emergence of ADA'DA-type (Y-series) non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs), the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of organic photovoltaic devices have been constantly refreshed and gradually reached $20 \%$ in recent years $1 9 \%$ for single junction and $20 \%$ for tandem device). The acceptors possess specific design concept, which greatly enrich the NFA types and have excellent compatibility with many donor materials. It is gratifying to note that the previously underperforming donor materials combine with these reguIated acceptors to shine again. Nowadays, the concept of modular design is widely used in the research of acceptors and donors, injecting new vitality into the field of organic photovoltaics. Furthermore, these acceptors also promote the research of multicomponent devices, tandem devices, bilayer devices, processing solvent engineering, and additive engineering. Herein, the latest progresses of polymer solar cells with efficiency over $1 7 \%$ are briefly reviewed from the aspects of active material design, interface material development, and device technology. At last, the opportunities and challenges of organic photovoltaic commercialization in the future are discussed.

# 1. Introduction

Due to the gradual depletion of fossil energy, the use of clean and renewable energy such as solar energy, wind energy, geo. thermal energy, and tidal energy more efficiently becomes a significantly important topic in the 21st century. Solar cell occupies a pivotal position in the exploration of new energy.[1] Compared with silicon, perovskite, and other inorganic photovoltaic materials, organic materials exhibit apparent advantages such as light weight and low cost in the solvent processing.[2-4] More importantly, organic semiconductors are more potential to realize flexibility to solar cells and roll-to-roll processability by solvent processing, which will greatly elevate production efficiency.[5-7] In addition, capture photons in specific bands can be obtained through rational materials design and device fabrication, which exhibits a broad application prospect in the fields of architecture and environmental engineering.[8]

Before the 1990s, it was the embryonic stage of organic photovoltaics, and the representative was the bilayer planar heterojunction devices fabricated in 1986.[9]

In 1995, Heeger et al.[10] developed a novel bulk heterojunction (BHJ) structure using MEH-PPV as donor and fullerene derivative (C60) as acceptor, which can achieve exciton separation and charge transport more efficiently. Since then, fullerene-based polymer solar cells (PsCs) have accomplished rapid developments and the device efficiency to more than $10 \%$ . At the same time, non-fullerene acceptors are developing silently, but all things come to those who wait. In 2015, Zhan et al.[11] reported a novel ADA-type fused-ring electron acceptors (FREAs), which authentically pushed the non-fullerene acceptors to new era. The new concept of modular design promotes the emergence of a large number of high-performance acceptors. Meanwhile, donor materials and interfacial materials matching FREAs have also been increasingly reported.[12,13]

In terms of device fabrication, broadening absorption spectra of materials will affects the open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ of binary devices, because the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ is decided by the difference between highest occupied molecular orbital (HOmO) energy level of donor and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy level of acceptor.[14] Therefore, the fabrication of multicomponent devices or tandem devices can effectively regulate the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the devices while broadening the absorption spectra. Furthermore, the third component can also adjust the blending morphology of donor and acceptor and/or increase the passway of energy transfer or charge transport.[15] In addition, the selection of suitable solvents and additives, and the improvement of the preparation of the active layer are beneficial to the further industrialization of organic photovoltaics.

In recent years, based on the continuous investigation of materials, device mechanisms, and device processing, especially the development of ADA'DA-type (Y-series) NFAs in 2019, the efficiency of PSCs has been greatly promoted to a new level, and the number of high-performance reports also demonstrates a rapid growth, as shown in Figure 1.[16-19] At present, the power convention efficiency (PCE) of single junction devices is $1 9 . 6 0 \%$ , and tandem device is $2 0 . 2 0 \%$ [20,21] In this review, we try to summarize the high-performance PSCs exceeding $1 7 \%$ in recent years from active materials, interface materials, and device engineering, including multi-component systems, tandem devices, solvent and additive engineering, and layer-by-layer processing.

# 2. High-Performance Photovoltaic and Interfacial Materials

In 1992, Heeger et al.[22] verified that excitons are separated efficiently at donor/acceptor interfaces and are not easy to recombination. And they utilized fullerene derivatives as the acceptors to fabricate PSCs.[23] Since then, fullerene acceptors (FAs) have been gradually investigated in the fabrication of organic photovoltaic devices. The device efficiencies of the fullerene system have been refreshed to about $12 \%$ by exploring the fullerene acceptors and their matching donors. However, FAs are not only poorly absorbed in the UV-vis region, but also demanded to adjust their energy levels. It was not until the 3D NFAs developed through the backbone twist strategy that the problem of molecular over-crystallization was effectively solved.[24] In 2015, Zhan et al.[11] reported an ADA-type FREA ITIC. The ADA-type acceptor with push-pull electronic structure possesses strong intramolecular charge transfer and broadens absorption spectrum than that of FAs. Furthermore, the miscibility between donor and acceptor, and phase-separation can be well regulated from the side chain. After that, FAs were gradually replaced by NFAs with the fused-ring structure. In 2019, Zou et al.[19] reported an ADA'DA-type FREA Y6, in which the related efficiencies of PsCs are increasingly promoted to the current level. Different from the previous ADA-type acceptors, Y6 replaces benzotriazole (BTz) with benzothiadiazole (BT) as electron-deficient central core and introduces alkyl chain to the $\beta$ position of dithiophene to balance solubility and crystallinity to with optimized the morphology of the blend film. Figure 2 briefly exhibits the development of Y6 and its derivatives with high performances. By regulating the fused ring, side chain and the end group based on Y6 molecular, the small molecule acceptors with ADA'DA fused-ring core can effectively broaden and reduce the absorption spectrum, reduce voltage loss $( V _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } )$ regulating aggregations to realize over $18 \%$ PCEs.

Meanwhile, the developments of high-performance polymer donors are from the earliest MEH-PPV to classical P3HT, and then to D-A copolymers regulated by electron donating unit (D) and electron absorbing unit (A).[25-27] The previous PPV and P3HT have large band gap $( > 1 . 9 \mathrm { e V } )$ , and the emergence of D-A copolymerization strategy gradually improves the application of intramolecular push-pull effect in polymer donors. A series of high-performance polymer donors with adjustable energy level and bandgap have emerged.[28] In particular, the emergence of ADA'DA-type acceptors gives a novel field for unit copolymerized donor materials. In addition, lots of interfacial materials in devices based on ADA'DA-type NFAs have been reported in recent years, including interface modification or doping, design new materials, and so on.[29-33]

# 2.1. Y-Series Non-Fullerene Acceptors

As shown in Figure 2, ADA'DA-type non-fullerene acceptors were first reported in 2017, and the emergence of Y6 in 2019 pushed these type acceptors to the forefront of the field of organic photovoltaics.[16,18,19] In 2017, Zou et al.[16] first introduced $\mathrm { B T z }$ into the electron-deficient central core of NFAs, and then they cooperated with Yang et al.18] extended the length of thiophene in the fused-ring skeleton. Next, end groups of INIC and INTC (non-halogenated dicyanomethylene derivatives) were introduced to synthesize the acceptor Y1 and Y2, respectively. In the same year, Zou et al.19] synthesized non-fullerene acceptor Y6 by applying BT substituted BTz as the central core, and introducing alkyl chain to the $\beta$ position of dithiophene to balance solubility and crystallinity. From the structural point of view, as shown in Figures 3 and 4, the molecular design of ADA'DA structures are demanded to focus on three aspects: 1) fused-ring engineering, heteroatom effect of electron-deficient central core and conjugated skeleton is conducive to the formation of a more stable quinone structure. 2) end group engi neering, optimizing the type and quantity of halogenation, and the symmetry of end groups can effectively regulate the absorption and molecular orientation of acceptor molecules. 3) side chain engineering, the solubility, crystallinity and aggregation behavior of the acceptor molecules can be effectively regulated by the position, shape, length, and angle of the side chains.[34] All the above strategies can be utilized to regulate the energy levels of materials to reduce the energy loss of devices. In addition, regulating the linking units of polymer acceptors to make the arrangement of macromolecular chains more tighten and order is another potential research direction.

# 2.1.1. Fused-Ring Engineering

Electron-deficient central core: Introduction of selenium atom with larger atomic radius and stronger electron cloud delocalization effect into fused ring acceptors has been favored by many researchers.[35,36] when thiophene is replaced by selenophene, the acceptor molecules tend to demonstrate stable quinone form, and exhibit improved electron mobility and photostability. For instance, Lin et al.[37] synthesized a selenium-substituted acceptor named Y6Se with lower Urbach energy, narrower bandgap, higher electron mobility and better photostability than that of Y6. The acceptor Y6Se afforded a PCE of $1 7 . 7 0 \%$ with short-circuit current $( J _ { \mathsf { S C } } )$ of $2 7 . 9 8 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ when blended with D18. Liu et al.[38] introduced selenium to electron-deficient central core and phenylalkyl to side chain, and the synthesized NFA BTSe-IC2F acquired excellent photovoltaic performance with balance solubility and crystallinity. Recently, acceptors based on quinoxaline and phenazine gave excellent photovoltaic performance, which should be further combined with the optimization of device fabrication.[39,40] Wei and coworkers[39] synthesized two NFAs $\mathrm { Q x } { } \mathrm { - } 1$ and Qx-2 by substituting quinoxaline for the central core of BT, and the related binary devices with PM6 obtained energy loss as low as 0.508 and $0 . 4 8 2 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ and the PCE of over $1 8 \%$ . Chen and coworkers[40] constructed ADA'DA-type acceptors (CH4, CH6) using phenazine as the central core, and further enhanced the intermolecular interactions through the central nuclear fluorination effect, thus exhibiting excellent nanofiber network morphology and photovoltaic performance of over $1 8 \%$

Fused-ring conjugated skeleton: It is a common method to design efficient NFAs by modulating push-pull electron effect of conjugated $\pi$ bridge (connected moiety between electrondeficient central core and end groups).[41] Moreover, the length of conjugated structure is closely related to formation of ordered intermolecular $\pi - \pi$ stacking and enhanced charge transfer.42] For example, the introduction of fused-ring thiophene, alkyl/ alkoxy substituted thiophene or electron-rich nitrogen atoms, selenium atoms are all feasible ways to extend the conjugate length of electron donor part.43-46] Notably, aforementioned selenium substitution strategy is still suitable for the regulation of conjugated skeletons.47] Jen et al.[48] introduced strong electron-donating selenophene as conjugate bridge and adjusted the length of $N$ -alkyl chain on the electron-deficient central core. They synthesized two NFAs (mBzS-4F, EHBzS-4F) with absorption edge up to $1 0 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . In which the binary device based on $\mathrm { P M } 6 { : } \mathrm { m B z } \mathrm { S } { - } 4 \mathrm { F }$ achieves a PCE of more than $1 7 \%$ . Wang et al.[49] demonstrated that the selenium substitution of conjugated skeleton will profoundly affect the molecular conformation and accumulation. More importantly, asymmetric selenium substituted acceptor A-WsSe-Cl induced a perfect trade-off among absorption, energy levels and crystallinity, which in turn gave the related device higher efficiency of $1 7 . 5 0 \%$

# 2.1.2. End-Group Engineering

Similar to the electron-deficient fused-ring central core engineering, end-group engineering is another common method to regulate electron push-pull capacity, which has obvious advantages in adjusting the long-range order of fused-ring elec tron acceptors.[50] In end-groups engineering, the specie, position and quantity of halogen have significant influence on the photovoltaic performance of materials.

The effects of substituted end groups of halogen elements from bromine to fluorine $( \mathrm { B r } < \mathrm { C l } < \mathrm { F } )$ on the order of acceptor molecules increase in turn.51] Wang et al.[52] utilized three different hetero-dihalogenated end groups (FCl-IC, FBr-IC, ClBrIC) to regulate the intermolecular packing and absorption based on Y6. Among them, Y-BO-FCl exhibits relative planar molecular geometry, minimum intermolecular packing distance and maximum intermolecular $\pi - \pi$ electron coupling, which contributes to enhancing charge transfer of the films. Hou et al.[53] indicated that it is necessary to control the bandgap of acceptor materials between 1.42-1.44 eV to further improve device efficiency. Therefore, they removed two chlorine atoms from the end groups of BTP-eC9, obtained small molecule acceptor eC9- 2Cl with the blue shift absorption of $2 0 \mathrm { n m }$

As the most electronegative element, fluorine is often used to regulate the intramolecular push-pull electron effect of NFAs to obtain more redshift absorption and lower LUMO level to promote the separation of excitons at donor-acceptor interface. Li and coworkers[54] systematically studied the effects of different fluorination patterns (direct fluorination BTF and indirect -CF3 fluorination BTFM) as end groups on the photovoltaic properties of the materials. Although BTF and BTFM have very similar absorption in solution, BTF has obvious absorption redshift with improved crystallinity in the thin film, and effectively reduces the LUmO level. When blending with polymer donor D18-Cl, the fabricated devices delivered good efficiencies (D18-Cl:BTF of $1 7 . 3 0 \%$ D18-Cl:BTFM of $1 7 . 1 0 \% ]$ Notably, Chen et al.[55] reported a novel electron acceptor BTP-H2 with mono-bromo groups, which realize fine-tuning of energy levels and enhance ordered molecular stacking, thus achieving efficient exciton separation and low $V _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ As a consequence, PM6:BTP-H2-based binary PSC obtained outstanding $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 9 3 2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ and high PCE of $1 8 . 5 0 \%$ . It is worth noting that F or Cl atoms can give higher electrostatic potential (ESP) values distributed on the main backbone of donors and acceptors. Hou et al.[56] indicated that increasing ESP difference between donor and acceptor can fine-tune the charge transfer ratio in its hybridization with the local exciton state and become the key to reduce energy loss. After rationally selecting different halogen-substituted donors and acceptors, they found that the PBDB-TF (PM6): BTP-4Cl system showed the optimal intermolecular interaction and blend morphology to allow balanced charge generation and recombination.[57]

Moreover, asymmetric end groups are also the research direction of ADA'DA-type acceptors.158-60] Compared with asymmetric side-chain and central core modification, end group is more efficient in shortening synthesis time.[61,62] Yan et al.[63] studied the effect of asymmetry halogen substitution on the properties of materials. They demonstrated that the acceptor BTP-2F-ThCl obtained by converting IC-2F group of the end group of Y6 into CPTCN-Cl possesses better-matched energy levels and enhanced $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ in the device with PM6 than that of IC-2F or CPTCN-Cl on both sides of the terminal group. Chen et al.[64] proposed that conjugate length and asymmetry of end group are beneficial to optimize charge separation, reduce energy loss and increase $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } .$ The resultant BTP-S8 and BTP-S9 delivered good efficiency upon blending with PM6.

# 2.1.3. Side-Chain Engineering

Regulating the length, orientation, symmetry, and position of the side chain to adaptation of materials and solvents, obtaining more ordered intermolecular packing, suitable phase separation adjusted absorption and electron transport performance is one of the commonly used structural optimization of ADA'DA type NFAs.65] In recent years, there are three routes for side chain engineering of ADA'DA-type acceptors: aromatic nitrogen heterocycles in electron-deficient central core (N-alkyl), aromatic nitrogen heterocycles in fused-ring conjugated skeletons (N-alkyl), and $\beta$ position of dithiophene in the fused-ring conjugated skeleton.

N-alkyl in electron-deficient central core: Zou et al.[66] balanced the crystallization and solubility of BTA (Y18) by regulating the length and shape of alkyl chains on the central nitrogen atom of $\mathrm { B T z }$ , and synthesized three NFAs of BTA-C1, BTA-C5, and BTA-C6. BTA-C5 possesses the most suitable solubility and crystallinity. On the contrary, linear alkyl substituted BTA-C1 and BTA-C6 exhibit relatively inferior solubility and excessive molecular aggregation. Jen et al.[67] synthesized a series of NIRabsorbing NFAs by introducing various linear and branched N-alkyl sidechains onto electron-deficient central core. They found that EHN6SEH-4F with longer $N _ { ☉ }$ alkyl group possesses stronger end-group intermolecular interactions, which delivered the device efficiency of more than $1 7 \%$

N-alkyl in fused-ring conjugated skeleton: Hou et al.[68] introduced three branched alkyl groups (2-ethylhexyl, 2-butyloctyl, 2-hexyldecy) to the nitrogen atom of BTP-4Cl dense ring conjugated framework pyrrole ring, and obtained three NFAs (BTP-4Cl-8, BTP-4Cl-12, BTP-4Cl-16). And only the blend film based on PBDB-TF(PM6):BTP-4Cl-12 shows the best balanced solution processability and aggregation. Zou et al.[69] optimized the side-chain of acceptor Y11 on two different nitrogen heterocycles. Y11-M removes ethylbutyl from BTz, leaving only one methyl. Y11-EB shortens the butyl branched chain of alkyl on pyrrole ring to ethyl. Due to the broaden absorption and morphology optimization, both of the binary devices with PM6 have improved $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and low energy loss, with PCE of $1 6 . 6 4 \%$ and $1 7 . 1 5 \%$ , respectively. Additionally, the aryl side chain can increase the compatibility between acceptor and donor, but excessive aggregation of the aryl near conjugated skeleton could affect charge transfer ability of the blend film. On the other hand, flexible alkyl chains will improve the intermolecular interaction of acceptors, but closely arranged alkyl chains will inevitably affect solubility. Therefore, introduced phenylalkyl to acceptor backbone, which is also a feasible method to balance solubility and crystallinity,[70,71] Liu et al.[38] introduced the bulky phenylalkyl group to the NFAs side chain to ease regulation the excessive molecular packing caused by the introduction of selenium.

Huang et al.72] regulated the length of inner asymmetric alkyl chains and synthesized two NFAs (EH-HD-4F and Bu-OD-4F). And EH-HD-4F with more suitable face-on orientation endowed the device with increased charge transport, reduced energy loss, and an outstanding efficiency of $1 8 . 3 8 \%$ In particular, Kim et al.51] indicated that inner side-chain chlorination can endow the acceptors (A3, A4) with enhanced compatibility and energetic interfacial interactions with polymer donors.

$\beta$ positions of dithiophene in fused-ring conjugated skeleton: Other than $N \mathrm { . }$ alkyl at the central core, the $\beta$ positions of the dithiophene unit are also commonly used for side-chain optimization. It is less difficult to synthesize from the side-chain of ADA'DA type NFAs than to modify the conjugated skeleton. Moreover, the alkyl chain located on the outside of the acceptor will cause the steric hindrance between fused ring and end group, thus reducing the number of spatial isomers to improve the photovoltaic performance of related materials.73,74] Many studies have also shown that branching of straight-chain alkyl substituents at $\beta$ position of dithiophene in fused-ring conjugated skeletons can alleviate the undesirable aggregation of acceptors.75,76] Hou et al.77 designed a chlorinated NFA BTP-4Cl-BO and introduced linear alkyl group with different lengths to optimize the morphology of active layer. Finally, BTP-eC9 based on nonyl-substitution has more ordered intermolecular packing and better electron transport. Chen et al.64] also indicated that shortening the length of linear alkyl side-chain (BTP-s9) is beneficial to increase molecular packing and reduce $\pi - \pi$ distance, thus optimizing the fill factor (FF) of related devices. Yan et al.176] investigated that replacing linear side chain of BTP-4F-PC6 with branched chain is conducive to inhibit the excessive intermolecular packing of acceptors. Due to the excessive phase separation alleviated by BTP-4F-P2EH and BTP-4FP3EH, the efficiency of PM6-based binary devices achieved $1 8 . 2 2 \%$ and $1 7 . 5 7 \%$ respectively. Sun et al.75] prepared the branched side-chain substituted acceptor L8-BO, which can endow the material with blue-shifted absorption and enhanced HOMO energy level. Matching with PM6, the PCE of single junction PSC is increased to $1 8 . 3 2 \%$ . Hu et al.[78] pointed out that the photovoltaic parameters of small molecule acceptors (BTP-4F-T2EH, BTP-4F-T3EH) with branched alkylthiophene side chains in organic photovoltaic devices are significantly better than those using branched alkyl side chains (BTP-4F-T2C8). Notably, Peng et al.79] proposed that the fuorination side-chain can also improve the crystallinity of acceptor (BTP-FTh), thus improving the efficiency of the devices.

Besides, the orientation and asymmetry of side-chains can also greatly affect the photovoltaic performance of NFAs. Li et al.80] investigated that the $\beta$ position of 2-ethylhexyl (mTEH) substituted at the outer thiophene exhibits a tighter $\pi - \pi$ stacking than that of the $\alpha \mathrm { \Omega }$ position (o-TEH), and the related device with PBQ6 obtained a PCE of $1 8 . 5 1 \%$ .Yan et al.[81] designed three NFAs (o-BTP-PhC6, $m$ -BTP-PhC6, and $p$ -BTPPhC6) with different side-chain orientations. Among them, the $m$ -BTP-PhC6 with a certain tilt angle to the phenyl demonstrated the best intermolecular packing and charge transfer performance, and the binary device based on PTQ10:m-BTPPhC6 exhibited the best optimized morphology with the PCE up to $1 7 . 7 0 \%$ . At the same time, they synthesized a series of asymmetric acceptors (Y6-1O, BTP1O-4Cl-C8, BTP1O-4Cl-C10, BTP1O-4Cl-C12) by combining the advantages of alkoxy and alkyl, improving the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the device while avoiding excessive molecular aggregation. The highest PCE based on binary device PM6:BTP1O-4Cl-C12 reached $1 7 . 1 0 \%$ [74]

# 2.1.4. Link Engineering of Small-Molecule Acceptors

Under long-term operation, polymer donors and small-molecule acceptors are easy to be separated seriously, which is an important reason for the poor stability of devices.[82-88] Considering the compatibility and stability of polymer materials, the long-term stability of all-polymer organic photovoltaic devices will be greatly improved.[89-91] However, there are few reports on high-performance polymer acceptors based on ADA'DAtype NFAs.[92] For instance, Yang et al.[93] reported a widely used polymer acceptor PY-IT with PCE of $1 5 . 0 5 \%$ . At present, the efficiency of PY-IT-based devices can afford over $16 \%$ through the optimization of fabrication techniques.9497] Especially blending with PQM-Cl, PYIT-based binary devices delivered a record high PCE of $1 8 . 0 0 \%$ [97] In addition, a series of developed polymer acceptors, such as PZT $\mathbf { \nabla \cdot } \mathbf { r }$ (PCE of $1 5 . 8 0 \% )$ [98] PJ1 (PCE of $1 5 . 8 0 \% )$ [9] PJTVT (PCE of $1 6 . 1 3 \% )$ ,[100] PYT-1S1Se (PCE of $1 6 . 3 0 \% )$ ,[101] and PY-V-y (PCE of $1 7 . 1 0 \% )$ ,[102] also exhibit good photovoltaic performance. Hence, the device efficiency based on polymeric small molecule acceptors is gradually close to that of small molecular acceptors, but there are still certain gaps to be overcome.[103-106]

However, the improvement of ADA'DA-type acceptors from fused rings, end groups and side chains cannot satisfy the development of this kind of materials, and the recently emerging high-performance polymer acceptors achieved by the regulation of linking units have shown potential advantages in arrange macromolecular chains more compact and order.105,107,108] Wei et al.[109] synthesized a novel oligomer acceptor 2BTP-2F-T by linking two ADA'DA-type NFA via thiophene unit. Blending with PM6, 2BTP-2F-T delivered an efficiency of $1 8 . 1 9 \%$ with low energy loss of $0 . 5 3 \mathrm { e V }$ and good stability. Zou et al.[110] prepared two A--A polymeric small molecular materials (QM1, QM2) through link engineering. Compared with small molecule acceptors, dimer acceptors show a helical structure, and their energy levels are dispersed over the entire longer molecular skeleton, resulting in a more delocalized LUMO level. The prepared devices with PM6 afforded $1 7 . 0 5 \%$ and $1 6 . 3 6 \%$ efficiency with good stability. Yan et al.[102] indicated that thiophene will induce adverse intramolecular torsion between linking unit and end group, as well as different molecular geometry. They found that polymer acceptor PY-V-ywith vinylene possesses less twisting degree between end group and link unit, and exhibits tighter interchain packing, which in turn endows higher carrier dynamics and increased the efficiency of binary all-polymer PSCs to over $1 7 \%$ for the first time. Peng et al.[111] prepared a series of polymer acceptors with different fuorinated end groups and connecting units of bithiophene fluoride. Combined with the morphological optimization by n-dopant and sequential deposition, the efficiency of PM6/PYDT-3F-based allpolymer solar cells reached a PCE of $1 7 . 4 1 \%$

# 2.2. Efficient Polymer Donors

Among the high-performance devices, the commonly used electron donor units (D units) are bithienyl-banzodithiophene (BDT),112,113] thiophene (T), and the commonly used electron absorbing units (A units) are benzodithiophene-dione (BDD),[11214) dithieno-benzothiadiazole (DTBT),15) quinoxaline (Qx),[116] as shown in Figures 5 and 6. In recent years, researchers have made unremitting efforts to adjust bandgap and energy levels, optimize morphology and intermolecular aggregation of blend films, and improve batch-to-batch reproducibility of polymers through unit copolymerization and skeleton regulation strategies.[117,118] In addition, based on the consideration of preparation cost, polythiophene (PT) donor materials have the advantages of low price and easy large-scale production. At present, the efficiency of PT-based binary devices can be over 16%.[119-121]

# 2.2.1. Unit Copolymerization Strategy

D-A copolymerization strategy: Optimizing the polymer structure to make it possesses deeper HOHO energy level is beneficial to improve the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the device without changing absorption. Ma et al.[122] synthesized PM7 with deeper HOMO level by halogen substitution strategy based on PM6. The efficiency of PM7:Y6 binary device is $1 7 . 0 0 \%$ . Ding et al.[115] synthesized a wide band gap polymer donor D18 using the D unit BDT and the planar symmetrical A unit DTBT. A noticeably high PCE of $1 8 . 2 2 \%$ was recorded for D18:Y6-based binary polymer solar cells. After then Yan et al.[123] synthesized D18-Cl with a deeper HOMO level than that of D18, which delivered $1 7 . 9 7 \%$ efficiency upon blending with Y6.

Furthermore, quinoxaline (Qx) has attracted extensive attention as A unit in polymer donors due to its low cost and facilitate molecular packing.[124] $\mathrm { ~ I ~ n ~ } ~ 2 0 2 1$ , Li et al.[116] synthesized Qx-based polymer donors (PBQ5 and PBQ6) combined with side-chain regulation strategy. The alkyl side-chain of PBQ6 is also connected with fuorothiophene, which exhibits better crystallinity and photovoltaic properties. The binary device based on PBQ6:Y6 delivered an excellent PCE of $1 7 . 6 2 \%$ In order to further increase the external quantum efficiency (EQE) value in the short-wavelength region $( < 6 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m } )$ , Hou et al.[53] designed a large bandgap (2.07 eV) Qx-based polymer donor PBQx-TF. PCEs up to $1 7 . 7 0 \%$ were achieved in PBQx-TF:eC9- 2Cl-based solar cells. They also designed a new polymer donor PQM-Cl with introduced methyl into Qx unit, and the efficiency of the PQM-Cl:PYIT all-polymer solar cells reached an astonishing efficiency of $1 8 . 0 0 \%$ [97] In addition, Hou et al.[125] designed another two kinds of 1,3,4-thiadiazole (TDz)-based polymer donors. They indicated that the aggregation of PB2 with plane conformation in solution is obviously stronger than that of PB1, and exhibits excellent efficiency in both binary and ternary devices.

What's more, Sun et al.1126] prepared benzodifuran (BDF)- based polymer donors, and Ding et al.127] further explored this kind of polymer donors through side-chain engineering, denoted as L3. They point out that the branched chain of L3 close to the skeleton reduced the torsion between BDT and T units with improved the planarity of polymer backbone. In conjunction with small molecular acceptor N3, PCE of more than $1 7 \%$ was achieved.

Ternary copolymerization strategy: Other than direct regulation of electron donor or electron acceptor moiety in polymer donor, the preparation of random copolymers by introducing a third component (donor unit or acceptor unit) into polymer skeleton can also achieve the desired properties.128-132] In particular, the molecular weight of polymer is closely related to its aggregation behavior and morphology of blend film. For instance, Zhang et al.133] investigated that 74 kDa PM6 can suppress the excessive molecular packing, form a suitable phase separation, which effectively promotes exciton dissociation and charge transport in the devices.

BDD with planar symmetrical structure has been widely reported because of its appropriate electron absorption properties and self-aggregation as acceptor unit for high-performance donors.[134] $\mathrm { ~ I ~ n ~ } ~ 2 0 2 1$ , Yang et al.[114] designed a new iBDD-Si (TIPS alternative to BDD isomer) unit that is superior to BDD, as segment into the backbone of polymer donor PM6, and synthesized a series of iBDD-Si based polymer donors (PM6-5Si, PM6-10Si, and PM6-15Si). The results demonstrate that the introduction of iBDD-Si unit can finely tune light absorption, energy level and morphology of the devices. Among them, the PM6-5Si (iBDD-Si account for $5 \%$ :N3 PSC presents the best PCE of $1 7 . 0 1 \%$ with $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of 0.852 V, $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 6 . 3 2 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , and FF of $7 5 . 8 1 \%$ . Many early NFAs studies were designed based on PM6 or PM7, and the performance of these two polymer donors was limited by the accuracy of molecular weight, resulting in large batch differences.135-137] In addition, there is no site for structural improvement.[138] The ternary random copolymerization strategy can effectively solve the adverse factors caused by the sensitivity of molecular weight $( 5 0 - 7 0 ~ \mathrm { k D a }$ of PM6 or PM7) and structural limitations.[139] Yan et al.[136] introduced A2 unit with alkyl side chain and electron-deficient fused-ring into the backbone of PM6/PM7 (Halogen non-uniform substitution), and the synthesized SZ4 with D-A1-D-A2 structure can effectively broaden the range of molecular weight corresponding to batches with high performance. Li et al.[139,140] used TzTz and TTz units as A2 moiety to give PM6 better molecular aggregation and domain purity. As a result, PM1 and PM6- $\mathrm { T z } 2 0$ exhibited the optimized performance and the device achieved over $1 7 \%$ device efficiency. However, ternary copolymerization strategy inevitably incurs cumbersome synthesis steps and more expensive cost. Conjugated polymer donors based on asymmetric monomers retain the characteristics of constructing irregular main chains to reduce molecular weight sensitivity, and can reduce the preparation cost at the same time.135] For example, Cao et al.135] removed the cyano group on one side of the 3,4-dicyanothiophene (DCT) unit, weakened the excessive molecular accumulation caused by bisymmetric cyano group. They indicated that the synthesis process is simplified to three steps, and the prepared PBCT-2F exhibits excellent properties performance in a wide molecular weight range (18-74 kDa).

Additionally, some halogenated solvents such as chloroform (CF), chlorobenzene (CB) and $o$ -dichlorobenzene (o-DCB) are commonly used in the preparation of PsCs. Based on the consideration of environmental problem, the development of non-halogen solvent process is the critical for industrial application. Bo et al.141] designed two polymer donor construction units (B2TCl-o, B2TCl-i), which were introduced into the backbone of PM6 by ternary copolymerization strategy. The related materials have improved solubility and reduced crystallinity, which can obtain the ability to dissolve in non-halogenated solvents (o-DMB) with good photovoltaic performance. Lu et al.[142] introduced ester-substituted thiazole (E-Tz) unit into D18 backbone, denoted as $\mathrm { O P z 1 1 }$ In conjunction with Y6, the blend film exhibited smoother morphology and more efficient exciton dissociation and charge collection. A PCE of $1 8 . 4 2 \%$ was achieved. Zhang et al.113] introduced carboxylate pyrazine $( \mathrm { C P z } )$ unit with similar structure to $\mathrm { E } { - } \mathrm { T } z$ unit into the main chain of PM6, and use the intramolecular interlocking force of N-S and $\mathrm { O - H }$ to promote the planarization of the polymer backbone, and the blend film with Y6 also exhibited good photovoltaic performance.

# 2.2.2. Skeleton Regulation Strategy

In order to improve exciton separation, charge transport and charge recombination, molecular regulation from polymer skeleton is a feasible method. Wang et al.[14] introduce iridium complexes with different concentrations to the conjugated skeleton of PM6 to adjust crystallinity and aggregation. The binary device based on $\mathrm { P M 6 \mathrm { - } I r 1 ( 1 m o l \% ) { : } Y ( }$ afforded the best performance with PCE of over $1 7 \%$ . Li et al.[145] broke the design mode of alternating copolymerization by increasing the proportion of F-BDT in the polymer skeleton, and retained the advantages of random strategy in improving the bulk properties of polymers. Moreover, the improvement of mechanical strength is an essential step for the application of organic devices.[146] For example, Kim et al.147] proposed to enhance the physical mechanical properties of psCs through skeleton regulation strategy.

Nowadays, many advanced organic photovoltaic devices often stiffen the main chain to improve the aggregation and charge transport of molecules, resulting in the decline of mechanical strength.[148,149] $\mathrm { ~ I ~ n ~ } ~ 2 0 2 1$ , Kim group introduced flexible units (alkyl chains) into the backbone of polymer donors to improve the bending resistance of materials. Ultimately, the polymer donor PM6 with $10 \%$ flexible unit (PM6-C10) delivered the best performance.[147] Zhang and coworkers[150] report that the PF1 synthesized by introducing thiophene fuoride into the PM6 skeleton possessed lower energy level, slight redshift absorption, and better crystallinity. # 2.3.2. Hole Transporting Materials

Similar to ETL, the function of HTL is to block electrons and transport holes, improve WF of electrode and form good Ohmic contact between ITO and active layer. In addition, HTL can smooth surface of ITO, which can reduce leakage current and adjust morphology of active layers.[13] In the studies of PSCs, PEDOT:PSS is favored attribute to its respectable conductivity and well-matched WF $( { \approx } 5 . 1 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ with numerous polymer donors.189] However, ITO will be corroded by acidic and hygroscopic PEDOT:PSS, causing in the diffusion of In ions into active layer, which in turn impairs the stability of device.190] Therefore, the research of HTL materials is mostly based on the improvement of PEDOT:PSS or search for alternatives to develop high-performance organic photovoltaics.[191-194]

In 2019, Lin et al.91] developed solution-processable liquid exfoliated ws2 and Mos2 nanosheets as ETL, which can modulate ITO with higher WF than that of PEDOT:PSS and applied it in the fabrication of PSCs based PM6:Y6, as shown in Figure 9a. They found that compared with ${ \mathrm { M o S } } _ { 2 }$ ${ \sf W S } _ { 2 }$ exfoliated sheets are thinner and exhibit more continuous exfoliation layer during spin coating, which endows PSCs with low bimolecular recombination and high efficiency.

In alternative materials for PEDOT:PSS, $\mathrm { M o O } _ { 3 }$ has attracted much attention because of its good charge transport properties and high stability. Normally, the post-treatment of high temperature annealing is essential to achieve required hole transport performance when prepared by sol-gel method. Interestingly, Tran et al.[192] reported the application of annealing-free aqueous $\mathsf { M o O } _ { \mathrm { x } }$ as solution-processed HTL in highperformance

NFA-based PSCs. In addition, polyoxometalate-based inorganic clusters (PIC) are considered as a feasible candidate for HTL because of its specific molecular structure, similar WF with metal oxides and water-alcohol solubility. To further overcome the drawbacks of low electrical conductivity and inappropriate energy level and broaden the application of PICs in the field of organic photovoltaics, Hou et al.1195] proposed to mix HPMO and $\mathrm { S n C l } _ { 2 }$ in methanol while maintaining a high WF and profoundly increasing electrical conductivity. Figure 9b shows the synthetic procedures and the structure of HPMO. As a result, ${ \mathrm { H P M O : } } { \mathrm { S n } } ^ { 2 + }$ as HTL delivered the devices based on PM6:BTPeC9 a PCE of $1 7 . 3 0 \%$

Besides the above mentioned materials, self-assembled (SAM) organic materials 2-(9H-carbazol-9-yl)ethyl phosphonic acid (2PACz) have also been investigated.[196] Lin et al.[29] demonstrated that 2PACz as HTL insert into the ternary PM6:BTP. $\scriptstyle \cdot \mathrm { e } \mathrm { C } 9 : \mathrm { P } \mathrm { C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B } \mathrm { M }$ PSCs can effectively restrain the bimolecular recombination and improve charge extraction. After that, Lin et al.[32] further explored the method of chemical modification to provide more precisely engineered SAM that can be utilized in the field of organic photovoltaics. Outperforming methoxy functional 2PACz, the larger dipole moment of Br-2PACz endowed the ITO/Br-2PACz electrode higher WF and afforded PM6:BTP- $\mathrm { e C 9 : P C _ { 7 1 } B M }$ based devices a respectable PCE of $1 8 . 4 0 \%$ . More importantly, after three flushing cycles, the ITO/Br-2PACz still possesses high stability and maintained over $1 7 \%$ PCE. What's more, Lin et al.[197] further developed the halogenation strategy of 2PACz and fabricated ITO/ halogen-2PACz-based devices approaching $1 9 \%$ efficiency.

As for the investigation on the modification of PEDOT:PSS, Lian et al.[193] indicated that the ${ \mathrm { M o S } } _ { 2 }$ quantum dots-composite HTL is able to profoundly improve the stability of PSCs. In addition, Park et al.198] inserted conjugated wPFsCz- to modify the interface between high-conductivity ITO and lowconductivity PEDOT:PSS. They found that the WPFSCz-layer can reduce the recombination due to eliminated interface trap position, changed the WF of ITO, and matched energy alignment. The PCE of PM6:Y6-based devices with WPFSCz-/ PEDOT:PSS HTL reached $1 7 . 3 4 \%$ which was higher than that $( 1 5 . 8 6 \% )$ of the devices with PEDOT:PSS HTL. Ma and coworkers[199] utilized high reactivity phenylethylamine derivatives with sulfonic acid group on PSS to doping PEDOT:PSS, and found that the increased number of hydroxyl groups on HTL layer results in the formation of more hydrogen bonds, which endows improved WF and conductivity. However, they also indicated that the increasing hydrogen bond may lead to poor surface roughness of HTL. Therefore, compared with phenylethylamine (PA) without hydroxyl substitution and dihydroxy substituted dopamine (DA), mono hydroxyl substituted tyramine (TA) gave the best device performance.

Furthermore, 2D transition-metal carbides or nitrides $\mathrm { M } _ { \mathrm { n + 1 } } \mathrm { X } _ { \mathrm { n } } \mathrm { T } _ { \mathrm { x } } ,$ which possess the advantages of water solubility, electrical conductivity and modifiable structure, have shown decent application in the field of PsCs.200,201] Wang et al.200] investigated that the conductivity, charge transport, and collection properties in the devices can be significantly enhanced when 2 wt $\%$ $\mathrm { T i } _ { 3 } \mathrm { C } _ { 2 } \mathrm { T } _ { \mathrm { x } }$ incorporated into PEDOT:PSS. Huang et al.[201] adjusted the WF of $\mathrm { N b } _ { 2 } \mathrm { C T } _ { \mathrm { x } }$ by alkali and annealing treatment, and constructed both inverted and conventional PSCs of over $1 7 \%$ efficiency based PM6:Y6 as active layer and $\mathrm { N b } _ { 2 } \mathrm { C T } _ { \mathrm { x } }$ as ETL.

Besides, it is notable that HTL-free high-performance devices present enormous potential for further commercial applications. For example, Sun et al.202] reported a general method for the construction of HTL-free devices utilizing o-DCB(odichlorobenzene): $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ co-solvent chlorinated ITO electrodes. Outperforming the devices with ITO/PEDOT:PSS, ITO-Cl-o$\mathrm { D C B } { : } \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ based devices exhibited surprising efficiency both in 5 and $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { m m } ^ { 2 }$

# 2.4. Energy Loss of Devices

As has been well investigated, one of factors that determined high PCEs of NFAs PSCs than that of fullerene system PSCs is the relative low energy loss $( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } )$ , which are composed of radiative recombination loss above bandgap $( \Delta E _ { 1 } )$ , radiative recombination loss below bandgap $( \Delta E _ { 2 } )$ and non-radiative recombination loss $( \Delta E _ { 3 } )$ , as shown in Figure 10 and the following equation.[203]

$$
\begin{array} { r l } & { E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } = E _ { \mathrm { g } } - q V _ { \mathrm { o C } } = ( E _ { \mathrm { g } } - q V _ { \mathrm { o C } } ^ { \mathrm { S Q } } ) + \left( q V _ { \mathrm { o C } } ^ { \mathrm { S Q } } - q V _ { \mathrm { o C } } ^ { \mathrm { r a d } } \right) + \left( q V _ { \mathrm { o C } } ^ { \mathrm { r a d } } - q V _ { \mathrm { o C } } \right) } \\ & { \qquad = \left( E _ { \mathrm { g } } - q V _ { \mathrm { o C } } ^ { \mathrm { S Q } } \right) + q \Delta V _ { \mathrm { o C } } ^ { \mathrm { r a d , b e l o w g a p } } + q \Delta V _ { \mathrm { o C } } ^ { \mathrm { n o n \cdot r a d } } = \Delta E _ { 1 } + \Delta E _ { 2 } + \Delta E _ { 3 } } \end{array}
$$

In the equation, $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ is the optical bandgap of active layer. $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } ^ { \mathrm { S Q } }$ is the maximum voltage predicted by Shockley-Quesser (SQ) theory. $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } ^ { \mathrm { r a d } }$ is the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ with only radiative recombination in the device. $\Delta V _ { \mathrm { O C } } ^ { \mathrm { r a d , b e l o w g a p } }$ is the voltage loss caused by radiative recombination from the absorption below optical bandgap.

![](images/34fc1ec0e626aaf8267e7d2553349e5f490c6e068ca304cd968fa4191893c9f5.jpg)

$\Delta V _ { \mathrm { O C } } ^ { \mathrm { n o n - r a d } }$ is the voltage loss from non-radiative recombination. Usually, $\Delta E _ { 1 }$ is unavoidable in photovoltaic processes for any kinds of solar cells, which is typically 0.25 or slightly higher. $\Delta E _ { 2 }$ is caused by radiative recombination from the absorption below the bandgap of active materials, which is greatly related to the offset of local excited state (LE) and charge transfer state (CT), as $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { L E - C T } } .$ For fullerene system devices, large $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { L E - C T } }$ (usually larger than $0 . 3 \ \mathrm { e V }$ is required) means large energy differences between donor and acceptor materials, which promises effective exciton dissociation. However, for successful donor/NFAs system, efficient exciton dissociation can be achieved even with very low $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { L E - C T } }$ $( \approx 5 0 \ \mathrm { m e V } )$ , therefore, corresponding $\Delta E _ { 2 }$ can be almost negligible, and the low $\Delta E _ { 2 }$ greatly reduce the $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of PSCs and ensure a relative high $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of devices. $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ is the non-radiative recombination loss at the donor/acceptor interface. Compared with $\Delta E _ { 2 }$ $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ contributes mostly to $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ , therefore, reducing non-radiative recombination loss is the key of reducing the $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of devices.[204] Table 3 is the $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ summary of the recent high-performance devices. It can be seen that $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ as low as $0 . 1 9 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ can be obtained, thus induces low $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of $0 . 4 9 6 \ \mathrm { e V }$ in PM6 and BO-4Cl:BO-5Cl system devices. To interpret the factors that influence $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ in high PCE PSCs, Gao and coworkers[205] combined temperature-dependent electroluminescence experiments and dynamic vibronic simulations, and highlighted an important role that thermal population of LE plays in low $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ system. Besides, the photoluminescence yield of the pristine materials determines the low limit of $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ The authors also predicted that $< 0 . 2$ eV $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ can be obtained without sacrificing charge generation efficiency of devices by carefully designing new donor and acceptor materials with high luminescence efficiency and complementary optical absorption bands.

# 3. Advanced Device Fabrication Technologies

In the previous chapter, the molecular design of active layer materials and interfacial materials in devices are illustrated. Meanwhile, the device performance can also be improved by advanced fabrication techniques. For instance, firstly, the third or fourth component donor/acceptor can be introduced to broaden the absorption spectrum of the active layer, regulate the energy levels alignment, and adjust the morphology of the blend film.218-220] Secondly, fabricating tandem devices will enhance the photon capture ability, thus $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ or $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ performances are superimposed. Thirdly, it should be noted that most active layer materials can only be dissolved in halogenated solvents.221 The developments of halogen-free solvent-possessed devices are also the research directions of scientists.22-224] Fourthly, select appropriate additives to regulate the aggregation behaviors of donors and acceptors during film formation processes.225,226] Lastly, in terms of fabricating active layers, layer-by-layer structure can be fabricated by sequential deposition (SD).[227,228] Separately forming donor layers and acceptor layers, can more accurately control the microstructure of the active layer, which benefits improving morphology stability without sacrificing exciton dissociation efficiency.[229] Besides, other active layer coating techniques (superior to spin coating) are investigated to match the large-area devices fabrication requirements. # 3.1.1. Solvent Engineering of BHJ PSCs

For high photovoltaic performance materials, the effect of the temperature-dependent aggregation (TDA) of materials in solutions can be used to remove the surface and internal defects of the materials, optimize molecular packing or increase the machinability of the materials in halogen-free solvent.[222,230] Peng et al.231] utilized the temperature sensitivity-induced the change of aggregation of BTP-BO-4Cl in toluene (Tol). Different molecular aggregation can be obtained by controlling the decay time of the solution. As shown in Figure 13a, PM6:BTP-BO-4Cl films exhibit changed morphology that cast from Tol solutions with different delay times. And the results pointed out that the blend film after being delayed for $4 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ possessed the optimal interpenetrating network structure and achieved an efficiency of $1 7 . 3 0 \%$

However, Jen et al.[234] presented that the strict control of delay times will inevitably increase the complexity of the fabrication process and seriously affect the repeatability of the devices. Therefore, they developed a feasible method to regulate the pre-aggregation behaviors of materials in non-halogenated solution only through rational material selection and solvent control. Contrasting to chloroform-treated films, donor, acceptor and blend films prepared by XT (o-xylene (XY):1,2,4-trimethylbenzene (TMB), ratio of 3.5:1) showed stronger absorptions (especially at a concentration of $5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \mathrm { \ m g \ m L ^ { - 1 } }$ , which demonstrated that the enhanced aggregation of materials in XT. In particular, Ma et al.232] indicated that common solvents (CF, CB) are not universal for all systems, and not all of them can endow the highest performance to the devices. They emphasized the effects of the inherent aggregation or crystallinity of the materials, the compatibility of the donor and acceptor and the volatility of the solvent on the solvent selection of the active layer, and proposed that CF is able to enhance the $\pi - \pi$ packing of PM6:Y6, but its effect on PM6:BTP-4Cl and PM6:BTPClBr2 is not obviously than that of CB and TMB, as shown in Figure 13b. Ultimately, hydrocarbon solvent TMB-processed devices based on $\mathrm { P M 6 { : } B T P { \cdot } { 4 } C l { : } P C _ { 7 1 } B M }$ obtained a champion PCE of $1 7 . 3 6 \%$ in the condition of water and methanol in air $3 5 \%$ relative humidity) and without solvent additive or posttreatment. Notably, different from the relatively high temperature requirement of TDA method, Guo et al.[235] developed a low-temperature $( < 2 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ aging-induced aggregation strategy to improve the device efficiency. Under the condition of over $1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ and a low temperature about $2 7 3 \ \mathrm { K } .$ the PM6 molecule in CF solution shows a transition from order to disorder, which further lead to the more balanced hole and electron mobilities $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ of 1.13 than 1.30 (without cold-aging). For environmental factors, such as $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ in air and solvents, may produce electron or hole traps, implying the unfavorable change of the device performance.236] Min et al.1236] proposed an effective method to remove the water-induced traps. CF treated by solvent-water evaporation (SwE) strategy can be used for subsequent films formation without the presence of water molecules. As a consequence, outperforming the conventional CF-processed devices based on PM6:Y6, SWE-CF is able to endow device with PCE of over $1 7 \%$

# 3.1.2. Additive Engineering of BHJ PSCs

The morphology control of active layers has been an enduring topic in the field of organic photovoltaics.[238] Over the years, incorporating solvent additives, such as 1,8-diiodooctane (DIO) and chloronaphthalene (CN), to blend solutions are the most common methods to optimize the morphology of the active layers.229,239,240] Such solvent additives are characterized by high boiling point and selective solubility to one of materials in the active layer.[241] In the process of subsequent thermal annealing or high vacuum treatment, the delayed volatilization effect of solvent additives is beneficial to regulate the selfaggregation or phase separation of donors and acceptors.[242] However, the development of NFAs with various structure and properties are not satisfied with the previous reported additives of fullerene system, so it is particularly important to develop efficient additives suitable for Y-series NFAs. At the same year, they prepared another n-type dopants diquat (DQ) by chemical reduction. After thermal annealing, Y6 exhibited a tighter molecular packing mode, which is more powerful to improve the charge transport capacity of the device.

# 3.1.3. Other Morphology Regulation Methods

Apart from the solvent and additive engineering to regulate the morphology of active layer, thermal annealing and solvent vapor annealing (SVA) are two another common morphology regulation methods.[261]

For thermal annealing, it is a most common and facile method to optimize the interpenetrating morphology of the active layer. And for instance, thermal annealing can increase the phase separation of donor and acceptor materials to an appropriated size, improve the molecular stacking to a favorite face-on orientation, improve the phase purity, thus improve the excitons dissociation efficiency, improve the charge transport and reduce the charge recombination rate. For SVA method, it means active layers are placed in an atmosphere of organic solvent, such as CB, CF, etc, to obtain molecular self-aggregation, and by regulating the annealing time to obtain optimized morphology.

With more and more high photovoltaic performance donor/ acceptor materials reported, a nano-fibril-like morphology is believed as a morphology that can achieve high photovoltaic performances.262] Hou et al.[125] elaborately designed two polymer donors of PB1 and PB2. The results indicated that PB1:BTP-eC9 film with "spheroid-like" morphology yield obviously low PCE than that of PB2:BTP-eC9 film with "fibril-like" morphology (Figure 16a,b), and the "fibril-like" morphology endows bicontinuous phase separation, which is beneficial to enhance the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF of PB2:BTP-eC9 device. Ding et al.[115] reported the fibril-like morphology of D18:Y6, and after SVA treatment, as shown in Figure 16c,d, the fibril sizes of D18:Y6 is obviously enlarged, therefore, the PCE can be achieved as high as $1 8 . 2 2 \%$ . Apart from binary devices, a multi-length scaled bicontinuous double-fibril network morphology was obtained in a carefully designed ternary donor/acceptor film, in which the phase separation, crystallinity and specific fibril widths are collectively manipulated. As result, the bicontinuous doublefibril network morphology ensures a reduced mixing zone and reduced recombination rate, thus a maximum PCE of $1 9 . 6 0 \%$ was obtained in single-junction, which is one of the highest PCE of single-junction devices.[20]

# 3.2. Multicomponent Strategies for High-Performance BHJ PSCs

Limited by the balance between light absorptions and charge carrier transport, the thicknesses of single junction devices are usually limited at $1 0 0 { - } 1 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ without sacrificing efficiencies.[263] However, too thin thickness inevitably suppresses photon harvesting.264] Therefore, the most direct preponderance of adding the third or fourth component to the active layer is to make up for the inherent defects (weak light absorption) of the host binary devices.[265] Apart from improving photon utilization, broadening spectrum, optimizing morphology, balancing excitons separation and charge transport, synergistically enhancing photovoltaic parameters and reducing energy loss are other ideal functions of the third component incorporating to the host system, and also are the focus in the field of organic photovoltaics.[209,266-268]

There are four mechanisms that can be used to describe the role of the third component in the host binary devices, as shown in Figure 17. 1) In the charge transfer mechanism also known as the energy cascade mechanism, the third component is equivalent to a relay located at the D/A interface of the host binary system, which is conducive to charge separation and transfer. The existence of this gradient energy structure not only improves the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of the device but also broadens the absorption spectrum.269] 2) In the energy transfer mechanism, a donor in D1:D2:A or an acceptor in D:A1:A2 harvests photons and transfers energy to the host D:A through Dexter or Forster energy transfer (FRET).270] The energy transmitter or photon harvester can be the third component or one of the host materials. In addition, the charge transfer mechanism and energy transfer mechanism may occur simultaneously in a ternary system.(271 3) In the parallel mechanism, the third component can form independent electrons and holes transmission channels with D or A in the host system, and the function of these two sub-cells will not affect each other.272] 4) The ternary system under the alloy mechanism is similar to the host binary system. Two donors or acceptors with good compatibility present as a mixed bulk with unified HOMO and LUMO energy levels.[273] Besides, one big characteristic of the device with alloy mechanism is the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ changing linearly with the changing of the third component.

# 3.2.2. Polymer Acceptors, Small Molecular Donors and Oligomer Donors as Guest Component

In terms of materials, in addition to the common small mole. cule acceptors and polymer donors as the third component, polymer acceptors and small molecule donors or oligomer donors can also be utilized to fabricate high-performance

PSCs. Yan and Li et al.321] incorporated an amorphous polymer acceptor (BN-T) as the morphology regulator to PM6-based devices. Furthermore, all-polymer solar cells are considered as a breakthrough of organic photovoltaic devices from laboratory to industrialization because of their better morphology stability, whereas, obtaining an appropriate phase separation in all-polymer solar cells is a big obstacle to achieving high photovoltaic performance.94,322] Min et al.323] prepared two fuorinated polymer acceptors (PY2F-T and PYT) with complementary absorption. Ultimately, PM6:PYT:PY2F-T based-ternary all-polymer solar cells obtained an excellent PCE of $1 7 . 2 0 \%$ with a FF of $7 6 . 0 0 \%$ , a $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 5 . 2 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and an energy loss of $0 . 4 4 3 \ \mathrm { e V } .$ The incorporation of PYT as the third component balances the phase separation and the crystallization of materials to promote the photovoltaic processes, such as exciton dissociation, charge transfer and extraction.

The design of small molecule donors and the strategy of building all small molecule solar cells have also attracted much attention because there exists no batch-to-batch problems caused by different molecular weight distributions of polymers.[324,325] Hou et al.324] designed a small molecule donor B1 and fabricated efficient ternary devices (PCE of $1 7 . 0 0 \% )$ with two small molecule acceptors BO-2Cl and BO-4Cl. However, there are more publications that reported the small molecule donor as the third component in polymer donor/NFA and achieved enhanced photovoltaic performances.[326-328] Bao and coworkers[329] indicated that the small molecule donor DRTBT-C4 show good compatibility with PM6 and Y6, and incorporating DRTB-T-C4 can significantly optimize the morphology of the blend film and obtain an outstanding FF up to $8 1 . 3 0 \%$ Similarly, Wei et al.[213] designed a small molecule donor BTID2F with a high HOMO level and excellent compatibility with PM6. As a consequence, ternary devices based on PM6:BTID2F:Y6 and PM6:BTID-2F:L8-BO obtained outstanding efficiency of $1 7 . 9 8 \%$ and $1 8 . 5 2 \%$ , respectively. Ma et al.330] utilized small molecular donor of BTR-Cl with miscibility differences from donor and acceptor, to improve the molecular crystallinity and continuity of vertical phase separation. Finally, the ternary devices with active layer thickness of 300 and $1 1 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ realized PCE of $1 5 . 5 0 \%$ and $1 7 . 2 0 \%$ , respectively.

In addition, the preparation of oligomer materials using basic $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ units is a feasible way to balance the merits and defects of small molecules and polymers.[65] Li and coworkers[331] designed and synthesized D-A-D-A-D type oligomer donor (5BDTBDD) and A-D-A-D-A type oligomer donor (5BDDBDT) as the third component. Cheng et al.[332] illustrated that the alloy-like structure formed by the host polymer and the oligomer with similar structures is an effective way to regulate the energy levels and molecular packing of the host polymer donor. Ultimately, the highest efficiency of ternary PSCs based on PM6:BDDBDTBDD (ADA-type donor):BTP-eC9 yielded over $1 8 \%$

# 3.3. High-Performance PSCs with Tandem Structures

Other than incorporating the third and fourth components in the active layer, organic photovoltaic devices with tandem structures can also be constructed to broaden the absorption spectrum and reduce energy loss.1342,343] More significantly, tandem PSCs are able to overcome the obstacle of low light utilization caused by the thickness limitation of single junction active layer. According to the superposition modes of the two subcells, the tandem PSCs can be divided to conventional series tandem devices, inverted series tandem devices and parallel tandem devices, as shown in Figure 19a-c. The $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the series structure device is approximately equal to the sum of each subcell, but the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ is limited by the minimum value of the subcells. Similarly, the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of the parallel structure device is the sum of the subcells, and the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ is also limited to the lowest value of the single-junction subcells.[344] However, due to the lack of more efficient and convenient fabrication process for parallel devices compared with series devices, articles related to high-performance devices are mainly reported based on series tandem structures.343,345] In series tandem devices, the interconnection layer (ICL) consists of an electron transport layer (ETL) and a hole transport layer (HTL), which provides an effective carrier recombination region to collect and transport electrons and holes.346] The following factors need to be considered in the selection and fabrication of the ICL: the energy level matching of the front and back cells, optical transparency, the protection of the deposition process to the front subcell.342,347]

Recently, Hou et al.21l reported a highly efficient tandem PSC constructed by an ICL composed of electron beam evaporated $\mathrm { T i O } _ { \mathrm { x } }$ (e $\left. \mathrm { T i O _ { x } } \right)$ and PEDOT:PSS. In addition, the fabrication processes of the devices need to be simplified to further promote their commercialization.

# 3.4. Stable Vertical Morphology via Sequential Deposition

The build-in electric field strength in organic semiconductors is not strong enough to separate excitons into free electrons and holes, so excitons must be diffused to the donor/acceptor interfaces to dissociate to free charges and then be collected by cathodes and anodes respectively.[353] The diffusion length of excitons is about $1 0 { - } 2 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , and the separation mainly occurs at the interface. In 1995, Heeger et al.10] fabricated solution processed PSCs with a blend casting active layer of bulk heterojunction (BHJ) structure for the first time. The interpenetrating network structure is formed by donor and acceptor in blend film, and the D/A interfaces exist in the whole active layer bulk to ensure sufficient exciton dissociation efficiency. However, once the donor and acceptor are blended, the interfaces are already formed, so it is difficult to accurately control the phase separation and the film microstructure from the device fabrication.1354] Besides, because of the large compatibility differences of high photovoltaic performance polymer donor and NFAs, the interpenetrating network of BHJ films are metastable structure for which donor and acceptor trend to form large self-aggregations that affects the stability of devices. Therefore, based on the consideration of ameliorating the above drawbacks, a series of PSCs with layer-by-layer (LBL)/quasi-planar heterojunction (Q-PHJ) structure prepared by sequential deposition (SD, also named as two-step process) method arises at the historic moment.227,228] At present, the highest efficiency of the devices fabricated by SD method has been more than $1 9 \%$ [216,355-357] The fabrication process of device by SD method is shown in Figure 20a, and the schematic of the photovoltaic physic processes and the morphology along the vertical direction of the BHJ and pseudo-bilayer films are shown in Figure 20b,c. As can be seen, because of the longer exciton diffusion length and shorter charge transport path, exciton dissociation, charge extraction are effectively enhanced.[358]

Compared with the BHJ structure, the obvious advantages of the LBL structure are vertical phase separation structure with go0d stability.[360-364] He et al.365] demonstrated that the D18/BTIC-BO4Cl film constructed by SD method is able to provide sufficient exciton dissociation and diffusion distance, besides, it possesses double channels to generate electrons and holes transport independently. Ultimately, the PCE of the device based on D18/BTIC-BO4Cl increased from $1 4 . 0 5 \%$ of BHJ to $1 7 . 6 0 \%$ of Q-BHJ. Jen et al.[358] reported that the out-of-plane $\pi - \pi$ stacking and the crystallinity of the PM6/N3 films with pseudobilayer (PB) structures are simultaneously improved, and displayed more regular domains, as shown in Figure 21a,b. For instance, the PL spectra of the front- or back-side of the BHJ films show almost no difference, which indicates the donor domains and acceptor domains are well blended. Expressly, in PB films, the PL signal of PM6 domain $( \approx 6 0 0 - 8 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m } )$ can be observed under back-side excitation, but disappear under frontside excitation, therefore, it is reasonable to believe the vertical phase separation structure formed. As a result, PB structure PSCs based on $\mathrm { P M } 6 / \mathrm { N } 3 { : } \mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ achieved a highest reported PCE of $1 7 . 4 2 \%$ at that time. Chen and coworkers[366] pointed out that LBL devices have a finer nanometer phase separation interpenetrating network than the BHJ devices, and proposed a swelling-intercalation-phase separation (SIPS) model to explain the morphology evolution of LBL devices during the sequential deposition, as shown in Figure 21c.

Significantly, the big obstacle of fabricating LBL devices by SD is to overcome the scouring of acceptor solvents to donor films. Wu et al.367] synthesized naphthalenothiophene imide (NTI)-based polymer donors with different solubility in chloroform by alkyl regulation strategy. Comparatively, it is more convenient and common to avoid scouring the donor films by selecting the solvents of the donor and acceptor.1368,369] Huang et al.1370] utilized an eco-friendly solvent protection (ESP) strategy to construct high efficiency LBL PSCs, which spincoating a protective solvent before spin-coating acceptor solution, as shown in Figure 21d. The protection factor $( \delta )$ was developed to assess the effect of protective solvent. Figure 21e,f illustrated eight solvents' contact angles on D18 films and the quantitative relationship between $\delta$ and PCE of D18/solvent/N3 system. As shown, as $\delta$ closes to zero, the PCE reaches the maximum of $1 7 . 5 2 \%$ with $n$ -octane as the protective solvent. Notably,

# 4. Summary and Outlook

The emergence of ADA'DA-type FREAs greatly promoted the developments of PSCs, and also re-kindled the PSCs' commercialization. However, challenges remain in many areas, such as developing new materials, reducing costs, advanced device structures, long-term stability, green-solvent and large-scale manufacturing. For ADA'DA-type NFAs and donor materials, by efficient molecular structure regulation strategies, the light absorption range, energy levels, aggregations and orientations can be further optimized to obtain broaden light absorption, appropriate micro-morphology and reduce energy loss of devices. Apart from designing novel materials, developing LBL, ternary and tandem structure devices are meaningful research directions to promote obtaining higher and more stable photovoltaic performances. In addition, the pursuit of higher photovoltaic performances of PsCs used to leads to other problems such as toxic synthesis and processing, reduced long-term stability, which seriously restrict further commercial applications. First, the volatile halogenated solvents are very toxic and will inevitably endanger the workers. It is still a challenge to fabricate high-performance devices with green solvents, and preparing materials that are easy to synthesize and dissolved in green solvents can be adopted cooperatively to optimize the morphology of films prepared by green solvents. Second, it is urgent to solve the long-term stability of the device under indoor and outdoor conditions (oxygen, moisture, heat, and illumination), including the stability of the material and the stability of the film morphology. These require in-depth explorations of the degradation mechanism of the devices and the development of more reliable encapsulation technology. In addition, all-polymer PSCs are an effective solution of obtaining high stability and remarkable efficiency at present.

However, in order to realize a great promotion for the PSCs' commercialization, the following issues should be emphasized, which are the further research direction of PSCs:

1. Develop high-performance materials that are insensitive to thickness, which can be adapted to future large-scale device fabrication. For current industry fabrication techniques, precisely controlling the thickness of every functional layer are of great challenge, so thickness-insensitive materials are necessary.   
2. Develop low-cost material systems with simple structures or synthesis processes without sacrificing the device efficiency. Although the novel ADA'DA-type non-fullerene acceptors can greatly improve the efficiency, they will inevitably introduce complex synthetic steps. On the other hand, the recently emerging non-fused ring electron acceptors with low synthesis cost and decent device efficiency achieved by non-covalent intermolecular conformational locking strategy, such as S...O and S . . . F.[383] It is meaningful to find a balance between highperformance ADA'DA-type NFAs and low-cost non-fused ring electron acceptors may be the key of the next stage.384-386]   
3. Break the existing mechanisms or develop new design concepts. Because of the limitation of existing mechanisms and materials concepts, the photovoltaic performances of devices have almost reached to their theory limit. To develop more excellent material systems and obtain big enhancement of PCE, it is of great necessity to break the existing mechanisms or materials design concepts.

Therefore, with the deeper understanding of the relationship of structure-property of PSCs, more efficient materials will be developed to obtain higher PCEs to satisfy the requirements of commercialization. Besides, with the research interests focus on stability, green solvent fabrication, large area fabrication, reducing fabrication cost, and so on, the obstacles in front of PsCs commercialization will be solved one by one in the near future.
