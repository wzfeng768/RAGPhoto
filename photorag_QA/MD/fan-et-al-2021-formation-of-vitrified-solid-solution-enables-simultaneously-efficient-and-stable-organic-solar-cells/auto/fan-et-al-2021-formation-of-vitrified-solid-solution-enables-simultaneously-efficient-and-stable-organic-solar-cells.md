ABSTRACT: Employing a ternary blend as absorber is an ingenious tactic to achieve broad spectral coverage in single-junction organic photovoltaics. The incorporation of a second acceptor with relatively lower electron affinity (EA) is important for attaining increased opencircuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ of ternary devices. However, the common way in achieving a lower EA through tailoring the skeletons may affect the miscibility between acceptors, which inherently restrict the dependence of $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ on compositions. Herein, we developed a low-EA acceptor through side-chain engineering to ensure similarity with another ultranarrow-bandgap acceptor, enabling a good miscibility between the two species and formation of a vitrified solid solution over a large range of compositions. As a result, continuously increased $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ is realized, while the other parameters are less composition-sensitive. Besides, the vitrification facilitates intimate intermolecular interactions in ternary blends, suppressing thermall induced crystalization. Ultimately, increased photovoltaic effciency and thermal stability are simultaneously achieved for the resulting ternary devices.

However, attention should be paid to the miscibility among components when designing NFAs with enlarged $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ especially in the $\mathrm { D } { : } \mathrm { A _ { 1 } } { : } \mathrm { A _ { 2 } }$ ternary systems, as a highly miscible model (also denoted as solid solution or electronic alloy) often sees a strong dependence of device $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ on ratios of components.12-14 Such a miscible ternary system can also be regarded as a intrinsically pseudobinary system, where the introduction of a third component would cause a more intimate mixing of the active components to improve the longterm stability of resulting devices against external perturbance.1s In order to ensure a good miscibility between two acceptors, a high degree of similarities in their chemical structures should be maintained.16 Therefore, the efforts in tuning the EA and $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of NFAs can be focused on modifying the side chains while slightly alternating the $\pi$ skeleton to minimize the impacts that may undermine the miscibility.

As a family of highly efficient NFAs, Y6 and its derivatives possess typical molecular packing modes with all the pendent chains filling in the structure cavities.17,18 A longer side chain in such acceptors may twist the molecular structure to reduce the degree of molecular frontier orbital delocalization to broaden the energy gap between EA and ionization energy (IE),l9 which leads to the lower EA needed in the pseudobinary systems. Besides, extension of branched alkyl chains that were viewed as hindering for molecular packing can now be used to enhance molecular interactions and structural order in these material systems20,21 However, the enhanced molecular interactions may create a negative effect in forming oversized phase separation to deteriorate the long-term morphological stability against photo- or thermo-stresses.2,23

In this work, we designed and synthesized an acceptor, BT4BO, with four prolonged pendent chains (Figure 1a), which has a distinctly reduced EA compared to the ultranarrowbandgap acceptor CHio07 while maintaining an extremely high miscibility between the two. A continuously increased $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ is realized in the ternary system based on a wide-bandgap donor PTzBI-dF (Figure 1d), while the short-circuit current density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \right)$ and fill factor are less sensitive to the ternary ratios, ultimately affording an optimal PCE of $1 7 . 8 0 \%$ . More importantly, we demonstrated that blending of these two compatible NFAs can form a vitrified solid solution over a largely varied ratios of components. This vitrification creates an intimately mixed nanomorphology in the ternary blends, suppressing the thermally induced crystallization of each single acceptor and freezing the finely divided phases during the aging process. Ultimately, simultaneously increased PCE and thermal stability are achieved in the developed ternary OSCs.

The synthetic procedure of BT-4BO containing four 2- butyloctyl (BO) branched side chains on the fused $\pi$ core was elaborated in the Supporting Information (SI). The terminal BO chain was initially incorporated onto the thienothiophene, which was then coupled with dinitrobenzothiadiazole before the Cadogan cyclization and introduction of another two BO chains onto the formed pyrrole rings (Scheme S1 in

Supporting Information). The nuclear magnetic resonance spectrometry (NMR, Figures S1 and S2) and matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF-MS, Figure S3) confirmed the structure of the acceptor. In contrast to the selenium-substitution strategy (CH1007, Figure 1a) that can narrow the optical bandgap $( \dot { E _ { \mathrm { g } } } )$ relative to $\Upsilon \check { 6 } , ^ { 2 4 }$ the introduction of four BO side chains blueshifts the absorption onset to $8 7 5 \ \mathrm { n m }$ forming a good spectral coverage with CH1007 (Figure 1b). A large bathochromic shift of $9 3 \ \mathrm { n m }$ from solution to film indicates good aggregation of BT-4BO molecules in solid state.

Since the absorption profile of blends is not the simple superposition of that from each component, we surmise these two acceptors might have good compatibility, which forms a solid solution with unique photoelectrical properties. Contact angle measurements (Figure S4) were performed to verify this hypothesis, with the surface energy $( \gamma )$ containing both dispersive $\mathsf { \bar { \Psi } } ( \gamma ^ { \mathrm { d } } )$ and polar $\left( \gamma ^ { \mathtt { p } } \right)$ parts extracted from the Owens methodology (Table S1). The donor-acceptor interfacial energy $\gamma _ { \mathrm { D - A } }$ and FloryHuggins interaction parameter $\chi _ { \mathrm { D - A } }$ that can both evaluate the miscibility between donor and acceptors were calculated from the equation of yp-A=V+YA-, $\begin{array} { r } { \gamma _ { \mathrm { { D - A } } } = \gamma _ { \mathrm { { D } } } + \gamma _ { \mathrm { { A } } } - \frac { 4 \gamma _ { \mathrm { { D } } } ^ { d } \gamma _ { \mathrm { { A } } } ^ { d } } { \gamma _ { \mathrm { { D } } } ^ { d } + \gamma _ { \mathrm { { A } } } ^ { d } } - \frac { 4 \gamma _ { \mathrm { { D } } } ^ { p } \gamma _ { \mathrm { { A } } } ^ { p } } { \gamma _ { \mathrm { { D } } } ^ { p } + \gamma _ { \mathrm { { A } } } ^ { p } } } \end{array}$ and $\chi _ { \mathrm { D - A } } = K \big ( \sqrt { \gamma _ { \mathrm { D } } } ~ - ~ \sqrt { \gamma _ { \mathrm { A } } } \big ) ^ { 2 }$ , respectively, where $\gamma _ { \mathrm { D } }$ and $\gamma _ { \mathrm { A } }$ are surface energy of the pure materials and $K$ is a positive constant.25,26

Donor-acceptor blends with a low $\gamma _ { \mathrm { D - A } }$ could have a minimized energy barrier to create a well-mixed network, while a low $\chi$ value also signifies an intimate mixing of two materials. From Figure 1d, we note PTzBI-dF:CH1007 has the lowest $\gamma _ { \mathrm { D - A } }$ of $0 . 4 6 8 ~ \mathrm { m N ~ m ^ { - 1 } }$ , but the highest $\chi _ { \mathrm { D - A } }$ of $0 . 1 6 4 ~ K .$ Such contradiction is also observed for PTzBI-dF:BT-4BO, showing the highest $\gamma _ { \mathrm { D - A } }$ of $0 . 5 2 1 ~ \mathrm { m N ~ m ^ { - 1 } }$ and the smallest $\chi _ { \mathrm { D - A } }$ of $0 . 0 3 5 \ K$ (Table S1). Nevertheless, all three PTzBI- $\scriptstyle { \mathcal { d } } \mathrm { { F } }$ :mixedacceptor blends show a low $\chi _ { \mathrm { D - A } }$ of ${ \sim } 0 . 0 5 ~ K ,$ while maintaining a medium $\gamma _ { \mathrm { D - A } }$ value, suggesting that the mixed acceptors have better compatibility with the donor compared to the neat ones.

Since the molecular energetics are important in determining the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of ternary OSCs, we use both ambient pressure photoemission spectrometry (APS) and cyclic voltammetry (CV) to evaluate the energy levels of materials involved in this study. The ionization energy (IE) determined by APS shows a similar trend as that from CV, while the APS method gives a lower IE than CV for all the materials especially the donors (Figure 2d, Table S2, PM6 is displayed in ref 27). Therefore, we observe an IE offset ${ \tt > } 0 . 3 \ { \tt e V }$ between donor and acceptor from APs results, which is favorable for efficient hole transfer.28 Compared with the commonly used donor PM6 (structure shown in Figure S5a), the donor PTzBI-dF has a higher IE ( $\mathrm { ~ \ ' ~ } \Delta \ = \ \sim 0 . 1 \ \mathrm { { \ e V } } )$ from both APS and CV results (Figures S5b, and S6b), suggesting an inherently higher $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ for PTzBI-dF-based devices than that of PM6-counterparts when coupling with an identical acceptor.

For the acceptors, neat BT-4BO exhibits a higher IE of 5.49 eV than that of $5 . 4 4 \mathrm { e V }$ for CH1007 (Figure 2a) and meantime a lower EA of $3 . 8 5 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ relative to $3 . 9 0 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for the latter (Figure 2c). These results are consistent with the larger optical bandgap of BT-4BO (Figure 1b). When BT-4BO and CH1007 were blended together with varied ratios, the mixed acceptors show continuously shifted IE and EA dependent on the components (Figure $^ { 2 \mathrm { b } , \mathrm { c } } ,$ , indicative of an effective electronic interaction between these highly miscible acceptors.29 The formation of electronic alloy enables the optimization of light harvest ability, hole transfer efficiency, and the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of derived devices. Therefore, an improved PCE is anticipated for the optimized ternary solar cells.

The miscibility between BT-4BO and CH1007 can also influence the molecular interactions and morphology nanostructures. Reflected ellipsometry with a temperature-controlled function (Figure S7) was employed to investigate the glass transition of materials as film samples (Figure S8), given that the film thickness $( d )$ and refractive index $\bar { ( \boldsymbol { n } ) }$ will mutate in the glass transition process.30 The temperature-dependent $d$ and $n$ for both polymer donors and small-molecule acceptors were recorded on slow cooling ( $1 ~ \mathrm { ~ K ~ } \operatorname* { m i n } ^ { - 1 } )$ , while the preheating was quickly performed to exclude the perturbance factors such as moisture absorption. For the soft polymers, we observe distinctly double linear regions (Figure S9), with the intersection of linear fitting for each region as the glass transition temperature $( T _ { \mathrm { g } } )$ .The consistency of $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ values obtained from $d - T$ and $n { - } T$ characteristics demonstrates the reliability of these results.

For the neat acceptors, the rigid nature of these fused small molecules makes the glass transition less discernible especially for BT-4BO (Figure 2f). However, the mixed acceptors of CH1007:BT-4BO (0.5:0.6) show clear glass transitions in both $n { - } T$ (Figure $2 \mathbf { g } ,$ and $d - T$ (Figure S10) plots, giving a comparable $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ . This value is close to that of BT4BO $T _ { \mathrm { g } } = 1 1 { \overset { \circ } { 9 } } ^ { \circ } \mathbf { C } )$ and slightly higher than that of $1 1 4 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for CH1o07 (Figure 2e), without considering the effect of film thickness. These results strongly suggest a formation of vitrified solid solution for the acceptor mixture, which can be further proven by differential scanning calorimetry (DsC) of different acceptors as the bulk state (Figure 2h). Neat CH1007 and BT4BO show a sharp melting peak in the frst heating, corresponding to melting temperatures $\left( T _ { \mathrm { m } } \right)$ of 264 and 316 ${ } ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ respectively. Note the decomposition temperature $\left( T _ { \mathrm { d } } \right)$ is over $3 3 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for all the acceptors (Figure S11). For the CH1007:BT-4BO mixture, only a single broad melting peak can be noticed, and the melting enthalpy is significantly decreased, conforming to the characteristics of vitrified alloy.

Grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) was used to reveal the influence of blending on the molecular packing of acceptors. The neat acceptors of CH1o07 and BT4BO show significantly different molecule packing characters as indicated by the 2D patterns. Similar to Y6, CH1007 shows intense (110) and (111) reflections at 0.31 and $0 . 3 9 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ in the in-plane $\left( \mathrm { I P } \right)$ direction (Figure 3a, Figure S12).31 BT-4BO exhibits an array of scattering spots appearing at the low. $q$ range $( 0 . 2 \mathrm { - } 1 . 0 \ \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 } )$ , corresponding to high-order interferences from the unit cell (Figure 3b). However, such scattering spots of BT-4BO are not observed in CH1007:BT-4BO blends with various weight ratios of 0.8:0.3, 0.5:0.6, and 0.3:0.8 (Figure 3c-e). Instead, the scattering signals of the blends are dominated by CHio07 (Figure 3g). Therefore, the crystallization of BT-4BO was substantially disrupted by CH1007 over the drying process of the blends, while CH1007 preserved the crystalline nature. Besides, both (110) and (111) peak areas of the acceptor blends decrease compared with neat CH1007 (Figure 3f, Table S3), indicating both acceptors are vitrified in the blending, validating the DsC trace of the CH1007:BT-4BO blend with one broad melting peak observed (Figure 2h).

To elucidate the effect of solid solution formation on the photovoltaic performance, these blended acceptors were used to match with a wide-bandgap polymer donor, PTzBI-dF. The photovoltaic parameters for OsCs based on different ratios of PTzBI-dF:CH1007:BT-4BO were summarized in Table 1 with relevant $J { - } V$ characteristics depicted in Figure 4a. The two host binary devices, PTzBI-dF:CH1007 (1:1.1) and PTzBIdF:BT-4BO (1:1.1), give a comparable power conversion efficiency (PCE) of ${ \sim } 1 6 . 5 \%$ . A modest $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 3 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ and a decent $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 7 . 0 2 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for the former are contrast to situation in the latter device, where a high $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 9 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ is achieved but with a low $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 4 . 5 6 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { \bar { c } m } ^ { - 2 }$ due to the lack of near-infrared (NIR) light harvesting (Figure S13a). Besides, BT-4BO devices show a slightly higher fill factor (FF) of $7 5 . 7 8 \%$ than that of CH1007 counterparts $\left( \mathrm { F F } = 7 3 . 3 7 \% \right)$ which might be partially attributed to the highly ordered crystalline nature of BT-4BO.

For all the devices, we observe gradually elevated $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ with increased BT-4BO content, while the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF of three ternary devices are less dependent on the compositions (Figure 4b). Therefore, all the ternary devices show improved PCE than the binary ones, with the champion efficiency of $1 7 . 8 0 \%$ achieved for devices with a blend ratio of 1:0.3:0.8. The change in $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ is reasonable given the shifting in the EAs of alloyed acceptors (Figure 2d). The external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra rationalize the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of the ternary devices. Taking the 1:0.5:0.6 devices as an example, although a slightly narrowed EQE response is presented relative to that of the 1:1.1:0 devices, the spectrum valley centered at $7 1 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ shown in the latter is nicely covered (Figure 4c, Figure S13b), thus yielding a comparable $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ value of $2 6 . 7 3 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { - 2 }$

To figure out where the high $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF in ternary devices of the PTzBI-dF system stem from, the steady-state photoluminescence (PL) of PTzBI- $d \mathrm { F }$ was measured and compared with the absorption of blended acceptors. We found in Figure 4d a fairly large overlap between the donor $\mathrm { P L }$ and the acceptor absorption, which can trigger an efficient Forster resonance energy transfer (FRET) from $\mathrm { P T z B I - } d \mathrm { F }$ to acceptors.2\* An intimate donor-acceptor mixing benefiting from the vitrification of acceptors would facilitate the hole transfer following the ultrafast FRET process, thus enhancing the internal quantum efficiency of the ternary devices. Through exponential ftting of the EQE tail measured by Fouriertransform photocurrent spectrometry (FTPs),32 we deduced the energetic disorder $E _ { \mathrm { U } }$ of both binary (1:1.1:0 and 1:0:1.1) and ternary (1:0.3:0.8) devices based on the PTzBI-dF system. From Figure S13c, a slightly higher $E _ { \mathrm { U } }$ of $2 4 . 2 \pm 0 . 3 ~ \mathrm { m e V }$ was observed for the BT-4BO binary, compared to $2 3 . 0 \pm 0 . 1 \ \mathrm { m e V }$ for the CH1007 counterpart, agreeing with the tiny increase of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ loss in the BT-4BO device ( $_ { 0 . 5 3 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ versus $0 . 5 1 ~ \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for CH1007 device). The 1:0.3:0.8 ternary device shows a slightly decreased $E _ { \mathrm { U } }$ of $2 3 . 5 \pm 0 . 1 ~ \mathrm { m e V }$ probably owing to the good compatibility of ternary blend.33

The different morphologies for PTzBI-dF-based blend films can be used to interpret the nanostructure-property relationships. From the surface profiles revealed by atomic force microscopy (AFM), one notes a large feature size for PTzBIdF:CH1007 blend with a high roughness of $3 . 5 2 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure Sa). The coarse surface is largely mitigated in the PTzBIdF:BT-4BO blend flm, while a unique strip structure with remarkably long-range ordering shows up (Figure 5b). Such surface nanostructures of the binary blends also extend to the bulk of respective films (Figure 5d,e), as shown by transmission electron microscopy (TEM). In contrast to both binaries, the blending of acceptors quenches the ternary films into fine structures with small grains, regardless of the blending ratios (Figure 5c, Figure S14). The 1:0.3:0.8 blend film exhibits the smoothest surface and smallest feature size among three ternaries. The fine phase-separated structure can only be detected at the scale of tens of nanometers by TEM (inset of Figure 5f). The formation of this morphology might be related to the vitrification of the acceptor mixture as discussed above. Such a morphology would eliminate the pinholes, overcoming the recombination pathways from structure traps. However, the loss of long-range ordering is in principle unfavorable for the charge transport. Therefore, the ternary devices only acquire a fill factor comparable to that of the binary one based on PTzBI-dF:BT-4BO.

Stability of both binary and ternary devices were investigated to correlate with their film properties. The maximum-power point (MPP) tracking was performed to give the steady-state efficiency of conventional devices by using the constant voltage approach. The PCE of the PTzBI-dF:CH1007 device decays by $6 . 3 \%$ after MPP tracking for $4 0 \ \mathrm { \ m i n }$ (Figure 6a). The PTzBI-dF:BT-4BO device has better stability against MPP tracking with PCE decreased by only $3 . 7 \%$ in the same duration. The ternary device of 1:0.3:0.8 shows a stability very close to that of BT-4BO-binary, possibly due to the smooth surface of BT-4BO-based active layers that can eliminate the contact loss. Inverted OSCs based on different active layers were fabricated to test the long-term stability against thermal stress. The gradient temperature was employed to reveal the device stability at different temperature stages. For all devices (Figure $6 \mathrm { b - d } )$ , thermal aging at $8 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ led to much quicker decay of photovoltaic parameters than that at $6 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } .$ .Besides, the fill factor has the biggest drop ( $\mathrm { \Delta } V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ shows slight decrease), while the $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ is rather robust. Compared to the severe decay of PCE by ${ \sim } 4 0 \%$ and ${ \sim } 4 7 \%$ for CH1007- and BT-4BO-based binary devices, respectively, the 1:0.3:0.8 ternary device retains $8 4 . 4 \%$ of its original PCE after being thermally annealed for $2 6 4 \ \mathrm { \textrm { h } }$ This decent stability of ternary devices is mainly ascribed to the slower decrease of FF especially in the $6 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ region, where less than $4 \%$ decay of FF and PCE were observed within $^ { 1 2 0 \mathrm { h } }$ (Figure 6d). The much more robust FF of ternary devices rationalizes the formation of a vitrified solid solution between the incorporated highly miscible acceptors.

In summary, a nonfullerene acceptor, BT-4BO, with lower electron affinity was developed by side-chain engineering. The maintained skeleton similarities with another ultranarrowbandgap acceptor, CHio07, enable an excellent miscibility between the two species. The highly miscible nature of CH1o07:BT-4BO blends contributes to the formation of a vitrified solid solution over largely varied components, as demonstrated by multiple thermal, electrical, and morphological characterizations. A continuously increased $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ is realized in the ternary system based on a wide-bandgap donor PTzBI-dF, while the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and fill factor are less sensitive to the ternary ratios, ultimately affording an optimal efficiency of $1 7 . 8 0 \%$ . More importantly, we demonstrated that the vitrification creates an intimate mixing of the ternary blends, which could suppress the thermally induced crystallization and freeze the finely divided phases in the aging process. Ultimately, a simultaneously increased photovoltaic efficiency and thermal stability are achieved in the resulting ternary solar cells.
