# Charge Carrier Dynamics in Planar Heterojunction Organic Solar Cells

Yexiao Huang, Sein Chung, Safakath Karuthedath,\* Catherine S. P. De Castro, Hua Tang, Minyoung Jeong, Shirong Lu, Kilwon Cho,\* Frederic Laquai, and Zhipeng Kan\*

The ionization energy (IE) offset of a donor-acceptor pair provides the driving force for hole transfer and subsequent free charge carrier generation in low-bandgap nonfullerene organic solar cells (OsCs). However, the interfacial energetic landscape in bulk heterojunction OsCs is determined by the materials' electronic structure and intermolecular interactions at the donor/acceptor interface, causing Iocal energy-level shifts and disorder. Herein, the impact of the IE offset on the charge transfer efficiency and charge carrier dynamics is systematically evaluated by characterizing PM6/ITIC, PM6/IT-2Cl, and PM6/IT-4CI planar heterojunction (PHJ) solar cells. Ultrafast spectroscopy and time-resolved charge carrier density measurements reveal that an IE offset of about ${ \approx } 0 . 5$ eV leads to efficient hole transfer and subsequent free charge generation. Furthermore, bimolecular charge recombination and consequently triplet generation are significantly reduced in systems with high IE offset. This work underlines the importance of sizeable donor-acceptor IE offsets in PHJ nonfullerene OSCs as critical for high-efficiency donor/acceptor material and device design.

# 1. Introduction

Organic solar cells (OsCs) are composed of organic semiconductors with diverse chemical structures, tunable energy levels, and low synthesis cost, providing unique device properties such as lightweight, flexibility, semitransparency, and portability.1-3] With the emergence of nonfullerene acceptors (NFAs), the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of single-junction OSCs have improved rapidly, and recently more than 19% was achieved.1410]

Due to the excitonic nature of the primary photoexcited states in organic semiconductors and their low charge carrier mobility, the conversion of singlet excitons to free charge carriers and subsequent extraction of free charge carriers is often accompanied by significant losses. In donor:fullerene OsCs, the donor material is the main photon absorber, and an electron affinity (EA) offset of about $0 . 3 \mathrm { e V }$ is required to guarantee efficient charge transfer (C1).[11-13] In contrast, when NFAs are used, both donor and acceptor absorb photons more or less the same extent; therefore both photoinduced electron transfer and hole transfer can occur. With an improved understanding of the critical role of energy and CT processes in low-bandgap nonfullerene OsCs, it was shown that the CT depends largely on the IE offset, rendering the EA offset less important.[14]

In fact, Yang et al. analyzed the relationship between the IE offset and exciton dissociation efficiency. They prepared OSCs with ITIC, IEIcO, and their derivatives with varying highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels paired with PBDB-T-F as donor polymer. They observed that the exciton dissociation process is severely hampered when the IE offset approaches zero. In contrast, larger IE offsets significantly increased the exciton dissociation efficiency.15] Likewise, Li et al. tested a set of organic semiconductors with IE offsets from $- 0 . 0 5$ to $0 . 2 1 \mathrm { e V } .$ They concluded that close-to-zero HOMO offsets are sufficient for hole transfer, whereas a negative IE offset caused severe charge recombination.[16] Efficient OSCs with minimal donor/acceptor offsets have been reported, but often the redox potentials were determined by cyclic voltammetry (CV).1619] Because the measurements were usually performed in a solution of $0 . 1 \mathrm { m o l } \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { n } { \cdot } \mathrm { B u } _ { 4 } \mathrm { N P F } _ { 6 } ,$ the estimated potentials are not the same as those expected for thin films in air and vacuum. The values often deviate from those obtained by other techniques (measured on thin films), including those from photoelectron spectroscopy in air (PESA) and ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS). The differences in results obtained by these techniques on state-of-the-art donor:NFA systems have recently been addressed by Bertrandie et al. and Karuthedath et al.20,21 Briefly, it appears that the NFA's quadrupole moment is a very important parameter impacting the energetic landscape at the donor-acceptor interface and can lead to a situation where exciton-to-CT-state conversion in low-IE offset systems is hampered in NFA-based systems. Consequently, it was suggested that efficient (near-unity) free charge generation in NFA-BHJ OSCs requires sizeable IE offsets of ${ \approx } 0 . 5 \mathrm { e V }$

Besides shifts in the energy levels, energetic disorder at the donor:acceptor (D:A) interface in a bulk heterojunction impacts the energetic landscape and can explain, in part, energy-level deviation contradictory findings [16,21-23] Sean et al. reported that intermolecular electronic coupling in organic semiconductors can cause energetic offsets of up to $0 . 3 5 \mathrm { e V }$ [24] These findings imply that energy-level shifts at the interface primarily result from the D:A BHJ structure. In other words, IE values obtained for neat films are not representative of the local energetics in a BHJ film.22,25] Contrary to the BHJ structure, energy-level bending was not observed in planar heterojunction (PHJ) films pre pared by sequential deposition of donor and acceptor using orthogonal solvents.[26] The PCE of PHJ OSCs improved significantly with the emergence of Y-type NFAs, and recently PCEs of $1 6 \%$ have been demonstrated with halogen-free solvents.27,28] Further improvements of the PCE still require a better understanding of the impact of energy-level offsets on charge generation and recombination in PHJ OSCs.

In this work, we investigated the role of the IE offset on the generation and recombination of charge carriers at the interface of a PHJ OSC. Devices with PHJ active layers were fabricated with ITIC and its chlorinated derivatives, IT-2Cl and IT-4Cl, as acceptors and the polymer donor PM6. We chose these acceptors since ITIC is a prototypical NFA routinely yielding PCEs over $10 \%$ and its energetics can be easily tuned by substituting the terminal functional groups, such as halogens, as done in many high-efficiency NFAs today.129,30] Changing the number of chlorine atoms attached to the acceptor, PM6:NFA IE offsets of 0.45, 0.51, and $0 . 5 5 \mathrm { e V }$ were achieved, comparable to the recently reported offset values.20] Interestingly, the performance of PHJ OsCs was better than that of the corresponding BHJ OSCs. In particular, the open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ of the PHJ OSCs was higher $( { \approx } 2 0 \mathrm { m e V } )$ compared to that of BHJ OSCs. Time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) and transient absorption spectroscopy (TAS) showed that both the acceptor-to-donor hole transfer efficiency (exciton-to-CT-state conversion) and the subsequent free charge generation yield increased with increasing IE offsets. Furthermore, the TRPL experiments showed a PL quenching efficiency (PLQE) of $8 0 . 2 \%$ $7 6 . 4 \%$ and $7 2 . 1 \%$ when the acceptor was selectively photoexcited in PM6/IT-4Cl, PM6/IT-2Cl, and PM6/ITIC, respectively. In NFA-based systems, PLQE is a measure of the hole transfer efficiency, and the PLQE values are in line with the performance differences of the PHJ devices. TAS measurements showed that the subsequent free charge generation efficiency was $9 7 \%$ for PM6/IT-4Cl, $89 \%$ for PM6/IT-2Cl, and $6 6 \%$ for PM6/ITIC. Interestingly, with increasing the IE offset, the bimolecular recombination rate reduced, which was confirmed by both TAS and charge extraction by linearly increasing voltage (CELIV). We conclude that in PHJ an IE offset of ${ \approx } 0 . 5 \mathrm { e V }$ is required for efficient exciton-to-CT-state conversion similar to BHJ OSCs.

Next, we studied exciton dissociation. Exciton dissociation and electron transfer from these donor to these acceptors have previously been discussed in BHJ materials[11,351, hence, we focused on the acceptor-to-donor transfer processes. To this end, we analyzed the TRPL spectroscopy data (Figure 2d-f and S4, Supporting Information). The hole transfer efficiency was determined from the lifetime quenching of the acceptor singlet exciton in the bilayer films relative to the neat acceptor films.[35] When the acceptors are photoexcited, the singlet excitons can dissociate into charges via hole transfer from the NFA to the PM6. The photoluminescence quenching (PLQ) efficiency and corresponding hole transfer efficiency were calculated; details are presented in the Section S4, Supporting Information. The inverse rate constant and other fitting parameters are shown in Table S3, Supporting Information. PLQ efficiencies of $8 0 . 4 \%$ $7 6 . 4 \%$ , and $7 3 . 1 \%$ were attained for PM6/IT-4Cl, PM6/IT-2Cl, and PM6/ITIC, respectively. These results suggest that the larger the IE offset, the better the PL quenching and, thus, the hole transfer efficiency. Having determined the singlet-exciton quenching efficiency, we discuss next the exciton dissociation into charges and subsequent charge carrier dynamics determined by ultrafast TAS. Figure 3a-c shows the picosecond-nanosecond (ps-ns) TA spectra of PHJ films of (a) PM6/IT-4Cl, (b) PM6/IT-2Cl, and (c) PM6/ ITIC after exciting at $6 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ with a ${ \approx } 1 2 0$ fs laser pulses. The photoinduced absorption (PA) band centered at $1 . 3 5 \mathrm { e V }$ was assigned to singlet-exciton-induced absorption of acceptor singlets, while the PA of PM6 overlapped with the photobleach (PB) of the acceptor. TA spectra of neat PM6 and acceptor films are shown in Figure S5, Supporting Information. As recently reported for PM6:ITIC derivatives, fast Forster resonance energy transfer (FRET) from PM6 to the acceptor outweighs electron transfer, and the subsequent hole transfer process becomes the primary charge generation channel.[21] Hole transfer from the acceptor to PM6 caused the delayed rise of the PB of PM6. The shaded areas in Figure 3a-c indicate the presence of long-lived states (charge carriers) in all three PHJ films. However, compared with bilayer films PM6/IT-IC or PM6/IT-2Cl, more carriers are observed in PM6/IT-4Cl, demonstrating a more efficient charge generation in PM6/IT-4Cl.

Next, we discuss the ps-ns carrier recombination in these systems. Figure 3d shows that the decay of the charge carrier population is different for PM6/IT-4Cl, it exhibits the slowest decay (blue curves). Furthermore, we observed triplet generation in ITIC and IT-2Cl PHJ films, yet not in PM6/IT-4Cl. We normalized the TA spectra to the PB band maximum, and for both ITIC and IT-2Cl, a low-energy band $( \approx 1 \mathrm { e V } )$ was observed at subnanosecond timescales. Notably, the TA spectra of all neat films showed the presence of triplet exciton-induced absorption at around 1eV, which is absent only in PM6/IT-4Cl bilayers. For comparison, the normalized TA spectra of neat and PHJ films are shown in Figure s5, Supporting Information. Interestingly, this suggests that the energetic landscape in PM6/ITIC and PM6/IT-2Cl films is such that it allows triplet formation, whereas this is not the case for PM6/IT-4Cl bilayers. To study the entire dynamic range of the carrier recombination and quantify the recombination dynamics, we next performed nanosecond-microsecond (ns-s) TA spectroscopy.

The $\pi - \pi$ superposition peaks of the donor (010) are located at $1 . 6 0 3 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ in the OOP direction for pure PM6 films, indicating face-to-face superposition.48] Similarly, pure IT-4Cl, IT-2Cl, and ITIC films show obvious $\pi - \pi$ stacking peaks in the OOP direction at 1.760, 1.764, and $1 . 7 2 4 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ . The detailed lattice constants and CCL are shown in Table S8, Supporting Information. To further understand the mechanism of molecular stacking, the (010) $\pi - \pi$ stacking peaks of these bilayer films were fit by multipeak Gaussian fits in the OOP direction, as shown in the Section S8, Supporting Information.49] The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of the PHJ and BHJ films are shown in Section S9, Supporting Information. Compared to PM6/ITIC and PM6/IT-2Cl systems, the $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance in PM6/ IT-4Cl films is smaller, indicating improved crystallinity, which is beneficial for intermolecular charge transport. In addition, the CCL of the bilayer film is longer than that of the BHJs (Figure S11, Supporting Information), indicating that component demixing in the BHJ is on a smaller length scale than it is in the PHJs. This is consistent with the enhanced electron and hole mobilities observed in PHJ OSCs.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we presented a systematical study of the impact of IE offset in PHJ OSCs based on ITIC derivatives having cascaded AIE when paired with PM6 as a donor polymer. We observed that hole transfer is the primary charge generation channel, and its efficiency is dictated by the IE offset. The acceptor endgroup chlorination not only improved the singlet-to-CT-state conversion but also changed the energetic landscape, in turn suppressing triplet state generation. Changes in the energetic landscape and local morphology upon acceptor chlorination reduced carrier losses by both geminate and nongeminate charge carrier recombination. In addition, more ordered donor and acceptor domains were obtained in PHJ films which led to enhanced charge carrier transport and improved carrier extraction. Our findings underline the importance of IE offsets for efficient charge generation in nonfullerene PHJ OSCs and provide guidance for designing efficient donor/acceptor combinations for OSCs.
