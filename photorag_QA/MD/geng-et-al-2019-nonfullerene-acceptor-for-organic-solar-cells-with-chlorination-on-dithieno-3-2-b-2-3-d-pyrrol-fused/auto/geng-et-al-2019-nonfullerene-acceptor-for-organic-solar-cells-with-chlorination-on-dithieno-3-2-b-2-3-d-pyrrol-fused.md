ABSTRACT: A narrow-band-gap molecular acceptor, IPIC-4Cl, featuring an indacenobis(dithieno[3,2-b:2',3'-d]pyrrol) (INP) core with 2-butyl-1-octyl side chains and chlorinated (dicyanomethylidene)-indan-1-one (IC) as electron-accepting end group, has been rationally designed as nonfullerene acceptors (NFAs) for organic solar cells (OsCs). The impact of chlorination on the acceptor unit is revealed by a comparison study with two counterpart NFAs bearing a fluorinated or nonhalogenated IC unit. The synergetic photophysical and morphological analyses reveal that PBDB-T:IPIC-4Cl blend possesses efficient exciton dissociation and charge collection integrated with higher crystallinity and optimized phase separation. Consequently, the OSCs constructed by PBDB-T:IPIC-4Cl obtain a champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of $1 3 . 4 \%$ with an extremely low energy loss of 0.51 eV. More encouragingly, we achieve a higher photovoltaic performance of $1 4 . 3 \%$ for ternary solar cells by combining an optimal amount of $\mathbf { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathbf { B M }$ with PBDB-T:IPIC-4Cl blend.

Compared to the fluorination strategy we mentioned above, the chlorinated terminal units possess a more red-shifted absorption window, enhanced crystallinity, and better device photostability, which is due to their stronger intramolecular charge-transfer (1CT) effect.17,18,36 In addition, the chemical synthesis of Cl substitutes is easier than that of F counterparts, which reduced the cost of the photovoltaic materials and made them more suitable for practical application. On the other hand, employing multiple photoactive materials to improve the absorption spectrum has been employed as a promising approach to realize high-performance organic solar cells.37 Recently, Zhu and Chen et al. reported that the use of fullerene derivative as a combinatory acceptor with polymer donor:- nonfullerene acceptor pair can be advantageous, as $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ or $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ can be functioned as good electron-transporting channel and solid processing-aid,9,38 while NFA material possesses a wide absorption window and highly tunable energy-level alignment. Via combination of these three components and the suitable blend morphology, it thus may be a judicious choice to construct high-performance organic solar cells.

In this contribution, we combined the strategy discussed above (chlorination and ternary system) and reported a highly efficient ternary system with PCE over $1 4 \%$ . Indeed, three INP-based NFAs (IPIC-4Cl, IPIC-4F, and IPIC, Figure 1a) with/without halogen-substituted terminal block (-Cl, -F, -H) were synthesized and studied systematically. Compared to IPIC, IPIC-4Cl and IPIC-4F exhibit narrower band gaps and downshifted energy levels [both the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO)]. When blended with polymer donor (PBDBT) featuring complementary absorption with our as-developed NFAs, three acceptor-derived blend flms show explicit differences in light-harvesting capacity, charge transport ability, and morphology. The IPIC-4Cl-based BHJ blend films exhibit a broad absorption range $\left( 3 0 0 - 9 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m } \right)$ and more balanced charge mobilities $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } = 1 . 2 6 )$ , resulting in higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and fill factor (FF) values than those of IPIC-4F and IPIC-based devices. Furthermore, IPIC-4Cl features an enhanced ICT effect and molecular packing in comparison with the other two counterparts. Consequently, the IPIC-4Cl-based OSCs enabled suppressed trap-assisted recombination, increased carrier density, and improved carrier generation and extraction efficiencies. Indeed, the PBDB-T:IPIC-4Cl-based binary OSCs exhibited superior efficiency of $1 3 . 4 \%$ . Upon addition of $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ functioning as the phase mediator and electrontransporting channel into PBDB-T:IPIC-4Cl blend, the OSCs show an impressive PCE of $1 4 . 3 \%$ (Tables S9 and S10 in the Supporting Information). These results strongly prove that our FREA molecular design strategy and further combination of $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ as a combinatory acceptor are effective for the construction of high-performance OSCs.

Thus, a series of FREAs based on the INP core unit with BO side chain (IPIC-4Cl, IPIC-4F, and IPIC) were successfully synthesized by Knoevenagel condensation between INP dialdehyde and different IC derivatives with $6 9 - 8 0 \%$ yields (Figure 1a). Figure 2e displays the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of the optimized OSCs. In comparison to IPIC- and IPIC-4F-based OSCs, IPIC-4Cl devices showed wider and stronger photon responses (over $6 0 \%$ of EQE value across the range $4 5 0 { - } 8 0 0 ~ \mathrm { \ n m } )$ . (3) The blend morphology was further optimized after incorporating $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ into the binary system38 (the details will be discussed below).

The further carrier extraction dynamics observation was examined by the TPC characterization. The charge mobilities were also measured for PBDB-T/ IPIC-4Cl/ $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ (1:1:0.3) ternary blend with the same method. As shown in Figure S11, the ternary blend film showed a $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ of $2 . 7 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of $2 . 6 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ ${ \mathrm { c m } } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } ,$ indicating a more balanced charge transportation due to a $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ $( \sim 1 . 0 \dot { 2 } )$ value very close to 1 in the ternary system. This more balanced hole/electron mobility in ternary blend is one of the reasons for the improvement of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and further PCE.

For better elucidation of the microstructure and molecular packing in the thin films, grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIwAXs) was performed for the neat acceptor materials and donor:acceptor blend films. The corresponding GIWAXS patterns are presented in Figure S12 and Figure 4. As depicted, IPIC-4Cl and IPIC-4F flms exhibit quite strong structure order, with more pronounced (1o0) diffraction (at $0 . 3 2 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ for IPIC-4Cl and $\mathbf { \hat { 0 . 2 9 \ A } ^ { - 1 } }$ for IPIC-4F) in the inplane (IP) orientation and strong $\pi - \pi$ stacking (at $1 . 8 2 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ for IPIC-4Cl and $1 . 9 2 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ for IPIC-4F) in the out-of-plane (OOP) direction, suggesting that halogenated acceptors have a preferred face-on orientation with respect to the substrate.45 In contrast, the IPIC pure film exhibits a broad (100) diffraction at $0 . 3 3 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ and $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak at ${ \approx } 1 . 9 5 \ \mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 } .$ indicative of its relatively weak crystallinity and disordered packing. The $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance $( d _ { \pi - \pi } )$ was evaluated to quantize the crystallinity, and we found that IPIC-4Cl neat film has the smallest $d _ { \pi - \pi }$ of $3 . 4 5 \mathrm { ~ \AA } .$ This indicates that IPIC-4Cl exhibits the strongest intermolecular aggregations, which is constant with the largest red-shift in the absorption spectrum of pure film. Upon further consideration of the blend films, the strong $\pi - \pi$ stacking peaks in the OOP direction of three blend films (located at 1.74, 1.75, and $1 . 7 7 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ corresponding to $d _ { \pi - \pi }$ of ${ \sim } 3 . 6 1$ , 3.59, and 3.54 A for IPIC-4Cl, IPIC-4F, and IPIC, respectively), demonstrating that IPIC-4Cl blend film preferred a more intense molecular packing.47 The phase separation of three blend films was further investigated by resonant soft X-ray scattering (R-SoXs) measurements. As in the $\mathrm { R } { - } \mathrm { S o X S }$ profiles shown in Figure 4e, three binary blends showed distinguishing phase separation for IPIC-4Cl, IPIC4F, and IPIC, respectively. Small domains in PBDB-T:IPIC4Cl film indicated that more D:A interfaces are produced in blend film to efficiently facilitate exciton separation,3 which contributes to the high $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ values for the binary devices. In addition to the GIWAXS and R-SoXS measurements, highresolution transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) technology were also performed to directly observe the blend morphology. As shown in Figure S13, an apparent aggregation feature of PBDB-T:IPIC-4Cl blend films was observed, and the rootmeant-square roughness (RMS) was increased to ca. $2 . 8 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ In contrast, the IPIC- and IPIC-4F-based films are featureless with lower roughness value. Such an optimized morphology of IPIC-4Cl flm is advantageous for charge generation and charge transport,54 explaining their higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF and better PCE than those of IPIC and IPIC-4F film.

In summary, we have developed three indacenobis(dithieno$[ 3 , 2 - b : 2 ^ { \prime } , 3 ^ { \prime }$ -d]pyrrol)-based NFAs with/without halogen-substituted end groups. Chlorinated NFA exhibits a narrower optical band gap of $1 . 3 2 \ \mathrm { ~ e V }$ with more intense molecular packing in comparison with fluorinated and hydrogenated substitutes, which is due to its stronger ICT effect. Indeed, the photovoltaic performance of PBDB-T:IPIC-4Cl OSCs exhibited an outstanding PCE of $1 3 . 4 \%$ with extremely low energy loss $( 0 . 5 1 ~ \mathrm { e V } )$ . The devices were further optimized by adding 30 wt $\% \mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ as a phase mediator to reveal a higher PCE of $1 4 . 3 \%$ .Our results indicate that the chlorination strategy is an efficient approach to design a novel NFA structure to realize high-performance OSCs.
