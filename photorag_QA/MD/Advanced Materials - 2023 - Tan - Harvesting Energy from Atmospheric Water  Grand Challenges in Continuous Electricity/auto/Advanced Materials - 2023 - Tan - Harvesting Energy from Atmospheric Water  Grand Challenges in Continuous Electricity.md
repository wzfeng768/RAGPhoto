# Harvesting Energy from Atmospheric Water: Grand Challenges in Continuous Electricity Generation

Jin Tan, Xiang Wang, Weicun Chu, Sunmiao Fang, Chunxiao Zheng, Minmin Xue, Xiaofan Wang, Tao Hu, and Wanlin Guo\*

Atmospheric water is ubiquitous on earth and extensively participates in the natural water cycle through evaporation and condensation. This process involves tremendous energy exchange with the environment, but very little of the energy has so far been harnessed. The recently emerged hydrovoltaic technology, especially moisture-induced electricity, shows great potential in harvesting energy from atmospheric water and gives birth to moisture energy harvesting devices. The device performance, especially the long-term operational capacity, has been significantly enhanced over the past few years. Further development; however, requires in-depth understanding of mechanisms, innovative materials, and ingenious system designs. In this review, beginning with describing the basic properties of water, the key aspects of the water-hygroscopic material interactions and mechanisms of power generation are discussed. The current material systems and advances in promising material development are then summarized. Aiming at the chief bottlenecks of limited operational time, advanced system designs that are helpful to improve device performance are listed. Especially, the synergistic effect of moisture adsorption and water evaporation on material and system levels to accomplish sustained electricity generation is discussed. Last, the remaining challenges are analyzed and future directions for developing this promising technology are suggested.

At present, moisture energy harvesting can be achieved mainly in three ways: 1) doing work driven by reversible deformation of moisture-responsive materials, 2) redox reactions triggered by absorbed moisture, and 3) ion diffusion driven by moisture-induced gradient energy. The first method converts environmental humidity changes into mechanical shifts of moisture-responsive materials, further driving piezoelectric or magnetoelectric generators to output electricity.[14,15] Although it is flexible and easy to operate, this method depends heavily on periodic humidity changes and responds slowly to external stimuli, which inherently generates pulse-type electric output with a long recharging time. Designing redox reactions in a moistureabsorption system is another promising way to harvest atmospheric water energy. A moisture-driven battery was reported to generate electricity through chemical reactions between active metal electrodes and moisture.[16] As the metal electrodes will be consumed during the reaction, performance attenuation of the battery is inevitable. The problem can be alleviated by implanting additional energy input into the system to keep redox reactions proceeding.[17] It was recently reported that the integration of hygroscopic hydrogel and water-splitting apparatuses driven by solar illumination showed even more attractive performance for a synergy of dehumidification and power generation, which provides an ingenious solution for durable energy conversion in ambient environment.[18] The third method is to drive ion migration by moisture-induced gradient energy (ion concentration or water content gradient), in which the chemical potential changes of water from gas to liquid or the phase transition energy is regarded as the energy source. Further development calls for a paradigm shift, from mechanisms and materials to system designs. Very recently, impressive works have been reported to significantly prolong the operational duration, which should be a prelude to continuous power generation of moisture energy harvesting devices.

# 2. Basic Properties of water and its Interaction With Hygroscopic Materials

A single water molecule consists of one oxygen atom and two hydrogen atoms. The outer $2 \mathsf { S } ^ { 2 } 2 \mathsf { P } ^ { 4 }$ orbital of the oxygen atom and the $1 { \mathsf { S } } ^ { 2 }$ orbital of the hydrogen atom are hybridized to form two $\mathrm { O - H }$ bonds as well as two lone pair electrons in a tetrahedral structure.[25] The two $\mathrm { O - H }$ bonds, with lengths of $0 . 0 9 6 \mathrm { n m }$ , form a V-shape with an angle of $1 0 4 . 5 ^ { \circ }$ (Figure 2a).[26] Due to the electron transfer from hydrogen to oxygen atoms, the water molecule is polarized to be positively charged near the hydrogen atom and negatively charged near the oxygen atom.(27] Consequently, hydrogen bonds are formed between adjacent water molecules due to electrostatic attraction. The tetrahedral structure of a water molecule determines that each water molecule can have four hydrogen bonds around it, and multiple water molecules can be linked by hydrogen bonds to form water clusters (Figure 2a).[28,29] In ambient environment, atmospheric water exists as clusters rather than an individual molecule, and the cluster distribution is dependent on the humidity of atmosphere or the water vapor pressure.[30]

Water clusters in the air could be extracted by hygroscopic materials, which refer to substances that take up water into their bulk phase.31-33] In fact, hygroscopicity is the nature of materials. Nearly all the materials naturally exhibit either weak or strong affinity to moisture through chemical or physical interactions (Figure 2b,c). Adsorption occurs when the free energy change $\Delta G$ is equal to the work done to bring a gaseous molecule to the adsorbed state.34] The free energy change could be described by

$$
- \Delta G = \int _ { P } ^ { P _ { 0 } } V d P = R T \mathrm { l n } { \frac { P _ { 0 } } { P } }
$$

where $P _ { 0 }$ and $P$ present the water vapor partial pressure of the ambient environment and the equilibrium vapor pressure of the adsorbate, respectively; $T$ represents the temperature, and $R$ represents the gas constant.|34] The water adsorption quantity can be further deduced according to the $D { - } R$ equation

$$
\chi _ { \mathrm { e q } } = \chi _ { \infty } \ e x p \left[ - k _ { \mathrm { d } } \left( \frac { \Delta G } { \beta _ { \mathrm { r } } } \right) ^ { 2 } \right]
$$

where $\chi _ { \mathrm { e q } }$ and $\chi _ { \infty }$ are the equilibrium and saturation adsorption quantity of water vapor $( \mathsf { g } \mathsf { g } ^ { - 1 } )$ $k _ { \mathrm { d } }$ is a constant determined by the structure of the adsorbent; and $\beta _ { \mathrm r }$ is the affinity coefficient.[35] The equation shows that the moisture adsorption behavior is affected by temperature, vapor pressure, and intrinsic properties of the adsorbent. For the adsorbent with abundant chemisorption sites, chemical absorption that extracts moisture through strong chemical bonding plays a dominant role in moisture harvesting. The chemical bonding occurs when the gaseous water encounters surface atoms of the adsorbent with chemical interactions, such as electron transfer, atomic rearrangement, or destruction and formation of chemical bonds.[36] The interaction is strong and usually irreversible without energy input. By contrast, physical adsorption shows obvious advantages in reversibility and easier processability. However, as water adsorption relies on the weak intermolecular forces between water molecules and the sorbent surface, the water absorption capacity is relatively low. It is important to note that the adsorption behavior of existing moistureinduced generators generally presents a union of chemisorption and physisorption. Based on the moisture extraction mechanism of the adsorbent, the fundamental characteristics of interaction between moisture and hygroscopic materials could be further understood.

The dynamic process of water phase transition from gaseous water to adsorbed water is often accompanied by the formation and destruction of hydrogen bonds, during which heat exchange, material deformation, or ion dissociation are constantly generated. In an isothermal and isobaric condition, the enthalpy change of the moisture adsorption system can be described by

$$
\Delta H = \Delta G + T \Delta S
$$

where $\Delta G$ and $\Delta S$ are variations of Gibbs free energy and entropy, and $T$ is the temperature. As the Gibbs free energy and entropy reduce when the gaseous water is transferred into the adsorbed state, $\Delta H$ of the system presents a negative value, which means the moisture adsorption is usually along with heat release (Figure 2d). The chemical absorption heat ranges from 80 to $4 0 0 ~ \mathrm { k J ~ m o l ^ { - 1 } }$ , while that of the physical adsorption presents a relatively low value of $2 0 \ \mathrm { k J \ m o l ^ { - 1 } }$ or less.[37,38] Another result of the interaction between water and hygroscopic materials is material deformation, usually swelling during hydration or shrinking during dehydration (Figure 2d).[39] The moistureinduced deformation can be achieved in a variety of material systems, such as polymers and biomaterials.[40,41] One important thing these materials have in common is that they are rich in polar functional groups, including hydroxyl, amino, and carbonyl, which enables the material to capture water molecules from air through hydron bonding. The adsorbed water molecules insert themselves into the intermolecular chains; and thus, result in the material swelling. By contrast, the material shrinking presents an opposite process of breaking hydron bonds and releasing water molecules. During the dynamic process of chemical bonds breaking and recombination, atoms at the end of the polar functional groups can be dissociated and released into free ions, which is termed as solvation effect (Figure 2e).[42] Such phenomenon can occur in several material systems including graphene oxide, cellulose, and polyelectrolytes.43-45] By artificially constructing asymmetrical material components or device structures, a concentration gradient of dissociated charge carriers can form across the material, leading to an induced potential between two electrodes.[46,47]

When the surface of hygroscopic material is charged due to the dissociation effect or functional treatment, interaction be tween water and the solid surface can induce a regular distribution of ions at the interface, which is named electric double layer (EDL).[48] This unique structure consists of two layers: an ion layer tightly attached to the solid surface, which is called the Stern layer, and an incompact layer enriched in counterions, which is called the diffusion layer (Figure 2f).49] The EDL leads to drastic changes in the potential at the interface, in which the electric potential at the shear plane is named the zeta potential.[50] It should be noted that zeta potential is a key index to reflect the surface electrical properties of materials and has a significant impact on the interaction between water and materials. Generally, the polarity and strength of zeta potential determine the type and number of the adsorbed ions as well as the ion-selective permeability of the solid channel, which is essential to electricity generation based on ion diffusion or confined flow. The interval between the closest bulk liquid region and the shear plane is the Debye length $( 1 { - } 1 0 0 \mathrm { n m } )$ , which is negatively correlated with ion concentration and valence state.[51]

# 3. Electricity Generation Based on Moisture Adsorption

As mentioned, the interaction between water and hygroscopic materials can give rise to various accompanying effects including material deformation, breaking and recombination of chemical bonds, release of heat and ions, and rearrangement of interfacial charges, which could be harnessed to design corresponding energy conversion devices. Current power generation based on moisture adsorption could be achieved in mainly three ways; as aforementioned, those are: 1) doing work driven by reversible deformation of moisture-responsive material, 2) ion diffusion driven by moisture-induced gradient energy, and 3) redox reactions triggered by absorbed moisture. The second can be further divided into streaming potential and moisture-induced ion diffusion. In this part, we summarize the electricity generation mechanism of the above methods in detail and point out their advantages and limitations.

# 3.1. Doing Work Driven by Reversible Deformation of Moisture-Responsive Materials

When ambient water is adsorbed by some hygroscopic materials under high humidity conditions, the adsorbed water molecules can insert themselves into the micropores or the intermolecular chains inside the material, resulting in the material expansion.[52,53] This expansion can be eliminated by expelling the bonding water at low humidity. Such reversible deformation of moisture-responsive materials under fluctuant humidity conditions provides a way to convert moisture energy into mechanical force, which can be further transferred into electricity by doing work on an electromagnetic or piezoelectric generator. It was first reported that the compatible integration of a moisture-responsive polymer film and a piezoelectric PVDF element can output alternating electricity of ${ \approx } 1 . 0 \ \mathrm { V }$ and $5 . 6 ~ \mathrm { n W }$ under varying humidity,[14] The electricity generation depends heavily on the fast and reversible deformation of the polymer film, which is achieved by developing a biomimetic synthesis strategy that enables the film to exchange water rapidly with the environment. The water-responsive polymer composite consists of a rigid matrix (polypyrrole) and a dynamic network (polyol-- borate), in which the borate ester crosslinking hub and intermolecular hydrogen bonding between the polyol-borate network and PPy are sensitive to water (Figure 3a,b). When the polymer composite is exposed to varying humidity, the hydrolysis and reforming of the borate ester crosslinking hub, as well as dynamic breaking and recombination of intermolecular hydrogen bonding, occur rapidly to alter the mechanical properties of the composite, thus resulting in the fast expansion and contraction of the film.

Enhancing the deformation size and energy density of the moisture-responsive material is an effective way to improve the electricity-generating performance. It was demonstrated that the energy-harvesting device made from a Bacillus spores-based actuator and an electromagnetic generator showed even more attractive performance (Figure 3c-e). It could deliver alternating electricity of ${ \approx } 1 . 0 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ and $0 . 7 ~ \mu \mathrm { W }$ when dry and moist streams of air were alternately applied (Figure 3f).[15] Remarkably, only ${ \approx } 3 ~ \mathrm { m g }$ of Bacillus spores were used to fabricate such a device. The impressive performance could be attributed to the large deformation rate $( 1 2 \% )$ and high energy density (more than $1 0 \mathrm { ~ M } ]$ $\mathrm { m } ^ { - 3 }$ ) of the Bacillus spores in response to the water gradient.

Although pioneering works have been made on harvesting moisture energy by integrating moisture-responsive materials and generator elements, the electrical outputs are still limited to be used for practical applications. Further enhancing the sensitivity and deformation rate of the moisture-responsive material can increase the electrical outputs, owing to the enhanced frequency and magnitude of driving force toward the generator element. This strategy can be achieved by precisely controlling the properties of adsorption sites. It was reported that a carbon nitride polymer film was designed and synthesized following this principle, in which only the adsorption sites (amino groups) were hydrophilic and the amino groups were mainly oriented horizontally to the film plane.54] when the film was exposed to air with humidity fluctuations, water molecules were adsorbed only on its growth side without permeating deeply across the film, thus leading to an impressive performance of ultrafast $( \approx 5 0 ~ \mathrm { m s } )$ and large actuating motion (10 o00 times greater than the film thickness). In addition to improving the performance of a single device, scaling up multi-energy-conversion units to fabricate macroscopic engines showed significant enhancement in power generation. A moisture energy harvester assembled by over 300 spores-based filaments and an electromagnetic generator could produce alternating electricity over $2 \textrm { V }$ and $6 0 ~ \mu \mathrm { W } ,$ which was sufficient to power low-consumption devices such as LED.[55] It should be noted that the essence of this power generation strategy was the electromagnetic or piezoelectric effect driven by the reversible deformation of moisture-responsive materials, which

# 3.2. Ion Diffusion Driven by Moisture-Induced Gradient Energy

As mentioned, some hygroscopic materials with abundant polar groups can catch ambient moisture and release free ions. Exposing such porous material with asymmetric structures or functional groups can artificially introduce a gradient of water content or ion concentration, which can be harnessed to design energyharvesting devices driven by moisture-induced gradient energy. When the hygroscopic material contains narrow channels with charged surfaces, streaming potential can be induced due to the directional motion of water. In another case, membrane potential across the device, which refers to the potential difference between two electrodes of the sandwich-type device, can be generated due to the diffusion of released ions. It is worthwhile to note that electricity can also be generated through interaction between polar liquids (ethanol, methanol, and acetonitrile) and carbon black film, in which electrons or holes are excited and pulled to induce an internal electric field across the electrodes.[9]

# 3.2.1. Streaming Potential

When ionic water contacts the solid material with a charged sur face, an electric double layer (EDL) will form at the liquid-solid interface. The EDL consists of a Stern layer tightly attached to the surface and an adjacent diffusion layer enriched in counterions. External forces, such as pressure, gravity, and electric force, can give rise to a shearing motion of the diffusion layer, which breeds the electrokinetic effect.l56] In the case that ionic water is driven to fow within a confined channel, the shearing motion of the diffusion layer can lead to an accumulation of the counterions at one end; thus, inducing an electrical voltage termed streaming potential (Figure 4a).157] The streaming potential (V) can be presented by

$$
V = { \frac { \varepsilon _ { \scriptscriptstyle 0 } \varepsilon _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { T } } \zeta } { \eta K } } \Delta P
$$

and the streaming current $( I )$ can be presented by

$$
I = - \frac { \varepsilon _ { \scriptscriptstyle { 0 } } \varepsilon _ { \scriptscriptstyle { \mathrm { r } } } a ^ { 2 } \pi } { \eta } \left( \frac { \Delta P } { L } \right) \zeta
$$

where $\varepsilon _ { 0 }$ is the electric permittivity of vacuum. $\eta , \varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ , and $K$ are the specific conductivity of the liquid, the relative permittivity, and the dynamic viscosity, respectively. $L$ and $\alpha$ are the capillary length of the nanochannel and the capillary radius, respectively. $\zeta$ is the zeta potential of the nanochannel surface and $\Delta P$ is the pressure difference.58] Both the streaming potential and current can be improved by enlarging the zeta potential of the material and raising the external pressure. In addition, it was reported that the streaming potential increases with the reduction of solute concentration.591 Reducing the channel diameter to nanoscale proportions can also increase the streaming potential, mainly resulting from the enhanced counterions flow due to the EDL overlap.[60]

The interaction between water and hygroscopic materials with charged surfaces can produce an EDL at the liquid-solid interface. When an asymmetric electrode structure with different exposed areas or moisture flow is artificially introduced into the system, a water content gradient can be generated across the material; thus, giving rise to an ionic water flow to induce a streaming potential/current. Several functional materials have been utilized for moisture-induced generators based on streaming potential, such as oxides,[61] biological nanofibrils,[62] and functional wood.[63] For example, the moisture-induced generator assembled by $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ nanowire networks could produce the pulsed electrical output of $0 . 5 \mathrm { V }$ and $4 \mu \ W \ c m ^ { - 2 }$ under alternating high and low humidity conditions (Figure 4b-d).[61] The voltage rose to the peak value over a $_ { 1 0 \mathrm { ~ s ~ } }$ time interval and decreased over the next $_ { 1 5 0 \mathrm { ~ s ~ } }$ Extensive efforts were subsequently invested to extend the duration of electrical outputs. It was reported that an open circuit voltage of ${ \approx } 0 . 1 2 \ \mathrm { V }$ was obtained in a bio-fiber-based moisture-induced generator, in which the electricity could output for over $5 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ due to the continuous water flow within the narrow channel (Figure 4e-h).[62] Although several materials have been demonstrated to harvest moisture energy based on the streaming potential, the device performances both in power output and operative duration are still limited and need to be further enhanced by efficient strategies. The first is to adjust the basic property of the solid channel. Reducing the channel diameter and improving the zeta potential of the charged surface have been demonstrated to enhance the electricity generation performance due to the enhanced ionic selectivity (Figure 4f,g).[64,65] The second is to induce unremitting water and ion flow inside the material, which should be the key factor in achieving continuous power generation. This strategy can be realized by regulating the moisture adsorption-desorption kinetics through the asymmetric design of material surface property or device structure. The third is to improve the efficiency of converting ion flow into electricity. Different from the traditional $\mathrm { \sf A g / A g C l }$ electrodes that are used to collect streaming current, inert metals or conductive carbon are usually adapted as electrodes in moisture-induced generators. Thus, the efficiency of converting ion current should be low due to the inadequate charge transfer between ions and inert electrodes. Therefore, well-designed electrodes on both material and morphology should be further developed to improve the conversion of charge transfer and collection. Importantly, the side reaction between active electrodes and materials or water should be specially noted and avoided.

# 3.2.2. Ion Concentration Gradient Induced Electricity

In addition to the streaming potential, electricity arising from ion diffusion can be generated in another way, by inducing an ion concentration difference across the material. It was first reported by Qu's group in 2015 that a graphene oxide membrane with a performed oxygen-content gradient can deliver pulsed electrical outputs of $3 5 ~ \mathrm { m V }$ and $4 . 2 \ \mathrm { m W } \ \mathrm { m } ^ { - 2 }$ under a humidity variation of $30 \%$ (Figure 4i).[19] The electricity generation relies heavily on the alternation of high and low humidity. At high humidity, the side of the film with high oxygen content adsorbs more moisture and releases protons from the functional groups, inducing a proton concentration difference across the film. The one-way diffusion of protons is thus produced to generate electricity with a pair of Au electrodes (Figure 4j). When the film is transferred into the environment with low humidity, the adsorbed water desorbs from the GO film and the migrated ions move back; hence, inducing negative potential and completing a cycle of electricity generation (Figure 4k,l). Since then, significant efforts were devoted to improving the performance of the device. It has been proved that several strategies, including material refinement,[66] surface treatment,[67] and interface engineering[21] can effectively enhance the electrical outputs. The demonstrated voltage of such moisture-induced generators increased considerably from $3 5 \mathrm { m V }$ to $1 . 5 \mathrm { V }$ over the past few years. However, such devices can only operate under dramatic humidity variation and generate pulsed electricity. It is necessary to extend the operational duration of the device to further improve its practicality.

For continuous electricity generation based on ion diffusion, a maintained ion concentration gradient is always needed. From this point, two strategies were proposed to meet the requirement. The first is enhancing the chemical gradient across the material to enlarge the induced ion concentration differences. To this end, the integration of a gradient layer, which is prepared by partially reducing a GO film, and a supplying layer, which is an unprocessed GO layer, are designed and assembled into the moisture-induced generator in 2018 (Figure 4m).68] Benefiting from the strong proton supply capacity of the unprocessed GO layer, the ion concentration difference between the two layers can sustain for a while, leading to a continuous ion diffusion and electrical output of $0 . 4 5 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for 500 s under static humidity conditions (Figure 4n,o). Introducing components with high water uptake and ion dissociation capacity into the material can further improve electrical outputs and the operational duration of the device. A moisture-induced generator based on GO/PAAS composite was demonstrated to deliver a voltage output of $0 . 6 5 \mathrm { V }$ for $1 2 0 \mathrm { h }$ [12] The improvement of the device performance could be attributed to the enhanced ion concentration gradient, which was brought by the continuous release of $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ under humidity conditions. The second strategy is to construct a water content gradient in the homogeneous material. An asymmetric structure of electrodes that only allows the hygroscopic material to adsorb moisture from one side can be designed to induce a water content difference inside the material. Such water content gradient can further give birth to unidirectional flows of water and ion to generate electricity in the external circuit. As the continuous electrical output relies heavily on the sustained water content gradient, the moisture adsorption-desorption kinetics of the material should be the key point to achieve long-term operation.169] It has been reported that a thin-film device made from protein wires can continuously output a voltage of $0 . 6 \mathrm { V }$ for 2 months.[70] The significant enhancement of operational duration results from the self-maintained water content difference within the film, which is formed by the appropriate moisture adsorption-desorption kinetics of the protein film under static humidity.

Despite both the value and duration of the electrical outputs having been greatly improved, two key issues are remaining to be addressed. The first is how to permanently maintain the ion concentration gradient across the material. The current strategies, as mentioned that pre-built chemical or moisture gradient to extend the ion diffusion time, can hardly prevent the gradual disappearance of ion concentration gradient during the electricity generation process. One possible solution is to introduce external energy sources, such as heat, illumination, and so on, to return the migrated ions or enhance the charge transfer. Recent advances in this regard have been reported, which will be discussed later.[71,72] The second is how the ion diffusion inside the material is converted into electron current in the circuit. To avoid the active reaction of electrodes, inert metals or conductive carbon are used as electrodes in such moisture-induced generators. However, the charge transfer mechanism among water, materials and inert electrodes is not yet clear, which seriously hinders the improvement of charge collection efficiency.

# 3.3. Power Generation Based on Redox Reactions Involving Moisture

In addition to being the solvent and electrolyte mentioned above, water can participate in various chemical reactions as a common reactant. For example, water can react with reactive metals to produce hydroxides and hydrogen, or be split into hydrogen and oxygen under bias voltage or illumination.73] Such water-involved redox reactions can be harnessed to design moisture energy converting devices after combination with hygroscopic materials.[74] From this point, a moisture-battery composed of metal electrodes and the hygroscopic gel is fabricated to directly convert chemical energy into electricity.[16] As shown in Figure 5a, the battery consists of Mg foil as the anode and foamed polyaniline (PANI) as the cathode. The porous PANI foam with strong polarity can spontaneously capture moisture from the air, which turns on the chemical reaction between water and $\mathrm { M g }$ electrode. The oxidation of $\mathrm { { M g } }$ gives rise to the loss of electrons and the creation of $\mathbf { M } \mathbf { g } ^ { 2 + }$ . Due to the low electronic conductivity $( 7 . 5 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \mathrm { ~ S ~ c m ^ { - 1 } }$ $1 \mathrm { k H z }$ of the PANI foam, the electrons coming from $\mathrm { { M g } }$ tend to run through the external circuit to split the adsorbed moisture into $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 }$ and $\mathrm { O H ^ { - 1 } }$ under the catalysis of cation of doped PANI. The moisture battery can output an open-circuit voltage of $1 . 8 4 \mathrm { V }$ with a specific capacity of $1 2 4 7 \mathrm { m A h g ^ { - 1 } }$ (Figure 5b). Similarly, a moisture battery composed of Li foil as the metal electrode and graphene oxide film as the hygroscopic element was fabricated to supply power for respiration-detective devices.[75] Although moisture-battery has shown advantages in power density and flexibility, shortcomings especially the relatively poor discharge performance of rapidly decaying electrical outputs would hinder its practical use.

Constructing a build-in potential along the direction of ion motion can effectively prolong the discharge time, owing to the enhanced ionization of adsorbed water molecules and one-way transfer of ion charge. The recently reported moisture-induced generator based on an ionic-diode-type hybrid membrane can continuously output a voltage of $0 . 8 – 1 . 1 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for over a month, while the short-circuit current undergoes gradual attenuation from 1500 to $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { n A } }$ during the period (Figure 5c-e).[76] The degradation in current can be attributed to the passivation of the metal electrode. As the chemical energy contained in reactive metal can be depleted, this moisture-induced generator is more like a one-shot battery. Recently reported autonomous electrical potential oscillations induced by moisture-activated chemical reactions provide one way to generate continuous alternating current outputs in polymer systems.[77] Another way to solve the problem is to introduce energy sources that can be obtained sustainably into the system to maintain the redox reaction. Tan's group has made a series of pioneering works in this region.78] By integrating water-splitting devices with hygroscopic hydrogel, they developed an atmospheric water-splitting system that can spontaneously adsorb moisture and produce hydrogen under illumination, which will be discussed in detail later. Due to the consumption of the metal electrode and low energy conversion efficiency, effective strategies to significantly improve sustainability and electrical output are still needed. The first is to design new redox reactions that can be reversed by extracting environmental energy such as illumination and heat. Second, the separation efficiency of cations and anions, as well as ion transfer behavior inside the electrolyte should be further enhanced. Last, both the material and structure of the electrodes should be designed specifically to enhance the efficiency of converting ion current and extract charges.

# 4. Materials for Harvesting Energy from Moisture

The essence of moisture energy harvesting devices (MEHDs) is materials with intrinsic properties tailored for both moisture adsorption and electricity generation. Generally, high-performance MEHDs require functional materials with the following characteristics: 1) appropriate moisture capture and water transfer capacities to enable the material to adsorb atmospheric water and maintain the water content gradient in a wide range of humidity; 2) superiorities in generating and separating charge carriers, which will facilitate the charge transfer to improve the electricity generation performance; and 3) robust mechanical and relative stable chemical properties to guarantee the device can operate for a long duration. To this end, diverse functional materials with porous structures, abundant polar functional groups, regulable conductivity, and other properties have been developed for high-performance moisture energy harvesting devices. In this part, we summarize the current material systems that are utilized for moisture energy harvesting in detail and discuss promising materials for continuous electricity generation.

# 4.1. Material Classification and Morphology

Graphene-based materials with adjustable capabilities provide imaginative spaces for developing high-performance energy conversion devices.79] Specifically, the basic properties of graphene oxide, such as conductivity, wettability, and surface charge behavior, can be regulated flexibly by dealing with oxygen-containing functional groups.[80-83] $\mathrm { I n } 2 0 1 5$ , Zhao et al. put forward for the first time that a GO film with the performed oxygen-content gradient can generate electricity under humidity variation.19] The functional GO film was obtained through a moisture-electric annealing (MeA) method to construct different $\mathrm { O / C }$ atomic ratios between different regions. The as-prepared GO film can provide $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of ${ \approx } 2 0 \mathrm { m V }$ and $I _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ % $\approx 5 \mu \mathrm { A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ under a $\Delta \mathrm { R H }$ of $30 \%$ . Subsequently, a moist-electric generator (MEG) based on the 3D GO was prepared through a freeze-drying strategy, yielding a higher power density of $1 \mathrm { m W } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ (Figure 6a).[43] The electricity generation relies heavily on the reversible changes in the state of materials during moisture absorption and desorption, which results in the release of dissociated ions from oxygen-containing functional groups and their back migration. Driven by the concentration difference, the dissociated ions transfer directionally from the high-concentration part to the low-concentration part; thus, generating the electrical output in external circuits. Therefore, the electricity generation should occur under the following two conditions: 1) functional group gradient inside the material by preprocessing; 2) humidity difference between the two ends of the material. To this end, various graphene-based generators have been invented, such as graphene quantum dots (GQDs),[66] highly oriented GO fiber,[84] all-printable GO functionalized paper (Figure 6b),[85] and 3D GO frameworks. The morphology changes of the material, from 0D to 3D, have significant influences on the basic properties of materials such as specific area, water transfer resistance, and saturated moisture absorbance and further determine the power generation performance of devices. For instance, the high specific area and intrinsic small size endow GQDs with superior capacities to provide plentiful water adsorption sites and lower water transfer resistance. As a result, a remarkable power output of $1 . 8 6 \mathrm { ~ m w } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ was produced by a GQDs-based MEG, which is prominently higher than previous GO sheets-based MEGs.66] Furthermore, 1D GO fiber, which is assembled through the highly oriented arrangement of GO sheets, was employed to fabricate a quasi1D MEG based on $\mathrm { r G O } / \mathrm { g G O } / \mathrm { r G O }$ structure. Benefiting from the rapid mass transfer kinetics along the 1D fiber, the device can deliver a voltage of ${ \approx } 0 . 4 \mathrm { V }$ and a current density of $1 . 0 6 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ [84] The enhanced power generation performance brought by superior moisture adsorption and mass transfer is also found in 3D GO structures as mentioned above. Specifically, when the parallel 2D GO nanosheets are scaled up into 3D GO frameworks, the plentiful hydrophilic groups and interlayer spaces enable the material to adsorb and accommodate more water; thus, presenting a remarkable improvement of saturated water absorbance from $1 8 . 3 ~ \mathrm { w t \% }$ to $3 3 . 1 ~ \mathrm { w t \% }$ [43] In addition, the hierarchic structures of micropores on GO sheets, microchannels within GO nanosheets, and internal gaps lead to rapid capillary pumping of water and effective transfer of ions. By rational configuration designs and large-scale integrated fabrication, graphene-based MEG can be economical, tunable, and flexible; thus, paving a promising way for the wide application domain such as supplying power for portable electronic devices.[86,87] However, the instantaneous electrical outputs still limited their practical applications, great efforts were then devoted to extending the operational time of the MEGs. Liu et al. found that water vapor adsorption on a piece of porous carbon film with two sides bearing different functional group contents could output a continuous voltage of ${ \approx } 6 8 ~ \mathrm { m V }$ for more than $^ { 6 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ [88] Both experiments and theoretical simulations reveal that the asymmetric ion dissociation of the functional groups produces an ion concentration gradient across the carbon film, which induces sustained proton diffusion to output continuous electricity. This strategy of constructing asymmetric structures within materials was also applied to other carbon materials. A MEG based on an asymmetric porous GO membrane (GOM) was demonstrated to produce $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $4 5 0 \mathrm { m V }$ and power density of $2 . 0 2 \mu \mathrm { W } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ at $8 5 \%$ RH. Furthermore, the device can maintain $90 \%$ of the original efficiency after a durability test for more than $1 0 0 \mathrm { { h } }$ at $1 6 - 2 6 \%$ RH and room temperature.[68] Compared with instantaneous MEGs, materials that are utilized for continuous electricity generation usually have enhanced functional group gradients to promote the continuous ion diffusion. However, the compact structure, large volume, poor electrical conductivity, and few active sites of GO sheets seriously hinder the adsorption of moisture and the transport of hydrated ions, which veritably affects the power generation performance.

Conductive polymers have drawn wide attention because of their outstanding mechanical, optical, and electrical properties.89,90 In addition, some polymers with abundant polar functional groups have shown great superiority in extracting moisture and releasing ions, which enable them to be the ideal materials for MEGs. Inspired by graphene-based MEGs, Xue et al. designed a MEG based on 3D polypyrrole (PPy) framework, producing a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of ${ \approx } 6 0 \mathrm { m V }$ and power density of ${ \approx } 6 . 9 \mathrm { m W }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ due to the anion diffusion within the material[91] Furthermore, 1D polypyrrole nanowires,[92,93] porous polydopamine (PDA),[94] and poly(4-styrensulfonic acid) with poly(vinyl alcohol) (PSS/PVA)[95] were subsequently demonstrated to be utilized for MEG after doping treatment to introduce ion concentration gradients within the materials. It is worthwhile to note that the MEG based on 1D polypyrrole nanowires produces a remarkable power density of $1 0 3 . 1 3 ~ \mathrm { m W ~ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ under the $\Delta \mathrm { R H }$ of $7 5 \%$ which is over ten times higher than that of 3D polypyrrole-based MEG.92] The significant improvement in power output can be attributed to the efficient and fast mass transfer along the 1D nanowires. Similar to the graphene-based devices, the polymerbased MEGs mentioned above were able to merely produce intermittent voltages. Increasing the proton concentration difference is an effective way to extend the operational duration. Xu et al. presented the first continuous polymer MEG based on a polyelectrolyte membrane of poly (4-styrensulfonic acid) (PSSA).[45] A piece of PSSA film $( 1 \times 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ was demonstrated to generate an open-circuit voltage of ${ \approx } 0 . 8 \mathrm { V }$ and a current density of ${ \approx } 0 . 1 \ \mathrm { m A } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ (Figure 6c). The enhanced electrical output, both in value and duration, can be attributed to the superior ion dissociation capacity of PssA.[96] Following this rule, other polymers including polyvinyl alcohol (PVA), polyacrylic acid (PAA), hydroxyethyl cellulose, Nafion, and natural polysaccharides have also been certified to produce sustained voltages. In addition to polymers with a single component, composites of polymer/GO and polymer/polymer have been proven to present even better performance for MEGs. Huang et al. fabricated a direct-current MEG based on the composite of porous GO and sodium polyacrylate (PAAS), which could produce a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of ${ \approx } 0 . 6 \mathrm { V }$ for more than $^ { 1 2 0 \mathrm { h } }$ under ambient environment.[12] Wang et al. developed a heterogeneous MEG constructed by combining polydiallyl dimethyl ammonium chloride (PDDA) with hybrid film (PSsA) (Figure 6d).[20] Under ambient environment, the heterogeneous MEG would adsorb moisture and generate a high Voc of ${ \approx } 1 . 3 8 \mathrm { V } ,$ which will be discussed in detail later.

Although the graphene-based and polymer-based MEGs mentioned above have made significant progress, the problems of high cost and complex processing cannot be ignored. Biologi cal materials thus come into researchers' view because of their biodegradability and low cost. Liu et al. innovatively demonstrated a bio-based MEG using protein nanowires, which were sheared from the microorganism Geobacter sulfurreducens, which produced a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 5 ~ \mathrm { V }$ and current density of $1 7 \mu \mathrm { A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for over 2 months (Figure 6e).[70] By surface modification with commercial carbon ink, the waste corn stalk $( 3 \times 1 \times 0 . 6 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 3 }$ ) could generate a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of ${ \approx } 0 . 6 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for more than $4 0 \mathrm { { h } }$ under $2 0 { - } 8 0 \%$ RH environment.[97] Electrospun cellulose acetate (CA) films with abundant micropores were also reported to yield a sustainable $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $\operatorname { 0 . 3 V }$ from moisture.44] Furthermore, a paper-based moistelectric generator, fabricated by sandwiching a piece of print paper with two perforated gold electrodes, was reported to yield a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $\phantom { - } 0 . 2 5 \mathrm { V }$ and an $I _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of ${ 1 5 } \mathrm { n A }$ under a relative humidity change $( \Delta \mathrm { R H } )$ of $7 0 \%$ [98] Although the print paper is insulated when it is dry, it can release protons to generate electricity after contact with water due to the massive hydroxyl and carboxyl groups on the paper fiber surface. Recently, gelatin and biological nanofibrils, including cellulose, chitin, silk fibroin, and amyloid, were also demonstrated to harvest moisture energy (Figure 6f).[62,99)

In 2018, titanium dioxide $\left( \mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 } \right)$ , a typical inorganic semiconductor material, was demonstrated for the first time to be employed in moisture energy harvesting devices, which produce a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of ${ \approx } 0 . 5 ~ \mathrm { V }$ and a power density of ${ \approx } 4 ~ \mu \mathrm { W } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ under humidity variation (Figure 6g).[61] With the intrinsic flexibility and stability of $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ nanowires under humidity, this device can maintain the power generation performance after $1 0 ~ 0 0 0$ bending cycles, exhibiting great superiority in flexible and long-term power supply. Since then, inorganic semiconductor materials have been the promising candidate materials for reliable MEGs due to their stability after interaction with water molecules and controllability of morphology. For instance, vertically aligned zinc dioxide nanowires were assembled with two electrodes into a sandwiched device for harvesting energy from various molecules including gaseous species exhaled from human breath.100] In addition, phase-engineered molybdenum disulfide nanosheets were employed to fabricate the moistureinduced electricity generator and output a voltage of $1 9 \mathrm { m V }$ and a current of $6 . 2 4 \mu \mathrm { A }$ [101] The electricity was generated mainly from proton diffusion, which is induced by the different proton release behavior of the two-phase molybdenum disulfide nanosheets under humidity. Furthermore, the typical semiconductor material of silicon was found to have promising potential in water energy harvesting.1102,103] Specifically, a hydrovoltaic device based on silicon nanowire arrays was demonstrated to produce a continuous voltage of $\mathrm { 0 . 4 V }$ and a current density of $5 5 \mu \mathrm { A }$ (Figure 6h).[102] The remarkable electrical outputs could be attributed to the strong interaction between the large charged silicon surface and water, as well as the prominent carrier and water transfer kinetics within the highly oriented silicon nanowire arrays. Although the continuity of electrical output is mainly maintained by the internal capillary water flow driven by evaporation, the promising characteristics of the highly charged surface and the outstanding carrier and water transfer properties enable nanostructured silicon to be an ideal candidate material for high-performance moisture energy harvesting devices.

To sum up, various functional materials with tunable properties including conductivity, wettability, and porosity have been developed for high-performance moisture energy harvesting devices. Generally, there are three key characteristics of these functional materials to achieve continuous and enhanced electricity generation: 1) hydrophilic surface with abundant chemical functional groups to enable materials to extract moisture from the air and release adequate amounts of ions; 2) high ionic conductivity to transfer the released ions and low electronic conductivity to hold back the flow of electrons and promote the charge separation; and 3) numerous nanoporous and robust mechanical structures to improve the moisture-absorbing and mass-transferring capacity, as well as prevent the performance collapse and ensure the long-term operation. In addition, the asymmetrical or gradient structures of both material composition and surface properties also play an essential role in electricity generation, which helps to enhance the ion concentration or water content differ. ences across the material. Although diverse material systems have been demonstrated to be employed in MEGs and output electricity of volt- and microamp-level, the specific device performance, such as long-term and cycling stability, mechanical strength, mass producibility, and cost, can be very different according to properties of different materials. Specifically, carbonbased materials including carbon nanoparticles and graphene oxide exhibit great superiority in high specific areas and tunable surface properties. However, the cycling stability and mechanical strength of these materials are relatively poor, mainly resulting from the irreversible reaction between water and functional groups and loose structures without skeletons. On the contrary, inorganic semiconductor materials show great stability under moisture conditions due to their high resistance to water and solid crystalline structures, while ion dissociation and water transfer kinetics are relatively weak. For polymers and biological materials, the solid skeleton structures and abundant functional groups enable these materials to be stable under humidity and continuously release ions, which do favor to extend the operative duration of the devices. Furthermore, the outstanding flexibility and biocompatibility endow them with great potential to be widely employed in self-powered wearable systems. The space for developing future high-performance materials is vast and still waiting for researchers to explore their use in MEG. The evaluation of power generation performance for these materials should focus on their areal or volumetric power density, operative time or cycling stability, mass producibility, cost, and so on.

# 4.2. Promising Materials for Continuous Electricity Generation

The sustained electric output of most MEGs relies on continuous water adsorption and ion migration; so, the capacity of materials to extract moisture and induce built-in gradient potential is particularly significant. The hygroscopicity of materials is usually regulated in two ways: 1) construct the gradient of functional groups within the material; 2) construct the humidity gradient within the material. Normally, the former way requires material treatments of surface modification, such as electrochemical polarization, laser irradiation, and plasma treatment. Functional materials with such treatments can present an asymmetrical hygroscopicity at different regions, which gives birth to the water content gradient and ion diffusion. In addition to the surface treatments, developing promising material systems to enhance the ability of ion dissociation paves another way to extend the operational duration, owing to the enhanced ion concentration gradient. The first is to construct promising composites combining the advantages of several material systems. Huang et al. fabricated a direct-current MEG based on composites of sodium polyacrylate (PAAS) and graphene oxide (GO), which could work under a wide range of temperatures $- 2 5$ to $5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ and relative humidity $( 5 - 9 5 \%$ RH) (Figure 7a).[12] Abundant micro pores and hydrophilic groups promote the adsorption of moisture (Figure 7b).[104] Benefiting from the outstanding hygroscopic capacity, the GO/PAAS composite with abundant oxygen-containing functional groups can release more free ions compared to GO, which enhances the ion transport and delivers a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of ${ \approx } 0 . 6 \mathrm { V }$ for over $^ { 1 2 0 \mathrm { h } }$ under ambient environment (Figure 7c,d).[105]

In addition to constructing composites, developing homoge. neous materials with appreciative adsorption-desorption kinetics to induce a water content in the material also shows promising potential to extend the operational duration. As shown in Figure 7e, a thin film of protein nanowires extracted from G. sulfurreducens has been reported to generate continuous voltage for 2 months and current for over $^ { 2 0 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ in ambient environment by harvesting moisture energy (Figure 7f).[70] Compared to other materials used in moist-induced electric generators, the protein nanowires film itself has a striking ability to keep a selfmaintained moisture gradient under a non-gradient ambient environment, which is depicted in Figure 7g. This self-maintained moisture gradient in the nanowires network was speculated to result from a vapor-pressure gradient across the thin film with a high density of nanopores which induce capillary pressure to lower vapor pressure. Under the moisture gradient, the mobile $\mathrm { H ^ { + } }$ ions will be hydrolyzed by adsorbed water and then diffuse to create an electric field which leads to the close-loop current flow (Figure 7h). Therefore, by self-maintaining a moisture gradient within the film, the special protein nanowires device is capable of generating continuous electric power for a long duration.

Further development for innovative materials with more outstanding performance should be considered from the following aspects: 1) Moisture adsorption-desorption kinetics: it is worthwhile to note that the moisture adsorption capacity of material should match its water desorption capacity to some extent, which is essential to sustain the water content gradient within the material. Materials with strong mois.ure adsorption capacity tend to present a quick response to the humidity stimulation, inducing a water content or ion concentration gradient to erupt considerable electrical outputs. Meanwhile, the long-term operational duration calls for the sustainability of the gradient energy. Therefore, the regulation of moisture adsorption capacity for materials used for MEG should consider abilities on both producing the gradient and maintaining it. 2) Generation, separation, and transport of carriers: the electricity generation is mainly determined by the unidirectional migration of carriers, which requires outstanding capacities of material to effectively generate, separate, and transport carriers (mainly ions). Great efforts have been made in these aspects, including developing new materials to generate more protons under humidity compared to typical GO, establishing a p-n junction to accelerate the separation of opposite carriers, and so on. Development of materials on both composition and structure is still necessary to further enhance the carrier dy. namics. One possible way is to construct oriented channels to accelerate ion transport. Furthermore, it has been reported recently that dragging the charge carriers (holes or electrons) inside the material by polar solutions can also generate electricity, which paves another promising direction for material exploration.(9l 3) Cycle performance and stability: the moisture adsorption and electricity generation processes are always accompanied with deformation of materials and destruction of functional groups. As a result, new materials for MEG should have robust mechanical performance and quick renewal capacity to handle the water-induced deformation and recover to the initial state in a short duration. 4) Electrode materials: both material and morphology should be well designed to improve the efficiency of collecting charges. In addition, the active reactions between electrodes and materials under humidity should be assessed and avoided as much as possible.

# 5. Advances in System Design

Multiple hygroscopic sorbents and functional materials with prominent performance have been utilized for several energy harvesting devices for both alternating and continuous electricity generation based on moisture adsorption. The electrical outputs of a single device have been increased from the millivolt and nano-ampere order to the volt and micro-ampere order, as well as the voltage output can sustain for more than 2 months. Further enhancement of both value and duration of electricity generation calls for ingenious system designs that work from mechanisms to operating models. Recently reported advancements point to possible ways. The first is to construct enhanced asymmetric structures of both materials or devices to induce maintained ion diffusion or water content gradient. Developing novel materials that can release more ions or control the moisture adsorptiondesorption kinetics is the key issue of this method. The second way is to introduce external energy sources including heat and illumination to increase the number of carriers. Some heat- or light-sensitive materials have been shown to produce electrons or holes under the external stimulus, which can be used to improve the generated electricity by harvesting multiple energies at the same time. Last, integrating water-splitting apparatuses with hygroscopic materials to fabricate an atmospheric water-splitting system has shown an alternative way to harvest moisture energy. In the following section, the mentioned advancements in system design and the corresponding features will be discussed in detail.

# 5.1. Enhanced Asymmetric Structures for Continuous Electricity Generation

To convert the energy carried by moisture into electric power, a sustainable ion concentration or water content gradient within the device is of great significance. In addition to developing new materials with homogeneous components, constructing enhanced asymmetric structures of either materials or devices paves another way to achieve the goal. Inspired by the asymmetric lipid bilayer present in cell membranes, a bilayer film of polycation poly diallyl dimethyl ammonium chloride (PDDA) and polyanion polystyrene sulfonic acid (PSS) mixed with polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) was designed to construct a biomimetic moistinduced electric generator (Figure 8a).[20] As shown in Figure 8b, the biomimetic double-layer generator is capable of continuously delivering a high voltage of $0 . 9 5 \mathrm { V }$ for ${ \approx } 2 5 8 \mathrm { h }$ with a retention of $67 \%$ under the ambient environment. The double-layer heterogeneous structure plays an essential role in long-period electricity generation, which enables the device to spontaneously extract moisture and release $\mathrm { C l ^ { - } }$ and $\mathrm { H ^ { + } }$ ions in the PDDA layer and the PSSA layer, respectively. As a result, a concentration difference of ions ( $\mathrm { C l ^ { - } }$ and $\mathrm { H ^ { + } }$ ) is gradually constructed in different layers. Benefiting from the continuous moisture extraction and ion dissociation in the bilayer structure, the well-established ions gradient can stably sustain and guarantee a constant supply of hydrolytic mobile charges $\mathrm { [ H ^ { + } }$ and $\mathrm { C l ^ { - } }$ , overcoming the inner electric field and leading to the enhanced electric output over a prolonged term. Notably, the special structure enables high scaling performance, and a high voltage of $1 0 0 0 \mathrm { V }$ can be continuously delivered for $^ { 1 6 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ with retention of ${ > } 8 0 \%$ by series connecting 1600 devices. Through simple chemical modification of natural balsa wood, a similar bilayer structure with a top "anionlayer" and a bottom "cation-layer" was engineered very recently, and the bilayer wood membrane could also deliver a long period of electric output due to the plentiful hydrophilic groups and aligned ions nanochannels within the balsa wood.[106] In addition to inducing maintained ion diffusion, the operational duration of moisture energy harvesting devices can be extended by constructing a sustained water content asymmetry across the materials. In this regard, a novel asymmetric hygroscopic structure (AHS), which is constructed by partially depositing the hygroscopic hy drogel onto a functionalized carbon layer (Figure 8c), has been reported to achieve durable electricity generation and storage.[107] The partially distributed hygroscopic hydrogel not only enables the device to adsorb moisture from the air but also firmly confines the adsorbed water and inhibits its diffusion to form a permanent wet-dry asymmetry across the carbon film. As a result, a sustained in-plane potential difference is induced between the wet and dry regions, owing to the surface potential changes of carbon film after interaction with water. Benefiting from the AHS, the device can output a continuous voltage of $0 . 6 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for more than $1 7 0 \ \mathrm { h }$ (Figure 8d). Furthermore, the mechanism of electricity endows the device with unique capacities of self-charging and external-charging, which present a promising potential for repeatable use.

The directed migration of the mobile charges is essential to the energy conversion process from moisture energy into electric power. The mobile charges are usually only driven by ions concentration difference which originates from the conformation gradient of the device or th-e applied moisture gradient. Further regulation of charge migration is needed to guarantee efficient electric output. The electrode-material interface plays an important role at the ends of ion flow, and an appropriate match between materials and electrodes will promote charge separation to enhance the output to a certain extent, which was confirmed by a Schottky contact Ag/GO interface with a rational work function match.21] Inspired by the semiconductor PN junction that separates photogenerated electron-hole pairs in a solar cell, an ionic diode-type hybrid membrane consisting of nano-porous carbon nanotube (CNT) and nanochannel anodic aluminum oxide (AAO) was designed, wherein the negatively charged CNT behaves like a P-type semiconductor and the positively charged AAO behaves like an N-type semiconductor. The built-in electric field formed in the asymmetric nanofluidic junction guarantees a continuous fow of ions and assists the high-efficient continuous electric output for at least 1 month.[76] The polyelectrolyte of poly diallyl dimethylammonium chloride (PDDA) and the nanostructured silicon were also demonstrated to construct a heterojunction that helps the charge carriers to separate and directionally move toward their respective electrodes.[108] By constructing two oppositely charged electrodes and the electrolyteloaded nanofibers, a novel electric double-layer capacitor-like moist-electric generator (Figure 8e) was designed to deliver continuous electrical outputs of $0 . 7 \mathrm { V }$ and $3 \mu \mathrm { A }$ for $^ { 1 2 0 \mathrm { h } }$ (Figure 8f), and it is noteworthy that this electric power generation process does not need a moisture gradient.109] When the moisture contacts the device, the pair of electrodes adsorb $\mathrm { H ^ { + } }$ and $\mathrm { O H ^ { - } }$ ions and then lead to the accumulation of $\mathrm { O H ^ { - } }$ and $\mathrm { H ^ { + } }$ , respectively. In the meantime, $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ and ${ \mathrm { R } } { - } { \mathrm { S O } } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ are released from the electrolyte sodium dodecyl benzene sulfonate (SDBS) and they are driven to directionally migrate under the electric field built by the differently charged electrodes, which results in electricity generation.

# 5.2. Light- and Heat-Enhanced Power Generation

Various forms of clean energy, including solar radiation, wind power, low-grade heat, and water phase change, synchronously exist in nature and promise avenues toward a sustainable and low-carbon society. For each form of energy, spontaneous energy harvesting technologies have been significantly developed, corresponding to solar cells, wind turbines, thermoelectric generators, and hydrovoltaic technologies. However, each energy conversion technology has its limitations; for example, solar cells stop harvesting solar energy at night or on cloudy days, and hydrovoltaic technologies have a low energy conversion efficiency. Therefore, a new energy conversion system capable of concurrently harvesting multiple forms of clean energy needs to be developed to pursue better performance. In this regard, hybrid devices that combine hydrovoltaic technologies with other energyharvesting units, such as silicon solar cells and dye-sensitized solar cells, have been proposed to achieve a higher power output as an all-weather power source.110111] Owing to ubiquitous vaporous water in the air, simple setup, and scalability, moisture-induced electric generators have great potential to be combined with other energy conversion ways. On the other hand, the insufficient energy conversion induced by moisture needs augmentation through synergies with other appropriate energy harvesting technologies.

# 5.3. Atmospheric Water-Splitting System

Producing green hydrogen based on atmospheric water-splitting systems paves another promising avenue for sustainable moisture energy harvesting. The proposed atmospheric watersplitting system including photocatalysis, photoelectrochemical (PEC) cell, and electrolysis, consisting of hygroscopic materials and water-splitting modules, can spontaneously adsorb moisture and continuously produce hydrogen driven by illumination or photovoltaics. Although the system does not generate electricity directly, hydrogen is regarded as the most promising green fuel, and the electricity can be delivered by its combustion without any pollution. The energy conversion process is sustainable due to the continuous redox reactions and no additional energy storage steps are needed, making this way a promising option for sustainable moisture energy harvesting. Recently, Tan's group has combined metal-based super hygroscopic hydrogels with photocatalysis and photoelectrochemical cells to achieve concurrent dehumidifying and hydrogen generation,[17,78,113] as shown in Figure 10a-c. The developed metal-based hygroscopic hydrogels are capable of rapidly adsorbing vaporous water more than four times their weights, guaranteeing sufficient water supply for photocatalysis and photoelectrochemical cells to continuously produce hydrogen. In the photocatalysis and photoelectrochemical systems, crystal plane engineering,18] a built-in electric field established by positively polarized ferroelectrics,[78] and band structure engineering were developed to enhance photogenerated charge's mobility.[113] Despite these enhancements improving the performance of water-splitting in such systems, the energy conversion efficiency is very low at the present stage. A direct moisture electrolysis module (Figure 10d) with a high Faradaic efficiency of ${ \approx } 9 5 \%$ was demonstrated recently,[114] wherein sintered glass foam soaked with sulfuric acid was sandwiched by electrolysis electrodes. As one of the best hygroscopic materials, sulfuric acid is capable of adsorbing moisture from the atmosphere even in very low humidity $5 \%$ or below). Therefore, glass foam soaked with sulfuric acid serves as a reservoir for watersplitting in a wide range of humidity. In the meantime, deliquescent sulfuric acid plays a role of an electrolyte in the electrolysis system. It is noteworthy that a hydrogen production tower constructed by five such modules and a solar panel were demonstrated in a practical environment for 2 days, and can produce $7 4 5 \mathrm { L }$ per square meter in a day. However, the presence of sulfuric acid poses a safety concern, which may hinder the practical application of such modules. Further development of green hydrogen production directly from moisture should focus on meeting both higher efficiency and safety.

# 6. Synergistic Effect of Moisture Adsorption and Water Evaporation

Almost all the aforementioned moisture energy harvesting de vices (MEHDs) set moisture adsorption as the only means to proactively regulate the water behavior inside the material. However, there are intractable problems with this design that need to be overcome. First, the material will gradually reach to saturated state with water, and then become no longer capable of further moisture adsorption. As the chemical potential changes of water from gas to liquid are regarded as the energy source, the energy conversion process cannot continue for long when this water phase change process stops. Second, the unidirectional ion diffusion, which produces the electrical outputs, is slow and can hardly sustain for long time when the moisture-induced ion concentration difference acts as the only driving force. As the moisture adsorption and ion diffusion processes continue, the water content and ion concentration gradient within the material will inevitably decrease, resulting in the attenuation of power generation. Therefore, the long-term maintenance of water content gradient and ion diffusion requires a water desorption process and external energy input.[64] Fortunately, the discovery of evaporation-induced electricity in 2017 exactly meets the requirements.8] By inducing water evaporation on the surface of the carbon black film, this technology can directly convert thermal energy from ambient environment into electricity. The recently reported evaporating potential indicates that the charge transfer between polar molecules and materials may contribute to electricity generation.[9] Specifically, when ethanol molecules are adsorbed by carboxyl-functionalized carbon black films, electron transfer from the polar molecules to carbon black occurs, dragging holes in the carbon black film. At the capillary front of the film, ethanol molecules evaporate rapidly and regain the transferred electrons, resulting in the high local hole concentration and electric potential in the film. Density-functional theory (DFT) calculations demonstrate that oxygen-containing groups also enhance the charge transfer from water molecules and water clusters to graphene film. The evaporating polar potential originates from the charge transfer between solid and evaporating molecules and converts environmental thermal energy into electricity, in which the contribution of ion diffusion is negligible. Consequently, a well-compatible way, that is integrating water evaporation with moisture adsorption in one device, can be shaped to produce a continuous electrical output. The introduction of evaporation not only prevents the water saturation inside the material but also provides an efficient way to maintain the unidirectional motion of water and carriers by harvesting environmental heat.

In general, the introduction of water evaporation not only addresses the intractable malady of water saturation but also provides an efficient way to utilize environmental thermal energy. Furthermore, promising materials, especially hygroscopic sorbents with outstanding adsorption-desorption kinetics and functional materials with a prominent capacity to transfer water, need to be further developed.

# 7. Conclusion and Perspectives

Our review of power generation based on interactions between hygroscopic materials and atmospheric water has presented the promising potential of harvesting moisture energy. Pioneering moisture-induced electricity devices, as well as diverse materials for high-performance electricity generation, have been developed based on different working mechanisms to produce alternating or continuous electrical outputs. The power density of moisture-induced electricity devices has been improved from $1 8 . 4 ~ \mathrm { n W } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ to $1 2 \mu \ W \tau m ^ { - 2 }$ in the past seven years. However, truly continuous operational moisture-induced electricity devices are still necessary to be developed considering the urgent need to offer stable power output as a power supply element. Several strategies, both in material synthesis and system design, have been proposed and significantly extended the duration of electrical outputs. Especially, integrating water evaporation with moisture adsorption has shown great superiority in extending the duration of current output, owing to the continuous diffusion of water and carriers driven by the evaporation. In addition, the unique design of this asymmetric structure enables the device to adsorb moisture and release heat from one side, and evaporate and extract heat from another, which provides a broad space for further improving the energy conversion efficiency by improving the mass and heat transfer process. Despite significant enhancements having been made in output power density and duration, moisture-induced electricity technology still faces grand challenges:

1) Fundamental understanding of the underlying mechanism of moisture-induced electricity remains a challenge. A practical case of moisture-induced electricity is usually the result of a combination of different mechanisms, which involves force-electric-thermal coupling processes at the interface. Thus, further efforts of decoupling numerous energy conversion paths and quantifying the contribution of each path are needed to deeply understand the electricity generation mechanism. Furthermore, the structure and physicochemical properties of water in confined spaces, as well as the charge transfer at the water-material interface should be stimulated using the cross-scale dynamic simulation method. Last, the influence of various factors including surface properties, channel size, temperature, and humidity on the water molecular migration and ion transfer characteristics should be explored. It is important to deeply understand such fundamental issues to guide specific material synthesis and device design.

2) The energy conversion efficiency and power density of the device need to be improved. The current moisture-induced electricity devices can output power density as high as $1 2 \mu \mathrm { W }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , with the highest energy conversion efficiency of ${ \approx } 1 -$ $2 \%$ , which is too small compared with typical energy conversion technologies such as photovoltaic and wind power. The limited power output can be attributed to the weak interaction between water and materials and the energy dissipation in non-essential parts such as breaking hydrogen bonds and releasing heat. The improvement of energy conversion efficiency calls for promising materials with precisely controlled properties and ingenious device structures that reduce energy dissipation. Furthermore, the charge capture efficiency at the electrode-material interface should be improved. The selection of electrode material and structure should be compatible with the power generation mechanism and device structure.

3) The duration of electrical outputs, especially the current output, still needs to be extended. For moisture-induced electricity based on ion diffusion, introducing external energy sources to return the migrated ions can be a way to recover the ion concentration gradient. Furthermore, generating electricity by drawing electrons or holes inside the material seems to be the solution to realize truly sustained electrical outputs. The recently reported evaporating potential, which generates electricity by polar molecules dragging the charge carriers inside the material, opens a promising way of achieving this goal. Further efforts should be made in developing new material systems with tunable properties of porosity, surface charge behavior, hydrophobic and hydrophilic performance, and so on.

With the ubiquitous nature of moisture and significant performance improvement of MEHDs, extensive applications beyond supplying power for electronics and sensors are supposed to be explored and developed. A prime possibility is constructing MEHD-based big data analytics by integrating MEHDs with high-accuracy sensors, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence, which can be designed to be a novel form of self-powered systems to automatically collect and analyze data as well as provide constructive guidance. Such a smart sensing system can be applied in many fields including healthcare, information security,[115] and environment monitoring. In addition, the enhanced power output and prolonged operative duration enable MEHDs to be flexible and reliable power sources for the Internet of Things (IoT). Further development of devices calls for a comprehensive design in terms of size, sensitivity, running conditions, and output performance according to the targeted application scenario. Furthermore, MEHDs hold great potential to be a promising platform for clean energy generation toward global lowcarbon energy systems due to their unique capacities to harvest energy without specific environmental requirements and combine it with other energy technologies as hybrid systems. The integrations between MEHDs and water-splitting systems, as well as MEHDs and energy storage devices, are the two typical examples of hybrid systems to achieve distributed energy generation and storage. In addition, the accompanying effect of heat and humidity regulation during the energy-generating process can be employed to design new air conditioners with low energy consumption. Considering the relatively low power output of MEHDs at this stage, improving the energy conversion efficiency and developing effective materials and integration technologies should be the key points to realize the wide application for future scaled energy harvest.

Overall, the rapid development of moisture-induced powergenerating technologies offers a unique opportunity for harvesting energy from the natural water cycle. The device performance has been dramatically improved from the original millivolt- to the volt-level over the past 7 years. Herein, we have highlighted grand challenges that need to be urgently addressed to achieve continuous moisture energy harvesting. With the deep cooperation between theorists and experimenters, we believe that significant improvement, both in output performance and sustainability, can be achieved soon to meet the requirement for clean energy in a low-carbon era.
