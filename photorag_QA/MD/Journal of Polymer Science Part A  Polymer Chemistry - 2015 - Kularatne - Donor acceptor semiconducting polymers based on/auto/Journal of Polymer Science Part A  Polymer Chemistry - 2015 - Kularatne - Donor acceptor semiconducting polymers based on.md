ABstrAct: Three donor-acceptor copolymers P1, P2, and P3 with $N , N$ -dodecylpyromellitic diimide as the electron-acceptor unit with three diethynyl-substituted donor monomers: 1,4- diethynyl-2,5-bis(octyloxy)benzene, 2,7-diethynyl-9,9-dioctyl-9Hfluorene,  and $^ { 3 , 3 ^ { \prime } }$ -didodecyl-5,5'-diethynyl- $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -bithiophene have been synthesized by Sonogashira crosscoupling polymerization. The synthesized polymers showed deep highest occupied molecular orbital energy levels and larger band gaps $1 > 2 . 5$ eV). Polymers P1, P2, and P3 underwent fluorescence quenching with [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM), indicating the intermolecular photo-induced charge transfer between the donor polymers and the PCBM acceptor. $\circledcirc$ 2015 Wiley Periodicals, Inc. INTRoDucton Imide-functionalized arylenes such as perylene diimide,1-3 phthalimides,4-6 and imide-functionalized thiophenes such as thieno[3,4-c]pyrorole-4,6-dione-9 copolymers have been studied extensively as electron acceptors in donor-acceptor (D-A) copolymers and have shown good performance in organic photovoltaic devices. These polymeric electron acceptors have been used in $n$ -channel organic field effect transistors (OFETS) owing to their good air stability and relatively high-charge carrier mobilities. Among these polymers, naphthalene diimides10-13 and perylene diimides1-3 have shown to be the most successful airstable and high-electron-transport materials in the class of aromatic imides. Naphthalene diimide-based copolymer achieved an electron mobility of $1 . 8 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ s ^ { - 1 }$ using topgate bottom-contact device architecture with poly(methyl methacrylate) as the polymeric dielectric.10 The main advantage of the imide-functionalized moieties is that the imide nitrogens allows the incorporation of alkyl or aryl side chains, perpendicular to the polymer backbone, which in turn enhance the solubility, the solid-state packing, and the morphology of the D-A polymers without disrupting the conjugation along the polymer backbone. In addition, the band gap of the D-A copolymers of imide-functionalized arylenes can be varied by changing the donor unit.14

Pyromellitic diimides, which have only a central benzene ring, are the smallest arylene core moieties among the class of aromatic dimides and have been widely used as building blocks for the synthesis of aromatic polyimides. Compared to naphthalene diimides and perylene diimides, pyromellitic diimides have a smaller molecular area for intermolecular $\pi$ electron overlap. Thus, only a limited number of reports have investigated their optoelectronic and electrochemical properties.15-18 Katz and coworkers15 first reported the use of this $N , N ^ { \prime }$ -fluorinated aryl-substituted pyromellitic diimides as small molecules in OFETS and displayed an electron mobility of $0 . 0 7 9 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ s ^ { - 1 }$

Improving the conjugation in this pyromellitic diimide core is crucial factor; however, only a limited number of reports have investigated the conjugation along the $N , N ^ { \prime }$ -substitution. Conjugation along the backbone can be increased by the substitution of conjugated moieties at the 3 and 6 positions of the pyromellitic diimides. Thus, we first attempted the synthesis of D-A copolymers of pyromellitic diimide with benzodithiophene as the donor moiety. However, we found that owing to electronic and intramolecular steric repulsion, substitution along this 3,6-positions on the pyromellitic diimdes is restricted. Guo and Watson19 also reported a similar behavior in which they failed in the synthesis of thiophene substitution at the 3 and 6 positions of the pyromellitic diimide. Reich16 synthesized a 3,6 and $N , N ^ { \prime }$ -linked pyromellitic diimide polymer which gave an electron-carrier mobility of $1 0 ^ { - 3 }$ to $1 0 ^ { - 2 } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } ~ s ^ { - 1 }$ when the polymer was blended with Phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester $( \mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M } )$ in a weight ratio of 1:9. This lower charge carrier mobility may be owing to the twist in the neighboring conjugated units in the polymer backbone which result in a decrease in the conjugation.2o Thus, the insertion of ethynyl linkages at 3,6-positions on the pyromellitic diimides can decrease the steric and electronic repulsions and is expected to provide a more planar polymer backbone.?o The alkyne linkage may not have the best delocalization and charge stabilization as compared to the alkene-containing compounds. Nevertheless, the alkyne linkages are more accommodating than alkenes to steric and conformational constraints.?1 Only a few systematic studies have been reported regarding the effect of intramolecular charge transfer between donor and acceptor groups in the presence of ethynyl linkages.21-26 It has been reported that owing to the electron-withdrawing nature of the ethynyl linkage, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOmO) level of the D-A polymers was decreased by $0 . 3 \ \mathrm { e V }$ as compared to the D-A polymer without the ethynyl linkage. This gave an enhanced $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and better power conversion efficiencies.7 However, another report showed that the introduction of an ethynyl linkage blue shifted the absorbance of the D-ethynyl-A polymer compared to the D-A copolymer?8 This behavior was attributed to the disruption of the push-pull effect in D-A copolymers caused by the presence of the ethynyl linkage.28 Therefore, it is not straightforward to predict the effect of ethynyl linkage on photovoltaic properties.

Much of the reports until now have not investigated the synthesis as well as the properties of D-A copolymers containing pyromellitic diimides. As such, we report here the synthesis, characterization, and optoelectronic properties of the three D-A polymers with pyromellitic diimide as the acceptor. Three ethynyl-substituted monomers that is 1,4- diethynyl-2,5-bis(octyloxy)benzene, 2,7-diethynyl-9,9-dioctyl9H-fluorene,  and $^ { 3 , 3 ^ { \prime } }$ -didodecyl- $^ { 5 , 5 ^ { \prime } }$ -diethynyl- $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -bithiophene were employed to generate the donor units.

Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectrums were acquired using a Nicolet 360 FTIR spectrometer with a single-bounce attenuated total reflectance accessory (diamond crystal).

# Optoelectronic Properties of the Synthesized Polymers UV-Vis Measurements

The Uv-Vis spectra of polymer solutions in chloroform solvent were carried out in 1-cm cuvettes using an Agilent 8453 Uv-Vis spectrometer. Thin films of polymer were obtained by evaporation of chloroform from polymer solutions on glass microscope slides.

# Cyclic Voltammetry

Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was obtained with a BAS CV-50W Voltammetric Analyzer (Bioanalytical Systems). Electrochemical grade tetrabutylammonium perchlorate (TBAP) was used without further purification. Acetonitrile was distilled over calcium hydride and collected over molecular sieves. The electrochemical cell was composed of a platinum electrode, a platinum wire auxiliary electrode, and an $\mathrm { A g / A g C l }$ reference electrode. Acetonitrile solutions containing $0 . 1 ~ \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ of TBAP were placed in a cell and purged with argon. A drop of the polymer solution was evaporated in ambient air. The film was immersed into the electrochemical cell containing the electrolyte and the oxidation potential was observed and recorded. All electrochemical shifts were standardized to the ferrocene redox couple at $0 . 4 7 4 \ \mathrm { V } .$

# Fluorescence Quenching Measurements

Photoluminescence spectra of the polymers in chloroform solutions were carried out in a 1-cm cuvette using a PerkinElmer LS50 BL luminescence spectrometer. # RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Sonogashira crosscoupling polymerization was employed for the synthesis of the alternating copolymers poly $\{ N , N ^ { \prime }$ -dodecylpyromellitic diimide-alt-1,4-diethynyl-2,5-bis(octyloxy) benzene} (P1), poly $\{ N , N ^ { \prime }$ -dodecylpyromellitic diimide-alt-2,7- diethynyl-9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluorene} (P2), and poly $\{ N , N ^ { \prime }$ -dodecylpyromellitic diimide-alt- $^ { 3 , 3 ^ { \prime } }$ -didodecyl- $^ { 5 , 5 ^ { \prime } }$ -diethynyl- $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ bithiophene} (P3) (Scheme 1). 3,6-Dibromo- $\cdot N , N ^ { \prime }$ -dodecylpyromellitic diimide was reacted with three different ethynylsubstituted monomers, namely 1,4-diethynyl-2,5-bis(octyloxy)benzene, 2,7-diethynyl-9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluorene, and $^ { 3 , 3 ^ { \prime } }$ didodecyl- $^ { 5 , 5 ^ { \prime } }$ -diethynyl- $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -bithiophene in the presence of bis(triphenylphosphine)palladium(Il) dichloride catalyst and copper iodide cocatalyst. The SEC analysis of the polymers revealed the molecular weights of the three polymers; P1: $M _ { \mathrm { n } } = 3 3 , 0 0 0 \ { \mathrm { g } } \ { \mathrm { m o l } } ^ { - 1 }$ polydispersity index $( \mathrm { P D I } ) = 2 . 2 7$ P2: $M _ { \mathrm { n } } = 3 7 , 0 0 0 ~ \mathrm { g } ~ \mathrm { m o l } ^ { - 1 }$ $\mathrm { P D I } = 2 . 2 9$ ; P3: $M _ { \mathrm { n } } = 1 6 , 0 0 0 ~ \mathrm { ~ g ~ }$ $\mathrm { m o l } ^ { - 1 }$ $\mathrm { P D I } = 2 . 8 4$ (Table 2). The polymers were purified by successive extractions with methanol, hexane, diethyl ether, and chloroform. The final polymer was obtained by the evaporation of the solution obtained from the chloroform extraction. The three polymers P1, P2, and P3 were soluble in common organic solvents such as chloroform, THF, and dichlorobenzene. The $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR analysis was used to characterize the synthesized polymers (Supporting Information material).

# UV-Vis Spectroscopy

The Uv-Vis absorption spectra were recorded for the three polymers in chloroform solutions and in thin films, which was obtained by drop casting the polymer solution in chloroform on a glass substrate (Fig. 1 and Table 2). The absorption spectra of the three polymers varied with the electrondonating moiety as all the three polymers have the same pyromellitic diimide as the electron-accepting moiety. The polymers P1 and P2 displayed two absorption maxima in solution, whereas the polymer P3 displayed only one absorption maxima (P1: 371 and $4 8 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ P2: 448 and $4 8 8 \ \mathrm { n m }$ and P3: $4 1 7 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ J. The absorption maxima observed for the polymers in the visible region (P1: $4 8 1 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ P2: $4 8 8 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ and P3: $4 1 7 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ is owing to the intermolecular charge transfer transition between the donor and the acceptor moieties.29

The absorption maxima observed in the UV region for P1 at $3 7 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ is owing to the 1,4-diethynyl-2,5-bis(octyloxy)benzene moiety and the absorption maxima observed at ${ \sim } 4 4 8 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ for the P2 polymer is owing to the 2,7-diethynyl9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluorene. The absorption spectra of the thin films for the polymers P1 and P2 (P1: 385 and $5 4 2 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ : P2: 456 and $4 9 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ were slightly red shifted relative to the absorption spectra of the solutions, indicating rigid-rod conformation both in solution and in solid state.30,31 However the polymer P3 displayed broader absorbance maxima with a red shift of ${ \sim } 6 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ (P3: $\lambda _ { \operatorname* { m a x } } = 4 7 1 \ \mathrm { n m } ]$ in thin films compared to that of the solution. This red shift observed for the polymer P3 indicates that there was enhanced packing, and conjugation in solid state compared to that of the solution state.32

# CV Studies

The deeper HOmO levels of the polymers may be owing to the electron-withdrawing nature of the ethynyl group.27 The lower HOMO level for the polymer P2 is a common phenomenon observed in all fluorene D-A copolymers and it is owing to the weak electron donation ability of the fluorene moiety.20,33 The polymers had relatively large band gaps; P1: 2.52 eV, P2: 2.98 eV, and P3: 2.57 eV. A similar trend was observed for the optical band gaps of the polymers with P2 giving the largest band gap of 2.36 eV, P1: $2 . 0 6 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ and P3 $1 . 9 9 \ \mathrm { e V } .$ The deeper HOMO levels of these polymers are expected to be beneficial for a larger open circuit voltage in bulk heterojunction solar cells. However, owing to the large band gaps $( > 2 . 5 $ eV) these polymers may not have efficient intramolecular charge transport and hence could give low currents.

# CONCLUSIONS

We have synthesized three D-A copolymers with $N , N ^ { \prime }$ -dodecylpyromellitic diimide as the electron-acceptor unit with three diethynyl-substituted donor monomers: (1,4-diethynyl2,5-bis(octyloxy)benzene, 2,7-diethynyl-9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluorene, and $^ { 3 , 3 ^ { \prime } }$ -didodecyl- $^ { 5 , 5 ^ { \prime } }$ -diethynyl- $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -bithiophene) by Sonogashira crosscoupling polymerization. The synthesized polymers showed a small red shift in the Uv-Vis absorption spectra when moving from solution to thin film, indicating better packing in the solid state. In addition, polymers P1, P2, and P3 underwent fluorescence quenching with $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ indicating intermolecular photo-induced charge transfer between the donor polymers and the acceptor $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ . These polymers showed relatively deep HOmO levels which are desirable for high open-circuit voltage in organic photovoltaic devices. The introduction of ethynyl linkages decreased the steric and electronic repulsions between the donor and the acceptor moieties, allowing the successful synthesis of D-A copolymers. However, the impact of the ethynyl linkage on the chargetransfer properties could have a negative effect, because the ethynyl linkages have been demonstrated to reduce the degree of conjugation of the D-A system.28
