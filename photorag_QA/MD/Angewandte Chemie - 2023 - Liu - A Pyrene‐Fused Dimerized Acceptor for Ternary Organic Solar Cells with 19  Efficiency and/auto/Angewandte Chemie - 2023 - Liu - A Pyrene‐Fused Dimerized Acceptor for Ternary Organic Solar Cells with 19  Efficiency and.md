# A Pyrene-Fused Dimerized Acceptor for Ternary Organic Solar Cells with $1 9 \%$ Efficiency and High Thermal Stability

Xucong Liut, Zhou Zhangt, Chao Wang,\* Cuifen Zhang, Shijie Liang, Haisheng Fang, Bo Wang, Zheng Tang, Chengyi Xiao,\* and Weiwei Li\*

Abstract: A pyrene-fused dimerized electron acceptor has been successfully synthesized and subsequently incorporated as the third component in ternary organic solar cells (OsCs). Diverging from the traditional dimerized acceptors with a linear configuration, this novel electron acceptor displays a distinctive "butterflylike" structure, comprising two Y-acceptors as wings fused with a pyrene-based backbone. The extended $\pi \cdot$ conjugated backbone and the electron-donating nature of pyrene enable the new acceptor to show low solubility, elevated glass transition temperature $( T _ { \mathrm { g } } )$ and low-lying frontier energy levels. Consequently, the new dimerized acceptor seamlessly integrates as the third component into ternary OsCs, enhancing electron transporting properties, reducing non-radiative voltage loss, and elevating open-circuit voltage. These merits have enabled the ternary OsCs to show an exceptional efficiency of $1 9 . 0 7 \%$ , a marked improvement compared to the $1 7 . 6 \%$ attained in binary OsCs. More importantly, the high $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ exhibited by the pyrene-fused electron acceptor helps to stabilize the morphology of the photoactive layer thermal-treated at $7 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , retaining $8 8 . 7 \%$ efficiency over 600 hours. For comparison, binary OSCs experience a decline to $7 3 . 7 \%$ efficiency after the same duration. These results indicate that the "butterflylike" design and the incorporation of a pyrene unit is a promising strategy in the development of dimerized electron acceptors for OSCs.

# Introduction

Organic solar cells (OsCs) have made significant improvements in the past years with power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) over $1 9 \%$ [I thereby underscoring their promising potential for widespread application. The innovation of new electron acceptors, featuring 2-(3-oxo-2,3-dihydroinden-1- ylidene)malononitrile (IC) as the electron-withdrawing terminal group, coupled with fused/non-fused conjugated backbones, has provided the opportunity to modulate the microstructure and charge generation dynamics within the photoactive layers.? This, in turn, has led to substantial enhancements in PCEs. Specifically, in 2015, the innovation of ITIC by Lin and co-workers marked a pivotal moment.3] ITIC demonstrated superior electron mobility, excellent absorption in the visible and near-infrared regions, and high electron affinity. Subsequent studies focused on tailoring the side chains, extending conjugation, and introducing structural modifications to enhance the performance of IC-based acceptors.4] Notably, acceptors within the Y-series, characterized by a fused D-A-D core, exhibit a combination of high absorption coefficients, narrow absorption spectra, elevated crystallinity, and diminished exciton binding energy, rendering their particular success in current OSC technologies.,4,c,s] However, there remains a continued requirement to innovate and develop new electron acceptors, aiming to further elevate the PCEs of OSCs and to address critical issues, such as stability.]

Recently, a set of dimerized and oligomeric Y-acceptors have emerged as notable contenders for OsCs.[d,7] As different from the single Y-series counterparts, these new acceptors show enhanced glass transition temperatures ( $T _ { \mathrm { g } } \mathrm { \bf s }$ usually above $1 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ [8] This characteristic ensures the maintenance of a stable morphology within bulk-heterojunction photoactive layers, thereby bolstering the thermal stability of OsCs. Most dimerized Y-acceptors have a longconjugated backbone with linear molecular configuration ("end-end" type, Figure 1a), following a two-step synthetic procedure: initially, Y-acceptors with asymmetric IC end groups are prepared, followed by via Stille cross-coupling reactions to yield acceptor dimers.!8a,9] Huang et al. also reported an efficient "one-pot" synthesis method for the production of linear oligomeric Y-acceptors.[7b] Another kind of dimerized Y-acceptors has been reported by Min et al.10] and Zhang et al.,[11] in which dimerized Y-acceptors were linked with alpha alkyl chains ("Y-alkyl-Y" type I and II, Figure 1a). Another alternative design strategy is to present dimerized acceptors featuring central cores or stairlike structures reported by Chen and co-workers[12] ("centercenter" type, Figure 1a).

Benzothiadiazole (BT) central core in Y-acceptors can be easily converted to diamine compounds, which can be reacted with diketones to form pyrazine-like compounds via dehydration reaction.a,3] This reaction has been widely used to construct conjugated materials. Chen et al. have demonstrated the utility of this reaction in introducing novel aromatic units to the central core, such as polyfluorosubstituted cores,14] enabling precise tuning of the energy levels and crystallinity of the resulting acceptors. Inspired by these reports, we envision that these reactions can be extended to construct new dimerized Y-acceptors. Specifically, multi-keto compounds can be employed in conjunction with diamine-substituted Y-acceptors to yield fused and dimerized Y-acceptors. Notably, such structures have been sparsely documented in the literature. While Ding1s] and Lin et al.16] have reported the transformation of diaminesubstituted Y-acceptors into dimerized structures through oxidation and cyclization, no extra building blocks were introduced as a fused core for dimer construction.

In this work, a novel dimerized Y-acceptor with a "center-fused" configuration, denoted as DP-BTP (Figure 1b), has been synthesized and applied into OSCs. The fusion of two $\mathbf { Y }$ -acceptors with pyrene at the central core was achieved by the dehydration reaction of diamine and 4,5,9,10-pyrenetetrone. The extra pyrene unit in the dimerized acceptor introduces distinctive properties, notably an extended $\pi \cdot$ -conjugated backbone and the electron-donating characteristics of pyrene, which impart the dimer with low solubility and low-lying frontier energy levels. Consequently, the new dimer could be perfectly applied into ternary OSCs (D18:N3:DP-BTP, Figure 1c)[17] as the third component to improve the electron transport and reduce the non-radiative recombination loss. As a result, the ternary OsCs provide a high PCE of $1 9 \%$ with simultaneously enhanced open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ , short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ and fill factor (FF) values. Furthermore, DP-BTP demonstrated a higher $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of $1 2 6 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ compared to the $8 8 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ observed for the single Y-acceptor, N3, which could significantly improve the thermal stability of OsCs. Our results underscore the efficacy of both the introduction of pyrene and the fused dimer design as compelling strategies in the development of new electron acceptors for achieving high-performance OSCs.

# Results and Discussion

The synthetic routes of DP-BTP are illustrated in Figure 2a, along with corresponding reaction conditions and yields for each step. Given the inherent instability of the aldehyde group and the exocyclic double bond, the pyrene-fused core, denoted as M3, was initially formed through a high-yielding $( 6 6 \% )$ dehydration reaction between the precursor M1, and tetrone compounds M2. Notably, M1 and M2 were synthesized according to the procedure reported in the literature.[2b,18] Subsequently,  the Vilsmeier-Haack reaction[9] was employed to modify aldehyde groups at the $\alpha$ -position of the pyrene-fused core, yielding M4. Ultimately, the pyrene-fused dimerized acceptor, DP-BTP, was synthesized through a typical Knoevenagel condensation[20] with M5 (difluorinated IC units) with a yield of $7 0 \%$ . Detailed synthetic procedures and comprehensive characterization information are provided in the Supporting Information. Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations revealed a highly planar configuration for DP-BTP (Figure 2b), with the lowest unoccupied molecular orbitals (LUMO) localized at the two "y" wings and the highest occupied molecular orbitals (HOMO) delocalized over the central core (Fig ure S1). This extended $\pi$ -conjugation and electron delocalization afford enhanced absorptive properties and efficient charge-transport pathways, contributing to improved solar light utilization and charge mobility within OsCs. Another interesting observation is that DP-BTP exhibits limited solubility in common organic solvents, such as chloroform. According to Lambert-Beer's law,[21] we measured the solubility of N3 and DP-BTP in dilute chloroform solutions after heating to $9 0 ^ { \circ } \mathbf { C }$ and cooling to room temperature (Figure S4). The detailed measurement method for the solubility test has been described in the Supporting Information. As shown in Figure2c, the acceptor N3 exhibited an excellent solubility of $1 5 5 . 9 0 \mathrm { m g / m L }$ , while DPBTP exhibited a markedly lower solubility of $1 . 6 0 \mathrm { m g / m L }$ which may be related to its highly planar molecular configuration. This discrepancy in solubility is advantageous for achieving favorable phase separation and morphology in blend films, as elucidated by previous studies.22] Electrochemical cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were performed to ascertain the HOMO and LUMO energy levels of DP-BTP (Figure S2), as depicted in Figure 2d. DPBTP displayed shallow LUMO and HOMO levels of $- 3 . 9 2 \mathrm { e V }$ and $- 5 . 6 7 \mathrm { e V }$ , respectively, relative to N3. This outcome is attributable to the highly conjugated nature of the molecules with the donating pyrene core, thereby contributing to the attainment of elevated $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } \mathrm { \mathbf { s } }$ in OSCs. The thermal property of DP-BTP was studied by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA, Figure S3) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC, Figure S3). The dimer DP-BTP exhibited excellent thermal stability with the decomposition temperature ( $5 \%$ weight loss) of $3 7 8 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , compared with $3 5 6 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for N3. There are no melting peaks or crystallization peaks in the DSC curves for both N3 and DP-BTP.

We then determined the $T _ { \mathrm { g } } \mathrm { s }$ of N3 and DP-BTP through UV/Vis deviation metric (DMT) results (Figure 3c-3d).24) Remarkably, the DP-BTP dimer exhibited a much higher $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of $1 2 6 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ than that of N3 $\left( 8 8 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } \right)$ . The $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of N3:DP-BTP $( 0 . 9 { : } 0 . 4 , w / w )$ blend was measured to be $1 1 9 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (Figure S7), suggesting an increased thermal stability. According to the molecular interaction-diffusion framework theory proposed by B. O'Connor and H. Ade et al.,24c] the diffusion of small molecular acceptors into the donor polymer follows Arrhenius behavior, with diffusion coefficients decreasing exponentially as the $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of the acceptors increases. A higher $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ necessitates greater activation energy for diffusion, resulting in a lower diffusion coefficient within the matrix. In the given hyper-miscible N3/DP-BTP alloy, the higher $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of DPBTP effectively impedes the diffusion of N3, leading to the formation of robust aggregates within the matrix. This, in turn, augments the thermal stability of the ternary system in thin films.

To discern this difference, we conducted a voltage loss $( V _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } )$ analysis. Electroluminescence (EL) measurements and highly-sensitive external quantum efficiency (sEQE) were employed to determine the charge-transfer states of these binary and ternary photoactive layers, in accordance with the Marcus theory,[27] as illustrated in Figure ${ 5 } \mathrm { a - c }$ and summarized in Table 2. The energy levels of the charge-transfer state $( E _ { \mathrm { C T } } )$ were determined to be $1 . 3 1 \mathrm { e V }$ $1 . 3 8 \mathrm { e V }$ and $1 . 3 1 \mathrm { e V }$ for D18:N3, D18:DP-BTP, and D18:N3:DP-BTP, respectively. The $E Q E _ { \mathrm { E L } }$ spectrum (sensitive EQE spectra calculated from the EL spectra, Figure 5d) of the D18:DP-BTP pair is almost an order of magnitude higher than that of D18:N3-based devices, suggesting a lower non-radiative voltage loss $( \Delta V _ { \mathrm { n r } } )$ In the ternary system, the presence of DP-BTP ensures a relatively low $\Delta V _ { \mathrm { n r } }$ in OsC devices. As summarized in Table2, it was observed that the pyrene-fused dimerized electron acceptor DP-BTP effectively mitigates both radiative and non-radiative voltage losses in OSCs. This would be attributed to the high fluorescence emission efficiency of pyrene units (Figure S13),[28] which contributes to elevated $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } \mathrm { \mathbf { s } }$ and enhances PCEs.

Furthermore, we measured the light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ dependent $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ characteristics of these binary and ternary OsC devices to explore charge recombination kinetics,29] as depicted in Figure 5e-f. Regarding the powerlaw dependence on the $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ of $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ the slopes for D18:N3, D18:DP-BTP, and D18:N3:DP-BTP are $1 . 1 9 \ \mathrm { k T / q }$ $1 . 1 1 \ \mathrm { k T } /$ q, and $1 . 1 8 \mathrm { k T / q }$ (here $\mathbf { k }$ is the Boltzmann constant, T is the absolute temperature, q is the elementary charge), respectively. This indicates bimolecular recombination dominance in these OsCs. We also investigated $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \propto P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } ^ { \mathrm { } \mathrm { } \alpha } \left( \alpha { \le } 1 \right)$ as shown in Figure 5f, where $\alpha$ denotes the extent of bimolecular charge recombination. A value of 1 would imply complete suppression of bimolecular recombination loss in devices.[28] Notably, the $\alpha$ values of D18:DP-BTP and D18: N3:DP-BTP systems are closer to 1 than that of the D18:N3 couple, signifying suppressed charge recombination in these systems. The charge recombination analysis suggests that the addition of DP-BTP to the D18:N3 system effectively suppresses charge recombination kinetics, consequently enhancing FF values in OSCs. Moreover, steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra were studied, as presented in Figure 5g-i. The PL quenching efficiency of D18:N3 and D18:DP-BTP blends were determined to be $9 1 \%$ and $8 6 \%$ respectively, indicating poor charge extraction efficiency.30] In contrast, in D18:N3:DP-BTP ternary blends, the PL quenching efficiency was enhanced to $9 7 \%$ ,suggesting effective charge transfer from acceptors to D18.30] This discrepancy in PL quenching efficiencies explains the enhanced $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ in ternary OSCs relative to the binary D18:N3 blend.

Furthermore, we studied the film morphology and molecular packing in these binary and ternary systems.

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) images in Figure S14 reveal a nano-scale interpenetrating network with fibrous film morphology. The measured root-mean-square (RMS) roughness values for D18:N3, D18:DP-BTP, and D18:N3: DP-BTP are $0 . 9 6 \mathrm { n m }$ $1 . 1 8 \mathrm { n m }$ and $1 . 0 5 \mathrm { n m }$ ,respectively. Interestingly, no significant morphology change was observed upon the addition of DP-BTP in D18:N3 blends. This phenomenon may be attributed to the formation of an alloylike structure. Furthermore, two-dimensional wide-angle grazing-incidence X-ray scattering (GIWAXs) measurements (Figure 6a-c & S15) were employed to probe the molecular packing motifs of these films. The In-plane (IP) and out-of-plane (OOP) line cuts of GIWAXS patterns are presented in Figure 6d-f, and the corresponding crystallographic parameters are summarized in Table S7. All films exhibited a mixed molecular packing mode with a face-on preferential orientation, as evidenced by the lamellar (h00) peaks at the $\mathrm { I P }$ direction and OOP $\pi - \pi$ (010) peaks. In comparison to D18:N3 blends, D18:DP-BTP blends exhibited an even more mixed packing motif. Specifically, D18:N3 blends displayed IP-direction lamellar (100) and (010) peaks at $q = 0 . { \dot { 3 } } \mathbf { 0 } { \dot { \mathbf { A } } } ^ { - 1 }$ and $1 . 7 4 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively, with corresponding coherence lengths $( C L )$ of $8 . 4 \mathrm { n m }$ and $2 . 4 \mathrm { n m }$ . On the other hand, D18:DP-BTP blends exhibited (100) and (010) peaks at $q = 0 . 3 1 \mathring { \mathbf { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ with a $C L$ value of $9 . 1 \mathrm { n m }$ and (010) peak at $q = 1 . 6 7 \mathring { \mathbf { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ with a $C L$ value of $2 . 3 \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. Notably, in D18:N3:DP-BTP ternary blends, the measured (100) and (010) peaks were observed at $q = 0 . 3 0 \mathring { \mathbf { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ and $1 . 7 3 \mathring { \mathbf { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ ,respectively, with corresponding calculated $C L$ values of $8 . 4 \mathrm { n m }$ and $2 . 3 \mathrm { n m }$ The addition of DP-BTP showed minimal influence on the packing motif and crystallization behavior of D18:N3 blends. Based on the absorption spectra, AFM images, contact angle test, and the GIWAXS patterns, we propose a plausible alloy-packing structure of N3 and DP-BTP (Figure 6g). In the D18:N3 blend, the moderate miscibility between D18 and N3 ensures isolated crystallization and optimal phase separation in films. The introduction of DP-BTP in the binary system results in the formation of an alloy with N3 due to preferential miscibility. This leads to the absorption of N3: DP-BTP being closer to that of N3 itself, rather than DPBTP. Additionally, due to the formation of alloy-like grains, DP-BTP cannot crystallize independently in ternary films. Ultimately, the formation of alloy-like acceptor domains, coupled with the high $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of DP-BTP, ensures good morphology stability of the acceptor domains and consequently, the long-term stability of ternary OSCs.

# Conclusion

In conclusion, this study introduces a novel pyrene-fused dimerized electron acceptor, DP-BTP, and explores its application in OSCs. The "butterfly-like" structure, characterized by a central pyrene backbone flanked by two Yacceptors, imparts DP-BTP with unique properties, including low solubility, high $T _ { \mathrm { g } } .$ and low-lying frontier energy levels. Incorporating DP-BTP as the third component into ternary OsCs significantly enhances electron transport properties, reduces non-radiative voltage loss, and leads to a remarkable PCE of $1 9 \%$ . Owing to the formation of alloylike acceptor domains, DP-BTP with high $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ could anchor N3 molecules and suppress N3 migration in films. This has further contributed to the thermal stability of the ternary OSCs. Through a comprehensive analysis of film morphology, molecular packing, and charge recombination kinetics, we elucidate the underlying mechanisms driving the enhanced performance. The findings presented here highlight the potential of DP-BTP as a promising electron acceptor for high-performance OsCs and underscore the importance of tailored molecular design in advancing organic photovoltaic technology.
