ABSTRACT: The stability of organic solar cells (OSCs) is an urgent problem for commercialization. In this work, a novel asymmetric molecule TB-4Cl was designed and synthesized. Quantum chemical computations revealed that TB-4Cl has a larger dipole moment of 1.98 Debye than that of Y6, which can induce a stronger intermolecular interaction. Without thermal annealing, devices based on PM6:TB-4Cl achieved a higher efficiency of $1 4 . 6 7 \%$ . Impressively, all of the devices showed a negligible difference in power conversion efficiency (PCE) before and after thermal-annealing treatment. Compared to the unencapsulated PM6:Y6-based devices, PM6:TB-4Cl-based devices exhibited improved thermal and air stability, evidenced by retaining around $7 5 \%$ (TB4Cl) and $60 \%$ (Y6) after being heated at $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ in nitrogen for $1 1 0 \mathrm { { \dot { h } } }$ and $6 5 \%$ (TB-4Cl) and $5 0 \%$ (Y6) in air for $^ { 9 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ . This work indicates that an $\mathrm { A { - } D _ { 1 } A ^ { \prime } D _ { 2 } { - } A }$ asymmetric molecule can be a promising candidate for achieving stable OsCs with high efficiency.

# INTRODUCTION

Bulk-heterojunction (BHJ) organic solar cells (OsCs) have occupied an important role in the field of renewable energy technology, owing to light weight, flexibility, and semitransparency.1-4 However, poor stability has always been a major obstacle to the commercialization of OSCs. The BHJ active layers usually exhibit a metastable state due to the characteristics of phase separation.' With time and heat accumulation, the donor/acceptor and their morphology including the crystalline state will be gradually changed, sequentially leading to a decline in efficiency. Hence, building a robust morphology seems particularly important. Choi et al. synthesized two materials, $\mathrm { I D T } ( \mathrm { T C V } ) _ { 2 }$ and $\mathrm { I D T T } ( \mathrm { T C V } ) _ { 2 } ,$ by introducing tricyanovinylene (TCV) to small molecule acceptors. Devices based on these two acceptors exhibited excellent long-term stability of more than $1 0 0 0 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ while retaining the initial power conversion efficiency (PCE), benefitted from their robust internal morphology and the low-lying lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels. On the basis of Y6, Chen et al. replaced F atoms in the end group with Cl atoms, obtaining two asymmetric acceptors YS2 and YS3. The YS2- and YS3-based devices could still retain $7 4 \%$ of the initial PCE in air for 20 days. Liao et al. reported ternary OSCs by introducing a delayed fluorescence (DF) emitter 3,4- bis(4-(diphenylamino)phenyl)acenaphtho[1,2-b]pyrazine-8,9- dicarbonitrile (APDC-TPDA) to the PM6:Y6 active layer. The PCE was maintained at around $8 0 \%$ of the initial values for the ternary OSCs heated at $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $2 0 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ but only $70 \%$ of the initial values for the binary OsCs at the same conditions.

By changing the molecular structure, the molecular properties such as frontier molecular orbital (FMO), solubility, absorption, and charge mobility can be effectively modulated for the purpose of enhancing the open circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ) _ { \cdot }$ short-circuit current density (J), ill factor (FF), and PCE.913 Introducing the electron-deficient core to acceptors for an A-- $\mathrm { D A ^ { \prime } D - A }$ structure has attracted more and more attention. Y6, as a star material with the ADA'D-A structure, has increased efficiency up to $1 8 \%$ recently, profiting from the $\mathbf { A - D }$ alternating structure, which reinforces the intramolecular push-pull electron effect and broadens the absorption spectra.4,1s Meanwhile, with the development of symmetric small molecule acceptors (SMAs), asymmetric SMAs have also cut a figure.16-18 Compared to symmetric SMA counterparts, asymmetric SMAs exhibited a stronger intermolecular binding energy and a larger dipole moment, which is beneficial to strengthen intermolecular interaction, leading to the improvement of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF in OSCs. K12, which is composed of fluorene, benzothiadiazole, and malononitrile, can be regarded as the first asymmetric SMA, but it achieved only $0 . 7 3 \%$ PCE.19 Sun et al. have also reported a series of asymmetric replacing atoms, and coupling different terminal groups. Among them, the asymmetric molecule TPTT-IC, reducing a thiophene based on IDTT, exhibited excellent performance. Compared with ITIC-based OSCs, the TPTT-IC-based OSCs have higher and more balanced hole and electron mobilities, thereby contributing to higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF.

Herein, we designed a new asymmetric acceptor TB-4Cl with an $\mathrm { A { - } D _ { 1 } A ^ { \prime } D _ { 2 } { - } A }$ structure. Benefiting from the asymmetric ladder-type core, TB-4Cl obtained a 1.98 Debye dipole moment, larger than that of Y6 (1.07 Debye), which can enhance the intermolecular dipole-dipole interaction. When blended with PM6, OSCs on the conventional device structure achieved $1 4 . 6 7 \%$ PCE without thermal annealing. However, the efficiency of PM7:TB-4Cl-based OSCs is lower, $1 3 . 6 7 \%$ . It is evident that when TB-4Cl is blended with PM6 or PM7, the PCE changes only a little before and after thermal annealing at $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ . Furthermore, a study on thermal stability was carried out. It can be concluded that through heating at $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { \textrm { \scriptsize 1 1 0  h } }$ in nitrogen, PCE of the unencapsulated PM6:TB-4Cl devices based on the inverted structure could still retain $7 5 \%$ of initial efficiency, while PCE of devices based on PM6:Y6 could merely retain $6 0 \%$ Placing the devices in air for $^ { 9 2 \mathrm { ~ h } , }$ those based on PM6:Y6 could only retain $5 0 \%$ of initial PCE, whereas those based PM6:TB-4Cl could retain around $6 5 \%$

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The synthetic route of TB-4Cl is shown in Scheme 1. After a five-step reaction, the dark blue target compound was obtained. TB-4Cl is fully confirmed by $\mathrm { \check { \Delta } H }$ nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, $^ { 1 3 } \mathrm { C }$ NMR spectroscopy, and time-of-flight mass spectrometry (TOF MS). Relevant spectrograms are shown in the Supporting Information. Thermal stability was evaluated by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) (Figure S1). The result shows that the decomposition temperature $\left( T _ { \mathrm { d } } \right)$ of TB-4Cl is $3 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ $5 \%$ weight loss), which indicates the excellent thermal stability of TB-4Cl. In the process of device fabrication, PM6 and PM7 were selected as polymer donors. (Their molecular structures are shown in Figure S2.)

To understand the molecular properties, quantum chemical computations were conducted with Gaussian 09, Revision E.01 (the alkyl chains were replaced with methyl groups for simplifying the calculations). First, a relaxed two-dimensional (2D) scan with a semi-empirical method was carried out, which simultaneously scanned the dihedral angle between the two end groups and the main core with one step every $3 0 ^ { \circ }$ (Figure S3). It can be concluded that the molecule has a lower energy when both dihedral angles are nearly $0 ^ { \circ }$ . Based on this result, the density functional theory (DFT) calculations at $\mathrm { w b } 9 7 \mathrm { x d } / 6 . 3 1 \mathrm { g } ^ { \ast \ast }$ were conducted to optimize molecular conformation (Figure 1a). The dihedral angles of $0 . 7 4 ^ { \circ }$ and $0 . 6 1 ^ { \circ }$ indicate that TB-4Cl has a planar structure, in favor of $\pi - \pi$ packing and electron mobility. The electron density distribution of LUMO and highest-occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) is shown in Figure S4. Then, intramolecular interaction was calculated with Multiwfn2-based reduced density gradient (RDG).27 The results (Figure S5) indicate that the planar structure is attributed to the noncovalent interaction between S and O atoms. Besides, the asymmetric structure leads an S-shaped conformation, which may enhance molecular packing.28 Then, the conformation of Y6 was also optimized through Gaussian at the same level, which is shown in Figure 1. Based on antiparallel packing induced by the dipole moment,29 the dimer models were also calculated (Figure $\left. 1 6 { - } \mathrm { d } \right)$ . As expected, TB-4Cl exhibits a larger dipole moment (1.98 Debye) than that of Y6 (1.07 Debye). Due to the asymmetric structure, there are two ways of antiparallel packing for TB-4Cl (TB-4Cl-2T2, TB-4Cl-1T1), however just one for Y6. $E _ { \mathrm { d - d } }$ of TB-4Cl-2T2, TB-4Cl-1T1, and Y6-dimer can be calculated, which is $- 4 . 0 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 7 }$ $3 . 3 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 6 } ,$ and $- 4 . 2 2 \times$ $1 0 ^ { - 8 } ~ \mathrm { e V } _ { \lambda }$ respectively. The result indicates that a larger dipole moment can induce a stronger dipole-dipole interaction.31,32 In addition, both TB-4Cl-2T2 and TB-4Cl-1T1 exhibit a stacking distance of 11.03 and 13.99 $\mathring \mathrm { A }$ respectively, which are longer than that of Y6-dimer. The intermolecular binding energy was further calculated and corrected by basis set superposition error (BssE), shown in Figure 1. It can be found that compared to Y6-dimer, TB-4Cl-2T2 and TB-4Cl-1T1 combine stronger, which is beneficial to limit the thermal motion of molecules and improve the stability.33 In the active layer, other than interaction with acceptors, TB-4Cl or Y6 can also go through an interaction with the donor PM6. Hence, the binding energy between donor and acceptor was calculated (polymer donor was simplified to three repeating units. Due to the huge dimer model, we mainly explore four models for each acceptor.), shown in Figure S6. It can be found that except when TB-4Cl or Y6 is in site 2, the binding energy is similar, but in other cases the binding energy of (PM6);:TB-4Cl is greater than that of $\left( \mathrm { P M } 6 \right) _ { 3 }$ :Y6. Higher binding energy means that the donor and acceptor are more tightly bound, probably resulting in good stability.

In the in-plane (IP) direction, the distinct lamellar packing peaks (100) are along $q _ { x \nu } = 0 . 3 0 \mathrm { ~ \normalfont ~ \AA ^ { - 1 } ~ } .$ whose corresponding lamellar distances $( d \doteq 2 \pi / | q | )$ are $2 0 . 9 { \mathrm { ~ \AA } } .$ In the OOP direction, there are the same $q _ { z } = 1 . 7 0$ A ( $\overset { \cdot } { d } = 3 . 7 0$ A), which is assigned to intermolecular $\pi - \pi$ stacking of TB4Cl, verifying that molecules in binary blend films tend to form a face-on orientation, contributing to charge transfer. Besides, the negligible differences barely prove the effect of thermal annealing on the morphology of blend films.

Based on the sensitivity to thermal annealing, we explore the thermal stability and air stability of the unencapsulated devices of PM6:TB-4Cl and PM6:Y6 based on the inverted structure (Figure 7). The specific data of $J _ { s c } , V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and FF are presented in Figures S9 and S10. As shown in Figure 7a, in the first $^ { 2 8 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ of heating up at $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ there is nearly no difference in devices based on PM6:TB-4Cl and PM6:Y6. However, with the devices heated continuously, PCE of the PM6:TB-4Cl-based devices declines less sharply than that of devices based on PM6:Y6. In the end, devices of PM6:TB-4Cl retain $7 5 \%$ of initial PCE and those of PM6:Y6 only retain approximately $6 0 \%$ . In Figure $^ { 7 \mathrm { b } , }$ it can be seen that the attenuation of devices based on PM6:TB-4Cl in air has been weaker than that of devices based on PM6:Y6 in air all the time. After being stored in air for $^ { 9 2 \ \mathrm { h } , }$ the devices based on PM6:TB-4Cl maintained $6 5 \%$ of initial PCE but those based on PM6:Y6 only retained $5 0 \%$ . In some cases, the stability can be attributed to the self-assembly of the acceptor and change of conformation in thermal or air condition. Then, we infer that due to the strong interaction between acceptor and acceptor/ donor, the acceptors' movement can be limited effectively so that it is hard to accumulate and change the conformation, which leads to the improved stability.46,47

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, on the basis of the A-D1A'D2-A structure, we successfully designed and synthesized an asymmetric acceptor TB-4Cl. Profiting from the asymmetric structure, TB-4Cl has a large dipole moment that can reinforce the intermolecular interaction. The distribution of ESP reveals that TB-4Cl has a more positive potential along the molecular skeleton and a more balanced positive and negative potential distribution on the terminal group, which are beneficial to charge transfer and $J { \mathrm { - } }$ aggregation. The OSCs based on PM6:TB-4Cl obtain $1 4 . 6 7 \%$ of PCE. It is interesting that all of the TB-4Cl-based OSCs exhibit very slight differences with or without thermalannealing treatment. The PCE of the unencapsulated PM6:TB-4Cl-based OSCs can still maintain about $7 5 \%$ after heating at $1 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ \mathrm { 1 1 0 \mathrm { h } }$ and above $6 5 \%$ in air for $9 2 \mathrm { h } ,$ both of which are higher than those of the PM6:Y6-based devices. The improved stability is attributed to strong intermolecular interaction. This work demonstrates that asymmetric SMAs can not only achieve high efficiency but also possess excellent stability, which have the potential to enter the solar cells market.

# EXPERIMENTAL SECTION

Synthesis of TB-4Cl. Compounds 2 and 3 were synthesized through the Stille coupling reaction in nitrogen and in the dark condition. Then, after Cadogen arylation reaction, the product reacted with 1-bromo-2-ethylhexane in the alkaline environment, eventually obtaining compound 4. Compound 5 was gained from the Vilsmeier-Haack reaction. Finally, via Knoevenagel, the dark blue solid TB-4Cl was obtained and purified by silica gel column chromatography and recrystallization. The specific synthetic process is described in the Supporting Information.

Device Fabrication. OsCs were fabricated in a traditional sandwich structure: ITO/PEDOT:PSS/active layer/PDINO/Al. First, the chloroform solution containing donor and acceptor with different weight ratios and concentrations was stirred for several hours in a nitrogen-filled glovebox. Then, after being cleaned with deionized water, acetone, and isopropanol, the ITO glass was treated with the UV-ozone for $2 0 ~ \mathrm { m i n }$ The PEDOT:PSS solution was spin-coated at $3 0 0 0 ~ \mathrm { r p m }$ onto the ITO substrates for $1 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ . While the PEDOT:PSS film was heated at $1 3 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ in air, 1-chloronaphthalene (CN) $( 0 . 5 \% , \bf { v } /$ v) was added into the chloroform solution. Then, the donor and acceptor were spin-coated on the PEDOT:PSs layer. With the PDINO spin-coated on the active layer, the devices were finally transferred to the evaporation tank to deposit $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ Al.
