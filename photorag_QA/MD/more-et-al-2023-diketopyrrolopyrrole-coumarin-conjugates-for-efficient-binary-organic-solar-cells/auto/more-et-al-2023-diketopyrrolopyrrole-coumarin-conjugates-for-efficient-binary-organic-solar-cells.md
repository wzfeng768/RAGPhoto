ABSTRACT: Two coumarin $\left( \mathrm { C o u } \right)$ -functionalized DPP derivatives, DPP-Cou and Cou-DPP-Cou, have been designed, synthesized, and characterized. The extension of the molecular backbone by an additional coumarin derivative in symmetrical Cou-DPP-Cou extends the absorption to ${ \sim } 7 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ $\left( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { \scriptsize ~ o p t } } = 1 . 6 7 \right.$ eV) compared to ${ \sim } 7 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ $\left( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \ \mathrm { \scriptsize ~ o p t } } \ = \ 1 . 7 8 \ \mathrm { \scriptsize ~ e V } \right)$ for asymmetric DPP-Cou. Thermogravimetric analysis showed higher thermal stability for symmetrical conjugates compared to the asymmetric one. Both materials showed complementary absorption spectra and well-matched Frontier molecular orbital energy levels with the low band gap fullerene-free acceptor Y7 and used as a donor in organic solar cells. After optimization using solvent vapor annealing treatment, the organic solar cells based on DPP-Cou:Y7 and Cou-DPP-Cou:Y7 attained overall power conversion efficiencies (PCE) of 9.63 and $1 3 . 4 8 \%$ respectively. The improved PCE for the Cou-DPP-Cou derivative is mainly due an appropriate morphology for faster charge extraction and lower charge recombination.

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Organic solar cells (OsCs) based on the bulk heterojunction (BHJ) active layer have emerged as one of the most promising green technologies owing to their advantages, such as costeffective solution-processed fabrication techniques, light weight, flexibility, large area printing, and semi-transparancy.1-7 In recent years, the power conversion effciency (PCE) of the OSCs based on the conjugated polymer as the donor and fullerene-free acceptor (FFA) attained PCEs in the range of $1 8 - 1 9 \%$ for the single junctions-13 and $2 0 \%$ for tandem solar cells,l4 respectively. At present, in all the effcient OSCs, the donors used are conjugated copolymers; however, these copolymers have many disadvantages, such as batch-tobatch product variation, difficult purifications, and high cost, which limit the commercialization of OsCs. As compared to the polymeric donor, small molecules exhibit distinct advantages of monodispersed molecular backbones with low batch-to-batch variation and could be advantageous for device applications.15-20 A recent investigation has shown that the allsmall-molecule OSCs have attained PCEs in the range of $^ { 1 6 - }$ $1 7 \%$ for small areas1-28 and $1 0 . 6 5 \%$ for $1 ~ { \mathrm { c m } } ^ { 2 }$ large-area osc processed by slot die-coating,29 which is comparable to OSCs based on the copolymer as a donor, indicating the potential of OSCs based on small-molecule donors.

Coumarin, a natural and synthetic heterocyclic compound, and their derivatives have been widely used for a range of applications such as dye-sensitized solar cells $^ { 3 0 - 3 2 } \mathrm { O S C s } , ^ { \forall 3 - 3 7 }$ organic light-emitting diodes,38 and as a fluorescence emitter39 because of their high stability, good structural flexibility, and intrinsic high photoluminescence (PL) quantum yields.4o A weakly fluorescent coumarin could exhibit excellent fluorescence on proper substitution on the core with various chromophores. Similarly, the optoelectronic properties of coumarin-based dyes can be tuned via proper substitution, which makes it a more fascinating and applicable candidate for real-world applications. OsCs based on coumarin derivatives have achieved excellent PCEs up to 15%.35,37

On the other hand, diketopyrrolopyrrole, i.e., 2,5- dihydropyrrolo[3,4-c]pyrrole-1,4-dione (DPP), a highly $\pi \cdot$ conjugated donor-acceptor chromophore, exhibited high absorption in the visible to near-infrared region, good thermal and mechanical stability, excellent electron affinity, and molecular planarity. The intense absorption of DPP derivatives in the visible and near-infrared spectra is due to the excitonic coupling in the solid state and extended $\pi \cdot$ conjugation.4' Due to such interesting properties, DPP was extensively used as an active material for high-effciency BHJOscs-4 ani hinfilm transistors,47,48 a1 and as fluorogenic probes.49 The attachment of alkyl chains in the nitrogen atoms of DPP derivatives can significantly enhance the solubility in organic solvents leading to the formation of excellent thin flms without crystallization.50 On the other hand, tuning 3,6-positions of the DPP core resulted in the perturbation of opto-electronic and chemical properties.1 The Frontier energy levels of the $\mathrm { D } { - } \mathbf { A } { - }$ based DPP derivatives can be easily adjusted by selecting appropriate functional building blocks, such as porphyrin,52,53 triarylamine,'4 and thiopheness derivatives, providing either ptype (donor) or n-type (acceptor) materials with efficient charge transport properties.+5,56 Using DPP-based small molecules as donor PCEs between 5 and $6 \%$ using fullerene derivatives44,57 and around $1 2 \%$ with FFAs have been achieved.8 The use of DPP-based small-molecule acceptors and polymer donors resulted in PCEs up to $1 1 . 5 \%$ in binary and $1 5 . 5 \%$ in ternary OsCs.52,59,60 Recently, all-small-molecule binary OSCs using DPP derivatives as the donor $( 1 1 . 6 1 \% ) ^ { 6 1 }$ or acceptor $( 9 . 0 5 \% ) ^ { \ \zeta s s }$ were also reported. For further development in the field, it is important to fine-tune molecular properties of DPP moieties by proper functionalization to improve the solar cell performance.

Based on the above facts to use coumarin as a donor (D) unit and DPP as a strong acceptor (A) unit, herein, for the first time, we have designed and synthesized two small-molecule diketopyrrolopyrrole-coumarin conjugates, i.e., Cou-DPP-Cou and DPP-Cou, and examined their optical and electrochemical properties. Optical absorption spectra of these conjugates are complementary to the FFA (Y7 in the present investigation), which is beneficial for light harvesting in a wide range of solar spectrum from 350 to $9 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ and can attain high short circuit current $\left( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \right)$ . Moreover, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels of both conjugates are properly aligned for efficient exciton dissociation and charge transfer. Binary OSCs prepared using Cou-DPP-Cou:Y7 and DPP-Cou:Y7 active layers after solvent vapor annealing (SVA) treatment exhibited PCEs of 13.48 and $9 . 6 3 \%$ , respectively.

2.4. Photovoltaic Properties. The photovoltaic performance of these two donors are examined and fabricated the OSC device with a conventional structure, i.e., glass/ITO/PEDOT/ PSS/active layer/PFN-Br/Al. The trap-assisted recombination in the devices is examined via the variation of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ with $P _ { \mathrm { i n } }$ The value of n obtained from the equation $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } =$ $\left( n k T / q \right) \ln \left( P _ { \mathrm { i n } } \right)$ for Cou-DPP-Cou-based OSCs is lower than that for the DPP-Cou counterpart, indicating that the trapassisted recombination is also suppressed for the Cou-DPPCou-based OSCs. The reduced bimolecular and trap-assisted recombination is beneficial for attaining the high values of FF and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } ,$ leading to high overall PCEs of OSCs based on CouDPP-Cou. The energy loss $\left( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } \right)$ for the OSCs can be predicted as $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } = E _ { \mathrm { g } } - q V _ { \mathrm { O C } } ,$ where $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ is the effective band gap of blend and is estimated from the $\mathrm { E Q E _ { o n s e t } } ^ { 6 5 }$ 65 The Eloss calculated for Cou-DPP-Cou and DPP-Cou are 0.46 and 0.50 $\mathrm { e V _ { \mathrm { 3 } } }$ respectively, suggesting reduced charge recombination for symmetrical Cou-DPP-Cou that could also support for higher $\dot { V } _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ 66,67

To investigate the effect of conjugation length toward charge extraction and extent of recombination in the devices, we have performed the transient photocurrent (TPC) decay and transient photovoltage (TPV) decay activities in these binary devices and shown in Figure 7. The charge extraction time estimated from the exponential decay of photocurrent are about $0 . 9 4 ~ \mu s$ for Cou-DPP-Cou and $1 . 0 5 ~ \mu s$ for DPP-Coubased devices, respectively. The lower value of charge extraction time indicates that after the exciton dissociation into free charge carriers, the holes and electrons are more effectively sweep-out and extracted by the anode and cathode in the Cou-DPP-Cou based OSCs than that for DPP-Cou counterparts, respectively. The charge carrier lifetimes are estimated from the TPV decay curves and are about 6.67 and 5.45 s for Cou-DPP-Cou- and DPP-Cou-based devices, respectively. The delayed charge carrier lifetime signifies the lower charge recombination in Cou-DPP-Cou-based OsCs. The faster charge extraction and lower charge recombination are well consistent with the higher $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ for Cou-DPP-Cou-based OSCs, which are also related to the high charge carrier mobilities and balanced charge transport.

Compared to DPP-Cou, with Cou-DPP-Cou, the $\pi - \pi$ and lamellar stacking distances were reduced and the corresponding CCLs were increased, signifying closer intermolecular stacking and improved crystallinity with an extended conjugation length (Figure S4). Moreover, the CCL of $\pi - \pi$ stacking are 2.16 and $2 . 2 4 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ for DPP-Cou:Y7 and CouDPP-Cou:Y7, respectively. The $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances for the DPP-Cu:Y7 and Cou-DPP-Cou:Y7 are about 0.372 and 0.366 nm, respectively. The sharper $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak with larger CCL and compact $\pi - \pi$ distance for Cou-DPP-Cou:Y7 suggests higher crystallinity and intermolecular packing, which is advantageous for the charge transport and consistent with the higher $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values. The results reveal that the improvement in the molecular crystallinity and packing by symmetry modulation of conjugated molecules can synergistically contribute to the optimization of blend morphology and thereby improve the OSC performance.

# 3. CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we have design, synthesized, and characterized two coumarin-diketopyrrolopyrrole conjugates for OSC applications. The molecules exhibited strong and broad absorption with low band gaps and low lying energy levels, which matched well with the Y7 acceptor. The Cou-DPPCou:Y7- and DPP-Cou:Y7-based binary BHJOSCs without any pretreatment generated PCEs of 9.72 and $6 . 3 7 \%$ respectively. After solvent vapor annealing with THF, the OSC based on the Cou-DPP-Cou:Y7 blends provided the best PCE of $1 3 . 4 8 \%$ which is among the highest value reported for DPP-based small-molecule donors. The higher performance for the Cou-DPP-Cou is mainly ascribed to the higher $J _ { \mathrm { S c } }$ and FF. These are obvious from the better absorption coverage from the 550 to $8 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { \ n m } }$ region, higher crystallinity, and intermolecular packing leading to a better morphology for Cou-DPP-Cou than that of DPP-Cou. Photophysical results further support the better charge transport and reduced recombination for the Cou-DPP-Cou:Y7 devices. The results demonstrated that the DPP-coumarin conjugate can be promising building block combinations for efficient OsCs. Structural modifications in this direction are under progress to further improve the OSC performance.
