ABSTRACT: Recently, many efforts have been devoted to developing novel polycyclic aromatics due to their unique optical and electronic properties and broad applications, such as in organic field-effect transistors, organic photovoltaics, and organic light-emitting diodes. Among various $\pi$ conjugated molecules, many truxene derivatives have interesting characteristics such as $C _ { 3 }$ -symmetry, strong blue emission, and a planar rigid structure. Moreover, compared with many other $\pi$ -conjugated aromatics, the synthesis and modification of truxene are particularly facile and diverse. In this account, we summarize investigations into truxene derivatives from synthesis and physical properties to applications in organic electronics.

Keywords: dendrimers, luminescence, organic semiconductors, self-assembly, truxenes

# 1. Introduction

Owing to their unique optoelectronic and self-assembly properties, resulting from delocalized $\pi$ electrons and a planar rigid structure, polycyclic aromatics have attracted great interest and present wide potential applications in organic electronic devices, such as organic field-effect transistors (OFETs),[1] organic photovoltaics (OPVs),2] and organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs).3] They have also been frequently used as building blocks to construct different supramolecular architectures through $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking and other kinds of intermolecular interactions. Therefore, the development of new polycyclic aromatics, especially large $\pi$ systems, in areas of organic electronics and functional nanomaterials has become a vital issue.

Among various polycyclic compounds, the heptacyclic polyarene truxene (10,15-dihydro- $. 5 H .$ diindeno $_ { 1 , 2 - \alpha ; 1 ^ { \prime } , 2 ^ { \prime } . }$ $c ]$ fuorene; Figure 1) has been recognized as an excellent starting material for the construction of larger polyarenes and bowlshaped fragments of fullerenes, liquid crystals, and $C _ { 3 }$ tripod materials in asymmetric catalysis and chiral recognition.4]

Truxene could be regarded as three fuorene moieties annulated to afford a two-dimensionally extended structure with threefold symmetry by sharing a central benzene ring. DFT calculations reveal that truxene has doubly degenerate HOMO and LUMO (Figure1) because of its threefold symmetry. Owing to the unique characteristics, including a planar and $C _ { 3 }$ symmetric structure, simple synthetic and functionalization methods, and electronic and optical properties, truxene derivatives have been widely utilized in various electronic and optical applications, such as OFETs, OPV cells, OLEDs, and data storage.

Herein, the synthesis, self-assembly, and optoelectronic applications of polycyclic aromatics based on truxene are reviewed. We discuss the design strategy; optical and electronic properties; and their device performance in OFETs, OPVs, and OLEDs.

# 2. Preparation of Truxene Derivatives

Generally, truxene can be synthesized by the acid-catalyzed trimerization of 1-indaones or 3-phenylpropionic acids, which could cyclize to give 1-indaones under acid reaction conditions. Because of poor solubility, truxene could not be further purified by recrystallization or flash column chromatography. However, the application of truxenes in organic electronic materials and supramolecular organization generally requires truxenes with reasonable solubility in common solvents. Thus, alkyl chains are usually introduced at the C5, C10, and C15 positions under basic conditions (Figure 2).

# 2.1. Dendrimers

Dendrimers, which are derived from the Greek word dendron, are characterized as regularly three-dimensional branched and monodisperse architectures with various properties that can be tailored and controlled, such as the size and shape of the molecule and the position of functional groups in the core of the molecule, in the branching units, and/or on the exterior surface. A substantial number of earlier works have focused on dendrimers with flexible cores and interiors, which significantly affect the overall shapes. By contrast, in the last two decades, there has been a dramatic expansion in the field of $\pi$ -conjugated dendrimers with rigid structures.7] These shapepersistent dendrimers exhibit interesting physical and chemical properties because of well-defined nanometer sizes and intrinsic rigidity. However, the building blocks of such dendrimers are limited to small and simple aromatics, such as benzene, thiophene, fuorene, and phenylene acetylene. Consequently, new families of $\pi$ -conjugated dendrimers with a novel conjugated core and branch segments have been developed to broaden their applications.

Truxene, by virtue of its unique $C _ { 3 }$ topology, is an attractive building motif for dendrimers through readily available functionalization at the C2, C7, and C12 positions and at the C5, C10, and C15 positions.

Starting from alkylated truxene, we developed novel dendrimers ${ \bf 1 } _ { O }$ and ${ \bf 1 } _ { I }$ (the first number represents the type of dendrimer and the subscript number represents the generation number of the dendrimers) containing up to nine truxene moieties in good yield through repeated FriedelCrafts acetylation and acid-promoted cyclotrimerization reactions[8] (Figure 3). It is worth mentioning that the benzene core was generated in situ from aryl methyl ketones by a triple aldolization reaction followed by dehydration. These dendrimers exhibit precise molecular weights $\mathrm { \Delta } M _ { \mathrm { w } } = 7 9 0 8$ for $1 _ { I _ { \mathrm { + } } }$ ) and large sizes of up to $5 . 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ in the planar conformation, as determined by molecular modeling. The absorption $\lambda _ { \operatorname* { m a x } }$ and $\lambda _ { \mathrm { o n s e t } }$ values for truxene, ${ \bf 1 } _ { O } ,$ and ${ \bf 1 } _ { I }$ show a small redshift with increasing generation number of the dendrimers, which indicates that the large torsion angle between the giant truxene moiety and benzene ring might affect full $\pi$ -delocalization of the whole molecules. The utilization of truxene as a building block breaks the fixed idea of constructing dendrimers with simple and small aromatics and provides new inspiration for designing novel $\pi$ -conjugated dendrimers.

Truxene is not limited to acting as branches in dendrimers. We synthesized a family of $\pi$ -conjugated dendrimers, ${ \bf 2 } _ { o }$ and $_ { 2 _ { l } , }$ based on truxene and thienylethynylene through a mixed divergent/convergent growth approach.9l Oligo (thienylethynylene)s (OTEs), which are known as molecular wires in electronic devices,10 are designed to serve as bridges with different lengths to connect the truxene cores (Figure 4). Longer OTEs near the center of the dendrimer not only allow the energy to be funneled inward, but also entail a greater void at the molecule interior and reduce congestion around building blocks in higher generations. The absorption intensity increases remarkably by increasing the generation of the dendrimers due to enhancement of the effective conjug. ation length. Compared with previous truxene-containing dendrimers synthesized through cyclotrimerization, the introduction of thienylethynylene greatly decreases the torsion angles between branches and cores, making the $\pi$ -conjugated skeleton more planar. The molar extinction coefficient of $_ { 2 _ { I } }$ is $2 . 7 \times 1 0 ^ { 8 } \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ .which makes it promising as a lightharvesting material.

Using universal force field (UFF) methods, these dendrimers exhibit a relatively flat conformation and the maximum radii extend rapidly with increasing generation number $3 . 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ for $2 _ { o } , 5 . 7 \ \mathrm { n m }$ for $^ { 2 } { \boldsymbol { \mathbf { \mathit { \varepsilon } } } } _ { }$ and $9 . 6 \mathrm { n m }$ for $\pmb { 2 } _ { 2 } )$ Notably, dendrimer ${ \bf 2 } _ { 2 }$ could even be observed by AFM owing to its supersized molecular structure. A similar strategy has also been applied to construct different shape-persistent conjugated dendrimers by using other conjugated branches. We made use of stilbenoid units as the bridge to link the trifunctional truxene cores (Figure 5).[11) Through efficient Suzuki and Horner-Wadsworth-Emmons reactions, dendrimers weighing up to 10973 Da were developed. According to molecular modeling, the diameter of $\mathbf { 4 } _ { 2 }$ containing 22 truxene units is about $7 . 9 \mathrm { n m }$ . # 2.2. Star-Shaped Aromatics

Star-shaped molecules have branched structures that consist of linear arms joined together by a central core. Similar to dendrimers, star-shaped molecules possess well-defined molecular structures as well as superior chemical purity. In addition, they retain the solution-processability and precisely designable properties of polymers in device fabrication. A starshaped architecture with conjugated arms would bring new electrical, optical, and morphological properties to the system.

We described a family of well-defined star-shaped compounds with two chromophores (truxene as the core and oligothiophenes as the branches) to understand the effect of conjugation length on optical properties (Figure7).3]

Through repetitive bromination and cross-coupling reactions, star-shaped compounds 5-8 with oligothiophene branches of different conjugated length were obtained. A continuous redshift of the absorption spectra was observed with a successive increase in the number of thiophene rings, which suggests the formation of a highly extended $\pi$ -delocalized system through the truxene core.

Maintaining the same truxene core, star-shaped molecules with different branches were investigated. When introducing oligo $\dot { \boldsymbol { p } }$ -phenylene) as the branches,[14] not only the absorption peaks redshifted, but also the glass transition temperatures increased with increasing effective conjugation length. Dendrimers  bearing  oligo(fluorene  ethynylene)15] and oligofluorene[16] branches were also developed and displayed similar redshifts of the absorption spectra with increasing effective conjugated length.

The construction of star-shaped molecules is not restrained by linking core and branches through single bonds. With the aim of expanding the library of truxene derivatives, a family of truxene-based polycyclic aromatics containing hexaperi-hexabenzocoronene (HBC) was constructed (Figure 8).[17)

Various aryl groups as the antenna were attached to the truxene arms, which efficiently enhanced intraand intermolecular energy transfer.

# 2.3. Dumbbell-Shaped Molecules

Dumbbell-shaped molecules couple two chromophore halves through either a flexible alkyl spacer or a rigid aromatic linkage. As another way to extend the $\pi$ -conjugated length, the synthetic approach to dumbbell-shaped molecules is more facile compared with that of star-shaped molecules. Due to the $C _ { 3 }$ symmetric structure, truxene plays the role of conjugated spacers or end groups rather than cores in the dumbbell-shaped molecules.

A series of extended $\pi$ -conjugated twin molecules based on truxene were synthesized, of which the truxene halves were connected by rigid 9,9-spirobifluorenylene, ethynylene, or vinylene linkages by $\mathrm { P d } ^ { 0 }$ catalyzed Suzuki coupling, Sonogashira coupling, and McMurry reactions, respectively (Figure 13).[24) The large steric end groups of truxenes effectively suppress the formation of aggregates of these $\pi$ -conjugated molecules. The optical properties of the twin molecules are strongly affected by the $\pi$ -linkages owing to their different $\pi$ -conjugated lengths and torsion angles with truxene. When truxenes were introduced as end groups into the OTE molecular wire system,[25] the ability to form a film was greatly enhanced, which facilitated their application in optoelectronic devices. In addition, the excited-state lifetime of OTEs converts from single to double exponential.

# 3. Self-Assembly to Supramolecular Organization

To arrive at organic electronics based on $\pi$ conjugated systems, a detailed understanding of the supramolecular interactions between individual $\pi$ -conjugated molecules has become one of the most challenging scientific research areas. Supramolecules can be formed by the self-assembly of organic molecules through different kinds of intermolecular interactions. The development of supramolecular chemistry paves the way to craft the growth of organic nanomaterials.

# 3.1. - Stacking

Among all noncovalent interactions, $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking is considered as one of the main driving forces for the formation of a 1D assembly of organic conjugated molecules. By modulating the size of conjugated planes; the type and length of the side chains; or the external conditions, such as temperature, solvent, or concentration, different morphologies, including wires and tubes, were achieved.

We developed a family of potential n-type semiconductors 37-39 based on truxenone, and investigated their different self-assembly behavior in various solvents (Figure 16).[27] As the oxidized counterpart of truxene, truxenone has three carbonyl groups mounted on the conjugated plane, which greatly reduces the electron density of the aromatic core, making it a candidate to transport electrons as an n-type material. Our results show that the substitution positions and the number of substituents on the conjugated core have a significant influence not only on the properties at the molecular level, but also on the assembly behavior during the formation of 1D microstructures. The dramatic difference in their self-assembly behavior, such as different morphologies and response towards solvent polarity, is rooted in their different intermolecular interactions, such as dispersion and electrostatic interactions.

The truxenone derivatives were synthesized by alkylation and oxidation in one pot under mild conditions. In follow-up work, we changed the solvent to the protic solvent ethanol and obtained nonoxidized product 41 under the same conditions; this indicated that the oxidation step involved the generation of carbon anions at the 5, 10, and 15 positions of truxene (Figure 17).28] The close match of the molecular size, shape, and symmetry between 41 and 42 presents an ideal platform to study the electron donor and acceptor (EDA) interactions. The absorption spectra and $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR spectroscopy experiments demonstrated the 1:1 stoichiometry of the complex, as expected for EDA interactions. As a step further, the co-assembly behavior of 41 and 42 was investigated in the bulk phase. The differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) and X-ray diffraction results indicated the formation of a new phase in the co-assembled 1D microstructures. The EDA interaction offers the possibility of creating one-dimensional pathways for both holes and electrons, while at the same time maintaining a large interface. In addition, the EDA interaction leads to a facecentered geometry in molecular stacking, which maximizes $\pi$ overlap between aromatic planes.

# 3.2. Hydrogen Bonding

Because of serious self-quenching in $\pi { - } \pi$ systems, it is challenging to obtain highly fuorescent self-assembled nanowires. On the other hand, hydrogen bonding that is geometrically matching and directional provides another interaction to form 1D nanowires with various properties. This can be considered as a potential solution to construct organic conjugated selfassembly structures for optoelectronic applications in chemical and biological fields.

We reported a supramolecular polymer constructed by multiple hydrogen bonds (Figure 18).[29] The threedimensional monomers have a shape-persistent core (truxene) with three conjugated arms (fluorene) perpendicular to the planar core. The introduction of multiple hydrogen bonds to the rigid 3D monomer not only provides the strong tendency of self-assembly in the vertical direction, but also lowers the possibility of the mismatch of hydrogen bonds. Moreover, the rigid 3D structure effectively reduces selfquenching in the solid state, leading to a high fuorescence efficiency in the solid state. As a result, nanofibers with a monomolecular width were formed from 44 along the direction of the hydrogen bonds in dilute solution, and possibly they were subunits of larger nanowires precipitated from concentrated solution.

We mounted benzothiadiazole and porphyrin units as the emitting chromophores on the side chains and observed the FRET process from rigid backbones to side chains both in dilute solution and in the solid state. When excited at the maximum absorption of the skeleton of the monomers, the emission from the functionalized side groups appeared, and the emission from the skeleton was dramatically quenched. The photophysical properties of the side groups were not affected by self-assembly in the solid state, which is rarely achieved in $\pi { - } \pi$ systems.

Compared with $\pi { - } \pi$ interactions and hydrogen bonding, the host-guest interaction is a more robust and directional noncovalent force. Applied in mechanical assemblies, the interaction between dibenzo[24]crown-8 and dibenzyl ammonium moieties has proved to be a reliable building style.31]

We developed a mechanically interlocked light-harvesting system, [3]rotaxane 45, efficiently through $\mathrm { { C u ^ { I } } }$ catalyzed azide-alkyne cycloaddition (Figure 20).32) Due to its easy functionalization, high fuorescence quantum efficiency, and suitable photophysical properties, the hexyl-substituted truxene units were introduced into the wheels as D units. Meanwhile, the oligo(para-phenylene vinylene) (OPPV) unit was introduced into the axis as the A. The azide-alkyne cycloaddition was demonstrated to be an efficient stoppering method in the synthesis of the rotaxane containing dibenzo[24]crown-8 and dibenzyl ammonium units, for which the yield was up to $8 0 \%$ . Moreover, in this [3]rotaxane, effcient energy transfer from the truxene-functionalized wheels to the OPPV-based axis through the dibenzo[24]crown-8 and dibenzyl ammonium interaction was observed, even in dilute solution. The unique topology of [3]rotaxane not only efficiently promotes the intramolecular energy-transfer process, but also prevents intermolecular aggregation in the solid state. When the A units were replaced with a zinc porphyrin, the same experimental results were obtained.33] Interestingly, the porphyrin-containing rotaxane showed a reversible conformational change in non-coordinating solvents, such as $\mathrm { C H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { C l } _ { 2 }$ which would be inhibited in a coordinating solvent, such as THF. Such interesting behavior was rarely observed in porphyrin derivatives.

Moreover, efficient energy transfer from the truxenefunctionalized wheels to the OPV-based axis could also be observed in hyperbranched[34] and linear[35] polyrotaxanes. By using dynamic/static light scattering and AFM, we investigated the topology of their aggregation, including ball-shaped nanoparticles and rigid rods, caused by different molecular structures.

# 4. Truxenes in Organic Electronics

# 4.1. OFETs

OFETs, the characteristics of which are modulated by an external electric field, are considered to be the most prominent constituents of modern microelectronics for fabricating flexible displays, sensors, and radiofrequency identification devices. For further improvement of the device performance, new materials with desired properties are needed and an in-depth understanding of the structure-property relationships is crucial.

A series of star-shaped oligothiophene-functionalized truxene derivatives 5-7 developed by our groups were demonstrated to be good air-stable materials in p-channel OFETs. An increase in the oligothiophene length of the star-shaped molecules from 1 to 3 thiophenes leads to a dramatic decrease in hole mobility from $1 . 0 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 }$ to $2 . 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ . By using powder X-ray diffraction and scanning electron microscopy, we clearly observed a transition from a polycrystalline to an amorphous state with a stepwise increase in the number of thiophenes at each branch. Therefore, the decrease in mobility may be attributed to the increase in the amorphous properties upon lengthening the thiophene arms.

# 4.2. Bulk-Heterojunction Solar Cells

As mentioned in the preparation of truxene derivatives, a number of $\pi$ -conjugated dendrimers and star-shaped molecules were developed because of their well-defined molecular structures, high purity, and strong absorption. A high molar extinction coefficient over a broad absorption region is a basic requirement for organic solar cell materials. Moreover, compared with small molecules with poor film-forming properties, the introduction of truxenes with hexyl substituents significantly decreases the aggregation of truxene derivatives and enhances the phase separation of D and A units in bulkheterojunction solar cells.

We synthesized two gradient $\pi$ -conjugated dendrimers, in which truxene units were employed as the nodes and oligo(thienylene vinylene)s (OTVs) with different lengths were employed as light-harvesting branching arms (Figure 21).36)

The introduction of truxenes dramatically improves the fluorescence quantum yields of dendrimers. In addition, these dendrimers maintain high molar extinction coefficients over a broad absorption region as well as good hole-transportation properties from the OTVs, both of which help to improve the light-harvesting ability of the dendrimers. Bulk-heterojunction solar cells with these gradient $\pi$ -conjugated dendrimers as D units and phenyl- $C _ { 6 1 }$ -butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) as the A unit showed a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of up to $0 . 4 0 \%$ . The relatively low PCE can be attributed, in part, to modest matching of the absorption spectra of these dendrimers with the solar spectrum.

To broaden the absorption bands of solar cell materials, designing $\pi$ -conjugated D units with a D-A structure is supposed to be an effective method to modulate absorption bands. We prepared a series of star- (48, 49) and dumbbell-shaped (33-35) D--A molecules based on three chromophores (truxenes as nodes, TPA moieties as the D units, and benzothiadiazole chromophores as the A units; Figure 22).[37] All D-t-A conjugated molecules show broad and strong absorption bands from 250 to $7 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ in thin films. By increasing the D-A ratio and changing the conjugated spacers between D and A, the PCE values of solar cells with these D--A molecules as the D and PCBM as the A continuously increased due to the increasing relative absorption intensity in the longer wavelength region. As a result, a PCE value of up to $0 . 5 4 \%$ was obtained.

# 4.3. OLEDs

Blue-emitting materials have attracted considerable interest due to their particular importance in the development of OLEDs for large-area fat-panel displays. Truxene can be regarded as three fluorene units annulated by sharing a central benzene. As a consequence, most truxene derivatives inherit the excellent blue-emitting property of fuorene, presenting great potential in blue-emitting OLED materials. However, the long-wavelength emission owing to the formation of ketone defects or excimers/aggregates prevents fluorene derivatives from being excellent blue emitters. Therefore, to develop truxene-based, highly stable blue emitters, rational molecular design to prevent the long-wavelength emission is required.

Although the absorption and emission bands are similar, the molar extinction coefficients (e) of 53 and 54 are almost 10 times larger than that of ter $( 9 , 9 ^ { \prime } )$ spirobifluorene). In addition, the emission spectra of these blue emitters are more thermally stable than those of alkyl-substituted polyfluorenes due to their rigid spiro architectures. To investigate the electroluminescence (EL) properties of these new blue emitters, OLEDs with 53-56 as the light-emitting layer were first fabricated in a configuration of indium tin oxide (ITO)/poly(3,4- ethylenedioxythiophene) (PEDOT)/poly(V-vinylcarbazole) (PVK)/1/1,3,5-tris-(2-NV-phenylbenzimidazolyl)benzene (TPBI)/Ba/Al. We further introduced an electron-rich unit (carbazole) and electron-deficient moieties (1,2,4-triazole and 2,5- diphenyl-1,3,4-oxadiazole) to this three-dimensional spirolinked truxene skeleton (Figure 25).(39] The modification of the chemical structures of the light-emitting molecules is also an efficient way to improve charge injection. Through the incorporation of charge-transport moieties on the main chain and side chains, balanced hole and electron transport was achieved in the emitting layer, and, at the same time, the complexity of devices was reduced.

# 5. Summary and Outlook

In this account, recent work from our group on the construction and application of truxene derivatives has been summarized. Due to the planar and $C _ { 3 }$ symmetric structure of truxene, many beautiful dendrimers, as well as star- and dumbbell-shaped molecules, have been synthesized. They exhibited good properties as organic semiconductors in optoelectronic devices. The development of organic electronics is advancing rapidly. Molecular design has led to remarkable advances in the creation of various organic semiconductors with diverse structures and properties. Truxene, as one of the giant polycyclic aromatic units, undoubtedly opens the door to developing high-performance semiconductors with new synthetic methods and new $\pi$ -conjugated building blocks.

On the other hand, as we mentioned above, most truxene derivatives are amorphous rather than crystalline in the solid state, which indicates that intermolecular packing is not ordered owing to the steric hindrance of alkyl chains and other groups at the C5, C10, and C15 positions, which results in a relatively low performance in organic electronic devices such as OFETs and solar cells. Moreover, the absorption spectra of truxene derivatives are not broad enough compared with some small molecules that show high PCEs when blended with PCBM in solar cells. As a consequence, the rational design of the molecular structure and intermolecular interactions is indispensable, especially the latter, which has a significant effect on the self-assembly behavior, charge transport between molecules, and further device performance. Through structure design and optimization, we look forward to achieving remarkable developments based on truxene derivatives in the future.
