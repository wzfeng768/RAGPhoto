Two novel isomeric polymers P(DPPT-TT) and P(DPPTT-T) based on thiophene-flanked diketopyrrolopyrrole, thieno[3,2-b]thiophene and thiophene building units were designed and synthesized via a new skeletal isomerization strategy. This study emphasizes on the impact of this skeletal isomerization on the photoelectric properties and organic field-effect transistor (OFET) performances of the resulting isomeric polymers. Both polymers show similar light absorption properties and excellent thermostability and have a comparable molecular weight. They also display typical $\mathsf { p }$ -type characteristics in air-tested OFETs. However, P(DPPTT-T) exhibits significantly better OFET performances. In particular, its average hole mobility reaches $2 . 1 4 ~ { \mathsf { c m } } ^ { 2 } ~ { \mathsf { V } } ^ { - 1 } ~ { \mathsf { s } } ^ { - 1 }$ , triple that of P(DPPT-TT). The higher mobility of P(DPPTT-T) might be attributed to the interconnected, smooth and thicker nanofibrillar network morphology and the relatively higher crystallinity of its thin films, and its conjugated chains possess a smaller $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance.

# 1. Introduction

The design and application of solution-processable conjugated polymer materials allow significant progress in promising organic optoelectronic devices.1–8 Among them, although p-type, n-type or ambipolar types of polymers with high field-effect mobility are gaining attention,9–14 the development of polymerbased semiconductor materials for application in organic fieldeffect transistors (OFETs) still presents lots of new challenges and new opportunities for researchers. For instance, how to design new electron-deficient aromatic building blocks based on polymers or new types of polymers, and how to review and meet the challenges of previous cognition on the association between polymer molecular structures and their charge transfer performance? Based on the reason above, a particular and interesting issue of research on isomeric conjugated polymers has gained our strong attention. The introduction of the isomerization concept into conjugated polymers is expected to be studied based on a specific perspective and give rise to a new research topic. We have reviewed and summarized the various side-chain or substituent isomerization processes and the skeletal isomerization types of conjugated polymers for OFETs (Scheme 1). Some representative examples15–28 are also listed in the ESI. $^ \dagger$ First, diversified isomeric polymers are fascinating and have received relatively limited attention. Second, they can exhibit differential photoelectric performances (e.g. charge carrier mobility), which contributes to revealing the relationship between the polymer molecular structure and performance of devices. Third, it is noted that they possess similar molecular weight parameters to compare reasonably. Among them, type (g) isomeric polymers have gained little attention to our knowledge, and it arrests our attention preferentially.

Incorporation of asymmetric units into polymer backbones provides a foundation for developing type (g) isomeric polymers. Diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP), one of the promising building blocks, has been widely explored via flanking symmetric aromatic groups (Scheme 2a) for constructing high-performance polymer semiconductors for OFETs.29–48 However, asymmetric aromatic group-flanked DPP (Scheme 2a)-based high-performance polymers have gained much less attention.49–54 Two alternative routes for preparing the asymmetric type of DPP unit are shown in Scheme 2b. In previous studies, the asymmetric type of DPP unit was obtained mainly by route A, and the studies usually focused on the effect of the flanked aromatic groups or/and $\pi$ conjugated units on the resulting polymers.49–54 In this work, we have designed new asymmetric types of DPP units via route B, and then focused on investigating the effect of skeletal isomerization on the resulting type (g) polymers (Scheme 2c). Specifically, two new asymmetric types of DPP based isomeric polymers,

Side-chain or substituent isomerization namely P(DPPT-TT) and $\mathbf { P ( D P P T T - T ) }$ (Fig. 1), were designed, synthesized and applied in OFETs. P(DPPT-TT)-based OFETs showed an average hole transport mobility of $0 . 7 3 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ with a current on/off ratio $\left( I _ { \mathrm { o n } } / I _ { \mathrm { o f f } } \right)$ of $2 . 5 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ and a threshold voltage $\left( V _ { \mathrm { T } } \right)$ of $1 0 . 2 \mathrm { V } .$ . However, $\mathbf { P ( D P P T T - T ) }$ -based OFETs showed more excellent hole transport properties with the average mobility reaching $2 . 1 4 ~ { \mathrm { c m } } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { \mathbf { V } } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { \mathbf { s } } ^ { - 1 }$ and the $I _ { \mathrm { o n } } / I _ { \mathrm { o f f } }$ around $4 . 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ and the $V _ { \mathrm { T } }$ below $2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ .

# 2. Discussion

# 2.1 Synthesis and characterization

The synthetic routes to asymmetric DPP isomeric polymers are shown in Scheme 3. The readily-available intermediate 1 was synthesized using a literature method.29 Both of the target asymmetric monomers M1 and M2 were easily synthesized from 1 followed by monobromination, Stille-coupling and bisbromination with a total yield of $2 7 . 5 \%$ and $3 8 . 5 \%$ respectively. Typical Stille cross-coupling polycondensation of 2,5-bis(trimethylstannyl)thieno[3,2-b]thiophene with monomer M1 and 2,5-bis(trimethylstannyl)thiophene with monomer M2 was successfully performed to prepare crude products of the polymers. They were then purified by Soxhlet extraction with dried methanol, petroleum ether, and toluene, and then with dried chloroform. The last chloroform fractions were collected, followed by precipitation with methanol to afford the final target DPP isomeric polymers $\mathbf { P ( D P P T - T T ) }$ and P(DPPTT-T). Their structures were characterized by $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR spectroscopy, elemental analysis and infrared spectrometry (Fig. S1, $\mathrm { E S I \dag }$ . Both polymers show good solubility at room temperature in chloroform, chlorobenzene, etc. They also exhibit high thermostability at decomposition temperatures $\left( T _ { \mathrm { d } } \right)$ over $4 1 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (Fig. S2, $\mathrm { E S I \dag }$ ) and their differential scanning calorimetry curves show that there are no obvious thermal transitions at the preset temperature range of $4 0 { - } 2 8 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (Fig. S3, ESI†). The molecular weight information of $\mathbf { P ( D P P T - T T ) }$ and $\mathbf { P ( D P P T T - T ) }$ was obtained by gel permeation chromatography (GPC) in chloroform $( 2 5 \ ^ { \circ } \mathbf { C } )$ and 1,3,5-trichlorobenzene (TCB) $\left( 1 5 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } \right)$ with a polystyrene standard calibration, respectively (Fig. S4 and S5, ESI†). Both polymers possess high tendency to aggregate at room temperature. They also show a similar molecular weight and polydispersity index, which contributes to a reasonable comparison.

# 2.2 Theoretical calculations

As shown in Fig. 2, the dimers of both P(DPPT-TT) and P(DPPTT-T) display three types of bonding modes, resulting in different gap units between the two neighboring DPP units. The former has I, II and III units and the latter has I, IV and V units. We carried out theoretical calculations to compare their backbone structure and frontier molecular orbital (FMO) energy levels and electron density distributions (the R group was simplified as methyl groups) (Fig. S6, ESI†). The absolute values of the backbone dihedral angles in these model molecules are less than $2 0 ^ { \circ }$ , indicating good co-planarity of the conjugated backbones. These model molecules also exhibit relatively similar FMO energy levels. The calculated electron densities of their lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels and highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels are well delocalized on the conjugated backbones. These results indicate that both polymers possess good backbones for charge carrier transport.

# 3. Conclusions

In conclusion, two new isomeric DPP polymers, $\mathbf { P ( D P P T - T T ) }$ and P(DPPTT-T), were designed and synthesized. Both polymers display similar physical and chemical properties and exhibit p-type charge-transport characteristics. Compared with $\mathbf { P ( D P P T - T T ) } .$ based OFETs, P(DPPTT-T)-based OFETs have a triple average mobility of $2 . 1 4 \ \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { \bf ~ V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { \bf ~ s } ^ { - 1 }$ under the same conditions, because P(DPPTT-T) films possessed an interconnected, smooth and thicker nanofibrillar network morphology, and a favourable ‘‘edge-on’’ molecular orientation and a relatively smaller $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance. These results demonstrate that the new skeletal isomerization strategy can be applied to optimize the molecular structure of high-performance semiconductors for application in OFETs.
