# Intrinsically Stretchable Polymer Semiconductors with Good Ductility and High Charge Mobility through Reducing the Central Symmetry of the Conjugated Backbone Units

Xiaobo Yu, Liangliang Chen, Cheng Li,\* Chenying Gao, Xiang Xue, Xisha Zhang, Guanxin Zhang, and Deqing Zhang\*

Intrinsically stretchable polymer semiconductors are highly demanding for flexible electronics. However, it still remains challenging to achieve synergy between intrinsic stretchability and charge transport property properly for polymer semiconductors. In this paper, terpolymers are reported as intrinsically stretchable polymeric semiconductors with good ductility and high charge mobility simultaneously by incorporation of non-centrosymmetric spiro[cycloalkane-1, $\bullet ^ { \prime }$ -fluorene] (spiro-fluorene) units into the backbone of diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP) based conjugated polymers. The results reveal that these terpolymers show obviously high crack onset strains and their tensile moduli are remarkably reduced, by comparing with the parent DPP-based conjugated polymer without spiro-fluorene units. They exhibit simultaneously high charge mobilities $( > 1 . 0 \mathsf { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathsf { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathsf { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ at $100 \%$ strain and even after repeated stretching and releasing cycles for 5o0 times under $50 \%$ strain. The terpolymer P2, in which cyclopropane is linked to the spiro-fluorene unit, is among the best reported intrinsically stretchable polymer semiconductors with record mobility up to $3 . 7 \mathsf { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathsf { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathsf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ at even $150 \%$ strain and $\mathsf { 1 . 4 c m ^ { 2 } V ^ { - 1 } } \mathsf { s ^ { - 1 } }$ after repeated stretching and releasing cycles for 1o0o times.

# 1. Introduction

Polymer semiconductors have been considered as the most promising materials for the next generation flexible electronics which will be utilized in wearable and implantable devices, due to their soft nature.1-9] The application scenarios of flexible electronics require semiconductors to possess comprehensive properties including high charge mobility, excellent mechanical fexibility, and stable semiconducting performance during long-term mechanical deformations.10-13] Over the past few years, new strategies have been explored to design and prepare stretchable polymer semiconductors. For instance, blending semiconducting polymer with elastomer has become a powerful way to endow semiconducting polymer with high stretchability.[14-16] It was also reported that polymer semiconductors with curvilinear microstructures can exhibit high mobility and stretchability simultaneously,[17] by delicate thin film geometry design and complex manufacturing process. In comparison with physical blending, developing intrinsically stretchable polymer semiconductors is a straightforward approach to simplify the fabrication process and thus hold tremendous potentials for highly-integrated devices and flexible electronics.[18-25]

However, it is a great challenge to integrate excellent electrical and mechanical properties in one semiconducting polymer through molecular design. Close packing of polymer skeletons is beneficial for charge carriers to transport from one molecule to the neighbors, and long-range ordered aggregates can reduce the probability of charge being trapped or scattered.7,26] Unfortunately, these characteristics can make polymer semiconductors rigid and brittle. As a result, the tensile moduli of most high mobility polymer semiconductors are several hundred MPa or higher, which may lead to significant degradation of semiconducting performance under deformation and thus largely reduce lifetime of the flexible devices.[27,28] In recent years, many endeavors have been focused on balancing the trade-off between charge mobility and stretchability. Hydrogen bonding units,[29,30] metal-ligand units,[31] small molecule additives,[32-34] and non-conjugated alkane units[35] were introduced into conjugated polymers to reduce the tensile moduli. Most of these cases are at the sacrifice of charge mobility in varying degrees, because of partial breakage of conjugation or weakening interchain packing order. Only in a few cases, stretchability of polymer semiconductors was boosted, while charge mobility was maintained or even enhanced.[23,24,36] These include i) the incorporation of ligand units into the conjugated backbone which can form weak cross-links between polymer chains through metal-ligand coordination,[36] ii) the partial substitution of the backbone units with conjugated rigid fused rings with bulky side chains,[23] and iii) the introduction of new co-monomer units into the backbones to generate conjugated random terpolymers.[24]

The key issue lies on synergistically regulating the mutually contradictory demands of micro-structures for charge mobility and stretchability. Thin films of semiconducting polymers usually involve complex multi-scale microstructures that are composed of crystalline domains and amorphous domains. Although the relationship between mechanical modulus and thin film crystallinity in different polymer semiconductors is elusive,[22] for a specific polymer semiconductor, it is generally considered that the modulus in amorphous domain is lower, while charge transport in crystalline domains is more efficient. The strain energy mainly dissipates in amorphous domain along with morphology evolution under deformation.37] It is generally accepted that small crystalline domains evenly distributed in amorphous domains is the ideal morphology for stretchable polymer semiconductors with high charge mobility.[33,37]

The random terpolymer strategy for constructing stretchable semiconducting polymers has been explored recently.[24] But, such terpolymers with good ductility and high charge mobility simultaneously are still limited. Moreover, the fundamental correlations between crystalline microstructure, mechanical, and charge transport properties need to be further clarified. Herein we report terpolymers (P2-P5, Scheme 1) as intrinsically stretchable polymer semiconductors by incorporating spiro[cycloalkane ${ \cdot } 1 , 9 ^ { \prime }$ fluorene] (spiro-fluorene) units into the conjugated backbones, aiming to tailoring the thin film crystallinity as illustrated in Scheme 1. These terpolymers entailing diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP), bithiophene, and spiro-fuorene units in their backbones exhibit good ductility and high charge mobility simultaneously. The selection of spiro-fluorene as the co-monomer unit is explained as follows: i) the backbone of DPP-based conjugated polymer P1 (see Scheme 1) is composed of DPP flanked by two thiophenes and bithiophene, which are centrosymmetric. Such structural feature of conjugated backbone is favorable for the formation of large ordered domains, and consequently DPPbased polymers usually show high charge mobilities;[38-46] ii) incorporation of spiro-fuorene units into the backbones to partially replace DPP (moieties) flanked by two thiophenes (as in P2-P6) will reduce the central symmetry of conjugated backbones. Moreover, the spiro-fuorene units can be randomly distributed into the conjugated backbones. Thus, it is expected that incorporation of spiro-fluorene units into the backbones is not favorable for the formation of large crystalline domains and thus thin film crystallinity of the conjugated polymers is reduced; iii) the spiro-fluorene units with cycloalkane rings show less steric hindrance by comparing with the DPP units with branching alkyl chains and the commonlyused linear alkyl-chain substituted fuorenes. Such structural feature of spiro-fluorene units is beneficial for interchain dense packing and thus charge transporting for the terpolymers; iv) the different membered rings on the spiro-fluorene units may provide additional possibility for finely tuning thin film crystallinity. The results show that thin film crystallinity for terpolymers is reduced by comparing with the parent polymer P1 without spiro-fluorene units in the backbone on the basis of absorption spectra and 2D grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXs) data. Concurrently, the coherence lengths of thin films of terpolymers P2-P6 are reduced, implying the formation of small crystalline domains in their thin films. Consequently, the crack onset strains for thin films of P2-P5 are significantly improved in comparison with that of the parent polymer, while the tensile moduli are remarkably reduced to less than $1 5 0 ~ \mathrm { M P a }$ . Among the terpolymers, P2 in which spiro-fluorene units in the backbone are linked to cyclopropane rings is the best intrinsically stretchable polymer semiconductor in terms of mechanical and charge transporting properties under strain. P2 shows record semiconducting performance with charge mobility higher than $1 . 4 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ both under mechanical deformation and against cyclic strains (see Table S1, Supporting Information). Our studies clearly demonstrate the importance of the selection of the co-monomer unit for developing the intrinsically stretchable polymer semiconductors by using the terpolymer strategy.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Synthesis and Characterization

Terpolymers were prepared by co-polymerization of 3,6-bis(5-bromothiophen-2-yl)-2,5-bis(2-decyltetradecyl) pyrrolo[3,4-c]pyrrole-1,4 $\cdot ( 2 H , 5 H )$ dione (M1), the respective dibromo-spiro[cycloalkane- ${ \bf \cdot } 1 , 9 ^ { \prime \prime }$ fluorene] with $5 { , } 5 ^ { \prime \prime }$ bis(trimethylstannyl): $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -bithiophene (M2) as outlined in Scheme S1, Supporting Information, and the synthetic details were provided in Supporting Information. The contents of spiro-fluorene units in the backbones can be finely varied by changing the feeding molar ratios among the respective monomers. The molar ratios of the DPP units (flanked by two thio. phenes) versus spiro-fluorene moieties for these terpolymers were tried to be optimized. Three terpolymers entailing the cyclopropane substituted spiro-fluorene moieties P2, P6, and P7, in which the molar ratios of DPP versus spiro-fluorene units are 5:1, 15:1, and 3:1, respectively, were prepared. It was found that P7 was insoluble in chloroform and chlorobenzene even after heating at $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ This is likely due to the significantly reduced content of side alkyl chains because of the replacement of DPP units by the spiro-fluorene moieties. Among the terpolymers, P2 shows the best stretchability (see below). For this reason, the molar ratio of DPP versus spiro-fluorene moieties is set as 5:1 for P3-P5. For comparison, P1 without spirofuorene units in the backbone was also prepared similarly. The molecular weights of P1-P6, which were determined with gel permeation chromatography (GPC), are in the range of $1 9 . 0 -$ $3 0 . 3 \mathrm { k D a }$ (see Table 1). Moreover, P2-P6 show similar cyclic voltammograms and thus similar redox potentials as P1 (Figure S1, Supporting Information). Consequently, these terpolymers show rather similar absorption maxima and highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO)/lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels as listed in Table 1.

Figure 1 shows thin film absorptions of P1-P6. The absorptions around $7 8 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ (0-0 peak) of P2-P6 are slightly blueshifted by comparing with that of P1. Terpolymers P2-P5 with the same contents of spiro-fluorene units, to which different cyclic rings are linked, exhibit similar absorption spectra. But, the absorption intensity ratios $I _ { 0 - 0 } / I _ { 0 - 1 }$ of P2-P5 are obviously lower than that of P1. Moreover, the comparison of the absorption spectra of P2 and P6 shows that the 0-0 absorption intensities decrease gradually by increasing the content of spirofuorene units (see Figure 1). The absorption intensity ratio $I _ { 0 - 0 } / I _ { 0 - 1 }$ of P6 is also lower than that of P1. According to previous reports,[23] it can be inferred that the interchain packing order is reduced for thin films of P2-P6 by comparing with P1. This is in accordance with the differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) data of P1-P6 (see Figure S3, Supporting Information). The phase transition temperatures of P2-P6 were observed to be 215, 253, 252, 257, and $2 3 2 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ which is lower than that of P1 $( 2 6 8 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ . Moreover, all terpolymers were found to possess lower heat of fusion than that of P1 (see Figure S3, Supporting Infor mation). In particular, P2 displays the lowest heat of fusion. These results hint that thin film crystallinity of P2-P6, in which non-centrosymmetric spiro-fuorene units are introduced into the backbones, is reduced by comparing with P1.

# 2.2. Thin Film Crystallinity

Thin films of P1-P5 on octadecyltrichlorosilane (OTs) modified $\mathrm { S i } / \mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ substrate were characterized with GIWAXs. The GIWAxs patterns and the corresponding in-plane and outof-plane line-cuts are shown in Figure 2 and Figure S4, Supporting Information, respectively. Both lamellar stacking of alkyl chains and interchain $\pi - \pi$ stacking were observed. The (010) $\pi - \pi$ stacking signals are observable at both in-plane and out-of-plane directions. All polymers show co-existence of edgeon and face-on packing modes, which is favorable for the multidirectional charge transporting.[47]

We used the relative degree of crystallinity $\mathrm { ( r D o C ) } ^ { [ 2 4 ] }$ to compare thin film crystallinities of P1 and the terpolymers P2-P5. The calculation of rDoC was performed by using the $I _ { 2 0 0 }$ intensity after normalizing thin film thickness. As summarized in Table 2, rDoC shows clearer trend that the crystallinities of the terpolymers P2-P5 are reduced by comparing that of P1. Among the terpolymers P2 thin film displays the lowest rDoC and thus lowest crystallinity. Furthermore, the (200) signals of thin films of P2-P5 become broader in comparison with that of P1. On the basis of the Scherrer equation,[48] the coherence lengths were estimated. As listed in Table 2, the coherence lengths of P2-P5 are shorter than that of P1. The short coherence lengths imply that polymer chains of P2-P5 tend to form small crystalline domains after introducing the non-centrosymmetric spiro-fluorene units in the backbones. Specifically, the coherence lengths for thin films of P3, P4, and P5 are longer than that for P2 thin film. This manifests that micro-structures of P2-P5 thin films are subtly affected by the sizes of cyclic rings attached to spiro-fluorene units. Such microstructure differences can be related to their different mechanical and charge transporting properties as it will be discussed below. Interestingly, the lamellar distance (due to the stacking of alkyl chains) and interchain $\pi - \pi$ stacking distance of P2-P5 were estimated to be ${ \approx } 2 1 . 8 8 $ A and ${ \approx } 3 . 7 6 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ , respectively, which were shortened by comparing with those of P1. The short lamellar and $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances observed for P2-P5 indicate that their polymer chains are densely packed to form short-range ordered aggregates, which enable thin films of P2-P5 to maintain good charge transporting properties although thin film crystallinity for P2-P5 is reduced.

Thin film of P6 was also characterized with GIWAXs. As listed in Table 2, the coherence length, interchain lamellar and $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances of thin film of P6 are obviously longer than those of P2, indicating that microstructures of these terpolymers are influenced by the contents of spiro-fluorene units in the backbones. Considering the fact that P2-P5 in which the molar ratios of DPP versus spiro-fluorene units are all 5:1 exhibit good solubilities in organic solvents and high thin film charge mobilities, the following mechanical and charge transporting studies are mainly focused on P2-P5.

To understand the influence of spiro-fluorene units in the backbones on the interchain packing and thin film crystallinity, DFT calculations of the backbone conformations for P1-P5 were carried out. In order to simplify the calculations, the conformation for the backbone segment with five DPP units and one spiro-fluorene unit was optimized, and the long alkyl chains in DPP units were substituted with methyl groups. In similar way, the calculation was also performed with the backbone segment entailing six DPP units, which was regarded as the model for the backbone of P1. As depicted in Figure 3, the optimized backbone segment with DPP and thiophene units is planar, which is favorable for interchain dense packing and formation of large crystalline domains. In comparison, the backbone segment after the incorporation of one spirofuorene unit becomes non-planar and bending, which is not beneficial for interchain packing to form large crystalline domains. The variation of backbone conformation is caused by structural features of the spiro-fluorene units. First, the two $\mathrm { C } { - } \mathrm { C }$ bonds which connect the spiro-fluorene with two thiophene units are not co-linear. In fact, the crystal structures of the dibormo-spiro-fluorene monomers were determined (see Figure 3a) and the two $\mathrm { C } { \mathrm { - B r } }$ bonds (which were transformed into the two $\mathrm { C - C }$ bonds) form an angle of $1 5 6 . 5 ^ { \circ }$ $1 5 8 . 0 ^ { \circ }$ $1 5 8 . 6 ^ { \circ }$ $1 5 8 . 1 ^ { \circ }$ , respectively and thus are not co-linear. The angle between the two $\mathrm { C } { \mathrm { - B r } }$ bonds of $2 ^ { \prime \prime } { , } 7 ^ { \prime \prime }$ -dibromospiro[cyclopropane- ${ \cdot } 1 , 9 ^ { \prime }$ fuorene] is $1 5 6 . 5 ^ { \circ }$ , which is slightly smaller than those of spiro-fluorenes with 4-6-membered rings in P3, P4, and P5. This structural feature of the spiro-fluorene with 3-membered cycloalkane may perturb the planar conformation of the DPPbased conjugated polymer distinctly. Second, the presence of spiro-fluorene units in the backbone will break the centrosymmetry of the DPP-thiophene backbone units which are centrosymmetric; moreover, the spiro-fluorene units are randomly distributed in the backbone.

Alternatively, thin films of P2-P5 show short lamellar and $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances (see Table 2) as discussed above. By comparing with P1, the DPP units in P2-P5 were substituted with the spiro-fluorene units which contain no bulky and long alkyl chains. As a consequence, the steric hindrance due to bulky and long alkyl chains linked to DPP units is reduced in P2-P5, and thus the polymer chains can be densely packed within their thin films, leading to short lamellar and $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances. The spiro-fluorene with 3-membered cycloalkane is more rigid and less sterically congested by comparing those with 4-6 membered rings. This structural feature is even more beneficial for interchain dense packing. This agrees well with the shorter interchain lamellar and $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances for P2 thin film. These results indicate that these terpolymers with spiro-fluorene units in their backbones can form relatively ideal microstructures to meet the structural demands for stretchability and charge mobility simultaneously.

# 2.3. Mechanical Properties

In order to study the effect of spiro-fuorene units on the mechanical properties of these terpolymers, the crack onset strains were measured in the following way. Specifically, thin films of the respective polymers spin-coated on OTS modified $\mathrm { S i } / \mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ substrates were carefully transferred to polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) substrates. Then, the PDMS substrates were stretched to different strains and fixed on glass slides. The top surface morphologies of the stretched films were examined with optical microscope. As shown in Figure 4, dense cracks emerged within thin film of P1 at only $5 0 \%$ strain, while cracks were observed for terpolymers P2-P5 at higher strain and thus thin films of P2-P5 show higher crack onset strains than that of P1. No cracks were formed for thin films of P2-P5 at $5 0 \%$ strain, but cracks appeared by increasing the strain to $7 5 \%$ . In comparison, only sparse cracks were detected within thin film of P2, even at $100 \%$ strain, and the crack density is lower at strain as high as $1 5 0 \%$ . P6, which also contains cyclopropane substituted spiro-fluorene units in the backbone as P2, but with low contents (the molar ratio of DPP versus spiro-fluorene units is 15:1), shows lower crack onset strain than that of P2 (see Figure S5, Supporting Information). This shows that the stretchable property of these terpolymers is influenced by the contents of spiro-fluorene units in the backbones. In addition, cracks were also observed under only $50 \%$ strain within thin film of terpolymer P8 (see Figure S6c, Supporting Information), which entails dimethyl-substituted fuorene as the comonomer unit (see Scheme 1). In fact, P8 shows better thin film crystallinity than P2-P5 since the rDoC of P8 thin film (see Figure S6b, Supporting Information) is higher than those of P2-P5. This manifests that the incorporation of spiro-fluorene units in the backbones is beneficial for improving the stretchable property of polymer semiconductors.

Furthermore, the tensile moduli were estimated with mechanical buckling metrologyl49-51] to explain why terpolymers P2-P5 possess higher fracture initiation strains than P1. The measurement details and calculations of moduli are presented in Supporting Information (Figures S7-s9 and Table s2, Supporting Information). As shown in Figure 4b, tensile moduli of P1-P5 were calculated to be 441.1, 83.7, 132.9, 139.6, 122.2 MPa, respectively. The significantly reduced moduli of P2-P5 clearly is in good agreement with the fact that thin films of terpolymers show lower crystallinity with small crystalline domains after the introduction of spiro-fluorene units into their backbones. The lowest modulus of P2 is consistent with its highest crack onset strain, showing that P2 has huge potential to maintain charge mobility under high strain. P3-P5 show slightly higher tensile moduli, indicating that the sizes of cyclic rings in the spiro-fluorene units in their backbones have subtle effects on the interchain packing and the microstructures of their thin films, which agrees well with the respective GIWAXS data (see Table 2).

# 2.4. Charge Transport Properties

The charge mobilities of thin films of P1-P5 were measured by fabrication of the respective field-effect transistors (FETs) with bottom-gate bottom-contact (BGBC) configuration under different strains $0 \%$ $5 0 \% { , } 1 0 0 \%$ and $1 5 0 \%$ strains) and after being subjected to stretching-releasing cycles for different times at $50 \%$ strain. Figure 5a shows the procedures to construct the FETs with stretched thin films. Specifically, the stretched thin films prepared with the same procedures as for crack onset strain measurements were transferred back to OTS modified $\mathrm { S i } / \mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ substrates with prefab drain-source gold electrode-arrays. The transfer curves measured under ambient conditions are shown in Figures S10 and S13, Supporting Information, and device performances are summarized in Tables S3 and S4, Supporting Information. For each stretched thin film, the charge mobilities were measured at both parallel and perpendicular directions to the stretching directions. The values of $I _ { \mathrm { o n } } / I _ { \mathrm { o f f } }$ were obtained in the conventional way with the respective transfer curves, while those of charge mobility and $V _ { \mathrm { t h } }$ were extracted from the respective plots $\mathrm { ( } I _ { \mathrm { D S } } \mathrm { ) } ^ { 1 / 2 }$ versus $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ (see Figures S10 and S13, Supporting Information) at low $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ region and the corresponding reliability factors which were calculated according to the previous report52] are provided in Tables S3-S5, Supporting Information.

At zero strain, thin films of P1-P5 show similar average charge mobilities, being higher than $3 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ as listed in Table S3, Supporting Information, although thin films of P2-P5 containing spiro-fluorene units in the backbones exhibit low crystallinity as discussed above. But, this may be compensated by the short interchain $\pi - \pi$ and lamellar stacking distances. This indicates that incorporation of small contents of spiro-fluorene units in the backbones of DPP-based conjugated polymers does not affect the charge transport property substantially.

At $5 0 \%$ strain, charge mobilities of P1 decreased obviously (see Figure 5b), and the average charge mobility at parallel direction (to the stretching direction) decreased from 3.48 to $2 . 6 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } ,$ The charge mobilities of P4 were also reduced when subjected to $5 0 \%$ strain. In comparison, the charge mobilities of P2 were just slightly declined, while those of P3 and P5 were slightly boosted at $5 0 \%$ strain at parallel direction. When the strain increased to $100 \%$ , the charge mobilities of P1 dropped quickly (see Figure 5b,c), while those of P2 were still higher than $3 . 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and those of P3-P5 were over $2 . 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ except P4 at the perpendicular direction. When the strain further increased to $1 5 0 \%$ , the semiconducting property of thin film of P1 became even poor with charge mobility as low as $0 . 1 8 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - }$ $\mathbf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ (see Figure 5c). Remarkably, charge mobilities of P2 were still maintained to be over $3 . 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ at both directions. For P3-P5, charge mobilities were higher than $1 . 2 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ at $1 5 0 \%$ strain at both directions (see Table S3, Supporting Information).

There is a slightly increase of charge mobility at the parallel direction for P3 and P5 under $5 0 \%$ strain, and for P2 and P5 under $100 \%$ strain (see Table S3, Supporting Information). This is probably owing to the strain-induced alignment of polymer chains. Dichroic ratios (absorbance ratios of the maximum absorption peaks around $7 8 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ in parallel and perpendicular directions) from polarized absorptions under different strains were measured to investigate the strain-induced alignment of polymer chains for P1-P5 (see Figure S19, Supporting Information). The dichroic ratios of P1 start to decline after strain $>$ $5 0 \%$ , while the dichroic ratios of P2-P5 thin films still increase under $50 \%$ strain, indicating that the chain alignment can occur under high strain for these terpolymers. Most notably, the dichroic ratios of P2 and P5 increase more distinctly. Consequently, the strain-induced alignment of polymer chains can probably cause anisotropic transporting behavior. Furthermore, the stretched thin films show decrease in charge mobility at the perpendicular direction. This partially be attributed to that the cracks which are generated under strain can be roughly perpendicular to the strain direction as shown in Figure S11, Supporting Information. Therefore, the charge transport pathway at perpendicular direction can be partially blocked.

We also investigated the variation of charge mobilities of P1-P5 after different stretching-releasing cycles under $50 \%$ strain in order to quantify the deformation durability. After the repeated stretching-releasing cycles at $5 0 \%$ strain only for 100 times, the charge mobilities of P1 were declined substantially down to 0.033 and $0 . 0 3 9 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ at the parallel and perpendicular directions (to the stretching direction), respectively (see Figure 5d,e; Table S4, Supporting Information). In comparison, charge mobilities of P2-P5 were measured to be higher than $2 { \mathrm { ~ c m } } ^ { 2 } \ { \mathrm { V } } ^ { - 1 } \ { \mathrm { s } } ^ { - 1 }$ except that of P3 at the parallel direction (1. $4 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ under the same conditions (see

Figure 5d). After thin films of P2-P5 were subjected to 500 stretching-releasing cycles under $5 0 \%$ strain, charge mobilities of all terpolymers started to decrease (see Figure 5d,e). But, charge mobilities of P2 were still higher than $2 . 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ at both directions, while those of P3-P5 were reduced to be $1 . 0 7 { - } 2 . 0 4 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ (see Table S4, Supporting Information). When the stretching-releasing cycles further increased to 1000, charge mobilities of P2 were still maintained to be 2.46 and $1 . 4 1 \ \mathrm { { \ c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { { \ V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { { s } ^ { - 1 } } } }$ at the respective parallel and perpendicular directions, whereas those of P3-P5 were declined to be lower than $1 . 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ . These results reveal that the incorporation of spiro-fluorene units in the backbones of DPP-based conjugated polymers can endow the polymer semiconductors with both good stretchability and high charge mobility simultaneously. P2 is among the best reported intrinsically stretchable polymer semiconductors in terms of good ductility (with elastic modulus as low as $8 3 . 7 ~ \mathrm { M P a }$ ) and high charge mobility (over $1 . 4 1 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ after 1000 stretching-releasing cycles at $5 0 \%$ strain).

Additionally, in order to further demonstrate the high performance of P2 as stretchable polymer semiconductor, the charge mobilities of P2 thin film in both parallel and perpendicular directions were measured after the thin films were separately subjected to $100 \%$ strain for one time and $5 0 \%$ strain for 1000 cycles (strain-release), followed by strain removal (see Figures S16,S18 and Table S5, Supporting Information). For comparison, charge mobilities of P1 were measured similarly. The charge mobilities of the strain-released thin films of P1 show a certain degree of recovery, but are still obviously lower than those without application of strain (see Figure S18, Supporting Information). In comparison, the charge mobilities of the strain-released films of P2 show no obvious changes compared to the those before strains were released. These results demonstrate the excellent stretchable property of P2.

It is noted that these terpolymers still exhibit high charge mobilities even cracks are formed in their thin films based on optical microscope and atomic force microscope (AFM) images (Figure $4 \mathrm { a }$ ; Figures S11,S14, Supporting Information). This is accountable by considering that the cracks are small and they are not densely packed and as a result the conducting paths are still connected. But, when cracks become large and more cracks appear, the semiconducting performances of polymer semiconductors are significantly deteriorated with low charge mobilities, because of the disconnection of conducting paths. In addition, wrinkles were observed in the AFM height images of thin films P1 and P2, which were first treated with strain and the stresses were released (see Figure S17, Supporting Information).

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we present new intrinsically stretchable polymer semiconductors by incorporating non-centrosymmetric spirofluorene units into the backbones of the DPP-based conjugated polymers. By comparing with the parent polymer without the spiro-fluorene units in the backbones, the resulting terpolymers P2-P5 show low thin film crystallinity on the basis of the thin film GIWAXS and absorption spectral data. However, the respective interchain lamellar and $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances are shortened because the spiro-fluorene units contain no bulky alkyl chains. Therefore, microstructures of thin films of these terpolymers become more satisfactory for the needs of stretchability and charge mobility simultaneously. The results demonstrate that thin films of P2-P5 show obviously high crack onset strains and their tensile moduli are remarkably reduced, in particular for P2. Delightedly, terpolymers P2-P5 exhibit much high charge mobilities $( > 1 . 0 7 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ at $100 \%$ strain and even after 500 stretching and releasing cycles under $5 0 \%$ strain. Remarkably, P2 with cyclopropane in the spiro-fluorene units exhibits record charge mobility up to $3 . 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ at even $1 5 0 \%$ strain, and in particular the charge mobility still can reach $1 . 4 \ : \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ : \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ : \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ after repeated stretching and releasing cycles for 1000 times. Therefore, P2 holds huge potential for applications in flexible electronics. Our studies highlight the importance of detailed co-monomer unit design for the development of high performance intrinsically stretchable polymer semiconductors.
