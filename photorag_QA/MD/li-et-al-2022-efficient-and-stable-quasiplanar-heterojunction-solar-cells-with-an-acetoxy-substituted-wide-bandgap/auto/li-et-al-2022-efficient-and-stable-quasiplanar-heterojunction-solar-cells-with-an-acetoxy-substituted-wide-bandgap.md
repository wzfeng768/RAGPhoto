ABSTRACT: Intermolecular interactions have fundamental importance in the control of active layer morphology, exciton generation, charge transport, and, thus, the overall photovoltaic performance. This is especially true for quasiplanar heterojunction $\left( \mathbf { Q } \mathbf { - } \mathbf { P } \mathbf { H } \mathbf { J } \right)$ polymer solar cells, because the bilayer device structure requires larger exciton diffusion lengths. However, little effort has been made to design polymer donors with additional organic functional groups intended to control intermolecular hydrogen-bonding interactions. Herein, we report two new copolymers for Q-PHJ solar cells synthesized by the addition of hydroxy (PNTB-OH) and acetoxy groups (PNTB-OAc) onto electron-deficient units. We have systematically investigated the influence of the hydrogen bond on electro-optical behaviors, crystallnity, photovoltaic properties, energy losses, photostability, and storage stability in both types of polymers. The single-crystal data reveals more regular stacking and order orientation driven by hydrogen bonding, of the acetoxy-substituted electron-deficient units. Q-PHJ organic solar cell (OSCs) were fabricated for both polymers with a high-performance nonfullerene acceptor N3. PNTB-OAc-based $\mathbf { Q } \mathbf { - }$ PHJ OsCs realized the highest photovoltaic performance of $1 6 . 5 3 \%$ , which is ${ \sim } 2 . 4$ times higher than $6 . 7 9 \%$ obtained from the PNTB-OH-based Q-PHJ OSCs. This high performance is attributable to low nonradiative energy losses, high and balanced electron/hole mobility, and bete crystallinity. In contrast, the PNTB-OAc film has a longer crystal coherence length, which is calculated from grazing-incidence wide-angle $\mathbf { X }$ -ray scattering (GIWAXS). Furthermore, the PNTB-OAc device demonstrated superior photostability and storage stability, retained more than $8 5 \%$ of the initial PCE after illumination for $\mathbf { 1 0 5 0 h }$ , and $9 0 \%$ of the initial PCE under nitrogen for $\mathbf { 1 6 0 0 6 }$ . This work highlights the importance of the acetoxy group to significantly control packing and crystallinity by hydrogen bonding, thus realizing efficient OsCs with durable device stability.

In contrast to their inorganic counterparts, organic photovoltaic materials possess unique features including simple inexpensive solution processability, low cost, flexibility, lightweight, semitransparency, and visual aesthetics. These inherent advantages led researchers to rationally develop high-performance organic semiconducting materials for organic photovoltaics.1-13 With the recent developments in nonfullerene acceptors (NFA),14-21 especially low-bandgap Y-series with near-infrared absorption, 22-27 the power conversion efficiency (PCE) of OSCs has been increased by more than $2 0 \%$ 28 To further boost the photovoltaic

performance, it is necessary to develop well-matched complementary wide-bandgap D-A-type polymer donors.

$\mathrm { D } { - } \mathbf { A }$ polymer donors have three major components: the conjugated backbone, side chains, and substituted functional groups, each of which has an important role in tuning the electro-optical properties and active layer morphology.29-34 In contrast to the design of a new D-A conjugated backbone, an approach involving rational side chains is relatively easier and more cost-effective and has been widely accepted in the development of polymer solar cells (PSCs).35-38 \*Alkyl chains are usually employed in $\mathrm { D } { - } \mathbf { A }$ polymer donors to ensure sufficient solubility for fabrication. They also have a subtle effect on the intermolecular packing and electro-optical properties and, consequently, on the photovoltaic performance. In 2018, Huang et al.39 reported three copolymers with different branched alkyl chains on the TzBI unit: P2F-EH, P2F-EHp, and P2F-EO. The increased number of carbons between the branching point and the conjugated backbone makes the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy level of the corresponding polymers decrease, influencing the crystallinity and aggregation. Several reports also have stated that, instead of alkyl chains, heteroatomsubstituted hydrocarbon chains could be employed to design new copolymers.40-43 In 2014, Wong et al.4\* introduced alkyl, alkoxy, and alkylthio side chains into a conjugated skeleton, producing PBDTT-TT, PBDTT-O-TT, and PBDTT-S-TT, respectively. The electron-donating effect of oxygen in the alkoxy side chain increased the HOMO energy level. But, unlike the oxygen atom in PBDTT-O-TT, the S atom with empty 3d orbitals in PBDTT-S-TT possesses $\pi$ -acceptor ability by forming a $\mathrm { p } \pi ( \mathbf { C } ) - \mathrm { d } \pi ( S )$ orbital overlap. Consequently, PBDTT-S-TT shows red-shifted absorption and a lower HOMO energy level, which matches best with acceptor materials. The introduction of steric siloxane-functionalized substituents on the PBDTT side chains was also used to tune the morphological and photovoltaic properties of the corresponding polymers. $4 5 - 4 8$ The PBDTFBTA-2T, PBDTFBTA-TSi, and PBDTFBTA-2Si donor polymers were reported by Peng et al.,49 and the influence of siloxane functional groups on photovoltaic performance was investigated. With a single siloxane functionalized unit, the polymer with asymmetric side chains (PBDTFBTA-TSi) showed better miscibility with the acceptor and facilitated suitable intermolecular interactions without affecting the energy levels.

In quasiplanar heterojunction $\left( \mathrm { Q } \mathrm { - P H J } \right)$ solar cells, the active layers were prepared by sequential processing with orthogonal solvents. During Q-PHJ flm preparation, the donor and acceptor were dissolved in two orthogonal solvents and then used for multiple spin coating to form a bilayer type active film only with a tiny nanoscale bulk heterojunction interface. For the polymer donor materials, appropriate solubility is vitally important to tolerate upper acceptor solution and then to achieve high-quality Q-PHJ flms, which can be directly manipulated by side-chain engineering. We reported two copolymers, PNTB6-Cl with linear alkyl chains and PNTB-Cl with branched alkyl chains, and investigated the relationship between solubility and photovoltaic performance.5o By replacing the branched alkyl chains with the linear alkyl chains, the polymer PNTB6-Cl not only withstood the use of chloroform in processing but also had a stronger intermolecular interaction with a shorter $\pi { \cdot } { \cdot } \pi$ stacking distance and exhibited the highest PCE of $1 7 . 5 9 \%$ . As discussed above, the engineering of side chains is important in the realization of high-performance PSCs. However, few efforts were made to design hydrogen-bonding-assisted molecules by substituting functional groups at the termini of alkyl chains. Hydrogen bonds originating from terminal functional groups can reduce the energy associated with reorganization by forming strongly interlocking intermolecular interactions. Recently, the hydroxysubstituted IC end group was shown to improve the hydrogenbonded intermolecular interactions to realize high-performance PSCs.51 The influence of hydrogen bonding on molecular packing and photovoltaic properties in Q-PHJ devices has been reported only infrequently.

Hydroxy (OH) and acetoxy (OAc) groups both are common in natural products and biological systems and contribute to intermolecular interactions and molecular orientation via hydrogen bonds. Herein, we rationally designed two electron-deficient units (A) with OAc and OH groups as substituents at the terminus of an alkyl chain and copolymerized them with BDTT-Cl derivative (M-1) electron-rich (D) unit, termed PNTB-OAc and PNTB-OH, respectively. We systematically investigated packing of the two electron-deficient units by the single crystal and studied aggregation of polymers by grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXs). Compared to the hydroxy group, the acetoxy group makes the electron-deficient units NTI-OAc have more regular hydrogen bonds and the polymer PNTBOAc possesses a more-ordered orientation. The Q-PHJ OSCs of both polymers were fabricated with a best-matched highperformance nonfullerene small molecule acceptor, N3 (see Scheme S2 in the Supporting Information (SI)). PNTBOAc:N3-based Q-PHJ OSCs achieved a much higher PCE of $1 6 . 5 3 \%$ while PCE of PNTB-OH is only $6 . 7 9 \%$ ,which is mainly attributed to ordered packing and stronger crystallinity. Significantly, the photostability and storage stability of PNTBOAc:N3-based Q-PHJ OSCs are much better than those of the PNTB-OH:N3-based devices, and they are also excellent, compared with other polymer donor materials. The utilization of such functional groups that can improve packing and crystallinity by hydrogen bonding will be a promising strategy to achieve high-performance Q-PHJ OSCs with excellent stability.

Both hydroxy and acetoxy groups with oxygen and hydrogen can enhance intermolecular hydrogen-bond interactions. As illustrated in Scheme 1 and Scheme S1 in the SI, both groups are substituents at the terminus of the alkyl chain of the electron-deficient unit. In addition, a simple one-step reaction can interconvert hydroxy and acetoxy groups by protection or deprotection, respectively. We selected 6-aminohexan-1-ol as the starting material. After the amidation reaction, NTI-OAc with acetoxy groups was synthesized with a yield of $8 4 \%$ by Suzuki coupling between compound 2 and 3,4-bis(4,4,5,5- tetramethyl-1,3,2-dioxaborolan-2-yl)thiophene (for details, see the SI). NTI-OH with a hydroxy group was synthesized by deprotection of NTI-OAc. To synthesize compound 4, bromination of NTI-OAc was performed, followed by Stille coupling with 2-(tributylstannyl)thiophene to introduce the thiophene $\pi$ bridge. Bromination of compound 4 yields the desired monomer (M-OAc) with an acetoxy substituent, and the monomer with a hydroxy group $\left( \mathrm { { \bf M } - O H } \right)$ was synthesized via a simple single-step deacetylation reaction of M-OAc with potassium carbonate. The polymers PNTB-OAc and PNTBOH were synthesized by Stille coupling reaction between the electron-donating units $\mathbf { \chi } _ { \mathbf { M } - 1 } ^ { 5 0 }$ and the corresponding monomers. The detailed reaction conditions are presented in the SI. The polymers were extracted by Soxhlet extraction, using methanol, chloroform, and chlorobenzene sequentially. The extracted polymers were only soluble in heated chlorobenzene, which allows orthogonal solvent processing matching with efficient nonfullerene acceptors. The average number molecular weight of both polymers was measured by hightemperature gel permeation chromatography at $1 5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ using trichlorobenzene as the eluent. The measured average number molecular weight $\left( M _ { \mathrm { n } } \right)$ and polydispersity index (PD1) of PNTB-OAc were $9 1 . 4 9 \mathrm { k D a }$ and 2.16 (Figure S1 in the SI), respectively. Similarly, the average number molecular weight $( M _ { \mathrm { n } } )$ and polydispersity index (PDI) of PNTB-OH were determined to be $5 7 . 2 0 \mathrm { k D a }$ and 1.88, respectively (Figure S2 in the SI). As shown in Figure S3 in the SI, the thermal stability was measured by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA), and $5 \%$ weight loss was recorded for PNTB-OAc at $4 0 9 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ and by PNTB-OH at $3 4 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ . Both polymers have enough thermal stability to tolerate device fabrication. Differential scanning calorimetry (DsC) measurements showed that both polymers have no obvious endothermic and exothermic peaks (Figure S4 in the SI).

The possible effects of hydrogen bonding on the intermolecular packing were investigated by single-crystal $\mathrm { X } \cdot$ ray diffraction analysis253 of crystaline NT1-OAc and NTIOH, which were grown by slow diffusion of $n$ -hexane into a solution of the compound in chloroform. As demonstrated in Figures $_ { \mathrm { 1 a - f } } ,$ NTI-OAc and NTI-OH possess different intermolecular hydrogen-bond interactions, which accounts for their different molecular packing. The acetoxy group in NTI-OAc forms hydrogen bonds with adjacent molecules with similar alignment and orientation. The carbonyl of the acetoxy group forms a hydrogen bond with the electropositive hydrogen on the core and the hydrogen of the acetoxy group forms a hydrogen bond with the carbonyl oxygen of the imide group. NTI-OAc possesses a shorter $\pi { \cdot } { \cdot } \pi$ stacking distance and ordered molecular packing in the solid state. Nevertheless, the hydroxy group in NTI-OH makes a hydrogen bond with adjacent molecules, at the same time attaining irregular alignment and orientation. Though intermolecular interaction and polymer packing are more complex than in the corresponding D and A units, the single-crystal Xray diffraction analysis reveals information essential to understanding the effect of hydrogen bonding on the photovoltaic performance.

To further probe the photovoltaic parameters of PNTBOAc- and PNTB-OH-based $\mathrm { Q } { \cdot } \mathrm { P H J }$ , the exciton dissociation efficiency was investigated through the photoluminescence (PL) quenching spectra of neat N3 film and the corresponding $\mathrm { Q } { \cdot } \mathrm { P H J }$ films of both polymers. As shown in Figure 3c, the fluorescence of the acceptor N3 was sufficiently quenched by PNTB-OAc and PNTB-OH. Among the two polymer donors, PNTB-OAc exhibits a higher PL quenching ratio $\left( 9 7 . 2 9 \% \right)$ than PNTB-OH $( 9 6 . 0 2 \% )$ , indicating more efficient exciton dissociation and charge transfer at the interface between N3 and PNTB-OAc.

To study the exciton dissociation and charge collection, the charge dissociation probability $P ( \mathrm { E } , \mathrm { T } )$ was calculated from the plot of the photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ vs effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ as shown in Figure S7 in the SI. The $P ( \mathrm { E } , \mathrm { T } )$ expression, under the condition of maximum power output, gave values of $9 2 \%$ and $60 \%$ of PNTB-OAc:N3 and PNTB-OH:N3, respectively. The high $P ( \mathrm { E } , \mathrm { T } )$ of PNTB-OAc:N3 indicates the efficient exciton dissociation and charge separation at the interface of donor and acceptor. At the same time, the slope of the curve was measured to study the charge recombination, $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ as a function of light intensity on a logarithmic scale, as shown in Figure 3d. PNTB-OAc:N3 exhibits a high slope of 0.97, suggesting a relatively weaker bimolecular recombination, which is consistent with the high FF value of ${ \sim } 7 3 . 7 1 \%$ . The charge mobility is explored through the space charge limited current (sCLC) with the device structures of $\operatorname { I T O } /$ PEDOT:PSS/polymer:N3/ $\mathbf { \mathrm { / M o O } } _ { 3 } / \mathbf { \mathrm { A g } }$ for hole mobility, and $\mathrm { I T O } / \mathrm { Z n O } _ { I }$ polymer: N3/PNDIT-F3N/Ag for electron mobility. As shown in Table S2 and Figure S8 in the SI, the measured hole mobilities of the PNTB-OAc:N3- and PNTB-- OH:N3-based Q-PHJ devices are $4 . 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $7 . 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \ \mathrm { c m ^ { 2 } \ V ^ { - 1 } \ } \mathrm { s ^ { - 1 } }$ respectively. The hole mobilities of PNTB-OAc and PNTB-OH neat films were also consistent with the result of Q-PHJ devices (see Table S2 in the SI). PNTB-OAc:N3 has greater hole mobility in the blend membrane, and this might account for the lower recombination losses. The electron mobilities for PNTB-OAc:N3- and PNTB-OH:N3-based $\mathrm { Q } { \cdot } \mathrm { P H J }$ devices are $3 . 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 }$ $\mathbf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $3 . 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ respectively. Higher hole/ electron mobility and also more balanced charge transport were exhibited by PNTB-OAc:N3-based Q-PHJ devices, which can reduce charge recombination.

In addition to $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF, the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ value of PNTB-OAc:N3- based $\mathrm { Q } { \cdot } \mathrm { P H J }$ devices was also slightly higher. GIWAXS measurements were used to understand the molecular orientation and crystallinity of each polymer neat film and the corresponding Q-PHJ film with N3 acceptor, as shown in Figures $_ { 5 \mathsf { a - e } }$ and the detailed calculatedss parameters are summarized in Table S4 in the SI. Both polymers displayed a strong (010) reflection peak at $q _ { z } = 1 . 6 7$ $\mathbf { \hat { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ $\left( d _ { \pi } \ = \ \bar { 3 } . 7 7 \ \mathring { \mathrm { A } } \right)$ in the out-of-plane (Oop) direction, suggesting a dominant face-on orientation. This face-on orientation can enhance vertical phase separation and is beneficial for efficient transportation of holes, implying that both polymers are suited to construction of the Q-PHJ. In the GIWAXS patterns of both Q-PHJ flms, a strong (010) reflection peak appears at $q _ { z } = 1 . 7 5 \mathrm { ~ \AA ^ { - 1 } }$ $\stackrel { \prime } { d } _ { \pi } = 3 . 5 9 \ \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ,$ in the out-of-plane (OOp) direction, indicating the molecular orientation remains face-on with N3. And the short $\pi { \cdot } { \cdot } \pi$ stacking distance of polymer neat films and $\mathrm { Q } { \cdot } \mathrm { P H J }$ films prove the presence of strong intermolecular stacking, which is conductive to charge mobility and $J _ { s c } .$ To gain further deep insight into the crystallinity, the crystal coherence length (CCL) was calculated from the full width at half-maximum (FWHM) of the corresponding polymers films. The crystal coherence length (CCL) of PNTB-OAc and PNTB-OH neat film in the (100) direction are 35.30 and $2 9 . 8 2 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ,$ respectively. In the (010) direction, the crystal coherence length (CCL) of PNTB-OAc is $9 . 8 1 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ,$ which is much longer than that observed for PNTB-OH (8.69 A). The polymer PNTB-OAc exhibits relatively longer CCL in both the (100) and the (010) direction, possibly originating from the hydrogen bond of the acetoxy group, and suggesting more crystalline and ordered film. The crystalline and ordered orientation in the flm facilitates efficient hole transportation, a fact which is confirmed by the space charge limited current (sCLC). These results are consistent with single-crystal data of acceptor-units and imply the presence of similar intermolecular interactions in the polymer film.

The morphology of PNTB-OAc:N3 and PNTB-OH:N3 $\mathrm { Q } \mathrm { - }$ PHJ flms were investigated by the measurement of atomic force microscopy (AFM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM), which affected the stability of organic solar cells. As illustrated in Figures S10(a) and $\mathrm { \dot { S } 1 0 ( c ) }$ in the SI, the PNTB-OAc:N3 $\scriptstyle { \mathrm { Q - P H J } }$ film exhibits a root-mean-square surface roughness $( R _ { \mathrm { q } } )$ value of $0 . 5 6 ~ \mathrm { \ n m }$ while that for PNTB-OH:N3 Q-PHJ flm is $2 . 3 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . In the TEM images displayed in Figures ${ \dot { \operatorname { S } } } 1 0 ( { \mathfrak { b } } )$ and Sio(d) in the SI, uniform distribution can be seen in the film with acetoxy-substituted polymer, which is consistent with results of GIwAXS and AFM. The photostability of the PSC is a key factor in realworld applications and is directly associated with active layer morphology. The photostability and storage-stability were measured in a glovebox under nitrogen, and corresponding graphs are plotted in Figures Sf and 5g. The photostability tests were performed under illumination for $1 0 5 0 \ \mathrm { h } ,$ and it was found that the PNTB-OAc:N3- and PNTB-OH:N3-based $\mathrm { Q } \mathrm { - }$ PHJ devices retain more than $8 5 \%$ and $7 0 \%$ of the initial PCE, respectively. After being stored under a nitrogen atmosphere for $1 6 0 0 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ the PNTB-OAc:N3- and PNTB-OH:N3-based $\mathrm { Q } \mathrm { - }$ PHJ devices retain more than $9 0 \%$ and $8 0 \%$ of the initial PCE, respectively. The superior PCE and better stability of the PNTB-OAc:N3-based Q-PHJ device were mainly due to the ordered crystalline structure. The introduction of the acetoxy group to manipulate the intermolecular interaction by hydrogen bonds is a promising strategy to develop OSCs with better efficiency and stability.

In this work, we have designed and synthesized the acceptor units NTI-OAc and NTI-OH by introducing hydroxy or acetoxy groups. Based on the single-crystal analysis, the acceptor units NTI-OAc exhibited ordered packing, because of the regular hydrogen bonds formed by the acetoxy group. The polymers PNTB-OAc and PNTB-OH were obtained, and the GIwAXS of the two polymers were measured to explore the packing pattern. Like the trend observed in the acceptor units, the polymer with acetoxy (PNTB-OAc) demonstrated more ordered packing. With its more ordered packing and stronger crystallinity, the polymer PNTB-OAc showed higher hole mobility than PNTB-OH. The PNTBOAc:N3-based Q-PHJ OSCs showed the highest PCE of $1 6 . 5 3 \%$ , which is 2.43 times higher than that of PNTB-OH:N3 $( 6 . 7 9 \% )$ . The ordered packing, stronger crystallinity, and better thermal stability of PNTB-OAc led its Q-PHJ device to demonstrate superior photostability and storage stability than that of PNTB-OH:N3. Over $8 5 \%$ of the initial PCE was also obtained in the $\mathrm { Q - P H J }$ device of PNTB-OAc:N3, even after illumination for $1 0 5 0 \ \mathrm { h } ,$ while $9 0 \%$ of the initial PCE was obtained after storage under a nitrogen atmosphere for $1 6 0 0 \mathrm { h }$ Our research demonstrated that the acetoxy group could finetune the polymer packing and crystallinity with intermolecular hydrogen bond, which also provides a practical strategy to develop highly stable and efficient photovoltaic devices.
