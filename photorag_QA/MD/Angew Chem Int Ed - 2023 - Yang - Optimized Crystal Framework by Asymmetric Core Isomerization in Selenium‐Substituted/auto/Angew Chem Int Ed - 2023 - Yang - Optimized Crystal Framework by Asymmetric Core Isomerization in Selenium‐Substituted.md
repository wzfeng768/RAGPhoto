# Optimized Crystal Framework by Asymmetric Core Isomerization in Selenium-Substituted Acceptor for Efficient Binary Organic Solar Cells

Can Yangt, Qiaoshi $A n ^ { + }$ \* Mengyun Jiang, Xiaoming Ma, Asif Mahmood, Heng Zhang, Xin Zhao, Hong-Fu Zhi, Min Hun Jee, Han Young Woo, Xilin Liao, Dan Deng,\* Zhixiang Wei,\* and Jin-Liang Wang\*

Abstract: Both the regional isomerization and seleniumsubstitution of the small molecular acceptors (SMAs) play significant roles in developing efficient organic solar cells (OsCs), while their synergistic effects remain elusive. Herein, we developed three isomeric SMAs (SCSeF, A-ISeF, and A-OSeF) via subtly manipulating the mono-selenium substituted position (central, inner, or outer) and type of heteroaromatic ring on the central core by synergistic strategies for efficient OsCs, respectively. Crystallography of asymmetric A-OSeF presents a closer intermolecular $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking and more ordered 3-dimensional network packing and efficient chargehopping pathways. With the successive out-shift of the mono-selenium substituted position, the neat films give a slightly wider band gap and gradually higher crystallinity and electron mobility. The PM1:A-OSeF afford favourable fibrous phase separation morphology with more ordered molecular packing and efficient charge transportation compared to the other two counterparts. Consequently, the A-OSeF-based devices achieve a champion efficiency of $1 8 . 5 \%$ ,which represents the record value for the reported selenium-containing SMAs in binary OsCs. Our developed precise molecular engineering of the position and type of selenium-based heteroaromatic ring of SMAs provides a promising synergistic approach to optimizing crystal stacking and boosting top-ranked selenium-containing SMAs-based OSCs.

# Introduction

Organic solar cells (OsCs) have attracted widespread attention benefitting from their intrinsic solution processibility, flexibility, transparency, lightweight, and ease of rollto-roll and scale-up fabrication.l with the innovation of material design?]  and the optimization of device engineering,3] the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of OSCs based on the acceptor-donor-acceptor (A-D-A) or acceptor-donor-acceptor'-donor-acceptor (A-DAD-A) type small molecular acceptors (SMAs) and polymer donor are undergoing an encouraging era with continuous breakthroughs.4l Despite tremendous efforts that have been made in the molecular engineering of SMAs, how to develop novel SMAs with precisely optimized electronic and crystal packing structures to better balance the trade-off among short circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ , open circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ and fill factors (FFs), and to achieve the further breakthrough in device performance of OsCs for future commercialization is still a dilemma needed to confront.5]

Recently, varied strategies, including central core modulation,] terminal group modification,4,7] and side alkyl chain engineering,] were adopted to optimize the properties of SMAs. Among these design strategies, the rational isomerization of the central core by altering the connection site or spatial order of the subunits has been proven to be the most challenging but effective approach to fine-tuning the molecular aggregate behaviors, optimizing crystal packing and morphological properties and thereby improving the photovoltaic performance of SMAs.l For instance, the symmetry-broken isomerization of central core with asymmetric geometry exquisitely increases dipole moment and intermolecular interaction, and achieves a delicate balance between crystallinity and the miscibility with the donor, which results in PCEs of PM6-based binary solar cells over $1 8 \%$ with remarkable FF and partially sacrificial $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ in some cases.0] wWhile device performance of the binary OSCs based on asymmetric central core-based SMAs with other popular polymers still lags. Therefore, it is still necessary to develop unique symmetry-broken SMAs that can obtain high-performance binary OsCs when blending with various donor materials.

On the other hand, the rational replacement of the central core subunit by various aromatic heterocycles has been proven to be an effective way to alteration of the electronic structure of the molecular backbone, optimizing intermolecular packing and blend film morphology, leading to enhanced photovoltaic performance of SMAs.1] For example, subtle replacement from sulfur (S) to larger radius selenium (Se) onto central core usually causes decreased optical band gap, close intermolecular $\pi - \pi$ stacking, and improved charge carrier transport owing to the much more quinoidal character of Se-based heterocycles and the more polarizable and more extended lone pair electrons of Se atoms and ${ \mathrm { S e } } { \cdots } { \mathrm { S e } }$ intermolecular interactions.12] Based on these merits, Se-containing SMAs-based OSCs have obtained a superior $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and remarkable FF, which plays an important role and potential in high-performance binary, ternary and tandem OsCs.[10,13] Despite the great advantages of Se-substituted SMAs in OSCs, the reported PCEs for most Se-containing SMAs-based binary OSCs still hover around $1 7 - 1 8 \%$ and have a large room to improve, which is mainly attributed to the relatively lower $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and inappropriate energy loss that may be caused by the mismatched polymer donor and the unfavorable blend morphology in comparison with S-containing counterparts. Hence, further precise molecular tailoring of Se-substituted SMAs and finding more suitable polymer donors to boost the PCE of corresponding OsCs is still urgent and necessary. Meanwhile, the single crystallographic analysis of SMAs is powerful for gaining direct insight into the molecular stacking motifs, charge transport potential and high performance of corresponding OSC.76,12,13,414 Morover, although two Sesubstituted types (electron-rich selenophene unit and electron-deficient selenadiazole) in the central core have been independently employed, the comprehensive study of the isomeric SMAs with different Se-substituted chemical environments and structural geometries on the photoelectric behavior, single crystal packing motif, and satisfactory device performance has not yet been reported due to the large challenge in the chemical synthesis and the single crystal cultivation of the Se-substituted asymmetrical SMAs.

To overcome these challenges, we successfully synthesized a group of novel A-DA'D-A type isomeric SMAs (symmetric S-CSeF, asymmetric A-ISeF and A-OSeF) with identical electron-deficient terminals and the isomeric central cores via subtly manipulating the mono-selenium substituted position (central, inner or outer) and type of heteroaromatic ring on the central core to systematically study the synergistic effect of the regional-isomerization and asymmetric Se-substitution strategies on physicochemical properties, single crystal packing, and morphological characteristics for further breakthrough in OsCs performance, respectively. Single crystals of these isomeric SMAs with different mono-selenium substituted positions and types have been fully obtained for the first time. The asymmetric A-OSeF crystal presents a more planar molecular conformation by unique intramolecular $\operatorname { S e } { \cdots } \mathrm { O }$ non-covalent interaction and a tighter and more ordered 3D network packing by the closest $\pi - \pi$ stacking and extra F..Se intermolecular noncovalent interactions compared to those of A-ISeF and SCSeF crystal, promoting the formation of a more effective multiple charge-hopping pathways. The successive out-shift of the mono-selenium substituted position in the isomeric central cores (from S-CSeF to A-ISeF, and A-OSeF) induces the gradually wider optical band gap, gradually improved electron mobility and gradually stronger crystallinity in neat films. Blending with the polymeric donor PM1 (Figure S1), the superior crystalline properties of A-OSeF were inherited, which obtains optimized nanofibrous morphology with proper phase separation and more ordered face-on molecular arrangement, leading to excellent photon harvesting, exciton dissociation and charge extraction and better-balanced carrier transport in corresponding device compared to the other two counterparts. As a result, the A-OSeF-based binary OSCs achieve a champion PCE of $1 8 . 5 \%$ , along with a higher $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 8 8 0 \mathrm { V }$ which outperforms the devices based on A-ISeF $( 1 7 . 6 \% )$ and S-CSeF $( 1 6 . 3 \% )$ ,respectively. Notably, our best PCE of $1 8 . 5 \%$ with a low energy loss $( 0 . 5 2 \mathrm { e V } )$ represents the best result for the reported Secontaining SMAs in binary OSCs, especially combined with the seldom-reported but great reproducible polymer donor PM1. Our study demonstrates that precisely modulating the position of mono-selenium atom and the type of Se-based heteroaromatic ring on the central core of SMAs along with the synergistic symmetry-broken modification is an effective approach to optimizing intermolecular crystal stacking and blend morphology, and promoting $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and FF effectively for Se-substituted SMAs-based binary OSCs with top-level PCE and low $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ simultaneously.

Molecular simulations using density function theory (DFT) at the B3LYP/6-31G (d, p) level were performed to explore the effect of core-isomerization on the molecular energy levels. As shown in Figure S3, their highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) orbitals are mainly focused on the center electron-donating $\mathrm { D A ^ { \prime \prime } D }$ $\mathrm { D } _ { 2 } \mathbf { A } ^ { \prime } \mathbf { D }$ , and $\mathrm { D } _ { 1 } \mathrm { A } ^ { \prime } \mathrm { D }$ cores and more HOMOs distributions are found on the D unit compared to the $\mathbf { D } _ { 2 }$ and $\mathbf { D } _ { 1 }$ unit. Their lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) orbitals are dispersed on the conjugated backbone and most of them are concentrated on the electron-withdrawing units. Compared with $\mathrm { S } { \mathrm { - } } C \mathrm { S e F }$ the LUMO and HOMO orbitals of A-ISeF and A-OSeF all exhibit an asymmetric orbital distribution and slightly elevated LUMO energy levels with a relatively larger band gap. To further understand the relationship between intermolecular interactions and molecular structures, the molecular surface electrostatic potentials (ESP) were investigated. As shown in Figure 1b, most of the molecular surfaces give positive ESP values, revealing their electron-acceptor feature. From S-CSeF to A-ISeF then to A-OSeF, the central cores present a gradually decreasing maximum positive ESP distribution value with the gradual out-shift of the mono-selenium substituent. Moreover, both A-ISeF and A-OSeF show a more obvious ESP distribution difference on two sides of the whole molecule owing to their asymmetrical molecular structure, resulting in a larger dipole moment of 0.41 Debye for A-ISeF and 1.63 Debye for A-OSeF than that of 0.24 Debye for S-CSeF, which can facilitate the molecular aggregation with enhanced intermolecular interactions and more effective intermolecular stacking.[15]

To reveal the effect of the position and type of the mono-selenium substitution on the acceptor geometry and intermolecular arrangement, X-ray single-crystallographic analysis of these isomers was presented. Single crystals of SCSeF, A-ISeF, and A-OSeF were cultivated in the chloroform by the solvent diffusion method using ethanol as the poor solvent. The detailed crystallographic parameters were summarized in Table S1.16 S-CSeF and A-ISeF present the same monoclinic crystal system but different space groups, while A-OSeF crystallizes in the triclinic unit cells with a P-1 space group, which can be ascribed to the switch of the mono-selenium substitution position. As illustrated in Figure 2a and Figure S4, all three isomers show helical and similar banana-shaped molecular geometries and two pairs of enantiomers (M/P and $M ^ { \prime } / P ^ { \prime }$ ) were observed in one unit cell, which could be arising from the twisted DA'D central core backbone and the similar intramolecular $\mathrm { S } { \cdots } \mathrm { O } { = } \mathrm { C }$ or ${ \mathrm { S e } } { \cdots } { \mathrm { O } } { \mathop { = } } { \mathrm { C } }$ conformational lock between the central core and terminal units. Compared to S-CSeF and A-ISeF, A-OSeF with selenium atom at the outermost position of core exhibits the smallest $\pi$ core torsion angles $( 1 . 9 ^ { \circ }$ and $3 . 7 ^ { \circ }$ rather smaller D-A dihedral angles ( $4 . 3 ^ { \circ } / 8 . 7 ^ { \circ }$ and $0 . 1 ^ { \circ } / 8 . 3 ^ { \circ }$ - due to the unique and shorter ${ \mathrm { S e } } { \cdots } { \mathrm { O } } { = } { \mathrm { C } }$ conformational lock, which helps to enhance the molecular conjugated backbone planarity and rigidity. In contrast, the central core of A-ISeF presents the worst planarity. This may be due to the large size of the embedded selenium atom in the inside position of the core causing the disorder of quinone structure, resulting in deformation of the central core plane compared to the almost quasi-planar core of the outermost Se-substitution molecule.

As depicted in Figure 2b, all single crystals possess similar four packing modes in adjacent $P ( P ^ { \prime } )$ and $M ( M ^ { \prime } )$ enantiomers, including between two half central core units with terminal unit in near mirror symmetric dimer (D-A/DA, Mode1), between two half central core unit with the terminal unit in a near central symmetric dimer (D-A/A-D, Mode2), terminal unit to terminal unit in near central symmetric dimer (A/A, Mode3) and terminal unit to terminal unit in near mirror symmetric dimer (A/A, Mode 4) (see Figure S5), which can provide multiple chargetransporting channels. Meanwhile, distinct differences in the details of intermolecular $\pi - \pi$ stacking modes of the three isomers are observed. In details, S-CSeF, A-ISeF, and AOSeF crystals contain three kinds of $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking between two terminal units with the $\pi - \pi$ space of ca. 3.49/3.50/3.41 A (Mode 1, $M / M ^ { \prime }$ or $P / P ^ { \prime }$ ), $3 . 5 2 / 3 . { \overset { \cdot } { 5 } } 3 / 3 . 4 0 { \overset { \circ } { \mathrm { A } } }$ (Mode 3, M'/P), $3 . 3 4 / 3 . 2 8 / 3 . 4 3 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ (Mode 4, $M / M ^ { \prime }$ or $P / P ^ { \prime }$ ), the $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking between two partial central core units with the $\pi - \pi$ space of $3 . 5 0 / 3 . 5 5 / 3 . 5 1 \mathrm { ~ \bar { A } }$ (Mode1, M/M' or $P / P ^ { \prime }$ ) and the $\pi - \pi$ stacking between one terminal unit and partial central backbone with the $\pi { - } \pi$ space of ca. $\bar { 3 . 4 7 / 3 . 4 0 / 3 . 3 7 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ } }$ (Mode 2, M'/P), respectively. Overall, with the out-shift of the mono-selenium substitution in the central core, the average $\pi - \pi$ stacking spaces gradually decrease from S-CSeF (ca. $3 . 4 6 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } { } \dot { }$ to A-ISeF (ca. 3.45 A), and then to A-OSeF $( \mathrm { c a } . 3 . 4 2 \mathring \mathrm { A } )$ , which suggests the intermolecular interactions gradually become stronger and thus help to enhance intermolecular charge transport and reduce charge recombination. Such compact intermolecular packing of A-OSeF may be due to its good backbone planarity and the outermost Se-atom participating in the unique intermolecular assembly. More specifically, subtle intermolecular noncovalent interaction differences will account for the gradation of stacking distances. As seen in Figure S6, multiple intermolecular non-covalent interactions can be found in the single crystals of all three isomers, including F--S, F--N, S..N, N..N, F..O and $\mathrm { o } { \cdot } { \cdot } \pi$ interactions. Differently, the unique $\operatorname { S e } { \cdots } \mathbf { N }$ intermolecular interactions (ca. 3.73 A) between two adjacent selenadiazole units, $\operatorname { s e } { \cdots } \pi$ intermolecular interactions (ca. $3 . 3 8 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } \mathring { \mathrm { \Omega } }$ from the inner selenophene unit and the end group, and $\mathrm { F } { \cdot } { \cdot } \mathrm { s e }$ intermolecular interactions (ca. 3.53 A) from the end group and outer selenophene unit in S-CSeF, A-ISeF, and A-OSeF crystals were observed, respectively, which could be responsible for their difference in intermolecular packing behaviors. The further packing framework is shown in Figure 2c, all three isomers form a similar 3D interpenetrating network packing structure with multiple elliptical frames through multiple intermolecular interactions. Moreover, A-OSeF forms elliptical orthorhombic frame structures with a length of $2 8 . 6 \mathrm { \AA }$ and a width of $2 3 . 3 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ , much smaller than those of A-ISeF $( 2 8 . 7 { \times } 2 3 . 8 \mathring { \mathbf { A } } )$ and S-CSeF $( 2 8 . 8 { \times } 2 3 . 8 \mathring { \mathbf { A } } )$ , arising from more compact and ordered intermolecular packing and stronger multiple intermolecular interactions, which could be beneficial for forming more efficient 3D charge transport channels and thus lead to higher charge mobility. These results demonstrate that combining the isomerization of mono-selenium substitution and the asymmetric skeleton has successfully manipulated and optimized the molecular planarity, intermolecular crystal packing and charge transport behaviors in the solid states.

The optical properties of these isomeric SMAs in solution and thin film are characterized by the Uv/Vis absorption spectra, which are shown in Figures 1c-d and Table1. In diluted chloroform solution, the absorption spectra of S-CSeF, A-ISeF, and A-OSeF display similar shapes and vibronic shoulders with high intensity in the range of $4 5 0 { - } 8 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ . The maximum absorption peaks of SCSeF, A-ISeF, and A-OSeF are almost the same (Figure 1c). From dilute solutions to spin-coated thin films, relatively broadened absorption with vibration shoulder peaks is observed and the maximum absorption peaks of S-CSeF, AISeF, and A-OSeF are red-shifted to 853, 843, and $8 4 0 \mathrm { n m }$ with the maximum absorption coefficient of $1 . 0 3 { \times } 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ $1 . 0 7 { \times } 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ and $1 . 1 4 { \times } 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ (Figure 1d), respectively, which indicates the existence of much more pronounced intermolecular aggregation and intermolecular $\pi { - } \pi$ packing interactions in neat thin films. To further probe the correlation between electron mobility and molecular stacking and crystallinity behaviors in these neat SMAs films, the two-dimensional grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (2D-GIWAXS) technique was performed. The 2D-GIWAXS diffraction patterns and line-cut profiles of relevant neat films are shown in Figure S8. The polymer donor PM1 and neat S-CSeF, AISeF, and A-OSeF films exhibit a preferentially face-on molecular packing orientation and good crystalline order with a strong $\pi - \pi$ stacking diffraction (010) peak at 1.69, 1.70, 1.71, and $1 . 7 2 \mathring { \mathbf { A } } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { i n }$ the out-of-plane (OOP) direction and a lamellar diffraction (100) peak at ca. $0 . 2 9 \mathring { \mathbf { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ in the in-plane (IP) direction, respectively. Furthermore, the electron mobilities of the three isomers in neat films were measured by a space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method. The results revealed that manipulating the mono-selenium substituent position in the central core is a feasible method to optimize the molecular packing orientation and charge transport of the neat films.

To probe the influence of mono-selenium substituted core isomerization on the photovoltaic performances, a series of OsCs with a conventional device architecture of ITO/PEDOT: PSS/PM1:17] SMAs/PDIN/Ag were fabricated and characterized. After the detailed optimization processes (Figure S10-S12 and Table S2-S4), the typical $J { - } V$ plots of the optimized OsCs are displayed in Figure 3a, and the corresponding device parameters are tabulated in Table 2. As a control, the S-CSeF-based OSCs yield a decent PCE of $1 6 . 3 \%$ , with an FF of $7 2 . 2 \%$ , a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 8 5 0 \mathrm { V }$ , and a $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 6 . 5 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ .The A-ISeF-based OsCs deliver a higher PCE of $1 7 . 6 \%$ with an improved FF of $7 4 . 6 \%$ ,a significantly increased $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 8 8 5 \mathrm { V }$ , and a comparable $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 6 . 6 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . Encouragingly, The A-OSeF-based OSCs achieve the champion PCE of $1 8 . 5 \%$ in the three isomers, along with a fairly higher $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 8 8 0 \mathrm { V }$ , an excellent FF of $7 7 . 3 \%$ , and a minor larger $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 7 . 2 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , which is among the best PCEs for SMAs-based binary OSCs and the record efficiency for binary devices based on the seleniumsubstituted SMAs (see Figure 3b and Table S5). In comparison, the performance enhancement of A-OSeF-based OSCs should be mainly attributed to the well-balanced trade-off among $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ FF, and $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ Considering the slightly blue-shifted absorption spectra of A-OSeF, the increase of FF and $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ in A-OSeF-based OSCs may profit from the preferable blend morphology, resulting from the asymmetric molecular structures with proper selenium-substituted position and type. In terms of the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ the meso-superior value for AOSeF-based OSCs is assisted by the upshifted LUMO energy levels and the reduced energy loss compared with SCSeF-based devices. Moreover, we also used other popular polymer donors (PM6 and D18) to fabricate the regioisomers-based OsCs and further investigate the effect of the regio-isomerization (see Figure S13 and Table S6). The efficiencies of PM6:A-OSeF and D18:A-OSeF-based OSCs are over $1 8 \%$ and better than these of S-CSeF and A-ISeFbased OSCs, which are comparable to the result of PM1:AOSeF-based OSCs. Such results suggest that A-OSeF can be matched well with a broader range of donor polymers, which has the potential to further improve the performance of OSCs.

The external quantum efficiency (EQE) tests were conducted to study the origin of the different $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ values and further test the accuracy among these three typical devices (Figure 3c). The $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ values calculated by integrating the EQE spectra are $2 5 . 7 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for PM1 : S-CSeF, $2 5 . 9 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for PM1:A-ISeF, and $2 6 . 4 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for PM1:A-OSeF devices, respectively, which conform well to those measured from the corresponding $J { - } V$ plots ( $\approx 3 \%$ errors). Compared with the S-CSeF-based devices, the A-ISeF-based and A-OSeFbased OSCs exhibit higher EQE in the $3 0 0 { - } 8 5 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ and lower EQE values over the $8 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ wavelength range. In consideration of the similar absorptivity of the three acceptors, the enhanced EQE should be mainly benefited from efficient exciton dissociation and charge extraction, while the lower EQE in the long wavelength range is attributed to the slightly blue-shifted absorption spectra. Figure 3d shows the internal quantum efficiency (IQE) and absorption spectra of the corresponding OSCs. The three OSCs display very similar absorption intensities in the range of 300- $8 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ The higher IQE values of the A-OSeF-based OSCs demonstrate more efficient exciton dissociation and charge transport compared to S-CSeF-based and A-ISeF-based devices.

To deeply understand the exciton dissociation and charge kinetics behaviors of these optimized devices based on the three isomers, the dependence of photocurrent $( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } )$ on the effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ was surveyed and plotted in Figure 3e. The $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ of the A-OSeF-based OSCs approaches saturation $( J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } )$ rapidly at lower $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ relative to two other devices, meaning efficient excitons dissociation and charge collection process in the corresponding devices. The ratios of $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \mathrm { \ddot { \Omega } } ^ { * } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ ${ J _ { \mathrm { p h } } } ^ { * }$ is the $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ under the short-circuit condition) and $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } { } ^ { \& } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ ${ J _ { \mathrm { p h } } } ^ { \& }$ is the $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ under the maximum power output condition) were generally used to characterize the exciton dissociation probability $( P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } )$ and charge collection efficiency $( P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } )$ . The $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ of PM1:A-OSeF devices are $9 7 . 5 \%$ and $8 9 . 2 \%$ , respectively, which are much higher than those of PM1:S-CSeF $9 4 . 6 \%$ and $8 4 . 0 \%$ ) and PM1:AISeF $9 5 . 3 \%$ and $8 5 . 8 \%$ devices. The results further demonstrate the most efficient exciton dissociation and collection behaviors in PM1: A-OSeF devices, revealing the origin of the highest $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF in the corresponding OSCs.

To further probe into the impact of Se-isomerization of SMAs on the charge carrier recombination degree, transient photovoltage (TPV) measurement was performed to assess the photocarrier lifetime $\left( \tau _ { \mathrm { p h o } } \right)$ of the OSCs. As illustrated in Figure 3f, with the out-shift of mono-selenium substituent from S-CSeF, A-ISeF, to A-OSeF, the $\tau _ { \mathrm { p h o } }$ gradually prolong from $1 4 . 0 \mu \mathrm { s }$ to $1 6 . 5 \mu \mathrm { s }$ to $1 8 . 6 \mu \mathrm { s }$ , implying the weakest charge recombination degree in A-OSeF-based OsCs. As presented in Figure $3 \mathrm { g }$ the charge-extraction time $\left( \tau _ { \mathrm { e x t } } \right)$ of the A-OSeF-based OSCs is calculated to be $0 . 2 7 \mu \mathrm { s }$ by fitting the transient photocurrent (TPC) decay curves, which is shorter than those of A-ISeF-based $( 0 . 3 0 \mu \mathrm { s } )$ and S-CSeFbased $( 0 . 3 2 \mu \mathrm { s } )$ devices. Such the shortest $\tau _ { \mathrm { e x t } }$ means the most efficient charge extraction in the A-OSeF device and it is closely related to charge mobility $( \mu )$ . Moreover, the $\mu$ values were estimated by employing the photo-induced charge extraction in linearly increasing voltage (photoCELIV).The $\mu$ ofA-OSeF-basedOSCs is $1 . 4 4 { \times } 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } .$ which is larger than that of $7 . 4 1 { \times } 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for S-CSeF-based and $1 . 2 8 { \times } 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ for A-ISeF-based deices. The electron $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } \right)$ and hole mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } \right)$ were further assessed through the SCLC method. The detailed results were shown in Figure 3i and Figure S14. The A-OSeF-based OSCs exhibit the highest and most balanced $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ values in comparison to the other devices, accounting for the highest FF and $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ values in the corresponding devices. Thus, these results demonstrate that precisely manipulating the position and type of mono-selenium substituent in the central core may be an efficient strategy to fine-tune the charge transport properties.

Based on the above characterizations and analyses, the improvement of the exciton and charge dynamics are bound up with the film morphology. The surface and bulk morphologies were measured by tapping-mode atomic force microscopy (AFM) and high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (TEM). As depicted in Figure $_ { 4 \mathrm { a - c } }$ , all the blend films display homogeneous surface morphology and the root-mean-square (RMS) roughness of PM1:S-CSeF, PM1:A-ISeF, and PM1:A-OSeF films are 1.47, 0.83, and $1 . 0 5 \mathrm { n m }$ ,respectively. In the AFM phase images (Figure S15), all these three blend films show clear phase separation features and PM1:A-OSeF films exhibit more regular fiber-like aggregates with proper fibril widths of $1 2 . 3 \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure S15), which is conducive to charge generation and transport.[18] Moreover, as observed in TEM images (Figure 4d-f), PM1:A-OSeF films exhibits much more homogeneous and denser domains with a more evident nanofiber-like feature compared with PM1:A-ISeF films and PM1:S-CSeF films, agreeing well with the results observed from their AFM images. Such favorable morphology features of the outermost Se-substituted A-OSeF-based blend films provide the optimized interface contact and thus be beneficial for the efficient exciton dissociation and charge carrier transport, resulting in the higher FF and $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ in corresponding OSCs.

To further investigate the influence of the asymmetric mono-selenium isomerization approach on molecular orientation, intermolecular stacking and crystallinity in the active layers, the GIWAXS technique was carried out. The 2DGIWAXS images and their 1D line-cuts along the OOP and IP directions are shown in Figure $4 \mathrm { g - h }$ . All three blend films adopt the predominant face-on molecular stacking arrangement with a notable (010) diffraction peak in the OOP direction and a pronounced (100) lamellar diffraction peak in the IP direction. The line-cuts of PM1:A-OSeF films exhibit the strongest OOP (010) and IP (100) diffraction peaks relative to those of PM1:S-CSeF and PM1:A-ISeF films, suggesting the most ordered molecular packing. Except for the positive effect of the isomerization of mono-selenium substituted position and type on $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF, the influence of the isomeric acceptors on energy losses $\left( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } \right)$ of the devices also plays a key role in enhancing performance. To further understand the intrinsic reasons for the largely varied $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ values in these isomeric SMAs-based OSCs, the detailed $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of their corresponding devices was quantitatively analyzed. As shown in Figure ${ 5 } \mathrm { a - c }$ , the optical band gaps $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { \tiny { P V } } } )$ values are 1.379, 1.400, and $1 . 4 0 0 \mathrm { e V }$ for PM1:S-CSeF, PM1:A-ISeF, and PM1:A-OSeF matrixes, respectively.[19] The total $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ are $0 . 5 1 5 \mathrm { e V }$ for A-ISeFbased and $0 . 5 2 0 \mathrm { e V }$ for A-OSeF-based devices, which are much lower than that of S-CSeF-based devices $( 0 . 5 2 9 \mathrm { e V } )$ demonstrating the asymmetric mono-selenium substitution at selenophene ring is conducive to suppressing the $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of the devices. According to the Shockley-Queisser (SQ) limit theory, the total $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ in solar cells can normally be categorized into three parts, named $\Delta E _ { 1 }$ (radiative recombination loss above the band gap), $\Delta E _ { 2 }$ (radiative recombination loss below the band gap) and $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ (non-radiative recombination loss), respectively.20] The FTPS-EQE and EL spectra of the devices are given in Figure 5d-f and the detailed $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ parameters of these devices were summarized in Figure $5 \mathrm { g }$ and Table S7. The A-ISeF and A-OSeF-based

OSCs show slightly smaller $\Delta E _ { 1 }$ than the S-CSeF-based device. Though the S-CSeF-based device shows slightly smaller $\Delta E _ { 2 }$ than the $\mathrm { A } { - } I \mathrm { S e F }$ and A-OSeF-based OSCs, the lower $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ for A-ISeF-based and A-OSeF-based OSCs should be mainly attributed to their comparatively low $\Delta E _ { 1 }$ and $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ relative to the S-CSeF-based devices. The $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ are 0.214, 0.194, and $0 . 2 0 3 \mathrm { e V }$ for S-CSeF-based, A-ISeF-based, and A-OSeF-based OSCs, respectively, which can also be calculated according to the formula of $\Delta E _ { 3 } = - \mathbf { k } T I n \mathbf { . }$ $( E Q E _ { \mathrm { E L } } )$ , wherein $\mathbf { k }$ is Boltzmann constant and $T$ is Kelvin temperature.13e-f,21] As presented in Figure 5h, the A-ISeFbased and A-OSeF-based OSCs show larger $E Q E _ { \mathrm { E L } }$ values $( 2 . 0 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ and $2 . 0 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ ) than that of S-CSeF-based devices $( 1 . 4 8 { \times } 1 0 ^ { - 4 } )$ , leading to lower $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ values $( 0 . 2 1 9 \mathrm { e V }$ and $0 . 2 2 0 \mathrm { e V } )$ compared to PM1:S-CSeF $\left( 0 . 2 2 8 \mathrm { e V } \right)$ ,which suggest the significant influence of momo-selenium substituent position on $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ . The $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ calculated by two different approaches show a similar variation trend for the three devices, again verifying moving mono-selenium substitution to the selenophene ring can be helpful for the decrease of voltage loss in OSCs. Furthermore, the Urbach energy $( E _ { \mathrm { u } } )$ of the three devices was analyzed to gain more insight into the non-radiative recombination loss through the exponential fitting of FTPS-EQE spectra. As depicted in Figure 5i, the $E _ { \mathrm { u } }$ values of A-ISeF-based and A-OSeF-based OSCs are 23.13 and $2 3 . 1 8 \mathrm { m e V }$ which are relatively smaller than that of $2 4 . 6 0 \mathrm { m e V }$ for S-CSeF-based devices, which implies the suppressed energetic disorder, and thus results in the decrease of the non-radiative recombination loss. By comparison, PM1:A-ISeF and PM1:A-OSeF achieve lower total $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ benefiting from the largely suppressed $\Delta E _ { 1 }$ $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ and the decreased energy disorders, which should be the key factor that accounts for their higher $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ in corresponding devices. Note that the A-OSeF-based device delivered the smallest $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ value of $0 . 5 2 \mathrm { e V }$ in the reported seleniumcontaining SMAs-based binary OSCs with PCE over $1 8 \%$ simultaneously (Chart S1), implying a better trade-off among $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ and PCE despite its relatively larger energy loss concerning that of A-ISeF, thus rendering the higher FF. Such results further illustrate the validity of subtly manipulating the mono-selenium to the outermost selenophene ring of SMAs in achieving the top-ranked efficiency and lower $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ in binary OSCs, simultaneously.

# Conclusion

To summarize, we designed and synthesized a class of novel A-DA'D-A type symmetric or asymmetric SMAs (S-CSeF, $\mathrm { A } { - } I \mathrm { S e F }$ , and $\mathbf { A } { \boldsymbol { \mathbf { \mathit { \mathbf { \Lambda } } } } } ( O \mathbf { S } \mathbf { e } \mathbf { F } )$ with three isomeric mono-seleniumsubstituted central cores by the successive out-shift of the mono-selenium substituted position in combination with manipulating the type of selenium-based heteroaromatic ring for efficient OsCs. The synergistic effect of the regional isomerization and symmetry-broken selenium-substitution of SMAs on optical and electrochemical properties, single crystal packing, photovoltaic parameters, and energy loss were thoroughly investigated and explicitly compared. The first time reported single crystallographic structures reveal that asymmetric A-OSeF with the outmost selenium-substituted central core shows a more planar molecular skeleton and a more compact $\pi - \pi$ stacking and more ordered 3D network packing due to the presence of extra intramolecular Se..O and intermolecular F..Se non-covalent interactions compared to those of A-ISeF and S-CSeF crystal, facilitating the formation of a more effective multiple charge-transport channels. From S-CSeF to A-ISeF, and A-OSeF, with moving the mono-selenium substitution position from the central to the inner and then to the outer on the central core, slightly elevated LUMO energy levels and gradually larger dipole moments were observed and the corresponding neat films present a slightly wider band gap and gradually higher crystallinity and electron mobility. When blended with the seldom-reported polymer donor PM1, compared to the devices based on A-ISeF $( 1 7 . 6 \% )$ and S-CSeF $( 1 6 . 3 \% )$ the A-OSeF-based binary OSCs achieve a champion PCE of $1 8 . 5 \%$ . The performance enhancement should be mainly attributed to the efficient charge generation and collection as well as the suppressed energy loss, benefiting from the most favorable nanofibrous morphology, and molecular packing in PM1:A-OSeF blends, as well as the appropriate band gap and elevated LUMO energy level of A-OSeF. Remarkably, the best PCE of $1 8 . 5 \%$ with an $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of $0 . 5 2 \mathrm { e V }$ is the record value among the binary BHJ-OSCs based on selenium-containing SMAs. Our systematical work demonstrates the importance and synergetic effect of selecting the position and type of selenium-based heteroaromatic ring of SMAs in manipulating crystal packing behaviors and boosting the trade-off among $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ and PCE in selenium-containing SMAs and provides insights into the synergetic design principles of SMAs toward future high-performance OSCs.
