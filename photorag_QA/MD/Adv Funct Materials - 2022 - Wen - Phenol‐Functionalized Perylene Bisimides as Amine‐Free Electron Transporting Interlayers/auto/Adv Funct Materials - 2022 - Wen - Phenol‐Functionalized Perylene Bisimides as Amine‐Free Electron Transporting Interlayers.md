# Phenol-Functionalized Perylene Bisimides as Amine-Free Electron Transporting Interlayers for Stable Nonfullerene Organic Solar Cells

Xinbo Wen, Yu Zhang, Guojing Xie, Rodger Rausch, Ningning Tang, Nan Zheng, Linlin Liu, Frank Wurthner,\* and Zengqi Xie\*

A new type of cathode interlayer composed of 2,6-di-tert-butyl-phenolfunctionalized perylene bisimide (PBI-2P) is successfully applied as an electron transporting layer for fused-ring nonfullerene organic solar cells (OSCs). The stable contact between these novel electron transporting layers and the representative nonfullerene acceptor Y6 greatly enhances the device stability compared to conventional amine-group containing cathode interlayers. Moreover, the easily formed biradical species in the interlayers yields rather good thickness tolerance of the PBI-2P layer in photovoltaic devices. The OSCs based on the PBI-2P interlayer show a power conversion efficiency up to $1 7 . 2 0 \%$ and good stability compared to amino-group functionalized interlayers. The findings demonstrate a promising design principle for cathode interlayer engineering based on pigment chromophores equipped with the 2,6-di-tert-butylphenoxy groups that are prone to form the respective ultrastable butylphenoxy radicals for stable nonfullerene OSCs.

# 1. Introduction

As one of the promising solar energy conversion techniques, organic solar cells (OsCs) made great progresses with power conversion efficiency (PCE) reaching meanwhile up to $1 8 \%$ mainly owing to the successful development of highly efficient fused-ring nonfullerene electron acceptors.[1-7] However, the aim of durability of nonfullerene OsCs still remains as a great challenge, partially due to the lame development of compatible and robust cathode interlayer (CIL) materials.[8-10] Previously, the application of amine-group containing cathode interlayers such as poly[(9,9-bis $3 ^ { \prime }$ -(N,N-dimethylamino) propyl)-2,7-fluorene)-alt-2,7-(9,9-dioctylfluorene)] (PFN),[11,12] polyethylenimine ethoxylated (PEIE),13,14] amine-functionalized perylene diimide (PDIN),[15,16] and the various analogues boosted the device performance in fullerene based OsCs. Here, the amine groups in cathode interlayer materials are regarded as necessary to tune the work functions (WFs) of the electrode and to help the electron extraction from the fullerene acceptors.17,18] with the remarkably fast development of new fused-ring nonfullerene electron acceptors,[19,20] little efforts so far were dedicated to new interlayer materials and as a consequence amine-containing cathode interlayer materials are utilized in nonfullerene OsCs as well.[21] However, albeit amazing PCEs have been achieved by this "copy" and "paste' approach,[8] the contact between the fusedring acceptors and the amine-group containing interlayer materials is unstable, and serious decomposition of both species is frequently observed because of the easily occurring electron transfer from amine groups to the acceptor molecules under light irradiation conditions.14,22] Thus, it is an urgent demand to develop specific interlayer materials designed specifically for the fused-ring nonfullerene acceptor systems toward durable OSC devices.

In this research article, an entirely new class of cathode interlayer materials is reported based on a perylene bisimide (PBI) dye equipped with two 2,6-di-tert-butylphenol units (PBI2P, shown in Scheme 1) which, as we will demonstrate, are nicely compatible to the fused-ring electron acceptors. Whilst such strongly sterically shielded phenols are appreciated as free-radical scavengers and oxidants in many technical applications due to the high stability of their phenoxy radicals (formed by deprotonation and oxidation),[23] it was only recently that they were also applied as substituents in a variety of pigment dyes including PB1s,[24] isoindigos,[25] and diketopyrrolopyrroles (DPPs)[26,27] to afford biradicals of unusually high stability and semi-conductivity. Here, we demonstrate for the first time the utilization of these functional dyes as cathode interlayer materials to replace the conventional amine-based materials. The device performance of OsCs using these phenol-functionalized pigment containing cathode interlayers is as good as or even better than the devices based on the amine-based cathode interlayers, and most importantly, the device stability under illumination is significantly improved. In addition, the easy formation of biradicals from PBI-2P endows these materials with a rather good thickness tolerance of the cathode interlayers in devices.[28-30]

# 2. Result and Discussion

PBI-2P was synthesized according to our previously reported method[31] by the reaction shown in Scheme 1. The details for the synthesis and characterizations are given in the Supporting Information. The multiple sterically demanding tert-butyl and iso-propyl groups attached on the phenol units and imide phenyl substituents greatly enhance the solubility of the molecules in common organic solvents. Assisted with the hydroxy groups, PBI-2P is even soluble in polar alcohol solvents like methanol or ethanol, i.e., the solubility in methanol is up to ${ \approx } 2 0 ~ \mathrm { m g ~ m L ^ { - 1 } }$ . For interlayer materials, a sufficiently good solubility in alcohol is one of the most essential issues in the solution processing of multilayer devices and further application in future inkjet processing OSCs.[32]

The optical properties of PBI-2P in three typical solvents with different polarity, methanol (MeOH), dichloromethane (DCM), and methyl cyclohexane were explored by UV-vis absorption and fluorescence spectroscopies (Figure 1 and Figure S5, Supporting Information). Compared with the bay-area twofold bromine substituted PBI (PBI-2Br) precursor, the absorption onsets and maxima of PBI-2P show pronounced redshifts (Figure 1a), indicating the electron-donating effects from the phenol groups to the electron-deficient PBI core.[33] Meanwhile, the electron-withdrawing effect on the para-position of the phenol enhances its acidity and thus affords easily deprotonation into phenolates and subsequent oxidation into radical and biradical species of PBI-2P.24] Indeed, electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) signals for the solids of PBI-2P indicate the presence of unpaired electrons (Figure 1b and Figure S4, Supporting Information), in accordance with our previous in-depth characterization of these species in solution.[24] As summarized in Figure S7 and Table S2 in the Supporting Information, the conductivities of PBI-2P $( 2 . 9 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \mathrm { ~ S ~ c m ^ { - 1 } } )$ are higher than that of PBI-2Br $( 0 . 9 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \mathrm { ~ S ~ c m ^ { - 1 } } )$ as desired for a sufficient electron transporting ability of the interface material to reduce the interface resistance.

Since PBI-2P is very soluble in most common organic solvents including chloroform, chlorobenzene, and methanol as discussed above, it is successful application as a cathode interlayer material is only possible in conventional device architectures where this interlayer was fabricated atop of the active layer from methanol, avoiding the possible corrosion of the active layer on substrate. To get more insight into the essential components in PBI-2P for this impressive functional property we also fabricated OSCs by using 2,6-di-tert-butyl-phenol as a cathode interlayer (Table S5, Supporting Information) and the results indicate this material had minor impact on the device performance. However, by using another 2,6-di-tert-butyl-phenol-functionalized pigment dye, i.e., twofold functionalized diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP-2P)[26] (for structural formula, see Figure S11, Supporting Information) as cathode interlayer, we could gain a good device performance with PCE value of $1 6 . 9 5 \%$ without further optimization (Table S6, Supporting Information). These results not only indicate the importance of having both phenolic and electron-withdrawing conjugated chromophores combined in a functional molecule but also suggest a wider potential for the future design of tailored cathode interlayer materials for different active layer materials that we aim to explore in our future work.

To better understand the effects of the PBI-2P interlayers in OSCs, the electron extraction dynamic was studied by transient photocurrent measurements, which is widely used to explore the intrinsic characters of the charge extraction process and recombination kinetics. When employing PBI-2P as a cathode interlayer, a much faster decay time of $0 . 9 6 ~ \mu \mathrm { s }$ , relative to the device with PFN-Br (1.34 s), PDINO $( 1 . 2 4 \mu \mathrm { s } )$ interlayers, and without cathode interlayer $( 3 . 1 0 ~ \mu \mathrm { s } )$ , was observed (Figure 2e and Table s7, Supporting Information), which corroborates the efficient charge extraction efficiency. These results confirm the improvement of the electron extraction by the PBI-2P layer and correspond well to the reduced $R _ { \mathrm { S } }$ increased $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values mentioned above. To investigate the electron transportation between PBI-2P and the nonfullerene acceptor we fabricated electron-only single carrier devices in the configuration of Al/Y6/PBI-2P/Al. As shown in Figure 2f, the sharper slope of the curve in PBI-2P containing device implies the desirable electron transport when compared with the other devices. Accordingly, we determined the apparent electron mobilities using space-charge limit current (SCLC) method. The device with neat Y6 film gave a poor SCLC mobility of $3 . 7 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ because of the unsatisfying electron transport toward the bare Al electrode. However, the device with PBI-2P cathode interlayer showed a value of more than two orders of magnitude higher, reaching $6 . 2 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ which is even higher than the values for PFN-Br and PDINO based devices (Table S8, Supporting Information).

For the promotion of highly efficient fused-ring nonfullerene OsCs toward commercial application it is crucial to achieve long-lived devices. However, only a small fraction of the scientific reports addressed this important issue so far. The majority of fused-ring acceptors contain similar as in the case of Y6 acceptor-donor-acceptor moieties with electron withdrawing dicyanomethylene indanone (IC) termini. For these materials several studies reported a quick decay of OsC performance due to decomposition processes which was attributed to the electron transfer from amine groups of the conventional cathode interlayer materials to the IC groups.[1,22] Accordingly, in the following we compared the stability of the devices based on PBI-2P, PFN-Br, PDINO and PEIE interlayers, exposing them under $2 5 \ \mathrm { ~ W ~ }$ white-LED light in a nitrogen-filled glovebox without encapsulation. As shown in Figure 3a the stability of PBI-2P based OsCs is obviously better than those of the other interlayers-based devices. The time for the decays to $90 \%$ of the initial PCE values is $^ { 1 2 \mathrm { ~ d ~ } }$ for PBI-2P based device while it is only ${ \approx } 6 \mathrm { ~ d ~ }$ for the others. In order to clarify the essential factors on the increased device stability for PBI-2P we compared the changes in the absorption spectra for a mixture of PBI-2P:Y6 (1:1.3 by weight) and the bilayer film of Y6/PEIE $( \approx 3 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ for each layer) on quartz substrates, with or without simulated solar light irradiation. As shown in Figure 3b, the blend film of PBI-2P:Y6 was quite robust under simulated sunlight according to the unchanged absorption spectra for Y6 (Figure 3b). In contrast, the color of the Y6 film spin-coated on PEIE layer suffered severe changes compared with that of the neat Y6 film on quartz substrates already in the absence of light, indicating undesired decomposition of Y6 caused by the PEIE interlayer (Figure 3c). Moreover, this process is accelerated by sun light irradiation, suggesting further chemical reactions between them as reported before by other groups.[19,21] The contrasting behavior, i.e., compatibility of PBI-2P and Y6 versus easy reaction between PEIE and Y6 can already be observed for mixtures of these materials in dilute chlorobenzene solution (Figure S12, Supporting Information). Indeed, hindered phenols were demonstrated to successfully act as antioxidants in the fullerene OSCs.[37] The existence of hindered phenol as additives in active layer is detrimental to carrier transporting but good for stability as stabilizer. However, as electron transporting layer, the stable contact between PBI-2P and fused-ring nonfullerene acceptors is apparently in favor of the trade-off between stability and high performance of the OsCs. Since it was reported that 2,6-ditert-butyl-phenol can be used as singlet oxygen quencher and stabilizer in organic electronics for suppressing photooxidation of molecules, 2,6-di-tert-butyl-phenol groups in PBI-2P are regarded as the key point for the increased compatibility with nonfullerene acceptors.[38]

With the fact in mind that the easy (photo-)oxidation of 2,6-ditert-butylphenol units affords PBI-2P radical and biradical species,[24] we further examined the effect of the PBI-2P interlayer thickness on the OsC device performance (Figures S13-S15,

Supporting Information, and Table 1). As summarized in Table 1 and Table S9 in the Supporting Information, a thickness increase from 10 to $2 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ leads to a decrease of the averaged PCE from $1 6 . 9 8 \%$ to $1 5 . 4 7 \%$ whilst a further increase of the interlayer thickness to $> 3 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ induced S-shaped $J { - } V$ curves (Figure S15, Supporting Information). In general, the thickness tolerance is related to the electron transport properties and S-shaped J-V kink will be present when it is beyond the optimal thickness range of the respective cathode interlayer.[39] Indeed, the thickness tolerance observed for PBI-2P is much better than that for PFN-Br as shown in Table 1 where a $5 \mathrm { n m }$ PFN-Br layer afforded a PCE of $1 7 . 0 6 \%$ while already for the $1 5 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ PFN-Br layer a serious decrease of the PCE to only $8 . 9 4 \%$ with S-shaped $J { - } V$ curve was observed. We also noticed that the thickness tolerance for PBI-2P is at the same level of PDINO that possesses relatively good electron transport properties due to the strong intermolecular $\pi - \pi$ stacking (Figure S17 and Table S11, Supporting Information).[15,16] Considering the relatively weak intermolecular stacking of PBI-2P due to the bulky substituents, we hypothesize that the easy formation of radical species in the cathode interlayer as evidenced by our EPR studies is responsible for the good thickness tolerance in device. As shown in Figure S4 in the Supporting Information, the EPR spectra implies that the electron acceptor Y6 could increase the production of radical species in PBI-2P, which might due to the proton interaction between IC termini and bulky hydroxyl groups that is easily deprotonated under base condition.(4,41] 1t can be seen from the X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy spectrum that the carbonyl signal is shifted by the functionalized PBI-2P (Figures S16 and S17, Supporting Information). Under working conditions of OSCs, the PBI-2P layer might denote protons to Y6 and electrons to the Ag anode along with the current direction consequently, which promotes the formation of more radical species in this layer and thus facilitates electron transport. There is potential for further improvement of the electron conductivity by specific doping, and the investigation is ongoing in our lab.

# 3. Conclusion

To summarize, we reported a new class of phenol-functionalized perylene bisimide based electron transporting materials that can be applied as solution-processable cathode interlayers in organic solar cells. The proton donating phenol groups, contrasting to the electron donating amino groups in the popular cathode interlayer materials, enable stable contact between the cathode interlayers and the fused-ring electron acceptors, which enhanced the stability of polymer-based OsCs. Further, a satisfactory thickness tolerance in device applications was observed which was attributed to the easy formation of PBI-2P biradicals. Accordingly, photovoltaic devices based on PBI-2P interlayers showed remarkable performance even when the interlayers thicknesses were up to $3 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . As a result, impressive PCEs up to $1 7 . 2 \%$ were demonstrated for PM6:Y6-based device by employing PBI-2P as the cathode interlayer. We expect that this successful example for an entirely new cathode interlayer material design will promote future studies on tailored materials for long-term stable nonfullerene OsCs on the way to commercial applications.
