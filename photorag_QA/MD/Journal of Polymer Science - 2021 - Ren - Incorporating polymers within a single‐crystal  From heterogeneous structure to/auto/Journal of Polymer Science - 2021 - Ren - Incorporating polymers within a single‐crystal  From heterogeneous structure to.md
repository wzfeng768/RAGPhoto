# Abstract

Single-crystals, commonly considered as homogeneous solids, are able to be internally interfaced abnormally with guest polymers, which can be found in the biominerals where single-crystals incorporate surrounding biomacromolecules to reinforce their mechanical properties. This unique feature combining heterogeneous structure and long-range atomic ordering have attracted abundant investigations of reproducing their synthetic analogs to expand the potential application scope. Here, we summarize the recent progresses in the synthetic single-crystal composites, where polymer guests are incorporated inside single-crystals to generate heterogeneous structures without interruption to the long-range ordering of crystal hosts. First, the uniform and patterned encapsulations inside the various single-crystals are concluded in the sequence of isolated and continuous polymer-based guests. In addition, the mechanisms are classified chemically and physically, and the corresponding controlled factors that govern the incorporation processes are discussed. Most importantly, typical attempts on the applications of these heterogeneous single crystals are introduced, including mechanical reinforcement, bandgap engineering, catalyst, self-healing, controlled release, and optoelectronic devices. We aim at stressing on the current and potential applications benefited from the unique structural properties of the polymer incorporated single-crystals, and accordingly propose the perspectives to accelerate the path from the structural analysis toward prosperous functions.

# 1 INTRODUCTION

Single-crystals are conventionally known as homogeneous solids with long-range ordering lattice and uniform chemical components. However, with the incorporation of biomacromolecules, heterogeneous structure often forms within biomineral single-crystals, which is achieved by living organisms to construct their hard tissues.1-11 As examples, echinoderms (like sea urchins and brittle stars) and mollusk shells (such as Atrina rigida and Pinna nobilis) have been widely recognized as heterogeneous structures consisting of biomacromolecules and calcite single-crystal.4-11 Although these biomacromulecular "impurities," including continuous networks4,' and discrete fibers or particles,3,8-10 are found to be incorporated inside growing single-crystals, they can hardly destroy the lattice arrangement of the single-crystalline host. The introduction of guest biomacromolecules is thought to be critical for enhancing mechanical properties of host single-crystals, especially their intrinsic brittleness.12 Therefore, such unique combination of internal heterostructure and long-range ordering lattice, as well as the consequent enhancements on mechanical properties have spurred a lot of interests in imitating these biomineralization processes with synthetic functional analogs.

On one hand, similar to the natural process, synthetic single-crystals could incorporate the surrounding aggregates during crystallization. These specific aggregates could be well-designed polymer-based components in many forms, such as isolated particles,13 micelles,14 fibers,15 and continuous gel networks16-18 and templates.9,20 Moreover, as-prepared inorganic nanomaterials are hard to be incorporated inside singlecrystals directly, but are able to be easily "brought into" single-crystals in the presence of gel networks.21,22 On the other hand, apart from the most common-seen calcium carbonate and hydroxyapatite among biominerals, diverse single-crystalline candidates of "artificial biominerals" have been investigated, including inorganics (e.g., $Z _ { \mathrm { { n O } } ^ { 2 3 } }$ and $\mathrm { P b I } _ { 2 } { } ^ { 2 4 }$ , organic small molecules (e.g., $\alpha$ glycine25 and anthracene?6) and macromolecules (e.g., proteins27). Very recently, the internal structure of a crystal could be even patterned sequentially with different incorporation states by dynamically oscillating the conditions instead of the traditional uniform incorporation.28 Meanwhile, mechanisms of the encapsulation of guest materials inside the single-crystals have been discussed frequently within these years.29,30 In detail, tremendous factors affecting the crystallization have been elucidated to govern the incorporation process, such as crystal growing rate and physical or chemical properties of both guest materials and host crystals.31,32

Consequently, the investigations of various singlecrystal hosts and polymer guests and the deep understanding of the incorporation mechanism significantly expand the horizons of the polymer incorporation inside single-crystals and ensure extensive potentials in the further application of those bio-inspired composites. In many cases, properties of single-crystal hosts regulated or even endowed by the guest incorporation materials have been studied, including not only the mechanical33 and semiconducting34 properties, but also the optical and magnetic properties.21 Besides, it has been reported that the encapsulation by single-crystal hosts improves the stability of incorporated guests obviously22 and enables controlled release of the guest materials.35 More importantly, the interpenetration texture within the heterogeneous structure combined with the long-range ordering lattice of crystal hosts enables a great potential in boosting optoelectronic properties. Lately, gel networks of conjugated polymers were incorporated into fullerene single-crystals, forming long-range ordered "bulk-heterojunction" between electron donor-acceptor pairs and leading to an enhanced charge-transfer process.36 In fact, the research of constructing and revealing the specific structures where single-crystal hosts incorporate polymer guests are heading toward achieving multiple functions and practical applications to solve a wide range of realistic issues.

Herein, we provide a primary review of those bioinspired composites about their heterogeneous but ordering structures constructed by the diverse polymer guests and single-crystal hosts. Also, the corresponding mechanisms and controlling parameters that influence the incorporation processes are discussed. Moreover, given by the unique structures, the regulations on the properties of corresponding hosts and guests as well as the multiple functions originated from the polymer incorporated single-crystals are elucidated (Figure 1). Of particular note is that the introduced polymer guests discussed in this paper are mainly in their different aggregation states (e.g., isolated particles/micelles/fibers or continuous gel networks/templates) or spatial distributions, yet the incorporation of polymers that molecularly dispersed37-41 inside crystal hosts may not be detailed. Also, in the most cases discussed in this paper, the host matrix retains its single-crystal nature, although pores and defects form inside due to the incorporation of guest polymers.

# 2 | STRUCTURAL FEATURE OF THE INCORPORATION

# 2.1 Uniform incorporation

# 2.1.1 | Incorporation of isolated nano-objects

Typically, the aggregation states of guest materials that are uniformly incorporated inside single-crystals can be categorized into two types, isolated nano-objects and continuous matrices. The nanoparticles (NP's), micelles or vesicles that assembled from block copolymers, as well as surface-functionalized polymer particles or even oil droplets, can be introduced inside single-crystals as the typical isolated objects. To obtain the heterogeneous structure, generally, these polymer aggregates are dispersed in the crystallization media followed by single-crystal growth. Wegner et al. first demonstrated the occlusion of polymer latex particles surface-functionalized by carboxylic groups into ZnO single-crystals.13,23,42,43 Similarly, incorporation of such latexes inside calcite33,44 and $\mathrm { { C u } } _ { 2 } \mathrm { { O } } ^ { 4 4 }$ singlecrystals was also investigated. Later, anionic diblock copolymers were synthesized by Meldrum et al. and organized into micelles with corona of carboxylate chains. Those micelles exhibited uniform and efficient occlusion inside calcite single-crystals (Figure 2A). The incorporation of the ${ \sim } 2 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ sized spherical micelles (Figure 2B) inside calcite host was confirmed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM), identifying the collapsed micelles after being engulfed (Figure 2C). Moreover, the calcite host maintained the long-range ordering lattice as investigated by the high-resolution imaging (Figure 2D) and selected area electron diffraction (SAED, Figure 2E). Accordingly, the synthetic crystals, with up to $1 3 \%$ weight ratio of occluded micelles, exhibited a composite structure analogous to the biogenic calcite single-crystals.14 In addition, block copolymerbased nanomaterials with diverse morphologies, such as spherical particles/micelles, worms or vesicles, were developed to be incorporated inside single-crystals.45-49

# 2.1.3 Multiphases incorporation

Other than the two primary routes, there was a combination where single-crystals incorporated both isolated nano-objects and continuous networks. The as-prepared Au and $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ NP's, which were unable to be occluded into calcite single-crystals directly, were dispersed in agarose solutions first and, after gelation, trapped in the gel networks.1 when calcite crystallized in such matrix, it incorporated both gel networks and NP's (Figure 5A,D). The coexistence of gel networks and NP's was illustrated by ADF-STEM and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX, Figure 5E). Also, the lattice image with the unaffected lattice fringes surrounding an individual incorporated NP, as well as a single set of SAED pattern, demonstrating the unspoiled long-range ordering of calcite hosts (Figure 5F). Similarly, it has been proved that not only Au and $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ NP's but also other nanomaterials involving ${ \mathrm { Z n O } }$ 68 $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ 34 quantum dots (QD's) (Figure 5G-N),22 carbon nanomaterials,69 and microgels1 could be incorporated into single-crystals along with agarose or xyloglucan gels. # 2.2 |  Nonuniform and patterned incorporation

In addition to the uniform incorporation where the guest materials distribute over an entire single-crystal, spatially nonuniform distribution of incorporated guest materials can be achieved in some cases. With coexistence of both guest-free and guest-incorporated states inside an individual single-crystal, there are two kinds of core-shell structures, that is, surface-confined and center-confined incorporation. For surface-confined incorporation, the guest NP's are incorporated only into the surface region of a single-crystal, forming pure crystal core with a composite shell layer.4,70-72 In contrast, center-confined incorporation, where the incorporated species only distributed throughout the center region (core) of a singlecrystal with a guest-free shell layer, was found in either particles'3 or gel networks28 incorporation. The probable forming mechanisms of these core-shell structured single-crystals with nonuniform incorporation will be elucidated in the next chapter. Other than the core-shell structure, crystals that forming at the interface of gel media and supernatant solutions incorporated gel networks only in one side of the crystals, which was found in many cases.61,64

Furthermore, crystallization media could be changed artificially to realize the coexistence of different incorporation states inside a single-crystal. As reported, copolymer worms in the solution media were exchanged to copolymer vesicles at a certain time during calcite crystallization, resulting in a patterned structure inside a singlecrystal where the incorporated worms and vesicles existed in the center and surface region, respectively.45 More importantly, if the two states of crystals with and without incorporation were defined as "1" and "0,"' respectively, the internal structure of an individual KDP crystal was able to be programmed with a sequence such as "110' or "01oi0' by oscillating the media during crystallization process.8 To realize this purpose, regulated thickness of the overgrown layer with ("1") or without $( \ " 0 ^ { \bullet } )$ gel incorporation was carried out by controlling epitaxial crystallization time of a KDP seed in gel or solution media, respectively. Thus, complicated sequence with the same neighboring states (e.g., "110") or with high length (e.g., O1oi0) was achieved by altering the crystallization media between gel and solution with precisely controlled crystallization kinetics (Figure 6A,B). Even so, the long-range ordering lattice throughout the entire patterned crystal still remained (Figure 6C,D). Similarly, patterned incorporation of polymer NP's inside calcite single-crystals was carried out by altering the crystallization media.2 As such, modulation of the spatial distribution of the incorporated guest materials inside a single-crystal enables designing a wide range of novel composite single-crystals with patterned fashions.

# 3 | MECHANISMS AND CONTROLLING FACTORS OF INCORPORATION

# 3.1 |Incorporation mechanisms

Within the past two decades, there were an increasing number of guest materials that could be incorporated inside single-crystal hosts, while still a large amount of materials exhibited incompatibility when embedded within single-crystals.31,32,74 Thus, it is of great significance to reveal the mechanisms for the incorporation processes and, to date, several related studies on the incorporation of isolated particles and gel networks have been carried out.

In situ atomic force microscopy $( \mathbf { A F M } ) ^ { 7 5 - 7 7 }$ has provided crucial insights regarding the detailed occlusion processes of isolated particles into growing calcite singlecrystals. As reported by De Yoreo et al.,29 the occluded particles first bound to the step edges of growing crystals and subsequently stop the propagation of the following steps screened by the binding particles. After passing beyond the particles, the advancing fronts closed eventually and, thus, engulfed the particles (Figure 7A). Accordingly, an efficient incorporation requires an intimate binding of the guest materials to the crystal surface, which will promote the incorporation in at least two ways. On one hand, guest materials that attached to the growing crystal surface are less likely to detach, improving the possibility of them to be incorporated. On the other hand, once the binding guest prevents the ions from accessing the step edges for further crystallization, the steps will be forced to propagate around the guest materials instead of growing underneath, leading to the incorporation. Besides, by virtue of such attachment and binding interactions, the free-moving particles in the solution media will be enriched into the growing singlecrystals, probably resulting in the higher content of particle incorporated in crystals.73 Thus, chemical and physical factors that promote the strong binding can facilitate the incorporation, which are discussed in the following context. Of particular note is that particles would be exhausted before the accomplishment of crystallization if the initial particle concentration in dispersion was less than a critical value, leading to the unique internal distribution of the particles with a pure crystal surface layer and the center-confined incorporation consequently.73

# 3.2 1 Chemical factors

The attraction between guest materials and crystal hosts contributes to the binding interaction significantly, which relies on the chemical properties of both guest materials and crystal hosts. PS latex particles were able to be embedded within the surface of calcite (surface-confined incorporation) once modified with carboxylic groups, 33 indicating that the electrostatic interaction between charged particles and ionic crystal might be crucial for incorporation. A series of NP's, with the steric stabilizer chains in the same overall degree of polymerization, were utilized to examine the effects of charge content to the extent of incorporation.79,80 The steric stabilizer chains were copolymerized in various molar ratios of anionic monomers, 2-(phosphonooxy)ethyl methacrylate, and nonionic monomers, glycerol monomethacrylate, leading to a systematically distribution of anionic charge densities (Figure 8A). As illustrated in Figure 8B-G, a transition from nonuniform incorporation to uniformly dense incorporation occurred during raising the proportion of anionic units in the chains.9 Instead of general electrostatic interactions, moreover, specific interaction, such as the intimate attraction between $\mathrm { C a } ^ { 2 + }$ and carboxylate groups, which are structurally similar to $\mathrm { C O } _ { 3 } ^ { 2 - }$ , might promote the incorporation of particles. In related works, a wide range of ionic functionalization, including carboxylate, phosphate, sulfate, sulfonate groups, and cationic quaternary ammonium groups have been performed on polymer latex particles44 or copolymer-based vesicles.47 Typically, carboxylate and phosphate groups favored the incorporation of guest materials in comparison with other functional groups in the case of vesicle incorporation.47 In addition to electrostatic attraction between ion counter-pair, coordinate bond alternatively played an important role in binding particles with crystal surfaces. Gold NP's were modified with nonionic PGMA and occluded efficiently into ${ \mathrm { Z n O } }$ crystals. The accomplishment of incorporation in the absence of anionic charges on the particles was presumably attribute to the strong adsorption of NP's on $\mathrm { Z n O }$ crystal surfaces given by the formation of chelate complexes between ${ \mathrm { Z n } } ^ { 2 + }$ and the pendent cis-diol groups on the PGMA chains.73 Similarly, gold (Figure $2 \mathrm { F } { - } \mathrm { K } ) ^ { 5 5 }$ and silica1 NP's functionalized with such hydroxyl groups were successfully occluded inside calcite single-crystals.

Silica gel networks have been used to study the influence of chemical interactions between gel and crystal in a series of works. Hydrogen bonds (H-bonds) that formed between hydroxyl groups on silica gel networks and F or O atoms were first demonstrated to facilitate the incorporation of silica gels into NaF and KDP single-crystals. As a comparison, the silica gel failed to be encapsulated within NaCl and KBr crystals where Cl and Br were unable to form H-bond with hydroxyl groups.64 Besides, the incorporation efficiency was enhanced after modifying the silica gel networks with amino groups in the case of $\mathrm { P b I } _ { 2 }$ crystals grown from organic silica gels. This promotion of incorporation was attributed to the stronger electrostatic interaction between $\mathrm { P b }  { \mathrm { I } _ { 2 } }$ and amino groups compared with that between $\mathrm { P b }  { \mathrm { I } _ { 2 } }$ and hydroxyl groups.34 Apart from the electrostatic interaction, hydrophobic interaction is beneficial for the incorporation inside the hydrophobic organic single-crystals. Owing to the repulsion between hydrophilic hydroxyl groups and hydrophobic hosts, silica gel networks were inconspicuously incorporated into PAHs single-crystals. After introduction of hydrophobic phenyl groups on silica gel networks, the incorporation became uniform and continuous. Moreover, the guest content in crystal hosts increased when raising the concentration of phenyl groups in the whole gel networks, by virtue of the hydrophobic attraction along with $\pi { - } \pi$ interaction.26

# 3.3 |Physical factors

# 3.3.1 Conformational freedom

The significance of conformational freedom of functional segments was initially raised when PS particles with the corona of flexible carboxylic chains were uniformly incorporated into calcite with a remarkably higher concentration.33 Conformational freedom heavily depended on the structural factors of steric stabilizer chains, among which the surface density and the length of these chains on the particles were verified. Armes et al.47,48,82 conducted the copolymerization of different monomers in the presence of silica NP's through reversible additionfragmentation chain transfer polymerization (RAFT) strategy (Figure 9A). By virtue of the RAFT strategy, the length (degree of polymerization) of the steric stabilizer chains was controlled precisely whereas the surface densities of these chains, as well as the vesicle core size, were kept in constant. As illustrated in Figure 9B-H, imperceptible occlusion of vesicles inside calcite single-crystals was found before grafting while nonuniform occlusion occurred after short chains were grafted. In sharp contrast, the incorporation of vesicles became uniform and dense after increasing the chain lengths. The higher level of conformational freedom determined by the longer chains ensured a stronger binding of vesicles to calcite surfaces and, thus, promoted the vesicle incorporation initially. However, as hypothesized by the authors, mutual electrostatic repulsion among the vesicles with excessively long steric stabilizer chains would stop the vesicles from approaching the calcite surfaces.47

On the other hand, copolymer particles with higher surface density of steric stabilizer chains are generally assumed to gain a higher level of conformational freedom. Nevertheless, it has shown that a higher surface density of those chains conversely hindered the incorporation of particles into calcite.2 This unexpected result was attributed to the probable interchain ionic crosslinking of poly(methacrylic acid) steric stabilizer chains by $\mathrm { C a } ^ { 2 + }$ . Dense steric stabilizer chains on a particle surface led to a high-crosslinking density of such chains and, in contrast, a decrement in the level of conformational freedom. This mechanism also helped to explain the surface-confined incorporation where almost all the occluded particles distributed in near-surface regions of single-crystals.33,48 The concentration of $\mathrm { C a } ^ { 2 + }$ continuously declined during calcite crystallization, which weakened the ionic crosslinking and ensured enough conformational freedom of particles for incorporation. Hence, for a maximized extent of incorporation, it is necessary to design the particles modified by the steric stabilizer chains with the optimum length and surface density.83

# 3.3.2| Crystallization kinetics and gel strength

In addition to the physical properties of guest materials, crystalization velocity78,8-87 determines the incorporation of guest materials as well. A larger hydrodynamic force given by a faster crystal growth rate intensifies the binding interaction between guests and crystal hosts as the growing crystal pushing the attached guest aggregates, which will restrain the guests detaching the growing surfaces and limit the mass transport toward the screened fronts. In a result, the incorporation can be promoted.

Moreover, such binding interaction would be physically strengthened, if the attached polymer aggregates against crystallization pressure were supported and confined by the interconnected networks, revealing the significance of gel strength. In related works, calcite singlecrystals were grown from two types of agarose gels with different gel strength respectively. Under a slow crystallization rate, the stronger gel networks were incorporated into calcite single-crystals while the weaker gel networks were pushed away.78 In another case, NaCl and KBr crystals that were unable to incorporate silica gel as discussed above, exhibited efficient incorporation of the mechanically stronger agarose gel.64 Since the functional group of both silica and agarose gel networks were hydroxyl groups, the difference in gel strength should be the main reason for the opposite results. More importantly, given by the speed-dependent mechanism, center-confined incorporation of silica gels can be achieved. During the crystallization process, growth rate of KDP single-crystals decreased continuously due to the ion consumption. When the growth rate declined to a threshold, the growing crystals tended to push away the surrounding gel networks rather than incorporating them, leading to a comparatively pure shell layer.28 Furthermore, after this patterned KDP single-crystal with "10' sequence (as elucidated in Section 2.2) obtained in gel media, nonsolvent was added above the gel again to further induce the supersaturation of KDP in the gel media. Consequently, such KDP crystals regained fast growth rate and restarted the efficient incorporation of gel networks, resulting in a "101' sequence.28 However, the interconnection of gel networks restricts the free motion of most segments in the networks. Thus, only gel segments nearby the growing crystal surfaces are able to be attached on the advancing fronts and enrichment are less likely to happen, which might be the reason of the conspicuously lower content of the incorporated gel networks (less than or about $1 \ \mathrm { w t } \% )$ than that of the occluded particles (more than $1 0 \ \mathrm { \ w t } \%$ ) inside singlecrystals.

For the crystallization kinetics that influence polymer incorporation, nucleation is worthy to be under consideration as well, which will consequently effect the formation of the composites. Surface chemical properties of a gold-coated porous polymer membrane imitating sea urchin skeletal plates were easily adjusted by selfassembled monolayers (SAMs). Interestingly, composite single-crystals were obtained only from the uncharged templates, whereas the negatively charged templates facilitated the formation of polycrystalline calcites,88 suggesting the significance of limited nucleation sites.

# 4 | INCORPORATION-INDUCED MULTIPLE FUNCTIONS

# 4.1 | Modifying the properties of single-crystalline hosts

Similar to the biogenic polymer/single-crystal composites, enhancement in the mechanical properties of synthetic single-crystals has been realized through the incorporation of polymer aggregates. As the incorporation of gel networks conferring improved mechanical properties and stability (e.g., against dehydration) on protein crystals,18,2766 it became feasible to handle and characterize the composite crystals at ambient conditions, such as recording full diffraction data sets. In addition to protein crystals, the mechanical reinforcement of calcite single-crystals with polymer incorporation14,29,3369 was studied initially with nano-indentation test (Figure 10A). In the case of polymer micelle incorporation,4 an increase of $1 6 \%$ in the hardness of the crystal hosts $( 3 . 0 \pm 0 . 1$ GPa) was found compared with pure crystals $( 2 . 6 \pm 0 . 1$ GPa), while the elastic moduli were lower $( 4 7 . 9 \pm 1 1 . 8 $ GPa for composite crystal and $8 9 . 9 \pm$ 12.4 GPa for pure calcite). After the nano-indentation test, dislocation of aligned slip planes appeared around the indent of a pure crystal, which, in contrast, did not happen in the micelle incorporated crystals (Figure 10a). According to the literatures, there were two mechanisms29,33,69 for illustrating the enhanced mechanical properties originated from the polymer incorporation.

Lattice strain of single-crystal hosts due to incorporation also affected the energy band structure, which played a key role in the electronic properties of semiconductors, as initially reported by Pokroy et al. in the case of incorporation of amino acids inside ${ \mathrm { Z n O } }$ crystals.89 Compared with optical bandgap $\mathrm { ( E _ { g } ) }$ of $3 . 2 8 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for pure ${ \mathrm { Z n O } }$ crystals, up to $0 . 1 3 \mathrm { e V }$ increase in the $\mathrm { E _ { g } }$ for amino acid incorporated $\mathrm { Z n O }$ were the direct evidences of such influences, and shifts in $\mathrm { E _ { g } }$ were relative to the interaction between crystal hosts and incorporated guests. # 4.2 Enhancing the incorporated guests

As particles were encapsulated into single-crystal hosts, properties of the guest particles can be influenced. CdTe QD's and two types of fluorescent polymer dots (Pdot's) entrapped by agarose gel networks were incorporated into calcite single-crystals, respectively (Figure 5F-M). All the incorporated NP's showed enhanced photostabilities and prolonged exciton lifetimes compared with NP's dispersed merely inside gels or solutions. Particularly, the photoluminescence (PL) intensity of incorporated CdTe QD's decreased slightly after continuous illumination of $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , while a more than $3 0 \%$ drop was shown in the PL intensity of solution/gel-dispersed CdTe QD's. The enhanced photostability and prolonged exciton lifetimes of incorporated QD's were attributed to the encapsulation by single-crystal hosts where discrete QD's were immobilized, avoiding self-quenching, and diffusing quenching agents, oxygen, were blocked owing to the lack of boundary or defect in single-crystals.22

For MOF's as crystal hosts, catalytic Pt NP's coated by polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) were occluded into MOF's and exhibited selective catalysis consequently, since only appropriately sized molecules would contact the incorporated Pt NP's by virtue of the molecular sieving of the MOF encapsulant.54 In addition, a pillared-layer MOF crystal, $\mathrm { [ N i ( H b t c ) ( b p y ) ] _ { n } }$ (btc $^ { } = 1 , 3 , 5$ -benzenetricarboxylate, bpy $=$ $^ { 4 , 4 ^ { \prime } }$ -bipyridyl), was used as a layered template with crosslinking copolymerization proceeded in the 2D nanointerstices of the MOF crystal, according to Uemura et al.93 Thus, 2D monosheets of PS were obtained through this spatial-confined strategy. More importantly, due to the lack of polymer chain entanglement, the PS monosheet showed unique thermal and mechanical properties such as a decrease in glass transition temperature $\mathrm { ( T _ { g } ) }$ and the absence of rubbery transition.

With the encapsulation of guest materials, the composites will be potentially powerful in controlled-release applications such as drug delivery if the single-crystal shell is sensitive to certain conditions. This assumption was primarily demonstrated by Ning et al. recently (Figure 12).35 As a typical model of hydrophobic drugs, dye-loaded oil nanoemulsions modified with amphiphilic copolymers were incorporated into calcite single-crystals and exhibited remarkable thermostability even after heated to $4 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ with the encapsulation by crystal hosts. Thus, long-term storage of environmentally sensitive contents was feasible. Then, such composite crystals were dissolved in a simulated gastric fluid $\mathrm { ( p H ~ 1 . 2 ) }$ with the incorporated guests released gradually. Since calcite start to be dissolved rapidly under highly acid conditions, the unexpected pre-release was avoided, enabling acidtriggered targeted delivery of the incorporated drugs.

# 4.3 |  Attempts in optoelectronic applications

For organic optoelectronic materials, "long-range ordering" and "hetero-structure' are two of the key structural factors. On one hand, in comparison of amorphous or polycrystalline states, "long-range ordering" always leads to improved properties of carrier transport (mobility),94,95 as well as exciton diffusion.96,97 A high mobility eventually results in a superior performance of single-crystalbased organic field-effect transistors (OFET's).98 On the other hand, "hetero-structure" guarantees a significant process, that is, charge separation or recombination, in photoelectric conversion materials. More importantly, it has been demonstrated that charge separation can be benefited by ordering.99,100 Thus, the combination of "long-range ordering"' and "hetero-structure" is of significance and has attracted a lot of scientific interests. In contrast to planar heterojunctions with long-range ordering,101,102 it is difficult to achieve long-range ordering in bulk-heterojunctions (BHJ's) with two interpenetrated phases that was commonly used in organic photoelectric conversion devices.103-105 Instead, due to the enormous nucleation events occurred inside the conventional donor-acceptor blends, only short-range ordered BHJ's have been fabricated (Figure 13A), which limits the further development of photoelectric conversion devices. Therefore, gel networks incorporation in a single-crystal was a promising strategy for further enhancing the optoelectronic properties of BHJ's by endowing BHJ's with long-range ordering (Figure 13B), which was demonstrated very recently.36 Gel networks of a typical polymer donor, poly[2-methoxy5-(2-ethylhexyloxy)-1,4-phenylenvinylene] (MEH-PPV), were incorporated in to fullerene (acceptor) singlecrystals (Figure 13C,D) and exhibited prominent fluorescence quenching, indicative of efficient charge-transfer at the D-A interface and the formation of long-range ordered BHJ. Furthermore, the PL lifetime of MEH-PPV in $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ single-crystals was much lower than that of the MEH-PPV blended with polycrystalline $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ suggesting a higher efficiency of charge-transfer given by the longrange ordered interface (Figure 13E). Finally, photoresistors based on the long-range ordered BHJs showed an over five times increase in photoresponsivity compared with the short-range ordered devices, revealing the great potential of such polymer/single-crystal composites in optoelectronic applications or as a platform to study the photophysical processes (Figure 13F).

Nevertheless, gently prepared single-crystals with polymer incorporation are always bulky with relatively large dimensions in every direction, which limits their applications toward novel optoelectronic devices with generally nanometer-sized thickness. Interestingly, in situ crystallization of 6,13-bis(triisopropylsilylethynyl)- pentacene (TIPS-pentacene), a typical high-mobility organic semiconductor, on a substrate in the presence of nanofibers would lead to the ribbon-like thin singlecrystals growing across the fibers with humbles fitting the shape of nanofibers (Figure 13G-K).15 Indeed, contact quality between fibers and crystals were demonstrated to be determined by the nucleation location (solution-air or solution-substrate interface).106 Thus, bulkcontact between two organic semiconductors was formed with fibers incorporated into single-crystal ribbons, and efficient charge-transfer between them was observed. More importantly, such TIPS-pentacene single-crystals retained a relatively high-hole mobility of over $1 ~ { \mathsf { c m } } ^ { 2 } ~ { \mathsf { V } } ^ { - 1 } ~ { \mathsf { s } } ^ { - 1 }$ by virtue of their unspoiled single-crystal nature. These results suggest a possible approach to the optoelectronic devices based on single-crystals with internal heterogeneous structure.

In addition to organic active materials, optoelectronic properties of organic-inorganic halide perovskites can benefit from the polymer incorporation as well. As Maheshwari et al. reported,107 methylammonium lead iodide $\left( \mathrm { { M A P b I } } _ { 3 } \right)$ perovskite single-crystals were grown from precursor solution containing PS solute. By virtue of the strong interaction between PS chains and perovskite crystals, the nucleation and growth processes were well controlled to obtain single-crystals with optimized size and quality, and, simultaneously, PS chains were incorporated into the perovskite single-crystals. As the insulating and hydrophobic PS chains were able to passivate the trap states,108 such PS-incorporated perovskite singlecrystals exhibited reduced ion migration, dark current, trap-state density, and carrier recombination, leading to improved carrier mobility, lifetime and diffusion length. Therefore, the stability and optoelectronic performance of the perovskite single-crystal was improved by PS incorporation. Moreover, chain length of the incorporated PS could play an important role in the enhancement of the optoelectronic properties.109

# 5 SUMMARY AND OUTLOOK

In summary, a wide range of polymer guests have been designed to be incorporated inside single-crystals, to generate the unique structure simultaneously possessing heterogeneous and long-range ordering features. The guest species can be either isolated or threedimensionally interpenetrated within the crystal host and even be encapsulated in a patterned manner. The mechanisms and the influence factor for the incorporation process have been concluded both chemically and physically. Thus, such synthetic single-crystals with polymer incorporation are suitable candidates for investigating the biomineralization course. Particularly, this kind of single-crystal composite enable huge potentials in the practical applications of various fields (Table 1), such as mechanical reinforcement, bandgap engineering, selfhealing, catalyst, controlled release, and optoelectronic devices. Nevertheless, the optimization and exploration of multiple functions are still imperative compared with the relatively well studied structures and mechanisms. Thus, guest materials, crystal hosts, and synergetic effects need to be further investigated.

Undeniably, the incorporation-induced functions highly relied on the content ratio and the size of the guest materials inside the crystal host in most cases. On one hand, it is necessary but challenging to further improve the content of incorporation without disrupting the single-crystalline nature. As a possible approach to improving the guest content, other strong interactions between polymer guests and crystal hosts that promote incorporation are worthy to be studied, such as Lewis acid-base interaction or even covalent bonding. On the other hand, according to the literatures that summarized in this article, isolated particles with diameters from several nanometers to about 500 nanometers and fibrous matrices with fiber diameters up to tens of nanometers were efficiently incorporated inside the single-crystal hosts. Since the lack of investigation of critical guest size for incorporation, the upper limit value of the guest content and size should be further clarified both experimentally and theoretically. On this basis, more applications of the polymer encapsulated single-crystals could be explored. For instance, the single-crystal with periodically patterned internal structure might be the adaptive model to fabricate 3D photonic crystal with a modulation of light (photon), which might be used in laser or quantum optics devices,11o although it is still challenging to accurately control the spatial distribution of guest materials inside a single-crystal in the scale of nanometers or several micrometers.

For crystal hosts, other than calcite that has been frequently studied, it is urgently important to seek more functional crystalline materials as single-crystal hosts. For example, with passivation of defects and limitation of ion migration, polymer-incorporated perovskite singlecrystals might be ideal active materials for field-effect transistors (FETs)11 and FET-based optoelectronic devices such as phototransistors112 and circuits.113 Furthermore, it is significant to precisely control the size and morphology of the host single-crystals, which is the other challenge of polymer incorporation. With the synthesis of the nanometer-sized single-crystal hosts that are sensitive to specific conditions (e.g., temperature, mechanical force, pH, or solvent) selectively, controlled release of guest contents can be achieved in many biomedicineinvolved situations, among which drug delivery could be extended even to targeted cancer therapy.114,115 In addition, gel network incorporation enables a bicontinuous structure where a single-crystalline host interfaces gel networks three-dimensionally. In combination with the superior optoelectronic properties of the long-range ordered semiconductors, gel incorporation is a possible strategy to fabricate high-performance BHJ-based organic optoelectronic devices, once the single-crystal composites are fabricated as thin-film structure with nano-sized thickness. More importantly, this strategy also provides a platform to further study the photophysical processes in BHJs with long-range ordering, such as exciton diffusion,116 charge delocalization,117 and subsequent free-charge generation,118 as well as charge recombination and consequent energetic loss,104 with little influence of structural or energetic disorder.

Synergetic effects between functional guest materials and host crystals will give abundant possibilities of functions. Except charge-transfer between donor and acceptor discussed above, selective catalysis given by the incorporation of active metal NP's into MOF's is a good example, which has been investigated a lot.119 However, most of the reported works focused on the enhancement of a certain component, and synergetic functions were discussed less frequently. Thus, massive efforts on synergetic effects are required to expand the scope of multiple functions of polymer-incorporated single-crystals.  Even so, the attempts on the application areas that have been extensively studied such as organic/perovskite optoelectronic materials and catalysis still reveal the great sustainability of this emerging bio-inspired single-crystalline research field.
