# $\pmb { \pi }$ -Extended chlorinated non-fullerene acceptors for achieving high performance organic solar cells†

Mingpeng Li,ad Zhixiang Li,ad Guangkun Song,ad Changzun Jiang,ad Xin Chen,ad Jian Wang,c Yang Yang,c Xiangjian Wan, ad Chenxi $\mathsf { L i } ^ { \mathsf { a d } }$ and Yongsheng Chen $\textcircled{1}$ \*abd

The photovoltaic properties and energy loss of organic solar cells (OSCs) based on non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs) are highly dependent on their molecular structures and morphologies. Herein, three NFAs, named $\mathsf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -2Cl, $\mathsf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -NCl and $\mathsf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -N2Cl with different end groups, are designed and synthesized by adopting $\pi$ -extension and chlorination modifications. As a result, a favorable morphology is achieved in a ${ \sf P M } 6 : \sfGamma ^ { \prime }$ -N2Cl based OSC and this system exhibits the highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of $1 4 . 1 3 \%$ with a $\mathcal { J } _ { \mathsf { s c } }$ of $2 0 . 3 1 \mathsf { m A } \mathsf { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of 0.927 V and a FF of $7 4 . 6 8 \%$ . Extending the conjugation of the end group narrows the molecular bandgaps and enables a lower $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of 0.686 and $0 . 7 0 6 \ \mathrm { e V }$ with nonradiative recombination losses of as low as 0.207 and $0 . 2 0 3 \ : \mathrm { e V }$ in $\mathsf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -NCl and $\mathsf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl based devices, respectively. This work provides an efficient strategy to optimize the molecular structures of nonfullerene acceptors and further improve the properties of OSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Owing to their merits of solution-processed large-area production and flexible and semi-transparent applications, organic solar cells (OSCs) have drawn considerable attention as one of the sustainable solar energy-harvesting technologies.1–7 In the past few decades, tremendous progress of OSCs should be mainly attributed to the invention of novel light-harvesting materials. Among them, the recently developed non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs) with an acceptor–donor–acceptor (A–D–A) architecture have attracted extensive attention due to their distinctive advantages of superior charge separation and transport features originating from their unique frontier electron density distributions.8 In particular in 2019, a Y-series acceptor, e.g. Y6, 9 emerged as a new-generation

NFA, and following this, further chemical modifications to its central cores,10–15 side chains16–19 and end groups20–27 have boosted the PCEs of state-of-the-art OSCs towards $2 0 \%$ .28–33 Fortunately, in view of the vast diversity of chemical structures of organic materials, there is no doubt that more molecules affording performances similar to or even better than those of Y-series NFAs might be designed and efficiently synthesized through straightforward synthetic routes. Hence, further innovative exploration for light-harvesting materials combining both efficient synthesis and high PCEs is not only indispensable for large-scale production of OSCs but also highly important in view of understanding the property–structure relationship.

Generally, subtle changes in the chemical structures of NFAs should induce huge variations in their photoelectric properties and device performance.34–41 However, for these modifications, end group engineering is an efficient and convenient method for obtaining varieties of receptors. And it is one of the key techniques for tuning energy levels and molecular aggregations. Now great efforts are focused on the substitution of halogen atoms on the end groups and asymmetric end groups. Chlorine substitution is a very successful strategy for end group control and could red-shift the optical band gap $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } } )$ and downshift energy levels. The $\pi$ -extension of the end group can be used in tandem to promote face-to-face packing and increase interplanar $\pi - \pi$ electronic couplings and photovoltaic efficiency.42 For instance, the comparison of IC and NINCN was previously reported by the Chen group43 and the Hou group,44 and they found that enlarging the conjugated area of the end group can enhance the molecular interactions and electron mobility. Lately, the Wei group combined the side-chain optimization and end-acceptor $\pi$ -extension with fluorination to investigate NFAs with BTP as the central core and found that the PM6:BTP-C9-N4F-based device exhibited the highest PCE of $1 7 . 0 \%$ due to the increased $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ .42 Similarly, the Tobin Marks group showed that end group combined $\pi$ -extension and fluorination is effective in contracting $\pi - \pi$ stacking distances, promoting close cofacial packing and increasing BHJ crystallinity via enhanced end group and other intermolecular interactions.38 Recently, the Huang group combined the strategies of noncovalent conformational locks and the $\pi$ -extended end group to precisely tune the electronic properties, charge transport, film morphology, and energy loss $\left( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } \right)$ by finetuning the degree of multi-fluorination.45 As a result, a record PCE of $1 4 . 5 3 \%$ in labs and a certified PCE of $1 3 . 8 \%$ for NFREAbased devices were obtained.

Fluorene as a planar and rigid skeleton is widely available and has been applied to the fused ring NFAs by our group to construct a FDICT central core.37 Note that it can be efficiently synthesized and easily scaled up to 10 grams in a laboratory after synthetic optimization. Now, by combining the FDICT central core with different electron-withdrawing end groups, a series of NFAs, e.g., F-2F, F-Br and FDNCTF, have been developed.43,46–48 A systematic investigation of OSCs based on the above F-series NFAs demonstrates rather favorable characteristics of both high $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and fill factors (FF), leading to very encouraging PCEs of $1 3 . 8 \%$ and $1 7 . 3 \%$ for single junction and tandem devices, respectively.49,50 However, we find that the relatively small $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } s$ caused by their insufficient light absorptions are the major limitation to further improving their performance. Therefore, further enhancing the near-infrared (NIR) absorption of structurally simple F-series NFAs by chemical modifications has been regarded as one important approach to achieving state-of-the-art OSCs and beyond for potential commercial application in the future.

In this work, by adopting combined $\pi$ -extension and chlorination modification a system of NFAs with the FDICT as the central core was synthesized and investigated. Three A–D–Atype NFAs, denoted as $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -2Cl, $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { \mathbf { - } } N C l }$ and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl (shown in Fig. 1a), were designed and synthesized. It is worth noting that three acceptors can be easily achieved in a few steps with high yields from commercial raw materials, demonstrating their great potential to meet the easy synthesis requirements for large-scale production. Furthermore, in view of the lightharvesting ability, the onset absorption of $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl redshifts significantly towards the NIR region with respect to that of other F-series NFAs mentioned above. As expected, when blending $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl with a well-known PM6 donor as the active layer of OSCs, a good PCE of $1 4 . 1 3 \%$ can be achieved with a markedly increased $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 0 . 3 1 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ compared to those of OSCs based on $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -2Cl and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -NCl (19.43 and $1 8 . 0 3 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ). Note that the $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -N2Cl system is one of the most efficient material systems for high performance OSCs based on the FDICT central core, in addition to its advantages in terms of simple synthesis and lower $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ .

# 2. Results and discussion

# 2.1 Materials and thermal stability

The synthetic routes to end group N2Cl and acceptors $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { \boldsymbol { \mathbf { - } } } 2 \mathbf { C l } , \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ - NCl and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { - } } \mathbf { N } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ are depicted in Scheme S1 $\bigl ( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \bigr )$ , and are similar to those of our previous reports.43,50 The involved reactions are all classical reactions with relatively mild conditions, in favor of large-scale production. The detailed synthesis procedures are described in the $\mathbf { E S I \dagger }$ and the chemical structures of the key intermediates and targeted NFAs have been fully characterized and confirmed by their $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR, $^ { 1 3 } \mathrm { C }$ NMR and HRMS analyses (Fig. S8–S18, ESI†). All NFAs exhibit good solubilities over $1 0 \ \mathrm { m g \ m L ^ { - 1 } }$ in common organic solvents, such as chloroform, chlorobenzene and toluene, which could be beneficial for solution-processing device fabrication. In addition, based on the acquired thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) curves shown in Fig. S1 $\bigl ( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \bigr )$ , all NFAs of $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { \boldsymbol { - } } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { I }$ , $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { - } N C l }$ and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl possess high thermal stability with thermal decomposition onset temperatures $5 \%$ mass loss) greater than $3 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ .

# 2.2 Light absorption and energy levels

The normalized ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis) absorption spectra of NFAs and PM6 in both chloroform solutions and thin films are shown in Fig. S2 $\left( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \right)$ and Fig. 1b, and the detailed parameters are summarized in Table 1. As expected, $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl displays the maximum absorption peak $\left( \lambda _ { \mathrm { m a x } } ^ { \mathrm { s o l } } \right)$ of $7 1 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ in chloroform solution, red-shifting by $2 1 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ and $7 \mathrm { n m }$ comparing to that of $6 8 9 \mathrm { n m }$ for $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { - } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ and $7 0 3 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ for $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { - } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ , demonstrating that the introduction of the $\pi$ -extended chlorinated end group could work as an effective strategy to construct NIR NFAs. From solutions to solid films, red-shifted absorption could be observed for all NFAs with maximum absorption peaks $\left( \lambda _ { \mathrm { m a x } } ^ { \mathrm { f i l m } } \right)$ at $7 3 8 \mathrm { n m }$ for $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { - } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ , $7 3 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ for $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } - N C l }$ and $7 4 5 \ \mathrm { n m }$ for $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl. Note that a $\sim 3 5 \ \mathrm { n m }$ red-shift has been observed for $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl from solution to a solid film, much larger than that of $\sim 2 8 ~ \mathrm { \ n m }$ for $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { - } N C l }$ , suggesting a stronger intermolecular action for $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl. 51 As a consequence, $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -2Cl, $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { - } N C l }$ and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl films show onset absorption peaks $\left( \lambda _ { \mathrm { o n s e t } } ^ { \mathrm { f i l m } } \right)$ at 788, 791, and $7 9 9 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , corresponding to optical bandgaps $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } } )$ of 1.574, 1.568, and $1 . 5 5 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ , respectively.

Note that the $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl film displays a highly strong absorption in a broad wavelength range of 500 to $8 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , which is complementary to that of state-of-the-art polymeric donors PM6, rendering $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl blends promising for efficient OSCs with relatively larger $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ than those of another two blends.

The electrochemical behaviors of $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -2Cl, $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { \cdot } N C l }$ and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl have also been investigated by cyclic voltammetry (CV) in Fig. S3 $\left( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \right)$ . The derived energy levels of the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) are presented in Fig. 1c and Table 1. The HOMO and LUMO energy levels of $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { - } N C l }$ locate at $- 5 . 7 5$ and $- 3 . 8 9 \ \mathrm { e V }$ , respectively. Note that downshifted HOMO of $- 5 . 7 6 \ \mathrm { ~ e V }$ and LUMO of $- 3 . 9 5 \ \mathrm { e V }$ can be afforded by $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl, giving rise to a much narrower bandgap of $1 . 8 1 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ in comparison to that of $2 . 0 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } – 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ . The largely decreased bandgap of $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl derived from CV is caused by its longer conjugation backbone after $\pi$ -extension and the chlorination of end group in good accordance with the bandgap obtained from its film absorption edge. The energy levels of $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { \boldsymbol { - } } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { I }$ , $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { - } N C l }$ and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl could well match with polymer donor PM6, affording sufficient driving forces for exciton dissociation.

# 2.4 Charge dynamics

To clarify the differences in EQEs and FFs of the three blends based OSCs, the exciton dissociation properties were first investigated by measuring the relationship of the photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ and effective voltage $\left( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } \right)$ . The exciton dissociation probability $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } ( E , T )$ could be indicated by the ratio of $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ to $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } ,$ where $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ is the saturated $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ at a sufficiently high $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } ,$ and all the photogenerated excitons could be dissociated into free carriers and completely collected by the electrodes.52 As shown in Fig. 2d, the values of $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } ( E , \ T )$ for PM6:F0 -2Cl, $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -NCl and $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl based OSCs are $9 7 . 0 \%$ , $9 6 . 5 \%$ and $9 7 . 4 \%$ , respectively, indicating more efficient and improved exciton dissociation for $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { \boldsymbol { - } } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { - } } \mathbf { N } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ based devices with respect to that of the $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { \cdot } N C l }$ based one.

In addition, the light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } ,$ which varies from 10 to $1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m W \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ) dependence of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ was also measured in order to investigate the charge recombination kinetics in OSCs, and the corresponding characteristics are plotted in Fig. 2e and f. Among these, the relationship between $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ should follow the equation: $\begin{array}{c} \begin{array} { r l } { J _ { \mathrm { s c } } } & { { } \propto } \end{array} P ^ { \alpha }  \end{array}$ , where $\mathscr { X }$ is defined as an exponential factor and could indicate the weak bimolecular recombination if the $\mathscr { X }$ value is close to unity.53 As illustrated in Fig. 2e, the $\mathscr { X }$ values of the three devices based on $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -2Cl, $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -NCl and $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } - N 2 C l }$ are estimated to be 0.990, 0.980 and 0.982, respectively, indicating that the bimolecular recombination is significantly suppressed in three systems. Additionally, the slope $\left( n k T / q \right)$ of $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ versus the natural logarithm of $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ is usually regarded as the indicator to reflect the charge recombination properties in OSCs under open-circuit conditions.54 As depicted in Fig. 2f, $\mathbf { P M 6 : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { \cdot } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ , $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -NCl and $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -N2Cl based devices exhibit the slopes of 1.28, 1.22 and $1 . 2 3 k T / q$ , respectively. The smaller slopes of $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl based devices suggest a lower amount of trap-assisted recombination losses, which should account for the slightly larger $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ with respect to that of $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -2Cl based devices.

Transient photocurrent (TPC) and transient photovoltage (TPV) measurements were also applied to monitor the charge extraction behaviors and charge carrier lifetimes, respectively. As it can be observed from Fig. S4a $\left( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \right)$ , the charge extraction time of $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -2Cl, PM6:F0 -NCl and $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl based devices is 0.18, 5.77 and $1 . 4 7 ~ \mu s$ , respectively, indicating that three systems show different charge transport properties with $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -2Cl and $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -N2Cl based devices showing even better properties. For TPV tests, a rapid voltage decay usually suggests a large charge recombination in OSCs.55 As shown in Fig. S4b $\bigl ( \mathrm { E S I \dag } \bigr )$ , the lifetime of charge carriers in the $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -NCl based device is calculated to be $0 . 5 4 ~ \mathrm { { m s } }$ , whereas much prolonged lifetimes of 1.57 and $2 . 1 5 \ \mathrm { m s }$ are observed in $\mathbf { P M 6 : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { \cdot } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ and $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } .$ - N2Cl based devices, respectively. It indicates that the carrier recombination is greatly inhibited in $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { \boldsymbol { - } } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { I }$ and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl based devices especially for that of $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } - N 2 C l }$ , which is in agreement with the suppressed trap-assisted recombination observed in Fig. 2f. Moreover, charge transport properties of the blended films were further investigated by the method of space-charge-limited-current (SCLC). The detailed plots and parameters are shown in Fig. S5 and Table S1 (ESI†). PM6:F0 - 2Cl and $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } - N 2 C l }$ photoactive layers exhibit overall ungraded hole/electron mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } \right)$ of $1 . 6 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } / 3 . 4 5 \times$ ${ 1 0 } ^ {  { - } 4 }$ and $\boldsymbol { 1 . 0 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } / 3 . 3 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V ^ { - 1 } \ { s } ^ { - 1 } } } }$ , respectively, better than that of $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -NCl based blends $( 0 . 9 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } / 2 . 9 8 \times$ $1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ . The enlarged hole and electron mobility of $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { \boldsymbol { - } } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { I }$ and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { - } } \mathbf { N } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ based devices should contribute to their improved $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF. Note that the $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -N2Cl blended films afford a higher and more balanced electron/hole mobility ratio than that of $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -NCl blended films, which should be responsible for the simultaneously enhanced $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF of $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl based OSCs.

To sum up, $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl based devices possess a slightly improved exciton dissociation efficiency, better charge carrier transport properties and a lower degree of charge recombination with respect to those of $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { \boldsymbol { - } } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { I }$ and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { - } N C l }$ based devices. We also found that a suppressed charge recombination and more balanced hole/electron mobility can be achieved, resulting in further improved $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF for $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -N2Cl based OSCs with respect to those of PM6:F0 -2Cl and $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -NCl based ones.

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) technologies were employed to explore the morphology and stacking structure of these four materials in the blend film and further analyze the differences in their morphologies. As observed from the AFM images in Fig. $_ { 3 \mathrm { a - c } }$ , these blended films all exhibited nanoscale phase separation. And the root-mean-square (RMS) roughness values of $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } { - } } 2 \mathbf { C l } .$ , $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } - N C l }$ and $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl blend films can be determined as 1.65, 1.59 and $1 . 4 4 \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. The blend films with great pconjugation end groups show clear domain sizes among these three materials. Note that PM6:F0 -N2Cl blend films exhibit the most uniform and smooth upper surface topographies than that of the other two films, which could ensure the intimate contact with the charge transport layers and thus the efficient charge transfer at the interface.56 Fig. 3d–f displayed the TEM images of these three blend films, demonstrating clear fiber structures and nanoscale phase separation in three blends, especially for a more dense film of $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl. In fact, the strategy of greater conjugation end groups should break the high crystallinity and avoid the formation of excessively large molecules, which is conducive to form better phase separation. This conclusion is further demonstrated in the GIWAX data. This finding explains the difference in charge recombination and thus the change in $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF parameters.42

# 2.6 $\pmb { E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } }$ analysis

To disclose the inner relationship of structure–energy loss, we analysed $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of each OSCs based on the detailed balance theory.59 According to the Shockley–Queisser (SQ) limit, $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ in OSCs contains three parts, as shown in the following formula:21

$$
\begin{array} { r l } { E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } } & { = ( E _ { \mathrm { g } } - q V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ^ { \mathrm { S Q } } ) + ( q V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ^ { \mathrm { S Q } } - q V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ^ { \mathrm { r a d } } ) + ( q V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ^ { \mathrm { r a d } } - q V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ) } \\ & { = \Delta E _ { \mathrm { l } } + \Delta E _ { \mathrm { 2 } } + \Delta E _ { \mathrm { 3 } } } \end{array}
$$

Among them, $E _ { \mathrm { g } } s$ of OSCs could be estimated from the intersection of the optical absorption and EL curves of the corresponding blends,60 being 1.642 eV for PM6:F0 -2Cl, $1 . 6 5 5 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -NCl and $1 . 6 3 3 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -N2Cl blends (Fig. S7a–c, $\mathrm { E S I \dag }$ ). Thus, the total $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } s$ of OSCs can be calculated based on the following equation: $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } = \big ( E _ { \mathrm { g } } - q V _ { \mathrm { o c } } \big ) ,$ being 0.725, 0.686 and 0.706 eV for $\mathbf { P M 6 : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { \boldsymbol { - 2 } } \mathbf { C l }$ , $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } - N C l }$ and $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl, respectively, demonstrating a great reduction of $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ after adopting $\pi$ -extension backbone in the $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl acceptor, as shown in Table 4.

In order to gain a deeper insight into the origin of $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ in OSCs studied here, all the three parts of $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } s$ have been evaluated. Non-radiative recombination loss $( \Delta E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } ^ { \mathrm { n o n - r a d } } )$ and energetic disorder, which are closely related to the tail states below the band edge and the over-all photon energy loss, need to be minimized to improve device performance.60 In the range of low photon energy, the optical absorption coefficient $\left( \alpha ( E ) \right)$ of disordered organic semiconductors follows the Urbach rule as described by the following formula:61

$$
\alpha ( E ) = \alpha _ { 0 } \mathrm { e } ^ { [ ( E - E _ { 0 } ) / E \mathrm { u } ] }
$$

where the two constants of $\alpha _ { 0 }$ represents the optical absorption coefficient at the band edge, $E$ is the photon energy, $E _ { \mathrm { U } }$ represents the Urbach energy which is usually described as a measure of energetic disorder.62 Generally, a smaller $E _ { \mathrm { U } }$ indicates a smaller degree of energy disorder, which should be in favor of the reduction of recombination losses in OSCs.63 As shown in Fig. 5b, according to the measured high-resolution Fourier transform photocurrent spectra and EQE spectra (FTPS-EQE), $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl and $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -NCl based devices afford slightly smaller $E _ { \mathrm { U } } \mathbf { s }$ of 30.3 and $3 1 . 2 \mathrm { \ m e V }$ , respectively, compared to $3 2 . 4 ~ \mathrm { m e V }$ of the $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } } .$ -2Cl based one. Note that the slightly reduced $E _ { \mathrm { U } }$ of $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } - N 2 C l }$ in comparison with that of $\mathbf { P M } \mathbf { 6 } \mathbf { : } \mathbf { F } ^ { \prime }$ -NCl was also confirmed by its sharper absorption onset (Fig. 1b) and slightly enlarged CCL (Table 3).13 The more ordered and compact molecular stacking of $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl should significantly reduce energetic disorder and thus mitigate the recombination loss of OSCs.63–65

# 3. Conclusion

A series of NFAs with $\mathbf { F D I C _ { 1 2 } T }$ as the central core such as $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { - } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { \mathbf { - } } N C l }$ and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl was reported. Because of the combined $\pi$ -extension and chlorination, $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { - } } \mathbf { N } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ shows a red-shifted absorption close to $8 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ and tighter molecular packing. And compared to $\mathbf { F } ^ { \prime } { \boldsymbol { - } } 2 \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ and $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } { - } N C l }$ , the $\pi$ -extension and chlorination of acceptor $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl lead to an upshifted HOMO energy level and an enhanced crystallization tendency. The $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } }$ -N2Cl based device exhibited the highest PCE of $1 4 . 1 3 \%$ due to the increased $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ . The system of $\mathbf { P M 6 : F ^ { \prime } - N 2 C l }$ showed excellent absorption and minimal recombination loss. The $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ trend in these devices is consistent with their $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ . When the end group was extended and chlorinated, $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ can be reduced to $0 . 6 8 6 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ and $0 . 7 0 6 \ \mathrm { e V }$ , smaller than that of the $\mathbf { F ^ { \prime } }$ -2Cl based device. The results confirmed that the combination of extending the end group and chlorination effectively improves the morphology of the active layer, weakens the self-aggregation of the polymer donor, and enhances the performance of the device. The efficient strategy of integrated regulation to design highly efficient NFAs optimizes their crystallization in blend films with a low nonradiative recombination loss. This work will stimulate further innovative explorations for active materials with both high efficiency and simple synthesis, aiming to boost OSCs towards commercial application in the near future.
