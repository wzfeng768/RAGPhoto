# A Universal Fluorinated Polymer Acceptor Enables All-Polymer Solar Cells with >15% Efficiency

Feng Peng, Kang An, Wenkai Zhong, Zhenye Li, Lei Ying,\* Ning Li, Zhenqiang Huang, Chunguang Zhu, Baobing Fan, Fei Huang, and Yong Cao

ABSTRACT: While great progress has been achieved in all-polymer solar cells (allPSCs), the efficiency of all-PSCs is primarily limited by polymer acceptors that lack a high extinction coefficient, high electron mobility, and good compatibility with polymer donors. Here we designed and developed a polymer acceptor PFA1 based on a nonfullerene acceptor framework with a fluorine substituent on the 1,1-dicyanomethylene3-indanone unit. In combination with an electron-donating polymer, PTzBI-oF, the blend film presents an extended and intensified absorption profile, enhanced electron mobility, and favorable film morphology. The optimized all-PSCs exhibit a remarkably high efficiency of $1 5 . 1 1 \%$ ,which is, to the best of our knowledge, the highest performance yet reported for an all-PSC. Of particular importance is the applicability of PFA1 as a universal polymer acceptor with a range of polymer donors to achieve mpressively high eficiencies. These properties enable a new molecular design strategy for the construction of polymer acceptors toward applications in high-performance all-PSCs.

ll-polymer solar cells (all-PSCs) exhibit unique A advantages of superior stability and mechanical flexibility and have great potential for practical applications as large-area and flexible devices.-5 However, the overall performance of all-PSCs has lagged behind those of their counterparts based on recently emerged non-fullerene small-molecule acceptors, mainly due to a lack of highperformance polymeric acceptors.6,7 To date, most reported ntype conjugated polymers are constructed with building blocks such as perylene diimide,8-10" naphthalene diimide,11-14 boron-nitrogen coordination units,15-17 etc. All-PsCs based on these n-type copolymers exhibited unsatisfactory power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of ${ < } 1 2 \%$ because their low absorption coefficients limit the use of solar photons in the low-energy region.18,19 To boost the performance of all-PSCs, it is imperative to develop novel polymer acceptors featuring a high extinction coefficient and broad absorption, to enable efficient light harvesting while maintaining high electron mobility and compatibility with various polymer donors.

Recently, considerable effort has been devoted to developing n-type polymers by incorporating a $\pi$ spacer to connect smallmolecule non-fullerene acceptors.20-241 The n-type polymers possess the advantages of small-molecule acceptors, such as strong absorption capability in the low-energy band and good miscibility with electron-donating polymers. For instance, the copolymerization of a non-fullerene acceptor (a Y6 derivative)

by using a thiophene unit or distannylated bithiophene imide as a $\pi \cdot$ spacer afforded impressive PCEs of ${ > } 1 4 \%$ when blended with the electron-donating polymer PBDB-T derivatives.26-28 It has been established that the incorporation of fluorine (F) atoms into a polymer backbone can tune the optoelectronic properties and enhance the inter- and intramolecular interactions of conjugated polymers.29,30 Fluorine is the most electronegative element and has a small van der Waals atomic radius of $1 4 7 { \mathrm { ~ p m } }$ which is comparable to that of the hydrogen atom $\left( 1 2 0 \ \mathrm { p m } \right)$ 31 suggesting that fluorination can be used to fine-tune the energy level of photovoltaic materials without increasing steric hindrance.3233

In this study, we designed a novel n-type copolymer, PFA1, based on the fluorinated 1,1-dicyanomethylene-3-indanone unit and a thiophene spacer unit (Scheme 1). Compared to its nonfluorinated counterpart (PY5T, molecular structure shown in Figure S1), PFAl exhibited a relatively narrow optical bandgap, high absorption coefficient, and enhanced electron mobility. In addition, we observed a much improved miscibility between PFA1 and a polymer donor PTzBI-oF (Figure S1), which contains a [1,2,3]triazolo $\left[ 4 , 5 - f \right]$ isoindole- $5 , 7 ( 2 H , 6 H )$ dione (TzBI) unit,34 leading to optimized microstructure morphology and an impressively high PCE of $1 5 . 1 1 \%$ . To the best our knowledge, this is the highest PCE value thus far reported for an all-PSC. Of particular importance is that PFA1 can be used as a general n-type polymer for matching with various polymer donors, such as PTzBI-Si, PM6, and PBDB-T, suggesting new opportunities for versatile applications of allPSCs.

As shown in Scheme 1, 4-bromo-5-fluorophthalic acid (1) was synthesized by oxidation of 1-bromo-2-fluoro-4,5- dimethylbenzene with potassium permanganate and then treated with acetic anhydride to obtain a high yield $( 9 2 \% )$ of 5-bromo-6-fluoroisobenzofuran-1,3-dione (2). Compound 2 was treated with tert-butyl acetoacetate in the presence of trimethylamine and acetic anhydride to afford the methylenated compound 5-bromo-6-fluoro-1H-indene-1,3(2H)-dione (3) in high yield $( 8 6 \% )$ .The following Knoevenagel condensation of 3 with malononitrile gave the structural isomers 2-(6-bromo-5-fluoro-3-oxo-2,3-dihydro- $1 H$ -inden-1- ylidene)malononitrile (4a) and 2-(5-bromo-6-fluoro-3-oxo2,3-dihydro-1H-inden-1-ylidene)malononitrile (4b). Proton nuclear magnetic resonance $\mathrm { \Omega ^ { \prime 1 } H \ N M R } )$ spectroscopy revealed that the 4a:4b molar ratio was approximately 0.9:1.0 (Figure S13), but these isomers had nearly identical polarity and were thus chromatographically inseparable. Thus, the mixture of 4a and 4b was reacted with dialdehyde 5 to efficiently yield dibromide monomer Y5-F-Br. Finally, a Stille polycondensation of Y5-F-Br and 2,5-bis(trimethylstannyl)thiophene afforded copolymer PFA1 in excellent yield $\left( { > } 9 0 \% \right)$ . The number-average molecular weight $\left( M _ { \mathrm { n } } \right)$ and dispersity of PFA1 are $1 3 . 3 5 ~ \mathrm { k D a }$ and 2.97, respectively, as measured via hightemperature gel permeation chromatography at $1 5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ using 1,2,4-trichlorobenzene as the eluent and polystyrene as the reference. From this molecular weight, the repeating unit of the resulting copolymer could be roughly estimated to be seven. Nevertheless, the resulting PFAl was readily soluble in chloroform and chlorobenzene at room temperature.

The monomer Y5-F-Br has isomeric forms, and thus, several PFA1 backbone conformations are possible. To identify the conformational torsion that is created by the $\mathrm { F }$ atom, we performed potential energy surface scanning of model compounds IC-H-T and IC-F-T (Figure 1a and Figure S2) as the representatives of PY5T and PFA1, respectively. The 3- methylthiophene unit was used to replace the central fused moiety, to simplify the simulation. IC-F-T exhibited a torsional potential barrier slightly higher than that of IC-H-T, which may be attributable to the existence of a noncovalent bonding interaction between the F atom and the S atom in the thiophene unit.3536 Such strong intrachain co-planarity and rigidity enhance the polymer oscillator strength and charge transport.

The accuracy of the obtained $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of the small area devices was confirmed by their external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra (Figure 2b), which showed that the PFA1-based device had overall EQE values significantly higher than those of the PY5T-based device. The enhanced $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of the PFA1-based device was attributable to the increased charge carrier mobilities (Table 1 and Figure S7) and the extended absorption profle of the blend flm (Figure 1c). Moreover, the PTzBI-oF/PFA1 blend film exhibited more pronounced quenching of photoluminescence than did the PTzBI-oF/ PY5T blend film (Figure S8), thus indicating that more efficient electron transfer occurred in the former film.39 From the $J { - } V$ characteristics under dark conditions (Figure S9a), a series resistance $\left( R _ { \mathrm { S } } \right)$ of $0 . 9 0 \Omega \mathrm { ~ c m } ^ { 2 }$ and a shunt resistance $( R _ { \mathrm { S H } } )$ of $2 . 6 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \Omega \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ were obtained for the PFA1-based device (Table S6).

The atomic force microscopy topographical image of the PTzBI-oF/PY5T blend film (Figure 3a) revealed a coarse surface with large-scale aggregations, whereas the $\mathrm { P T z B I - o F } /$ PFA1 blend film had a uniform and relatively smooth surface with a much smaller root-mean-square value of $1 . 9 \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure 3c). Two-dimensional grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIwAxs) was used to study the molecular crystallites in pristine and bulk-heterojunction films. The asprepared PY5T and PFA1 pristine flms displayed similar scattering features that can be assumed as a "face-on" orientation, as evidenced by the intense $\pi - \pi$ stacking peaks $\left( q = 1 . 6 4 ~ \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 } \right)$ in the out-of-plane (OOp) direction (Figure S11). Close inspection into the low- $q$ region indicated lamellar stacking, which gave an elliptical signal $\left( q = 0 . 3 6 \mathrm { \AA } ^ { - 1 } \right)$ and showed that there were dual-peak characteristics in the inplane (IP) direction. The PFAl flm also had a lower polymorph fraction and thus denser lamellar stacking than the PY5T film.

Both blend flms showed similar face-on" textures and $q$ positions, whereas the PTzBI-oF/PFA1 blend showed a sharper lamellar stacking peak in the in-plane direction, resulting in a crystalline coherence length of $8 2 \mathrm { ~ \AA } ,$ which was greater than the value of $6 5 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ for the PTzBI-oF/PY5T blend film (Figure S11 and Table S8). We thus concluded that the fluorine in PFA1 would bring better-developed lamellar stacking and phase separation and enhanced its miscibility with the electron-donating polymer PTzBI-oF, which increased the level of charge transport and exciton dissociation and thus dramatically increased the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF of the resulting device.

To probe the universality of the use of the novel fluorinated polymer acceptor PFA1 for the construction of all-PSCs, we fabricated devices by integrating PFA1 with each of three extensively used polymer donors: PTzBI-Si, PM6, and PBDBT (the molecular structures are shown in Figure S1).33,42,43 Figure 4 shows the $J { - } V$ and EQE spectra of these devices, and Table S9 summarizes the detailed photovoltaic parameters. It is interesting to note that these devices showed impressively high PCEs of $1 1 . 9 4 \%$ $1 3 . 7 5 \%$ and $1 4 . 2 0 \%$ for PTzBI-Si-based cells, PM6-based cells, and PBDB-T-based cells, respectively. Of particular importance is the fact that these devices presented relatively high EQE values $\left( { > } 6 0 \% \right)$ in a wide range $\left( 4 5 0 - 8 4 0 \ \mathrm { \ n m } \right)$ , resulting in obvious enhancement of lightharvesting capability, and thus a $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ greater than those of their counterpart cells based on the n-type polymer N2200 as the acceptor.7,42 From the AFM and TEM images of the PFA1 blended with polymer donors of PTzBI-Si, PM6, and PBDB-T (Figure S12), one can clearly observe a relatively smooth and uniform film, indicating that PFA1 presented good miscibility with all three copolymers. We also noted that when PBDB-T was the polymer donor the devices based on PFA1 exhibited a PCE slightly lower than that of $1 4 . 4 \%$ achieved by PY5T (also denoted as PJ1-H), which can be attributed to the relatively low molecular weight of the PFA1 sample, as the photovoltaic performance of the PY5T-based device highly relied on the molecular weight.27 These observations illustrate the great promise of PFA1 as a general electron-accepting polymer for the construction of all-PSCs with high photovoltaic performance.

In summary, a novel high-performance polymer acceptor PFA1 was developed by incorporation of fluorine substitute. PFA1 exhibited an extended absorption profile associated with an extinction coefficient that is more intense than that of its nonfluorinated analogue. The optimized all-PSCs based on PTzBI-oF/PFA1 achieved a high PCE of ${ > } 1 5 \%$ which is the highest value thus far reported for all-psCs. Detailed investigations demonstrated that PFAl exhibited better miscibility with PTzBI-oF than that of its counterpart polymer acceptor PY5T, thus leading to more efficient charge separation and transportation in the blend flm. More interestingly, PFA1 was compatible with a range of polymer donors, affording all-PSCs with impressively high PCEs. These results demonstrated the promise of PFA1 as a universal polymer acceptor for the development of high-performance allPSCs.
