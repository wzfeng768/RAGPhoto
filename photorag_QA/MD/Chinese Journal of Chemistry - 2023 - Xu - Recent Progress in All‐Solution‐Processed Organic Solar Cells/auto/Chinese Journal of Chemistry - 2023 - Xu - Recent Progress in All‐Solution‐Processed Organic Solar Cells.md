# 1. Introduction

Organic solar cells (Oscs) have attracted extensive attention from academia and industry due to their unique advantages such as lightweight, solution processability, and mechanical flexibility.1-3] With the rapid development of active layer materials and device engineering, Oscs have achieved power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) exceeding $1 9 \%$ [411] However, despite these advances, indium tin oxide (ito) bottom electrodes and vacuum-deposited metal top electrodes remain the common electrode combination for high-efficiency devices, which do not give full play to the inherent advantages of Oscs. Notably, Oscs fabricated via a fullsolution process can enhance production efficiency and reduce costs, meeting the needs for commercial-scale and highthroughput manufacturing.1-13] However, the PCEs of all-solutionprocessed devices are relatively low, with the highest PcE of $1 5 . 2 6 \%$ [14] Iagging behind the devices based on ITO bottom electrodes and vacuum-deposited metal top electrodes. Therefore, more attention should be devoted to realizing high-efficiency all-solution-processed OsCs.

The device architecture of all-solution-processed Oscs is depicted in Figure 1a, wherein each functional layer is fabricated by solution processing. Figure 1b illustrates the commonly employed processing technologies, such as knife coating, slot-die coating, spray coating, and inkjet printing.15] For all-solution-processed OSCs, a high-quality solution-processed electrode that can replace ITO electrode and vacuum-deposited metal electrode is a critical consideration, and this paper focuses on this aspect.1618) The widely used materials for solution-processed electrodes mainly include siver nanowires (Agnws) -20 conducting polymers,[212) and composite conducting materials.2-24) However, there are till! several limitations for the solution-processed electrodes, leading to device performance degradation. For instance, AgNws electrodes with high roughness easily penetrate transport and active layers, resulting in reduced device efficiencies or even short circuits.252) Besides, the weak conductivity and acidity of conducting polymers have been considered to be responsible for the low efficienc   tay of the diel Ths improving the quality of solution-processed electrodes and addressing their imperfections are urgent problems to be solved. In addition, high-performance active layer and interface layer materials are indispensable components for allsolution-processed oscs.29-31] High-performance donor and acceptor materials, fine-tuning the crystallinity and vertical phase separation morphology of the active layer can facilitate charge transfer and reduce charge recombination.32) Besides, high-efficiency interface layers can fine-regulate the energy level and enhance the stability of the interface between active layer and electrode.33] Fortunately, the dedicated efforts of researchers have yielded promising results, and Table 1 provides representative works of all-solution-processed OsCs.

According to the different top electrode materials (AgNws, conducting polymers and composite conducting materials), this paper reviews the recent advances of all-solution-processed OsCs. Our research endeavors center on investigating the interplay between electrode materials, electrode properties, and their impact on device performance. In addition, the influence of active layer and interface layer materials on the device performance is briefly discussed. Finally, several recommendations for future directions of all-solution-processed OsCs are presented.

# 2. All-Solution-Processed OSCs Based on AgNWs Top Electrodes

AgNws is a metallic nanomaterial with high electrical conductivity, good mechanical property, and solution-processability, an ideal candidate for the fabrication of solution-processed electrodes.50-51) Although much success has been achieved in all-solution-processed OsCs with AgNws top electrodes, it still suffers from some problems, e.g., the breakpoints of AgNws, resulting in the reduction of conductivity. In addition, the AgNws with rough surface may puncture transport and active layers and easily cause short circuits. Therefore, reducing the electric resistance and surface roughness of AgNws films is the prerequisite for the application of AgNWs electrodes in all-solution-processed OsCs.

In 2013, Guo et al. realized semitransparent all-solution-processed OsCs using spray-deposited AgNws top electrodes and doctor-bladed AgNws bottom electrodes with aluminum doped ZnO (AzO) as electron transport layers (ETLs) (Figure 2a).36] This innovative configuration and processing technology of electrodes contributed to significant improvements in the performance of semitransparent inverted all-solution-processed OsCs. Specifically, the sprayed femtoliter-volume aerosol droplets dry rapidly in microseconds, therefore, the electrode solvent effects little on the underlying layer, avoiding unwanted interactions. More importantly, the bottom electrode AgNWs were wrapped around by AZO and the holes of AgNWs were effectively filled by AzO (Figure 2b), which efficiently reduced the roughness of AgNws (from ${ \sim } 1 1$ nm to $\sim 5 \ \mathsf { n m }$ . Finally, the resulting fill factor (FF) of the device remarkably reached $6 3 \%$ which is a significant milestone in the field of all-solution-processed OsCs. Later, in 2015, Guo et al. used doctor-bladed high-reflection ${ \mathsf { A } } { \mathsf { g } }$ ink as bottom electrodes to prepare all-solution-processed OsCs on glass and PET substrates, respectively.38] Compared with AgNWs, Ag ink has lower roughness and is more suitable for a series of OsCs, preventing shunt in devices. With this, the PCEs of all-solution-processed tandem OSCs $5 . 8 1 \%$ on glass and $4 . 8 5 \%$ on PET) were achieved. This result provides a reference value for realizing low cost tandem OsCs. Similar to the spray deposition method, the inkjet printing method can effectively avoid solvent erosion between the electrode and the interface, improve the flatness of the printed electrode, and is widely used in preparing Oscs based on solution-processed AgNWs top electrodes. In 2016, Brabec et al. demonstrated that AgNWs with an average length of ${ 1 0 } \mathsf { n m }$ could be inkjet printed by industrial print heads without pre-processing, e.g., ultrasonic cutting.37) The inkjet printed AgNWs electrode had a uniform distribution and the Osc based on all-inkjet-printed cathode and anode produced an efficiency of $4 . 3 \%$

Due to the high roughness of AgNws, the contact resistance of the device increases with the device area, which makes the preparation of large-area devices based on AgNWs electrodes a challenge. Through rational module design and process optimization, Tam et al. patterned large-area all-solution-processed OsCs based on AgNWs electrodes into small-area series solar stripes.52] Thus, the challenges of limited critical interconnect area contact surfaces, high contact resistance, and low carrier transport capacity resulting from the AgNws with rough surface have been successfully addressed. Finally, a set of all-solution-processed OsCs modules exhibiting PCEs up to $5 \%$ with an active area of $3 . 5 ~ { \mathsf { c m } } ^ { 2 }$ were fabricated (Figure 2c). This result provided a solution for the complete roll-to-roll process of flexible translucent photovoltaic modules, which is suitable for large-scale commercial production.

As well as electrode materials, the PCEs of all-solution-processed Oscs heavily depend on the performance of active and transport layers. Therefore, it is necessary to summarize the synergistic effects of the active layers and the transport layers on the all-solution-processed devices based on the AgNws top electrodes. In the past few years, significant efforts have been focused on improving the PCEs of all-solution-processed OsCs devices based on AgNws top electrodes by designing and synthesizing highefficient transport layer and active layer materials. Among them, non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs) are high-performance acceptors used in the active layer and poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene): poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOt:Pss) is widely used as the hole transporting layer (HTL).21,53-54) However, charge extraction and wetting issue between NFAs and PEDOT:PSS may limit the PCEs of the inverted devices. In 202o, Sun et al. adopted a layer of hydrogen molybdenum bronze $( \mathsf { H } _ { \mathsf { X } } \mathsf { M } \mathsf { o } \mathsf { O } _ { 3 } )$ between the non-fullerene active layer and PEDOt:PsS and successfully solved these problems. The deep fermi level $( - 5 . 4 4 ~ \mathsf { e V } )$ of solution-processed $\mathsf { H } _ { \mathsf { X } } \mathsf { M } \mathsf { o } \mathsf { O } _ { 3 }$ can extract holes effectively (Figure 2d). At the same time. ethanol-processed $\mathsf { H } _ { \mathsf { X } } \mathsf { M } \mathsf { o } \mathsf { O } _ { 3 }$ with good wettability can successfully solve the wetting issue between NFAs and PEDOT:PSs. AgNWs embedded into the polyimide film substrate were used as top electrodes of the devices. All-solution-processed flexible OSCs (Figure 2e) with PCEs of $1 1 . 9 0 \%$ $( 0 . 0 4 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ and $1 0 . 3 0 \%$ $( 1 ~ { \mathsf { c m } } ^ { 2 } )$ were finally obtained and a useful reference for the preparation of Iarge-area all-solution-processed OsCs was successfully provided.

To fundamentally address the inherent wettability problem of NFAs and PEDOT:PSS in inverted devices and improve the PCEs of all-solution-processed devices with AgNws electrodes, Zhou et al. replaced counter ion PSS in PEDOT:PsS with perfluorinated sulfonic acid (PFSA) and the final product was defined as PEDoT:F.4) The alcohol/water-soluble PEDOt:F avoided long-standing shortcomings of the conventional PEDOt:Pss formulation dispersing only in water, thereby the wetting issues between NFAs and PEDOT: PSS have been successfully solved. with $\mathsf { P M } 6 { : \mathsf { P C } } _ { 7 1 } \mathsf { B M }$ :BTP-eC9 blend and spray-coated AgNws top electrode, the small-area $( 0 . 0 4 1 ~ \mathsf { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ device achieved a PCE of $1 5 . 2 6 \%$ . This result improves a range of properties of the conducting polymer and contributes to the production of stable and efficient all-solutionprocessed OSCs.

With the continuous development of all-solution-processed OsCs based on AgNws top electrodes and the improvement of PCEs, the application of the devices has been further expanded. In 2015, Fang et al. integrated an OSC with polyimide (Pl)/AgNWs electrode and a single-electrode triboelectric nanogenerator (STENG) into the solution-processed hybrid cell.55)] Triboelectric nanogenerator (TENG) has many advantages of low cost, easy manufacturing and lightweight, which are similar to oscs.] More importantly, the solution-processing technique of TENG is compatible with solution-processed Oscs. Combining the advantages of OsCs and TENG, the hybrid cell could collect solar energy and mechanical energy independently or simultaneously under different light and dark conditions without being restricted by environmental transformation (night, rain, etc.). With the advantages of cost-effective and mass production, the hybrid cell can be used as home power source or supply power for various wearable electronic devices, such as human subcutaneous medical monitoring, free from dependence on external power sources. This result is of great significance to expand the application field of OsCs. In 2016, Arumugam et al. used spraying technology to prepare Oscs on fabrics for wearable energy collection.4 Woven 65/35 polyester cotton fabrics were used as the target substrates of the devices. The highest PCE of the device manufactured on the fabric substrate was $0 . 0 2 \%$ with P3HT:ICBA blend. This result lays the foundation for low cost photovoltaic equipment used in textiles. Furthermore, in 2018, Beeby et al. realized low temperature $( < 1 5 0 ^ { \circ } \mathsf C )$ OsCs on polyester/cotton fabrics based on spray-coated AgNWs top electrodes.135] Using P3HT:ICBN as the active layer, the highest PCE of $1 . 2 3 \%$ was obtained on polyester/cotton fabric, further expanding the application of photovoltaic in textiles.

Among the transparent electrode materials that can be processed in solution, the AgNws have outstanding advantages such as high conductivity, controllable aspect ratio and strong bending durability, making them a popular choice for flexible and solutionprocessed electrodes.57 However, high contact resistance at the junctions of AgNws may reduce device efficiency. The high roughness and low adhesion strength to the substrate of AgNWs have been responsible for reduced mechanical stability. In addition, prolonged exposure to humid air leads to oxidation of AgNws, which negatively affects electrode conductivity, device efficiency, and environmental stability.50] To overcome these issues, several solutions have been proposed, including covering

AgNWs with conducting polymers and doping metal oxides,[58] embedding AgNWs into substrates,' 5,59] and using external forces to make the AgNWs flatter.46]

# 3. All-Solution-Processed Oscs Based on Conducting Polymers Top Electrodes

Conducting polymer films as solution-processed electrodes are widely used in Oscs because of their lightweight, good mechanical properties, and high transparency in the visible region.60] In addition, the widely used conductive polymer PEDOt:Pss, can be used as both a HTL and an electrode, reducing device functional layers and thus improving device stability, and reducing costs, meeting the requirements of roll-to-roll Iarge-scale printing.

In 2016, Zhan et al. developed all-solution-processed OSCs with PEDoT:Pss as both top and bottom electrodes,1 demonstrating the versatility of PEDOT:PSS as applied to transparent and flexible OsCs. About $50 \%$ AVT of the transparent flexible device was obtained and two active layer systems of fullerene $( \mathsf { P 3 H T : P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathsf { B M } )$ and non-fullerene (P3HT:IDT-2BR) were used to fabricate devices separately. Higher PCEs were achieved using P3HT:IDT-2BR as active layers due to the strong absorption of non-fullerene materials in the visible and/or near-infrared (NIR) regions, easily tunable energy levels, and finely tunable crystallinity of the non-fullerene system.153,61] Reliable methods for the application of all-solution-processed Oscs based on conducting polymers electrodes were obtained in a field of power windows and tandem OSCs.

Despite the numerous advantages of conducting polymer electrodes, their conductivity falls slightly short of ITO. Therefore, enhancing the conductivity of PEDOt:Pss electrodes is of crucial importance for the advancement of PEDOT:PSs. Two methods are commonly used to enhance the conductivity of polymer electrodes. One is treating the conducting polymer with acid or solvent to improve the conductivity.6-63 Another method is adjusting the ratio of commonly used conducting polymers PH 1000 (PEDOT $: { \mathsf { P S S } } = 1 : 2 . 5$ and AI 4083 (PEDOT: ${ \mathsf { P S S } } = 1 : 6 )$ I in the electrodes to improve the conductivity.

Tong et al. transferred $\mathsf { P } \mathsf { H } \ 1 0 0 0$ with ethylene glycol (EG) as top electrodes,43 which have great conductivity $( 6 8 6 5 \cdot \mathsf { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ and high transmittance $( 7 5 \% - 8 5 \% )$ in the visible range. With the improved conductivity of the HTL through the modulation of the proportion of $\mathsf { P } \mathsf { H } \ 1 0 0 0$ and Al 4083, high-performance multi-junction, flexible all-plastic OsCs were obtained. The devices are capable of powering electronic devices such as light-emitting diodes (LEDs) and liquid-crystal displays (LCDs) and also can be used to facilitate the decomposition of water. This result provides a promising solution for portable and wearable electronic devices with significant commercial value. In 2020, Kim et al. used EG as the auxiliary agent to reduce the sheet resistance to $2 6 0 ~ \Omega / \mathsf { s q }$ and improved the conductivity $( 4 4 3 5 \cdot \mathsf { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ of conducting polymer electrodes by adjusting the optimal ratio to $\mathsf { P } \mathsf { H } \ 1 0 0 0$ and Al 4083 $\left( { \mathsf { P } } { \mathsf { H } } \ 1 0 0 0 + { \mathsf { A } } | \ 4 0 8 3 + 1 2 \ { \mathsf { v } } { \mathsf { o } } | \% \ { \mathsf { E } } { \mathsf { G } } , ^ { 1 4 0 } \right]$ where the volume ratio of $\mathsf { P } \mathsf { H } \ 1 0 0 0$ to Al 4083 is 2:1). The highest PCE of $2 . 0 4 \%$ was finally obtained by using conducting polymers as both HTLs and electrodes. Fan et al. utilized PEDOT:PSS as both bottom and top electrodes, in conjunction with PM6:Y6 blend, designed and prepared all-solution-processed Oscs in air condition, with the highest PCE of $1 1 . 1 2 \%$ . Superimposing two PEDOT:PSS thin layers that were gradually treated with sulfuric acid and ethylene glycol, the internal insulating PSS component of the PEDOT:PSS was largely removed, and the formation of large agglomeration was inhibited (Figure 3a). PEDOT:PSS electrodes with high conductivity and relatively stable electrochemical behavior were obtained. The highest conductivity of PEDOT:PSS electrode was $4 2 0 0 \ 5 { \cdot } \mathsf { c m } ^ { - 1 } ,$ and the figure of merit (FoM) was about 100. In addition, the photoactive layer was treated by solvent vapor annealing (svA), and the phase-separated morphology of photoactive layers was refined (Figure 3b), thus the charge carrier mobility of the devices has been improved. The device remained a high PCE (about $91 \%$ of the initial value) (Figure 3c) after being stored in an inert gas atmosphere for ${ 3 0 ~ 0 }$ All-solution-processed and air-processed OsCs in this result can facilitate the development of roll-to-roll printing photovoltaic devices.

The inherent hygroscopic problem of PEDOT:PSS makes it unstable under wet conditions. When used as electrodes, the hygroscopicity and swelling of PEDOt:Pss films tend to cause device shunts, resulting in device failure. In 202o, Bihar et al. introduced the methoxysilane-based cross-linker (3-glycidyloxypropyl)trimethoxysilane (GOPS) in PEDOT:PSS with ${ \sf O } _ { 2 }$ plasma treatment and.a solvent post-treatment to prevent shunts of devices.42] Fully inkjet-printed ultralight flexible OsCs (Figures 3d, e) were obtained with the highest PCE of $3 . 6 1 \%$ . Notably, the packaged device exhibited excellent stability in a real-world wet environment, suggesting potential for high-humidity applications (Figure 3f).

As mentioned above, conducting polymers have the advantages of easy preparation, good air stability, and are widely used as electrode materials for all-solution-processed OsCs. However, there are still some problems in the application of conductive polymer. For example, the commonly used conductive polymer PEDOT:PSS has inherent acidity, high hygroscopicity, and insulating property of PsS, which may limit the performance and stability of devices. To address these issues, washing to remove insulating Pss and weak acid treatment may be effective in improving the conductivity and stability of conducting polymer films. In addition, novel conductive polymers with high conductivity, low acidity and hygroscopicity can be developed to further improve the performance of all-solution-processed Oscs based on conducting polymer electrodes.

# 4. All-Solution-Processed Oscs Based on Composite Conducting Materials Top Electrodes

In the previous two sections, the all-solution-processed OSCs based on pure conducting polymer top electrodes and pure AgNws top electrodes have been discussed. Both AgNws and conducting polymers have their own advantages and inherent drawbacks. In other words, the properties of high-quality electrodes, such as low roughness and high conductivity, cannot be simultaneously satisfied by simple AgNWs or conducting polymer electrodes. To further optimize the electrodes of all-solutionprocessed OsCs and improve the PCEs of the devices, researchers innovatively combined conducting materials to form composite conducting electrodes. In this way, not only the advantages of different conductive materials can be combined, but also the shortcomings of different electrode materials can be solved.

In 2014, Yim et al. used P3HT:PCBM as active layers to prepare translucent all-solution-processed OsCs with AgNws as bottom electrodes and PH 1000/(graphene oxide) GO/AI 4083 as top electrodes (Figure 4a).45] GO coated Al 4083 films were more uniform, with a decreased roughness from $7 . 8 \ \mathsf { n m }$ to $6 . 6 \mathsf { n m }$ (Figure 4b). In addition, spin coating $\mathsf { P } \mathsf { H } \ 1 0 0 0$ on hydrophobic Al 4083 has resulted in uneven coating, which in turn leads to device failure. With the excellent hydrophilicity of GO, it could effectively prevent the damage of electrode layer caused by capillary force during spin coating and drying process, thus improving the yield to approximately $8 5 \%$ . Comparable PCEs to those of ITO-based devices were achieved, whether illuminated from the cathode $( 2 . 3 \% )$ or the anode $( 2 . 0 \% )$ (Figure 4c).

In 2014, Nickel et al. developed all-solution-processed OsCs on PET substrates using AgNWs and PEDOT:PSS composite top electrodes.47] To prepare top electrodes, PEDOT:PSS was spincoated onto the active layer, followed by drop-casting of AgNWs to enhance electrode conductivity. Low surface roughness metalorganic decomposition (MOD) silver ink (TEC-PR-O10, Ink Tec) was used as the bottom electrode and a PCE of $4 . 7 \%$ was obtained with $\mathsf { P T B 7 : P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathsf { B M }$ blend. Notably, the device has excellent tensile resistance and can withstand $14 \%$ tensile strain. In 2016, Czolk et al. used pre-printed micro silver mesh on PET (named AgPET) as bottom electrodes,48] and PEDOT:PSS:AgNWs combined electrode (named HEY) as top electrodes (Figure 4d). Both HYE and AgPET exhibit high transmittance, resulting in a comparable AVT of the device $( - 7 9 \% )$ to that achieved with ITO on PET (Figure 4e). To broaden the scope of device applications, such as power windows and transparent roofs, a ternary active layer consisting of PffBT4T-2OD: $\mathsf { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathsf { B M } : \mathsf { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathsf { B M }$ was utilized to fabricate a large-area device with an impressive PCE up to $5 . 9 \%$ (Figure 4f). Later, in 2017, Koppitz et al. fabricated all-solution processed flexible OsCs with HYE serving as both the bottom and top electrodes.46] For the bottom electrodes, HYE was applied onto the substrates using a doctor-blading technique. After evaluating the conductivity and transparency of HYE films with varying thicknesses, an optimal thickness of $1 5 0 \ \mathsf { n m }$ was selected (Figure $4 { \tt g } )$ . Moreover, the AgNWs were firmly integrated into PEDOT:PSS via hot pressing, resulting in a further reduction of composite electrode roughness (Figure 4h). This integration effectively prevents device failure caused by protruding AgNws and significantly improves the bending performance of devices (Figure 4i). This technique is suitable for the industrialization of roll-to-roll all-solution-processed OSCs.

In 2023, Huang et al. reported ferroconcrete-like AZO@AgNWs@AzO (AAA) electrodes. AAA electrodes provide 3D long-range pathways for charge transfer and collection, and have excellent tensile mechanical properties. The device based on the TPU@AgNWs front electrode and AAA top electrode presented high average transmittance (AvT) of $2 6 . 6 4 \%$ and a PCE of $1 0 . 9 0 \%$ was obtained with PM6:BTP-eC9 blend. This result has great potential that can be used to power a watch under light and used as an electronic skin.

In short, composite electrodes using a variety of conductive materials can concentrate advantages, avoid defects, and improve the efficiency and stability of the devices. The improved mechanical and photoelectric properties enable devices to be applied in more scenarios and open up a new way for the application of all-solution-processed OsCs. For the future development of composite materials, the interaction between different conductive materials can be explored to further improve the performance of composite conductive materials.

# 5. Conclusions and Perspectives

In the past few years, significant advances have been achieved for all-solution-processed Oscs, primarily through research efforts on developing high-quality solution-processed electrode materials, designing and synthesizing high-performance photoactive layer and transport layer materials, and optimizing device structures. However, despite these remarkable improvements, there are still several unresolved issues in the field of all-solution-processed Oscs. Accordingly, based on the above discussion, this paper proposes several recommendations for the future directions of all-solution-processed OsCs.

(1) A high-quality solution-processed electrode is an indispensable component for achieving high-performance OsCs fabricated via a full-solution process. Generally, high-quality electrodes should possess minimum resistance, good stability, and low roughness simultaneously.

(2) The application of high-performance photovoltaic materials such as NFAs has significantly improved the PCEs of all-solution-processed Oscs, but the preparation cost is high. Therefore, it is necessary to obtain low cost[64-68] and high efficiency photoactive layer materials by simplifying the synthesis route and reducing the synthesis complexity.

(3) Exploring the application of low-temperature interface materials to avoid damage to the substrates in the roll-to-roll printing process is essential for all-solution-processed Oscs. In addition, thickness-insensitive buffer layers such as NDI-core (NDI-N, NDI-Br, PNDIT-F3N and PNDIT-F3N-BR)69-70) and PDI-core (PDIN, PDINO, PDINN and PDINN-F) can enhance the stability of the device during production.' [71-75]

(4) The spin coating technology commonly used in the laboratory is difficult to control the uniformity of film thickness precisely, which is not suitable for large-area and full-solution processing of Oscs. To overcome this limitation, it is imperative to explore alternative techniques compatible with large-scale roll-toroll printing, e.g., knife coating, slot-die coating, spray coating.
