# High-Performance Semitransparent Organic Solar Cells with Excellent Infrared Reflection and See-Through Functions

Di Wang, Ran Qin, Guanqing Zhou, Xue Li, Ruoxi Xia, Yuhao Li, Lingling Zhan, Haiming Zhu, Xinhui Lu, Hin-Lap Yip, Hongzheng Chen, and Chang-Zhi Li\*

Clean energy production and saving play vital impacts on the sustainability of the global community. Herein, high-performance semitransparent organic solar cells (ST-OsCs) with excellent features of power generation, being see-through, and infrared reflection of heat dissipation, with promising perspectives for building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPVs) are reported. To simultaneously improve average visible transmittance (AVT) and power conversion efficiency (PCE), formally in a trade-off relationship, of ST-OSCs, new ternary blends with alloy-like near-infrared (NIR) acceptors are employed, which are effective to improve device efficiency while maintaining visible absorption unchanged, resulting in PCEs of $1 6 . 8 \%$ for opaque devices and $1 3 . 1 \%$ for semitransparent OSCs (AVT of $2 2 . 4 \%$ and infrared photon radiation rejection (IRR) of $7 7 \%$ . Further, multifunctional ST-OsCs are realized via introducing simple, yet effective photonic reflectors, together with optical simulation, leading to not only perfect fitting of the visible transmittance peak $( 5 5 5 \mathsf { n m } )$ to the photopic response of the human eye but also an excellent IRR of $90 \%$ $( 7 8 0 - 2 5 0 0 \ : \mathrm { n m } )$ , along with $23 \%$ AVT and over $12 \%$ PCE. This is thought to be the best-performing multifunctional ST-OsC with promising prospects as BIPVs in terms of power generation, heat dissipation, and being see-through.

Semitransparent organic solar cells (ST-OsCs) exhibit promising features as building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPVs), such as power windows.,2] Particularly, organic semiconductors can be tailored to harvest a specific band of photons, e.g., absorbing near-infrared (NIR) photons while remaining visible range of photons transmitted.3-7] These are advantages of ST-OsCs that sharply differ from inorganic photovoltaics with few tunabilities on optical properties. In recent years, notable progress has been made for ST-OsCs, along with the development of near-infrared (NIR) absorbers and semitransparent device architecture.8-20] In general, efforts are mainly to improve the power conversion and seethrough function of devices, wherein two parameters are intrinsically in a trade-off relationship. For the perspective as power windows, multifunctional ST-OsCs[13,14] are desirable to possess features of power generation, being see-through and infrared reflection of heat dissipation. Power windows with both energy generation and saving functions should be highly promising to improve building energy efficiencies. It is because infrared photons are inclined to photon-thermal conversions for the heating inside of the building, and electricity spent on the cooling of building accounts a large portion of energy consumption. These factors promote researchers developing new types of ST-OSCs that can pass the visible range of photons for see-through, and absorb NIR photons for power conversion while rejecting low energy IR photons for heat dissipation.[13,14]

This requires a new design of ST-OsCs that can precisely and efficiently utilize different band of photon energies. For instance, photons (in wavelength of $3 8 0 { - } 7 8 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ ) matching the photopic response of the human eye should be transmitted, to ensure good average visible transmittance (AVT) of ST-OSC.16] Note AVT is calculated by weighting the integration of the transmission spectrum measured against the photopic response of the human eye.[21] NIR and IR photons $( 7 8 0 - 2 5 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ weight ${ \approx } 5 3 \%$ of solar spectra, wherein NIR photons can be utilized for a photon-electrical conversion, and low energy IR photons should be reflected for heat dissipation. To this purpose, multifunctional ST-OsCs are desirable to simultaneously achieve good parameters of PCE, AVT, and infrared photon rejection rate (IRR). Under the guidance of optical simulation, the STOSCs based on PBDTTT-E-T: IEICO binary blend yield a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of $6 \%$ , meanwhile, with $2 5 \%$ AVT and IRR of $80 \%$ has been achieved.[13] Further, we have recently developed ST-OsCs through precise control of device layout, reaching near $30 \%$ AVT and PCE of over $7 . 3 \%$ , as well as an excellent IRR of over $90 \%$ [14] Nevertheless, continuous efforts are needed to improve multifunctional ST-OsCs, therefore transiting its infancy to mature with practical relevance.

Recent studies indicate that the third chromophore into donor: acceptor binary blends, that is the ternary strategy, can regulate the absorption and morphology of blend to improve external quantum efficiencies (EQEs) and PCEs of OSCs.[22-27] ST-OSCs based on a ternary blend of J71:PTB7-Th:IHIC lead to a relatively high AVT of over $2 1 \%$ , with a remarkable colorrendering index (CRI) approaching 100 and PCE of $9 . 3 7 \%$ [28] Along with the improvement of PCE, the third component with complementary absorption in visible range could decrease the AVT of ST-OsCs employing ternary blends. Considering the critical trade-off between photovoltaic and optical properties of ST-OsCs, we propose to develop NIR ternary blend, wherein the third NIR component is compatible with origin donor or acceptor to form an alloy-like phase,29,30] for improving photovoltaic performance, while remaining visible absorption unchanged. That should be beneficial to improve light utilization efficiency (LUE)[31) of ST-OSCs (LUE is the product from multiplying AVT to PCE, as a useful performance indicator to ST-OSCs). Recently, we have developed NIR acceptor, BTTPC,[32] with strong absorption up to $8 8 9 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ which shares the same backbone with the excellent Y6 acceptor.[33,34] It has been revealed that BTTPC with extended conjugated terminals enables fast hole transfer to polymer donor under a small driving force.32] Thereby, we explore effective approaches that can address the critical trade-off between photovoltaic and optical properties of ST-OSCs.

Herein, high-performance ST-OsCs are developed with excellent features of power generation, see-through, and infrared reflection for heat dissipation. To simultaneously improve AVT and PCE, formally in a trade-off relationship, of ST-OsCs, we fabricated new ternary blends with alloy-like near-infrared (NIR) acceptors that are effective to improve device efficiency while remaining visible absorption unchanged, resulting in PCEs of $1 6 . 8 \%$ for opaque devices and $1 3 . 1 \%$ for semitransparent OSCs (AVT of $2 2 . 4 \%$ and IRR of $7 7 \%$ , representing so far the most efficient ST-OSCs with AVT over $20 \%$ . A small ratio of BTTPC into PBDB-TF:Y6 blend leads to the improved hole-transfer rate and mitigated charge recombination in ternary blends. Further, multifunctional ST-OsCs are realized via introducing a simple, yet effective photonic reflector, together with optical simulation, leading to not only perfect fitting of visible transmittance peak $( 5 5 5 \mathrm { n m } )$ with the photopic response of the human eye but also excellent IRR of $90 \%$ $( 7 8 0 - 2 5 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , along with $23 \%$ AVT and over $12 \%$ PCE. To our knowledge, this is the best-performed multifunctional ST-OsCs with desirable functions of power generation, infrared reflection, and see-through. Overall, this work provides prototype ST-OSCs and their construction methodologies with promising perspectives as BIPVs.

The above results indicate that the proper addition of NIR BTTPC into PBDB-TF:Y6 blend results in the fine formation of fibrous aggregates, while remaining the backbone ordering of Y6, resulting in the improved charge dynamics and transport in ternary blends, thus simultaneously improving $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } , \ : J _ { \mathrm { s c } } ,$ and FF values of ternary OSCs. This ternary strategy is effective to improve device overall efficiency while remaining no obvious change on the visible range absorption, therefore readily addressing the trade-off between photovoltaic and optical performance for semitransparent OsCs. In order to precisely using the different band of photons, we designed ST-OsCs in the structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/active layer/PFN-Br/ultrathin Ag without and with distributed Bragg reflector (DBR) layer (Supporting Information), with the assistance of optical simulation. The simple photonic reflector of DBR layers atop of ultrathin transparent Ag electrode is to fine adjustment of visible transmittance peak and reflection of IR photons of ST-OSCs.

Device A represents semitransparent ternary OsCs (STOSCs) with $1 2 { \mathrm { ~ n m ~ A g } }$ only, and device B represents ST-OSCs with $1 4 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ ultrathin Ag and DBR layer, wherein BHJ layer is PBDB-TF:Y6:BTTPC ternary blend and DBR layer consist of a pair of lithium fluoride (LiF)/molybdenum oxide $( \mathrm { M o O } _ { 3 } )$ bilayers atop of ultrathin Ag electrode. Meanwhile, the light utilization efficiencies $( \mathrm { L U E } = \mathrm { A V T } \times \mathrm { P C E } _ { i }$ is of $2 . 9 3 \%$ for device A. A photonic reflector consisting of an alternative stacking of transparent dielectric layers with a large contrast of refractive indexes can modulate the reflectance and transmission of the incident light.
