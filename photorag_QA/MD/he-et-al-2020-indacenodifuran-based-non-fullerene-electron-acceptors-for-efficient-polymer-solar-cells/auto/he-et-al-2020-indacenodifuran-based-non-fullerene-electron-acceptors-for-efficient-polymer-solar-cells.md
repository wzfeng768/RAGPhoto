# First furan-based non-fullerene electron acceptors for polymer solar cells

![](images/cb8e96f9650187dd28a8daa239b257de349c8bed3fdb18cfb5f9e276cfa453fe.jpg)

ABSTRACT: Two indaceno $\left[ { 1 , 2 - b : 5 , 6 - { b } ^ { \prime } } \right]$ difuran-based non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs) IDF-IC and IDF-4F were designed and synthesized. IDF-IC and IDF-4F showed strong absorptions in the visible and near-infrared (NIR) region with narrow bandgaps of $1 . 5 6 { - } 1 . 6 2 \ \mathrm { ~ e V }$ The power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of PM6:IDF-IC and PM6:IDF-4F devices were $7 . 8 0 \%$ and $7 . 8 1 \%$ respectively, with open-circuit voltages $\left( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } \mathrm { ^ 3 } \right)$ of 0.905 and $0 . 7 3 6 \mathrm { V }$ , short-circuit current densities $\left( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \mathbf { \ ' } _ { \mathbf { s } } \right)$ of 14.55 and $1 7 . 4 9 \ \mathrm { m A } /$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { \hat { 2 } } .$ and fill factors (FFs) of $5 9 \%$ and $6 1 \%$ . These results demonstrate that the fused-ring furan-based NFAs could be one of the promising building blocks in constructing high performance NFAs.

Starting from the indacenodithiophene (IDT) unit, numerous NFAs such as IDT-IC, ITIC, BTA1,8 $m$ ITIC, IT-4F,10 and ITIC-Th11 (Figure S1) have been designed and synthesized in order to maximize the PCEs of PSCs. However, the further design strategy of, i.e., extending the conjugation length to NIR absorption, typically requires a tedious, high-cost synthesis and a complicated purification process, which undoubtedly hinder their mass production. In addition to this, NFAs with a largely extended conjugation length also possess the low-lying lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO), which is undesirable for the open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ of PSC, since $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ is in inverse proportion to LUMO of NFA regardless of other factors such as nonradiative energy loss and an interfacial layer. In addition, regarding the device optimization with these NFAs, the small amount $\left( 0 . 1 - 3 \% \right)$ of solvent additive such as 1,8-diiodooctane (DIO) is generally needed to promote the favorable bicontinuous morphology; however, this tedious process is not easy to control and is also unfavorable for the printing or roll-to-roll processing technologies. Thus, it will be more desirable to develop NFAs with a facile synthesis and good compatibility with an additive-free fabrication process for NFPSC applications.

Furan is a heterocyclic aromatic unit, which has a replacement of oxygen compared to thiophene and can be obtained easily from a natural product. Other groups as well as ours have applied the furan unit in the development of conjugated polymer donors, and the furan-based polymers have shown exceptional results when compared with those of their thiophene counterparts.12 Currently, however, there are no such reports focusing on the furan-based NFAs. It is known that furan is a more electron-rich block than thiophene; the enhanced electron-donating capability will be able to induce a red-shifted absorption, which is preferred in covering more sunlight, and the furan unit also has a large dipole moment, which may generate various dipole interactions with the enhanced intramolecular charge transfer (ICT).13 In addition, the small size of the furan unit may produce stronger $\pi - \pi$ molecular stacking and interactions.

Herein, we designed and synthesized a furan-based fusedring unit, indaceno[1,2-b:5,6- $\cdot b ^ { \prime }$ ]difuran (IDF), and we expanded it into NFAs to synthesize two IDF-based NFAs, IDF-IC and IDF-4F (Figure 1a). As expected, IDF-IC and IDF-4F possessed efficient ICT with a red-shifted absorption compared to the thiophene counterpart. The optical bandgaps were then reduced to 1.62 and $1 . 5 6 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for IDF-IC and IDF4F, respectively. The photovoltaic performances of IDF-IC and IDF-4F as electron acceptors in NF-PSCs were investigated with various polymer donors. IDF was synthesized from diethyl 2,5-dibromoterephthalate and 2-n-butyltin furan via Stille coupling and cyclization reactions.15 IDF-CHO was prepared from the hydroformylation of IDF using $\mathrm { D M F } / \mathrm { P O C l } _ { 3 }$ . IDF-IC and IDF-4F were obtained from IDF-CHO, 1,1-dicyanomethylene-3-indanone (IC), and difluorinated IC (IC-2F) through Knoevenagel condensation at room temperature (Figure 1a). IDF-IC and IDF-4F show good solubility in organic solvents such as chloroform, chlorobenzene, and dichlorobenzene. The geometry and electronic structure of IDF-IC and IDF-4F were investigated using a density-functional theory (DFT) method. Figure 1b demonstrates that IDF-IC and IDF-4F both possess a planar geometry with the molecular dipole moments of 2.33 and 1.97 D, respectively, which can facilitate $\pi$ electron delocalization and enhance charge carrier transport properties. Additionally, the LUMO and HOMO distributions of IDF-IC and IDF-4F delocalize over their whole backbonel these are similar to the LUMO and HOMO distributions of IDT. $\mathrm { { I C } ^ { 7 } }$ (Figure S2). The electrostatic potentials $\left( \mathrm { E S P s } \right)$ of IDF-IC and IDF-4F were further investigated (Figure 1c). This shows that most parts of IDF-IC and IDF-4F have a positive ESP distribution while the negative regions mainly locate on oxygen (O), cyano (CN), and fluorine (F) groups. The largely distributed positive ESP will direct the intermolecular electric field (IEF) from IDF-IC (or IDF-4F) to the polymer donor, which is favorable for exciton dissociation and charge extraction.16

The UV-vis absorption spectra of IDF-IC and IDF-4F in chloroform solution and film states (Figure 2) were measured. The UV-vis spectra of IDT-IC were also listed in Figure 2. The absorption band of IDF-IC and IDF-4F gave a significant red-shift compared to that of IDT-IC. For IDF-IC, the redshifts of the absorption band are 34 and $^ { 2 9 } \mathrm { n m }$ in the solution and film states, respectively, compared with that of IDT-IC.7 This red-shift is mainly due to the strong electron-donating capability of the furan unit in IDF.17,18 The introduction of fluorine atoms in IDF-4F leads to a further red-shifted absorption band in comparison with that of IDF-IC. More interestingly, the absorption spectra of IDF-IC and IDF-4F films exhibit a shoulder absorption band at ${ \sim } 7 4 0$ and ${ \sim } 7 7 0$ nm, indicating stronger $\pi - \pi$ molecular stacking in film states. This phenomenon was also found in other NFAs with Jaggregation behavior.19-21 The absorption edges of IDF-IC and IDF-4F in films were at 760 and $7 9 5 \mathrm { n m }$ , corresponding to the bandgaps of 1.63 and $1 . 5 6 \ \mathrm { e V } _ { \mathrm { \Omega } }$ respectively.

Cyclic voltammetry measurements were performed to measure the energy levels of IDF-IC and IDF-4F (Figure S3). Their LUMO energy levels are estimated to be $- 4 . 1 3$ and $- 4 . 2 7 \ \mathrm { ~ e V }$ for IDF-IC and IDF-4F, respectively, which are lower than the LUMO energy level $\left( - 3 . 8 8 \ \mathrm { \ e V } \right)$ of IDT-IC. The slightly lower LUMO energy level of IDF-4F is attributed to the introduction of strong electronegative fluorine atoms.223 The HOMO energy levels are calculated to be $- 5 . 7 5$ and $- 5 . 8 3 \mathrm { ~ ~ e V }$ for IDF-IC and IDF-4F, respectively, which can ensure enough HOMO offset for efficient exciton dissociation. The lower energy levels and smaller bandgap of IDF-IC than of IDT-IC benefit from synergistical stronger electronegativity and the smaller atom radius of the furan unit.

In order to better understand the photovoltaic performances of IDF-IC and IDF-4F in NF-PSCs, the devices with polymers J52, J61, PBDB-T, PM6, and J71 were fabricated (Figure S4 and Table S1). The devices were processed without any solvent additive and the thermal annealing process. We observed that, even with matched absorption and energy levels between polymer and IDF-IC (or IDF-4F), the devices exhibited various performances. In the cases of polymer donors

J52, J61, PBDB-T, and J71, the IDF-IC devices gave a better photovoltaic performance than IDF-4F devices, whereas the PM6:IDF-4F device possessed a higher PCE than the PM6:IDF-IC device. The highest PCE for the PM6:IDF-4F device is $7 . 1 1 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 8 1 0 \mathrm { V } , J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $1 4 . 5 8 \mathrm { m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } .$ and FF of $6 0 \%$ . The difference of photovoltaic performances with various polymer donors should be attributed to the different energy level, morphology, and miscibility between polymer and IDF-IC (or IDF-4F).

A further optimization of the photovoltaic performances of PM6:IDF-IC and PM6:IDF-4F devices was performed; this found that thermal annealing can significantly improve the device performance. The PM6:IDF-IC device after thermal annealing at $1 3 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ showed an increased PCE of $7 . 8 0 \%$ with $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 9 0 5 \mathrm { V } , J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $1 4 . 5 5 \mathrm { m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ and FF of $5 9 \%$ (Table 1), in which the increases are mainly from the large enhancement on $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF. In a PM6:IDF-4F device, the thermal annealing further improved the PCE to $7 . 8 1 \%$ with $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 7 3 6 \mathrm { V } , J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $1 7 . 4 9 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { \hat { / } c m } ^ { 2 } .$ and FF of $6 1 \%$ . The high $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ in IDF-IC device is very consistent with its higher-lying LUMO energy level. The higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ in the IDF-4F device should be from the more desirable interpenetrating network between PM6 and IDF-4F as induced by F--F interactions.42s We also listed the photovoltaic performances of other NFAs, which have chemical skeletons similar to those of IDF-IC and IDF-4F but possess more complicated synthetic routes. As seen from Table 1, the device from IDT-IC only gave a PCE of $3 . 1 6 \%$ with a significantly low $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF. In other NFAs, IDF-IC and IDF-4F devices also showed a comparable or higher photovoltaic performance (Table 1 and Table S2).26

The external quantum efficiencies (EQEs) of PM6:IDF-IC and PM6:IDF-4F devices were measured to investigate their photoresponses. As shown in Figure 3b, both devices have a broad photoresponse in the region $3 0 0 { - } 8 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . It was also found that the photoresponse of the PM6:IDF-IC device is mainly contributed by PM6 in the short wavelength region with the maximized value of $45 \%$ ; the photoresponse from IDF-IC at the longer wavelength region is slightly stronger with the maximized EQE of $4 8 \%$ .The maximum EQEs contributed from PM6 and IDF-4F were increased to $60 \%$ and $6 8 \%$ respectively. These results are consistent with the measured $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ in both devices.27 More interestingly, compared with PM6:IDF-IC, the EQE spectrum of the PM6:IDF-4F device is obviously red-shifted in comparison with the absorption spectrum of a neat IDF-4F flm (Figure S5), which is beneficial from the improved photon absorption of IDF-4F and photon-to-carrier conversion in the spectral edge region. One of the reasons is believed to be caused by the synergistic F.F effect and enhanced J-aggregation in the PM6:IDF-4F film as well as the IDF-4F's negative ESP fluorine (F) groups that may enhance the electron transfer from PM6 to IDF-4F.20,21

To gain more insight into exciton dissociation, the exciton dissociation probabilities $\left( P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } \mathbf { \ ' } _ { s } \right)$ of PM6:IDF-IC and PM6:IDF-4F devices were measured, and the corresponding curves were shown in Figure 3c. Generally, the photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ follows the equation $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } = J _ { \mathrm { L i g h t } } - J _ { \mathrm { D a r k } } ;$ the exciton dissociation probability can be calculated from $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } = J _ { \mathrm { p h } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t . } }$ The $J _ { \mathrm { s a t . } }$ values of PM6:IDF-IC and PM6:IDF-4F devices were 16.2 and $1 8 . 4 \mathrm { \ m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ , respectively, under the short-circuit condition. The $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ values of PM6:IDF-IC and PM6:IDF-4F devices were calculated to be $8 4 . 8 1 \%$ and $8 5 . 7 1 \%$ respectively. The slightly improved $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ of PM6:IDF-4F may be related to the enhanced intermolecular interaction and charge transport by $\mathrm { F }$ atoms and negative ESP, which can be verified by the charge-recombination. The charge-recombination of IDF-IC and IDF-4F devices were investigated by the dependence of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ on light intensity $( P ) _ { \ast }$ and their curves were shown in Figure 3d.29 The power law dependence, ${ \cal J } _ { s c } \propto { \cal I } ^ { a }$ was observed with a $= ~ 0 . 7 8 8$ and $a ~ = ~ 0 . 8 3 2$ for IDF-IC and IDF-4F devices, respectively. Consequently, the better exciton dissociation and weaker bimolecular recombination of PM6:IDF-4F device synergistically contributed the higher $J _ { s c }$

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) were conducted to investigate the surface morphologies of PM6:IDF-IC and PM6:IDF-4F blend films. As shown in Figure $^ { 4 \mathrm { a } , \mathrm { b } _ { \mathrm { i } } }$ the film of PM6:IDF-IC exhibited relatively flat surfaces with small aggregation, and the rootmean-square surface roughness (RMS) value was $3 . 0 3 \mathrm { n m }$ . The film of PM6:IDF-4F, however, exhibited a smoother surface morphology with a decreased RMS roughness value of $1 . 3 7 \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure 4d,e), showing a good miscibility and compatibility between PM6 and IDF-4F. These results demonstrate that the introduction of F atoms on the end group of IDF-IC could induce the smaller phase separation via strong F..F intra/ intermolecular interactions, which will be beneficial for the preferred exciton dissociation and charge transport.30 Similar results can also be found from TEM images. As shown in Figure 4c,f, the PM6:IDF-4F film possesses a more uniform and interpenetrating network morphology whereas small aggregates can be observed in the PM6:IDF-IC film. These results thus partly explained the better exciton dissociation, weaker bimolecular recombination, and higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ in the PM6:IDF-4F device.

We have synthesized two fused-ring furan-based NFAs IDFIC and IDF-4F through the easy and facile synthetic routes. IDF-IC and IDF-4F possessed the reduced bandgaps of 1.62 and $1 . 5 6 ~ \mathrm { e V } _ { \mathrm { \Omega } }$ respectively, and strong $\pi - \pi$ molecular stacking.

In NF-PSCs, the PM6:IDF-IC and PM6:IDF-4F devices provided PCEs of $7 . 8 0 \%$ and $7 . 8 1 \%$ Further detailed investigations on the exciton recombination and surface morphologies were also conducted. This study demonstrates that fused-ring furan-based NFAs will be among the outstanding candidates for developing highly efficient NF-PSCs.
