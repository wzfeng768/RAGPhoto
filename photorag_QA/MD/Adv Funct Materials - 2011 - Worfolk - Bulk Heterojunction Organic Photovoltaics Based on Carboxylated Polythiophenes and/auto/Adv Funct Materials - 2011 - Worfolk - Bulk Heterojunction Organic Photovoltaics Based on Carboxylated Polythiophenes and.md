# Bulk Heterojunction Organic Photovoltaics Based on Carboxylated Polythiophenes and PCBM on Glass and Plastic Substrates

Brian J. Worfolk, David A. Rider, Anastasia L. Elias, Michael Thomas, Kenneth D. Harris,\* and Jillian M. Buriak\*

The optoelectronic and photophysical properties of four regioregular poly[3-(carboxyalkyl)thiophene-2,5-diyl] (P3CAT) with different carboxyalkyl chain lengths (propyl to hexyl) are reported. Each P3CAT is combined with [6,6]-phenyl- $\mathsf { \pmb { C } } _ { 6 1 }$ -butyric acid methyl ester to form the photoactive bulk heterojunction layer for organic photovoltaic devices. The extent of hydrogen bonding and polymer crystallinity in the films has been determined through infrared spectroscopy and $\pmb { \chi }$ -ray diffraction. The mechanical properties of films are analyzed with nanoindentation, and the measurements suggest that P3CATs are suitable for use in flexible devices. Power conversion efficiencies of up to $2 . 6 \%$ and $1 . 6 \%$ are obtained for devices fabricated in air, and supported on glass and flexible poly(ethylene terephthalate) substrates, respectively.

# 1. Introduction

Organic photovoltaics (OPVs) are an appealing choice for nextgeneration solar cells due to their highly tunable optical and electronic properties as well as their potential for low cost rollto-roll manufacturing on flexible substrates.[1-13] The photoactive layer of excitonic OPVs typically consists of a mixture of a light-absorbing, electron-donating semiconducting polymer, such as a regioregular poly[3-(alkyl)thiophene] (P3AT), and an electron-accepting fullerene, commonly [6,6]-phenyl- ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 6 1 } { \mathrm { . } }$ butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM).[14-22] This donor-acceptor mixture is most often employed in a phase-separated and disordered bicontinuous interpenetrating network known as the bulk heterojunction (BHJ), and the arrangement has been intensely studied and optimized in recent years for the efficient promotes interfacial electron transfer, reduces charge trap sites and extends device lifetime.17,40,41,44 47] It is therefore a fruitful approach to investigate this strategy in BHJ OPVs comprised of a class of poly(alkylthiophene)s bearing carboxylic acid groups.

In an effort to establish poly[3-(carboxyalkyl)thiophene-2,5- diyl]s (P3CATs) as suitable donor materials for BHJ OPVs, we investigated the polymer series containing carboxyalkyl sidechains $[ - ( \mathrm { C H } _ { 2 } ) _ { \mathrm { x } } \mathrm { C O O H }$ ; for $_ x$ ranging from 3 to 6]. The series includes poly[3-(3-carboxypropyl)thiophene-2,5-diyl] (P3CProT), poly[3-(4-carboxybutyl)thiophene-2,5-diyl](P3CButT),  poly[3- (5-carboxypentyl)thiophene-2,5-diyl] (P3CPenT) and  poly[3- (6-carboxyhexyl)thiophene-2,5-diyl] (P3CHexT). P3CATs have recently been used in dye-sensitized solar cells where the free carboxylic acid groups served as anchoring groups binding to $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ .148-55] The suitable optical and electronic properties, and the potential for hydrogen bonding and further derivitization of the P3CAT series has been recognized, and has led to other applications in chemical[56-59] and biological sensors,[60] fieldeffect transistors[61] and single-walled carbon nanotube/polymer nanohybrids.[62]

We report the optoelectronic and mechanical properties of this carboxyl-functionalized polythiophene series. Despite numerous statements of mechanical flexibility as a driving factor in OPV research,11,12,63-67] measurement of OPV mechanical properties is uncommon in the literature,[64] and thus we devote attention to this aspect of characterization. P3CAT:PCBM films are incorporated into BHJ OPVs with a device architecture of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/P3CAT:PCBM/Al on both borosilicate glass and flexible poly(ethylene terephthalate) (PET) substrates (Scheme 1). Device performance was shown to be strongly dependent on the ordering of the polymer in the bulk heterojunction.

# 2.1. Optoelectronic Properties of P3CATs

To determine and contrast the behavior of regioregular P3CATs in the context of OPVs, the optoelectronic properties were studied. The P3CAT derivatives were found to be highly soluble in pyridine and appeared orange to red in colour (see Supporting Information for photographs of solutions and films). # 2.2.2. Hydrogen Bonding and Molecular Structure

To cater to the solubility requirements of both components, a mixed solvent system was chosen. P3CATs are soluble in pyridine, dimethylformamide and dimethyl sulfoxide,[58,61] and sparingly soluble in chlorobenzene and dichlorobenzene. Conversely, PCBM is highly soluble in chlorobenzene and dichlorobenzene and only marginally soluble in pyridine, dimethylformamide and dimethyl sulfoxide. Pyridine and chlorobenzene were selected as cosolvents for P3CAT:PCBM mixtures as their boiling points ( $1 1 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and $1 3 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ respectively) and enthalpies of vaporization $( 4 0 . 2 \mathrm { k J } \ m \mathrm { o l } ^ { - 1 }$ and $4 1 . 0 \mathrm { k J } \mathrm { m o l } ^ { - 1 }$ respectively) are similar and are consequently volatilized at similar rates during casting procedures.80,81] Initial solubility experiments revealed that PCBM precipitates in mixtures with high pyridine content (Supporting Information), which therefore limits the solvent composition to include no more than $2 5 \% \mathrm { v } / \mathrm { v }$ pyridine. Characterization of P3CAT:PCBM solutions or cast films was always performed using solvent ratios empirically determined to produce the optimum power conversion efficiency in completed OPV devices (vide infra). For the distinct P3CAT:PCBM combinations, the optimum solvent ratios were identified as 1:7, 1:8, 1:6, and 1:7 pyridine:chlorobenzene for alkyl chain lengths $x = 3$ , 4, 5, and 6, respectively.

The extent of hydrogen bonding in P3CATs, in both solid and solution phases, can be assessed with infrared (IR) spectroscopy.143,56,58,82,83] The polymers were cast from the optimized solvent compositions onto KBr discs, and the carbonyl regions of the IR spectra of the films are shown in Figure 4. The carbonyl regions for all spectra consist of two peaks corresponding to free $\mathrm { C } { = } \mathrm { O }$ stretches at higher energy $( \sim 1 7 3 3 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ and hydrogen bonded $\mathrm { C } { = } \mathrm { O }$ vibrations at lower energy $( \sim 1 7 0 0 \ c m ^ { - 1 } )$ [83] All P3CATs have significant hydrogen bonded $\mathrm { C } { = } \mathrm { O }$ vibrational modes, pointing to interchain cross linking of the polymers. The hydrogen bonded $\mathrm { C } = \mathrm { O }$ peaks for P3CButT and P3CHexT are shifted to higher energy compared to P3CProT and P3CPenT by approximately $1 4 ~ \mathrm { c m ^ { - 1 } }$ , indicating stronger hydrogen bonding interactions in the carboxyalkyl chains of these polymers.[84] The extent of hydrogen bonding can be assessed from the relative peak areas of the free and hydrogen bonded $\mathrm { C } { = } \mathrm { O }$ vibrational modes. By this analysis, P3CPenT has a significantly larger degree of hydrogen bonding, while P3CProT exhibits the lowest degree of hydrogen bonded molecular interactions (Supporting Information). It is important to note that the IR spectra of BHJ films were further complicated by an overlap of the hydrogen bonded $\mathrm { C } { = } \mathrm { O }$ stretch with the ester stretch in PCBM $( \sim 1 7 3 6 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ and prevented complete quantification of the extent of hydrogen bonding in these BHJ films.

To better understand the structure and morphology of P3CATs in BHJ films, glancing angle X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was performed on P3CAT:PCBM samples cast on silicon wafers using the optimized casting conditions. Figure 5A shows the XRD spectra of the four P3CAT:PCBM films, offset for clarity. A distinct peak corresponding to the interdigitation of the carboxyalkyl chains (100) was evident for all films, and as expected, this peak shifted to higher d-spacings with increasing alkyl chain length as previously observed with P3ATs.[85] For P3CProT, P3CButT, P3CPenT, and P3CHexT, the d-spacings were $1 2 . 1 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ 14.1 A, $1 6 . 8 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ and $1 9 . 0 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ respectively, and this trend is summarized in Figure 5B. This figure also includes the (100) d-spacings previously reported for alkyl chain lengths of 2 and 7.58,61] An obvious linear relationship exists between (100) d-spacing and carboxyalkyl chain length. The (200) peak was also present in all spectra, and the corresponding d-spacing also increased with lengthening of the carboxyalkyl chain. Other characteristic peaks were detected in the XRD spectra at $2 \theta = 1 9 . 7 ^ { \circ }$ which correspond to d-spacings of $4 . 5 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ in all films. This peak has been previously observed, and represents dense stacking of PCBM molecules normal to the film plane.[86,87] It is interesting to note that the intensity of this $2 \theta = 1 9 . 7 ^ { \circ }$ peak is significantly stronger in P3CProT and P3CButT samples, suggesting a greater degree of complex molecular arrangement of PCBM in these BHJ films. Similarly, an additional PCBM peak was observed in the P3CProT:PCBM and P3CButT:PCBM spectra at $2 \theta { = } 1 0 . 2 ^ { \circ }$ (indicating a d-spacing of 8.7 A), which also corresponds to the packing of PCBM molecules.[86] The size of polymer crystallites can be calculated from the Scherrer equation for the (100) diffraction peaks.88,89] Using this equation, the crystallite sizes for P3CProT P3CButT, P3CPenT, and P3CHexT BHJ films were determined to be $4 . 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ $4 . 7 \ \mathrm { n m }$ $9 . 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , and $4 . 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ respectively (Supporting Information). In the P3CPenT:PCBM BHJ films, the polymer P3CAT crystallite size is approximately double that of the other polymers.

Despite this, specific mechanical criteria for OPV materials have not been clearly established, particularly as the mechanical properties of many leading candidates remain completely unknown, and reports of mechanical studies on OPV active layer materials are uncommon.[66,97-100] Tabulating these properties for donors, acceptors, and BHJ blends will assist in establishing acceptable ranges, and understanding how variations in chemical functionalization and morphology affect mechanical behavior. Two measurable parameters are Young's modulus and hardness. A higher modulus is generally indicative of a lower flexibility, and as such lower values are preferable. Hardness characterizes the resistance of a material to plastic deformation and is therefore linked to durability.

One method of characterizing the mechanical properties of thin films and coatings is nanoindentation. This technique entails driving a hard tip into a material while recording the load-displacement curves; from these experiments, the Young's modulus and hardness of the layer can be determined.[101,102] Nanoindentation is routinely used to characterize the mechanical properties of polymer films[103] including poly(ethylene terephthalate) (PET),[104] poly(methyl  methacrylate),105] shape memory polymers,106] polyfluorene derivatives,[107] poly $p$ phenylene vinylene),[108] PEDOT:PSS,[109] as well as hybrid CdSe/polymer,[110] and fullerene films.[111,112]

The Young's modulus of cast P3HT has been measured by other techniques, and values of ${ \sim } 0 . 7 \ \mathrm { G P a } ^ { [ 9 7 , 9 8 ] }$ and $1 . 3 \mathrm { \ G P a } ^ { \left[ 9 9 , 1 0 0 \right] }$ were reported. We substantiated these values for P3HT with nanoindentation, observing a Young's modulus of $1 . 4 \ : \mathrm { G P a }$ , and a hardness of $0 . 0 8 \ : \mathrm { G P a }$ . The P3HT:PCBM system has also been studied, and the BHJ film was found to have an elastic modulus of ${ \sim } 6 . 2 \mathrm { G P a }$ approximately five times larger than pure P3HT.[99]

For P3CAT-only films, we measured average indentation moduli in the range from 3.8 to $1 . 5 \mathrm { \ G P a }$ , and hardness values from 0.17 to $0 . 0 9 \mathrm { G P a }$ , as shown in Table 3 and the Supporting Information. Shorter carboxyalkyl chain lengths lead to greater moduli and hardness, and, in each case, the values for P3CAT films exceed those measured for P3HT. In OPV devices, P3CATs are blended with PCBM, and therefore, the mechanical properties of the combined BHJ films are of the greatest interest in the context of OPV devices. In the case of P3CProT:PCBM and P3CButT:PCBM mixtures, rough surfaces were observed optically, and inconsistent nanoindentation data was recorded as the measurement spot was varied over the sample. Consistent measurements were collected, on the other hand, for P3CPenT:PCBM, P3CHexT:PCBM and P3HT:PCBM films, (Supporting Information) and these samples appeared smoother by optical microscopy. In each case, the introduction of PCBM significantly increased the Young's modulus of the films, with moduli ranging from 5.3 to 4.0 GPa and hardness ranging from 0.26 to 0.19 GPa for P3CPenT:PCBM, P3CHexT:PCBM and P3HT:PCBM, respectively (Table 3). These increased values are consistent with the literature, as the "filler effect' routinely leads to increased mechanical properties, i.e., larger modulus and hardness, in particle-loaded polymer films.[113,114]

Materials with high elastic moduli and hardness tend to be more resistant to plastic deformation and creep than softer counterparts, and in the past, hydrogen bonding in polymer films has been shown to significantly increase moduli and hardness.115-117] Hydrogen-bonded P3CATs reflect this trend, as each material measured showed greater indentation modulus and hardness than nonhydrogen bonded P3HT. Less variation in mechanical properties was observed in the series of measurements of P3CATs in the presence of PCBM, but both P3CAT:PCBM mixtures were harder than P3HT:PCBM. This may lead to improved durability. The moduli and hardness of P3CAT and P3HT films are on par with reported values for common flexible substrates, include poly(ethylene terephthalate) (PET) and poly(ethylene naphthalate) (PEN), which have Young's moduli of 2.3 and $3 . 3 \ : \mathrm { G P a }$ and hardnesses of 0.27 and $0 . 6 5 \ : \mathrm { G P a }$ respectively,[10,67] The BHJs, however, have larger moduli than the substrate layers, suggesting that the PCBM will dominate the overall mechanical properties of the photoactive layer.

# 2.3. Bulk Heterojunction Photovoltaic Devices

The optoelectronic and photophysical properties of the P3CATs were found to be appropriate for incorporation into BHJ OPVs. Devices were fabricated on ITO substrates coated with a $2 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ hole-transporting interfacial layer of PEDOT:PSS. A PEDOT:PSS layer enhances hole collection and increases opencircuit potential $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ due to improved ohmic contact with the photoactive layer.[118-120] The active layer, 1:1 P3CAT:PCBM, was spin-cast in air on top of annealed PEDOT:PSs films and Al was thermally deposited to complete the cell. The photovoltaic performance of cells was tested in air under simulated AM $1 . 5 \mathrm { ~ G ~ }$ conditions with power calibrated to a NREL certified KG-5 reference Si cell, which typically minimizes spectral mismatch.[121] Four $0 . 1 5 5 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ devices at each solvent composition were fabricated and their short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ open-circuit potential $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ , fill factor (FF) and power conversion efficiency $( \eta )$ were calculated from $J$ V curves. Figure 7A depicts the relationship between photovoltaic parameters and the pyridine composition (in chlorobenzene) for P3CPenT:PCBM. The ITO/PEDOT:PSS/P3CPenT:PCBM/Al device architecture exhibited favourable PV performance at ${ \sim } 1 4 \%$ pyridine in the casting solvent, or a 1:6 pyridine:chlorobenzene ratio. The $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } ,$ FF, and $\eta$ exhibit similar trends of decreasing performance with increasing pyridine content. The $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ increases, however, with higher pyridine fractions. These trends are attributed to the formation of PCBM nanoclusters as a result of large scale phase separation occurring in mixtures with greater pyridine content as evidenced by roughness in the cross-sectional scanning electron images (SEMs) (Supporting Information). Within the P3CAT series, there does not appear to be a direct trend between photovoltaic performance and carboxyalkyl chain length, and the only measured PV parameter which varies greatly is the short-circuit current, which may be the determining factor in device performance. The $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ can be predicted from the band offset between the acceptor LUMO and donor HOMO. From the energy level diagram in Figure 2, the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ is expected to be slightly smaller for P3CProT and P3CPenT compared to P3CButT and P3CHexT, and this effect is clear in Table 2. The P3CPenT:PCBM films also have a bathochromic shift from the absorbance spectroscopy of films, while the other P3CATs are blue-shifted (Supporting Information). In order to test these materials in flexible BHJ OPV devices, assembly was carried out on ITO-coated PET substrates for P3CHexT:PCBM and P3HT:PCBM and the resultant $J { - } V$ curves and PV parameters are summarized in Figure 8B and Table 2. The photovoltaic characteristics of the P3HT:PCBM device on PET-ITO was very similar to Al-Ibrahim et al. who report PCEs of $1 . 5 4 \%$ , with a $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $6 . 6 1 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and a fill factor of 0.39.[126] The P3CHexT:PCBM device had comparable performance to the P3HT:PCBM device with a lower $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ but higher $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and fill factor. In comparing the P3CHexT:PCBM BHJ on borosilicate and flexible PET-ITO, most PV parameters are similar with the exception of a slightly lower $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } ,$ which may be attributed to decreased transmission in PET-ITO films.[127] These results are comparable to similar reported flexible organic solar cells in the literature.65,128-131] The nanoindentation results of the combined polymer:PCBM films suggests that the carboxyfunctionalized polythiophenes are suitable for use on flexible substrates. However, a major limitation in the device architecture is the far larger modulus of ITO $( 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { G P a } )$ [132] which is more prone to cracking than P3HT and the P3CAT series. If ITO can be replaced with a mechanically robust material, the carboxy-functionalized polythiophenes would have similar mechanical stability as P3HT but would be more resistant to deformation.

# 3. Conclusions

We investigated the optoelectronic and photophysical properties of a series of poly[3-(carboxyalkyl)thiophenes] with alkyl length, $_ x$ ranging from 3-6, and it was determined that absorption coefficients and band offsets were appropriate for incorporation into bulk heterojunction photovoltaics with PCBM. OPV devices were fabricated, and the highest power conversion efficiency of $2 . 6 \%$ was observed for the P3CPenT:PCBM film. This particular blend film had significantly larger polymer crystallite size and hole mobility aiding in charge transport through the OPV device. The superior performance of the P3CPenT member of this family of polymers may be the result of a delicate balance between solubility and BHJ morphology resulting from its solid-state properties. We are now attempting to fully understand the organization and assembly of these carboxyfunctionalized polythiophenes to understand and control the bulk heterojunction morphology.

Characterization: Absorbance spectra were measured on an Agilent UV-vis spectrometer, and spectra were normalized to the intensity of $\lambda _ { \mathrm { { m a x - a b s } } }$ . Solution photoluminescence (PL) spectra were recorded with a Photon Technology International fluorescence spectrophotometer excited at $5 0 0 \ \mathsf { n m }$ with a 75 W Xe lamp. The thickness of the film was measured relative to silicon substrates, where part of the film had been removed with a razor to create a step. Films were analyzed using $1 0 \times$ lens with fine control set to $0 . 4 \times$ . Three scans were collected and averaged using a vertical scan range of $2 \ \mu \mathsf { m }$ and the resulting surface profile was averaged along a $0 . 5 ~ \mathsf { m m }$ wide line perpendicular to the flm edge. Absorption coefficients were measured using a Perkin-Elmer NIR-UV Spectrophotometer on P3CATs spin-coated on fused silica substrates. Spectra were measured in transmittance mode and corrected for transmission losses in the silica substrates. The measurement was performed at normal incidence to minimize reflectance and scattering Iosses. The absorption coefficients were then calculated from $\alpha = - | { \boldsymbol { \mathsf { n } } } T / L$ where L is the P3cAT film thickness, $T$ is transmittance and $\alpha ,$ the absorption coefficient.

Solid-state Photoluminescence Spectroscopy: Solid-state photoluminescence spectroscopy was performed at room temperature, excited at $4 4 2 \ n m$ with a He-Cd laser. Irradiance was filtered with a $4 7 5 \ \mathsf { n m }$ light pass filter and detected using a USB2000 Ocean Optics charge coupled detector (range $3 0 0 { - } 7 0 5 0 ~ \mathsf { n m }$ . The spectral response was normalized using a standard blackbody radiator with a dark spectrum subtracted from all spectra with an integration time of $5 0 0 ~ \mathsf { m s }$ except for the P3CHexT and P3CHexT:PCBM flms, which were measured with an integration time of $1 0 0 ~ \mathsf { m s }$ . To account for differences in film thickness between the polymeronly and combined flms, the polymer-only absorbance spectra were normalized to the combined BHJ flm and the thickness normalization factors were applied to PL spectra of P3CAT-only films.

Cyclic Voltammetry: Cyclic voltammetry was performed with a Princeton Applied Research Potentiostat model 2273 in a standard three-electrode electrochemical cell with all potentials referenced to $\mathsf { A g / A g ^ { + } }$ electrode. A Pt counter electrode was used, and the working electrode was a Pt disc $( 0 . 0 7 ] \ c m ^ { 2 }$ coated with the dried flm of interest. The electrolyte solution contained $0 . 1 \ m o l \ L ^ { - 1 }$ tetra(n-butyl)ammonium hexafluorophosphate $( \mathsf { n B u } _ { 4 } \mathsf { N P F } _ { 6 } )$ in acetonitrile. All scans were performed at a scan rate of $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { m V / s }$ , and the reference potential was calibrated to a ferrocene redox couple.

All acquisitions were obtained using 32 scans with $4 ~ { \mathsf { c m } } ^ { - 1 }$ resolution. KBr pellets of P3CAT powder were prepared by grinding KBr and the P3CAT and forming a pellet with a press. Solution FTIR samples were prepared by dropcasting a solution of either P3CAT-only or P3CAT:PCBM from the optimized solvent composition (vide infra) between two KBr pellets before the solvent evaporated. FTIR flms were prepared by drying the aforementioned FTIR solution samples.

Peaks in the X-ray diffraction pattern were identified in terms of the Bragg angle, 20, and calibrated to a $\mathsf { L a B } _ { 6 }$ NIST standard (SRM-660b).
