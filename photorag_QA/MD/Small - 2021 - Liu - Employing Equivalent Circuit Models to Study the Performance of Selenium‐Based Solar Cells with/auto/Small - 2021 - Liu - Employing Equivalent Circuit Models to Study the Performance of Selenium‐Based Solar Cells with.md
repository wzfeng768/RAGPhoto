# Employing Equivalent Circuit Models to Study the Performance of Selenium-Based Solar Cells with Polymers as Hole Transport Layers

Wenbo Liu, Fei Yu, Weijun Fan, Wei-shi Li, and Qichun Zhang\*

Selenium(Se)-based solar cells (SsCs), known as one of the oldest solar cells, have regained intense attention due to the advantages of Se including direct bandgap, good stability, and single absorber. Among all kinds of device structures, conventional n-i-p SsCs with top organic hole transport layers (HTLs) show great potential since organic HTLs could be welldesigned to smoothly extract holes from the Se single absorber and protect the Se surface. However, till now, the performance of Se solar cells with organic HTLs is not as good as expected. To address this issue, herein, the SSCs are first presented with organic polymers as the HTLs with the improved efficiency up to $4 . 3 \%$ , which is the highest one in organic HTLsbased SsCs. Additionally, comparing with perovskite solar cells, it is found that the recombination process is the key factor that influences the performance of ssCs. It is believed that the further optimization of the Se active layer and the design of new and suitable organic HTLs for SSCs should be the main focus to suppress the undesired recombination processes of Se flms. Such realization would boost the efficiency of the as-fabricated SSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Inspired by the discovery in the photoconductivity of Se in 1873[1] and the first demonstration of selenium-based solar cell (ssC) in 1883,2] a large number of light absorbers as active elements in solar cells have been developed. For example, commercialized crystalline Si-based photovoltaics has been widely used all over the world,3] while Copper-Indium-GalliumSelenide (CIGS) or Copper-Zinc-SeleniumSulfur (CZTS) based materials show great potential in tandem solar cells, which have been demonstrated to utilize natural sunlight in different wavelength ranges.[46] Dyes for dye-sensitized solar cells, organic compounds, and perovskite-based photovoltaic materials have also been proven to provide various possibilities to design and fabricate solar cells.7-12] However, compared to the above-mentioned devices, the development of SSCs is very slow. The possible reasons include the difficulty in modifying the Se films and the ambiguous carrier dynamics of devices.13] In recent years, several attempts have been conducted to resolve these problems. For example, the Cadmium Selenide or Tellurium (Te) discontinuous layers have been found to assist the adhesion and the crystallization of Se films on the substrate,[14,15] where the uniform and welloriented polycrystalline trigonal Se(t-Se) could be achieved.[13,16] Moreover, different fabrication methods including spin-coating way, thermal evaporation processing, and melting technology have been also reported to improve the qualities of Se films as well as to ensure the adoption of Se films under different situations.17-19] Among these methods, thermal evaporation technology has become the major way to fabricate the photoactive trigonal Se (t-Se) films.13,20,21] Especially, the gradient thermal evaporation method has been found to efficiently improve the performance of SsCs.[22] Although the fabrication methods are relatively mature, the generation/recombination, extraction, and transport of photo-induced carriers in the as-fabricated devices are not sufficiently explored, which weakens the theoretical establishment to further improve the performance of SsCs.[13] This gap strongly encourages us to adopt two different equivalent circuit models in this research and explore the electrical properties and carrier recombination processes of SsCs.

At the early stage of the development of SsCs, the structures of ssCs are usually the configuration of Indium Tin Oxide (ITO)/electron transport layer (ETL)/Se/electrode, which does not contain any HTLs.[23] In recent years, multiple-layered device structures containing HTLs designed for perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have been adopted in the SSCs.24-26] For example, Haight et al. reported the usage of molybdenum oxide $( \mathrm { M o O } _ { 3 } )$ as the HTLs and achieved the highest efficiency of ssCs with all inorganic carrier transport layers.22] Recently, organic materials, which could be used as the donors in the organic solar cells or HTLs in the PSCs have also been employed in the SSCs.[27-30] It is well known that organic materials have several advantages such as the diversity of molecular structures and easily modified electrical and optical properties.[31-33] $\operatorname { I n } 2 0 1 6$ , Kanatzidis et al. reported that poly[bis(4-phenyl)(2,5,6-trimethylphenyl)amine (PTAA) could be used as HTLs in SSCs with an efficiency of more than $3 . 5 \%$ [17] Meanwhile, poly(3-hexylthiophene-2,5-diyl) (P3HT)-based SSCs were also demonstrated as the HTL in the SSCs.20] These results might suggest that applying the organic materials as the carrier transport layers could be the solution to address the low-efficiency issue of SSCs.

Because diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP)-based polymers have several advantages including high mobilities, good stabilities, and good ability to extract holes,[34,35] they have been widely used as the donors in organic solar cells or as the HTLs in PSCs.34,36-40] For example, in 2016, Brabec et al. used the DPPbased polymers as HTLs in the PSCs and achieved an efficiency of more than $20 \%$ [40] Our group also adopted DPP-based polymers as the HTLs in PSCs.[41] Based on our experience and the advantages of DPP-based polymers, in this research, we believe that the DPP-based polymers would be an ideal top HTL in the SSCs, where such a configuration could allow us to carefully investigate the negative factors that reduce the performance of SSCs through two equivalent circuit models.

Two DPP-based polymers P5NH and P25NH have been used as the HTLs of the SSCs. The chemical structures of P5NH and P25NH are provided in Figure 1. The well-matched energy levels of P5NH and P25NH with Se active layer and the proper morphology of P5NH and P25NH on the Se substrate is beneficial for achieving high efficiency. Compared with that of P25NHbased SSCs, the higher mobility of P5NH leads to better efficiency $( 4 . 3 \% )$ , which is the highest efficiency among all reported SSCs with organic HTLs (Table S1, Supporting Information). The electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIs) characterization and detailed analysis of the current density-voltage $\left( J - W \right)$ curves of PSCs and SSCs under dark have been carefully discussed with the assistance of two equivalent circuit models. Our results reveal that the optimization of the Se layer to decrease the carrier recombination in the Se film and the design of the suitable organic HTLs to suppress the surface recombination could be promising directions to boost the efficiency of SSCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

Figures 1a,b display the chemical structures of P5NH and P25NH and Figure 1c shows the energy level diagram of each material used in the SsCs. The energy level data of all materials are achieved from the reported works.[16,41] The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) of the P5NH $( - 5 . 4 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ and P25NH $( - 5 . 1 8 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ are much higher than the valence band maximum of Se film $( - 5 . 6 4 ~ \mathrm { { \ e V } ) }$ , indicating that both P5NH and P25NH could sufficiently extract the holes from the Se films without any energy barriers. On the other hand, the conducting band minimum $( - 3 . 8 \ \mathrm { \ e V } )$ of the Se layer is much lower than the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of P5NH $( - 3 . 4 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ and $\mathrm { P } 2 5 \mathrm { N H }$ $( - 3 . 5 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ , implying that there exists an energy barrier effectively preventing the diffusion of electrons into these polymer HTLs and blocking the carrier recombination. Figure S1, Supporting Information shows the absorption of P5NH and $\mathrm { P } 2 5 \mathrm { N H }$ films, which show slight differences. The molecular weight and elemental analysis table reported by Gel permeation chromatography of these two polymers have been provided in Table S2, Supporting Information.

The ETL is $\mathrm { S n O } _ { 2 }$ with the advantages of high mobility and good stability, which has been proven to be a suitable ETL for SSCs.42-44] The discontinuous Te layer has been demonstrated to improve the adhesion of the following Se layers,[22] which was thermally evaporated on the $\mathrm { S n O } _ { 2 }$ substrate. After a gradient annealing treatment, the polycrystalline t-Se films would form.16] The HTLs adopted here are P5NH and $\mathrm { P } 2 5 \mathrm { N H }$ with the optimized concentration of $6 ~ \mathrm { m g ~ m L ^ { - 1 } }$ and the electrode is Ag. Figure 2b shows the $J { - } V$ curves of P5NH- and P25NH-based SSCs with the best performance. Table 1 summarizes the parameters of SsCs with these two different polymers as HTLs. The P5NH-based device shows a short circuit current $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ of $8 . 9 9 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , an open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ of $8 5 8 . 3 \ \mathrm { m V } ,$ a fill factor of $5 6 . 0 \%$ , and a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of $4 . 3 \%$ . The P25NH-based device exhibits a $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $8 . 1 2 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $7 3 6 . 3 ~ \mathrm { m V } ,$ and fill factor of $5 7 . 6 \%$ , and a PCE of $3 . 4 \%$ The overall performance of fifteen P25NH-based devices is much lower than that of P5NHbased SSCs (Figure 2e-h) and is similar to P3HT-based SSCs reported in our previous work.[45] The steady-state photocurrents (Figure 2c) of SSCs with P5NH and P25NH HTLs under continuous simulated AM1.5G 1-sun illumination are matched well with $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ (Figure 2e). In Figure S2, Supporting Information, both P5NH or P25NH-based devices with the glass/ITO/ $\mathrm { S n O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { P } 5 \mathrm { N H }$ (or P25NH)/Ag structure show very low EQE and the integrated $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ for P5NH or P25NH-based devices is close to zero, which proves that the current contribution from the HTM has a slight influence on the photogenerated current of P5NH or P25NH-based SSCs. The reason for the higher performance of P5NH-based SSCs has been further explored by the dark current measurement. Dark saturation current density $J _ { 0 }$ can be determined by the intercept at zero voltage of the linear regression from the semi-logarithmic $J { - } V$ plot in the region close to the diode turn-on voltage (which would be calculated in the below part).[46] High $J _ { 0 }$ indicated a high leakage current density and significant carrier recombination outside the depletion region, which would negatively affect the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ [46] From Figure 2d, $J _ { 0 }$ of P25NH-based SSCs could be estimated to be much higher than that of P5NH-based SSC, proving that the higher $J _ { 0 }$ is one of the reasons leading to lower $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and fill factor. The device performance distributions of fifteen devices have been shown in Figure 2e-h. The $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of P5NH-based SSCs is higher than that of P25NH-based SSCs.

The morphologies of $\mathrm { I T O / S n O _ { 2 } / T e / S e }$ with/without HTLs have been studied by the X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern measurement. The solid forms of Se could be generally classified into amorphous states (red amorphous, black amorphous, and vitreous Se) and crystalline forms ( $\alpha$ monoclinic, $\beta$ monoclinic, ymonoclinic, and trigonal).[13] Trigonal Se (t-Se), whose structure contains helical chains arranged in a hexagonal array, is the only thermodynamically stable crystalline semiconductor.[16] In Figure 3 and Figure S3, Supporting Information, the XRD patterns of Se film with/without HTLs on the IT $\mathrm { O } / \mathrm { S n O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { T e }$ substrate show the typical features of t-Se films with the peaks locating at $2 3 . 5 ^ { \circ } ( 1 0 0 )$ $2 9 . 7 ^ { \circ } ( 1 0 1 )$ $4 1 . 3 6 ^ { \circ } ( 1 1 0 )$ $4 5 . 4 0 ^ { \circ } ( 1 1 1 )$ and $5 1 . 7 6 ^ { \circ } ( 2 0 1 )$ .[16] By comparing the XRD patterns of these three samples shown in Figure 3, no obvious differences have been found, indicating that the P5NH and P25NH films have little influence on the crystallinity of Se films and would not change the morphology of Se films physically. Another point that should be noticed is that no intense peaks of P5NH and P25NH films have been found, implying that the P5NH and P5NH films are amorphous.

The morphology of Se films and HTLs has been further explored by atomic force microscope (AFM) images. As shown in Figures 4a,b, the Se surface displays a typical polycrystalline surface with distinguished large grains and grain boundaries. The large grains and low density of grain boundaries exhibit the high qualities of crystalized Se films. From the 3D AFM images in Figures 4e,f, the P25NH and P5NH surfaces exhibit evenly distributed small islands, confirming that both HTLs could evenly cover the Se substrate. The root mean square (RMS) roughness of the pristine Se surface (Figure 4a) is $2 9 . 6 2 9 \ \mathrm { n m }$ while the RMS roughness of P5NH and P25NH covered on the Se substrate are $2 0 . 5 9 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure 4b) and $2 1 . 1 7 1 \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure 4c), respectively, indicating that both P5NH and P25NH could smooth the Se surface and form a compact contact with the electrode. The morphologies of P5NH film or P25NH film covered on the Se substrate are smooth and uniform, which would lead to good electrical contact. The results of XRD and AFM imply that the P5NH and $\mathrm { P } 2 5 \mathrm { N H }$ have little influence on the morphology of Se films and show similar roughness on the Se substrate. Therefore, morphological factors would not lead to the performance disparities of SSCs with P5NH or P25NH HTLs.

Electrical impedance spectroscopy (EIs) measurement has also been conducted to explore the electrical properties of SSCs. In Figure 5 and Table 2, the series resistance R1 of P5NH-based SSC $( 1 7 . 2 3 \ \Omega )$ is similar to that of P25NH-based SSCs (18.27 Q), indicating that the series resistances show slight differences in these two kinds of HTLs. Figure 5b shows the changes of the recombination resistance R2 with the varying applied voltage, where the recombination resistance R2 of P5NH-based SSCs is an order of magnitude higher than that of P25NH-based SSCs. Since the only difference between these two kinds of SSCs is HTL, the result suggests that P5NH could strongly suppress the Se surface recombination, leading to the higher $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of P5NH-based device.47] In our previous works, we have found that the amino group of P25NH and P5NH could passivate the perovskite surface, and due to the high ratio of amino groups, P25NH shows better abilities of passivating the perovskite surface and higher recombination resistance (Table S3, Supporting Information).[41] But here, P5NH-based SSCs show higher recombination resistance, therefore, the amino group may not be able to stabilize the lone pair of electrons on the Se surface. The suppressed surface recombination rate could be due to the higher mobility of P5NH $( 5 . 1 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 2 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ : ( \mathrm { V } { \cdot } \mathrm { s } ) ^ { - 1 } )$ (Table S4, Supporting Information).[48] When compared with the EIS parameters of PSCs with the similar structure shown in Table S3, Supporting Information, the recombination resistance of PSCs with P5NH or $\mathrm { P } 2 5 \mathrm { N H }$ HTLs is much larger than that of SSCs with P5NH or P25NH, leading to higher $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of PSCs with P5NH or P25NH. Therefore, the main reason for the low $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of SSCs could be the lower recombination resistance.

Both P5NH and P25NH possess the matched LUMO and HOMO with Se active layer, which would benefit the carrier transport. The XRD pattern (Figure 5) clearly indicates that P5NH and P25NH films on the Se substrate are amorphous and have a slight influence on the Se films. From the AFM images, one could find out that both P5NH and P25NH could smooth the Se surface and evenly cover the Se substrate. The well-matched LUMO and HOMO and the proper morphology of P5NH and P25NH on the Se substrate could explain their better performance than previously reported works but could not explain the reason to cause the performance differences of ssCs based on these two polymers. From analyzing the two equivalent models mentioned above, one could find that smaller recombination losses of photogenerated carriers are the reason for the better performance of P5NH-based SSCs. The difference between P5NH and P25NH is the ratio between two repeat units of the DPP mainchains, which results in the difference in mobilities.[52] By comparing the mobility data shown in Table S4, Supporting Information, the reason for smaller recombination losses of P5NH-based SSCs is attributed to the high mobility of P5NH.[48]

# 3. Conclusion

In this work, P5NH- and P25NH-based SSCs have been presented. With the advantages of matched energy levels with

Se films, proper morphologies on the Se substrate, and high mobility, the efficiency of P5NH-based SSCs has been pushed to be $4 . 3 \%$ . Through analyzing two equivalent circuit models, we could find out that compared with P25NH-based SSCs, the suppressed recombination processes of P5NH-based SSCs, due to better mobility of P5NH, is the main reason for the higher performance of SSCs with P5NH HTL. Designing and adopting suitable HTLs is very important for achieving SSCs with high PCE. Through our discussion on the comparison with similar perovskite solar cells, we found that suppressing the carrier recombination of Se films could be an effective way to improve the performance of ssCs. Our work provides a revealing insight into the carrier dynamics of the SsCs and proposes possible ways to further improve the efficiency of SSC.
