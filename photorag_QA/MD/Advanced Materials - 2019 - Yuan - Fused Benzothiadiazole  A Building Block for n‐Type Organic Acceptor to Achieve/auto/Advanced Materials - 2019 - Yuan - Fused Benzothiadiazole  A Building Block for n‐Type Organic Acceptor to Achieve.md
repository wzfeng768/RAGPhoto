# Fused Benzothiadiazole: A Building Block for n-Type Organic Acceptor to Achieve High-Performance Organic Solar Cells

Jun Yuan, Yunqiang Zhang, Liuyang Zhou, Chujun Zhang, Tsz-Ki Lau, Guichuan Zhang, Xinhui Lu, Hin-Lap Yip, Shu Kong So, Serge Beaupre, Mathieu Mainville, Paul A. Johnson, Mario Leclerc, Honggang Chen, Hongjian Peng, Yongfang Li,\* and Yingping Zou\*

Narrow bandgap n-type organic semiconductors (n-Os) have attracted great attention in recent years as acceptors in organic solar cells (OsCs), due to their easily tuned absorption and electronic energy levels in comparison with fullerene acceptors. Herein, a new n-OS acceptor, Y5, with an electron-deficient-core-based fused structure is designed and synthesized, which exhibits a strong absorption in the $6 0 0 { - } 9 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ region with an extinction coefficient of $\mathsf { 1 } . 2 4 \times \mathsf { 1 0 } ^ { 5 } \mathsf { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ , and an electron mobility of $2 . 7 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathsf { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathsf { V } ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 }$ . By blending Y5 with three types of common mediumbandgap polymers (J61, PBDB-T, and TTFQx-T1) as donors, all devices exhibit high short-circuit current densities over $2 0 \mathsf { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . As a result, the power conversion efficiency of the Y5-based OSCs with J61, TTFQx-T1, and PBDB-T reaches $1 1 . 0 \%$ $1 3 . 1 \%$ , and $1 4 . 1 \%$ , respectively. This indicates that Y5 is a universal and highly efficient n-OS acceptor for applications in organic solar cells.

The performance of organic solar cells (OsCs) has rapidly improved over the past few years. $[ 1 - 7 ]$ Recent materials design studies have primarily focused on developing high-quality n-type organic semiconductors (n-Os), named nonfullerene acceptors (NFAs), which pair with commercially available donor polymers.8-17] These polymer:NFA solar cells have achieved power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) over $1 3 \%$ in singlejunction devices.18-20] The most significant advantages of NFAs are: i) strong absorption in the visible-near-infrared region, ii) easily adjustable energy levels, and iii) tunable crystallinity to control active layer morphology. Typically, acceptor-donor-acceptor (A-D-A) type NFAs featuring a bulky multifused electron-rich core flanked with two electron-deficient moieties have shown great efficiencies.21-23] Moreover, Yan et al. reported a pair of donor and acceptor materials, named P3TEA and $\mathrm { S F } { \cdot } \mathrm { P D I } _ { 2 }$ which have a negligible driving force yet exhibit nearly $90 \%$ internal quantum efficiency (IQE).[24] As NFA-based OSCs have demonstrated high quantum efficiency with negligible driving energies, this opens a new door into the molecular design of high-performance NFAs for OSCs.

were specially designed and synthesized for photovoltaics.

Sellinger's group reported a series of n-OS acceptors based on a central BT moiety flanked with vinylimides (PI-BT and NI-BT). By combining a common polymer donor, P3HT, the PI-BT and NI-BT-based OSCs exhibit low PCEs (less than $3 \%$ ) due to their poor short-circuit density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ values (4.7 and $0 . 5 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ respectively), arising from rather low external quantum efficiencies (EQEs).33] McCulloch and co-workers employed BT as a $\pi$ bridge to extend the NFA's conjugation, which can improve the charge transport and afford electron-deficient character outside of the target molecule (IDTBR). As a result, the device based on IDTBR:P3HT showed a PCE of $4 . 1 1 \%$ [37] Later, this group demonstrated that IDTBR in ternary solar cells can yield an efficiency of $1 1 \%$ [38] Compared with ITIC (indacenodithiophene-based NFA; Figure 1a), the BT-based NFAs still do not show satisfactory PCEs.

To shed light on the potential of the BT unit to develop new NFAs for OsC applications, we considered herein introducing BT unit into central core to form an electron-deficient-corebased fused structure (DAD) for enhancing the performance in devices. By using DAD fused core one could also adopt a relatively planar structure rather than a twisted one and hence facilitate the electron migration from the donor to acceptor since the planarized DAD enhances the delocalization of $\pi$ electrons.39] The new ladder-type DAD fused core together with 1,1-dicyanomethylene-3-indanone (INCN), named Y5 (Figure 1b), possesses a narrow bandgap of $1 . 3 8 \ \mathrm { e V } .$ Synthesis of Y5, (2,2'-((2Z,2'Z)-((12,13-bis(2-ethylhexyl)-3,9-diundecyl-12,13-dihydro[1,2,5]thiadiazolo[3,4e]thieno[2",3"':4',5'] thieno[2',3':4,5]pyrrolo[3,2-g] thieno[2,3':4,5]thieno[3,2-b]- indole-2,10-diyl)bis(methanylylidene))bis(3-oxo-2,3-dihydro1H-indene-2,1-diylidene))dimalononitrile), was carried out through a Knoevenagel condensation reaction by introducing a ladder-type electron-deficient-core-based central fused ring (TPBT) and INCN as end-cap group with a high yield of over $8 8 \%$ . The detailed synthetic processes for the compounds are provided in the Supporting Information. The structure of the target small molecular acceptor Y5 was confirmed by nuclear magnetic resonance spectra and matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time of flight (MALDI-TOF) mass spectrometry (Figures S1-S3, Supporting Information). Thermal properties of Y5 were determined by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DsC). As shown in the TGA plot in Figure S4 (Supporting Information), the onset temperature with $5 \%$ weight loss of Y5 was $3 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , which indicates good thermal stability for the application in OsCs. DSC plot of Y5 in Figure S4 (Supporting Information) shows clear melting temperature $( T _ { \mathrm { m } } )$ at $3 3 1 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and recrystallization temperature $( T _ { \mathrm { c } } )$ at $3 0 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , indicating clear crystalline nature of the nonfullerene acceptor.

Visible-near-infrared absorption spectra of Y5 in solution (chloroform) and in thin film at room temperature are shown in Figure 1c. In dilute chloroform solution, Y5 shows a strong absorption from 600 to $7 8 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ with the maximum absorption wavelength at $7 1 8 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . A $6 5 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ redshift can be observed from solution to film state, signifying that much stronger $\pi - \pi$ intermolecular interactions are formed in the Y5 film. The absorption onset of the Y5 film is located at $9 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } = 1 . 3 8 \ \mathrm { e V } _ { \cdot }$ with an absorption coefficient of $1 . 2 4 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ , a redshift of $1 2 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ relative to that of ITIC (Figure S5, Supporting Information). The redshift of the absorption spectrum may be explained by the fused DAD core replacing the fused D core, which increases intramolecular electronic interactions.

The corresponding absorption properties and electronic energy levels of Y5 are summarized in Table S1 (Supporting Information).

From grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) analysis of Y5 in neat film (Figure 1e), Y5 shows a preferred face-on orientation with a strong $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak in the out-of-plane (OOp) direction at $q = 1 . 7 3 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ $( d \approx 3 . 6 3$ A). Interestingly, there exist two diffraction peaks in the in-plane (IP) direction at $q = 0 . 2 9 0 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ $( d \ \approx \ 2 1 . 5 \ \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } }$ and $0 . 4 3 0 \mathring { \mathrm { ~ A ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ $( d ~ \overset { \cdot } { \approx } ~ 1 4 . 6 ~ \overset { \cdot } { \mathrm { A } } )$ , suggesting the co-existence of two distinct structure orders (Figure S6, Supporting Information).[40] Such structure orders are beneficial to charge transport. As a result, the electron mobility $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ of Y5 is $2 . 1 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ with an average value of $1 . 7 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ (measured by the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method) (Figure S7, Supporting Information).

All calculations were carried out with Gaussian 16 revision B.01.[42] To facilitate the DFT calculations, alkyl side chains on the thiophene units were reduced to ${ \mathrm { - C H } } _ { 3 }$ groups but the entire 2-ethylhexyl side chains on the nitrogen atoms were included. The HOMO is principally concentrated on the central donor unit, while the LUMO is less dense on the central unit, extending over the end groups. As shown in Figure S8b,c (top and side views, respectively) of the Supporting Information, the acceptor consists of two planar units, with a twist in the center due to the $N _ { ☉ }$ tethered alkyl groups lying above and below the central donor unit. The DFT geometry of Y5 is reported in Table S5 (Supporting Information). In principle, successful NFAs should match with different polymer donor structures and lead to excellent photovoltaic performance. However, many reports on high-performance NFA-based OsCs focus on one specific D-A structured polymer donor. Here, we first selected three common different medium-bandgap polymer donors based on different A-units: dithienobenzotriazole, benzodithiophene-4,8-dione, and fluorinequinoxaline, named J61, PBDB-T, and T'TFQx-T1, to blend with Y5 (see Figure 2a). The reported PCEs in the literature of J61, PBDB-T, and TTFQx-T1 blended with ITIC acceptors are $9 . 5 3 \%$ [43] $1 1 . 2 1 \%$ [44] and $1 0 . 5 2 \%$ [45] respectively. As shown in Figure 2b, the optical band gaps of the three polymers are 1.93, 1.82, and $1 . 7 0 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ respectively, which have well matched complementary absorptions with the n-OS acceptor Y5. Energy diagrams of the three types of polymer donors and the acceptor Y5 used in the photovoltaic devices are shown in Figure 2c. It can be seen that all polymers have similar HOMO energy levels, which suggests the devices have analogous $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ values.

In order to investigate the scope of Y5 in OSC applications, the conventional configuration of ITO (indium tin oxide)/ PEDOT:PSS (poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate)/active layer/perylene diimide functionalized with amino N-oxide/Al was fabricated based on the three types of polymer donors. Current density versus voltage $\left( J - W \right)$ curves and the corresponding photovoltaic data of the optimized devices under illumination of AM 1.5G, $1 0 0  { \mathrm { ~ m w ~ } }  { \mathrm { c m } } ^ { - 2 }$ are shown in Figure 2d and Table 1. The J61-based device processed with $0 . 5 ~ \mathrm { v o l } \%$ chloronaphthalene (CN) solvent additives and annealing at $1 1 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ exhibits a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 9 0 \mathrm { V } ,$ $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 1 . 1 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and an fill factor (FF) of $5 8 . 3 \%$ , corresponding to PCE of $1 1 . 0 \%$ . The TTFQx-T1-based device with solvent additive and annealing shows a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 8 9 \mathrm { V } ,$ $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 1 . 2 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and an FF of $6 9 . 6 \%$ , leading to PCE of $1 3 . 1 \%$ . The PBDB-Tbased device at optimum condition exhibits a highest PCE of $1 4 . 1 \%$ $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } = 0 . 8 8 \mathrm { V } , J _ { \mathrm { s c } } = 2 2 . 8 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and $\mathrm { F F } = 7 0 . 2 \% )$ , compared to the devices of J61 and TTFQx-T1 as donor polymer. It is notable that the PCEs of $1 3 . 1 \%$ and $1 4 . 1 \%$ are some of the highest values reported in the literatures to date for T'TFQx-T1 and PBDB-T-based OSCs. The inverted devices based on $\mathrm { I T O } /$ $\mathrm { Z n O / P B D B  – T : Y 5 / M o O _ { 3 } / A g }$ configuration were measured by South China University of Technology (SCUT). The optimized device of PBDB-T:Y5 also exhibits a PCE over $14 \%$ with $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of $0 . 8 7 \mathrm { V } ,$ $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 2 . 6 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and an FF of $7 1 . 4 \%$ (Figure S9a, Supporting Information). Meanwhile, we also selected the lowbandgap polymer PTB7 $\mathrm { \cdot } \mathrm { T h } ^ { [ 4 6 ] }$ and another medium bandgap polymer PPDT2FBT[47,48] as donors to further investigate the universality of Y5 in OSC application. As shown in Figure S10 and Table S2 (Supporting Information), the PCEs of the OSCs based on PTB7-Th and PPDT2FBT blended with Y5 acceptor are $1 0 . 6 \%$ and $1 1 . 3 \%$ ,respectively. The results further confirm that Y5 is a universal and efficient n-OS acceptor for OSC applications.

The corresponding EQE spectra of the devices, as shown in Figure 2e, display a high and broad photoresponse of the three devices from 300 to $9 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . The EQE response of the PBDBT:Y5-based OSCs is stronger than those of the TTFQx-T1:Y5- based and J61:Y5-based devices in the region of $6 1 0 { - } 8 1 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ resulting in a higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ value. The calculated $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ values integrated from the EQE spectra are 20.2, 20.6, and $2 2 . 1 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for the J61, T'TFQx-T1, and PBDB-T-based OSCs, respectively, which is in good agreement with that measured from the $J { - } V$ curves (within $5 \%$ mismatch). The IQE of the PBDB-T-based inverted devices has been calculated based on reflection and EQE shown in Figure S9b (Supporting Information). The maximum IQE plateau reaches $80 \%$ from 550 to $8 2 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , suggesting the effective charge carriers collection at the electrodes.

In considering the photovoltaic performance of the PBDBT:Y5-based OSCs with PCE over $14 \%$ , the PBDB-T:Y5 system has been chosen to further investigate the structure-property relationships. We explored three aspects in detail to clarify the observed high photovoltaic performance: (i) charge transport efficiency, (ii) molecular packing orientation, and (iii) blend film morphology.

Light intensity $( P )$ dependence of photovoltaic performance was further explored to understand the geminate recombination behavior in the devices. In general, the relationship between $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ could be expressed as $\begin{array} { r } { J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \propto \ : P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } ^ { \mathrm { } } { } ^ { \alpha } } \end{array}$ where the powerlaw component $( \alpha )$ value approaches unity when bimolecular recombination is negligible.[52] The $\alpha$ value of optimized film (0.97) was closer to unity than that of the as-cast film (0.95) (Figure S11c, Supporting Information). Although the difference was not significant, the results showed that bimolecular recombination was suppressed more effectively in the optimized films. Figure S11d (Supporting Information) shows the semilogarithmic plot of relationship between $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } .$ In particular, by following theoretical considerations, $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ linearly depends on ln $P$ with a slope of $n k T / q$ $( 1 < n < 2 )$ , where $k$ is Boltzmann's constant, $q$ is elementary charge, $n$ is a scaling factor, and $T$ is Kelvin temperature.] In our cases, the slopes for the as-cast device and the optimized device were $1 . 0 1 ~ k T / q$ and $1 . 0 5 ~ k T / q$ respectively. These results indicate that the bimolecular recombination is dominant in the devices. In addition, we measured the photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ versus the effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ to evaluate the process of exciton generation, dissociation, and charge collection efficiency.[53] As shown in Figure S11e (Supporting Information), under short-circuit conditions, the exciton dissociation probability $( P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } = J _ { \mathrm { p h } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } ,$ where $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ stands for saturation photocurrent density) in the optimized device is higher $( 9 5 . 9 \% )$ than that of the as-cast device $( 9 4 . 5 \% )$ . The increased $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ value indicates that more efficient exciton dissociation and charge collection occur in the optimized device. The corresponding data of the PBDB-T:Y5 OSC are summarized in Tables S3 and S4 (Supporting Information).

The thin film nanostructure of BHJ blend has significant effects on the charge transportation and OsC performance. GIWAXS was used to investigate the structure order of BHJ thin films before and after device optimization. The patterns and corresponding intensity profiles in the OOP and IP directions are presented in Figure 4a,b and Figure S12 (Supporting Information). The $\pi - \pi$ stacking in the OOP direction of active layer, usually defined as face-on orientation, is more favorable for charge transport between the top and bottom electrodes of OsCs. As shown in Figure 4a,b, both blend films show a preferred face-on orientation with $\pi - \pi$ stacking in the OOP direction. Compared with as-cast film, optimized film exhibits much sharper $\pi - \pi$ stacking and the $d$ spacing at $3 . 6 3 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ( q = 1 . 7 3 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 } )$ . A well-developed (100) peak was seen for the optimized film with a packing distance ${ \approx } 2 0 . 9$ A $( q = 0 . 3 0 0 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 } )$ with higher intensity and full width at half maxima than that of the as-cast film, indicating a higher crystallinity.

Figure 4c-f shows surface and bulk morphology of the photoactive layers, measured by atomic force microscopy (AFM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). All the blend films were prepared under the same conditions with the optimized photovoltaic devices. From Figure 4c,d, although both blend film surfaces were quite smooth with low root-mean square roughness $( R _ { \mathrm { q } } )$ , compared to the as-cast film, the optimized film shows slightly lower $R _ { \mathfrak { q } }$ value on a scan area of $5 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } } \times 5 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } }$ . The phase separation was further visualized by TEM. After the additive and annealing treatment (Figure 4f), the TEM image of the blend film is uniformly distributed and shows fiber-like interpenetrating network nanostructures, indicating that PBDB-T and Y5 can be well mixed with suitable phase size, which is beneficial for effective exciton dissociation and charge transport.

In summary, a fused BT-containing NFA (Y5) with A-DADA molecular structure was designed and synthesized, where TPBT and INCN were used as central core and end group, respectively. Y5 exhibits wide and strong absorption covering the range from 600 to $9 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ with peak extinction coefficient of $1 . 2 4 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ , a low optical bandgap of $1 . 3 8 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ suitable energy levels, and preferred face-on orientation as an n-OS acceptor in OsC applications. With three different types of common medium-bandgap polymers (J61, PBDB-T, and TTFQx-T1) selected as the donor polymers, all the OSC devices exhibited high $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ values over $2 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . High PCEs of singlejunction OSCs were achieved at $1 1 . 0 \%$ $1 3 . 1 \%$ , and $1 4 . 1 \%$ for the devices based on J61, TTFQx-T1, and PBDB-T, respectively. This work demonstrates that the fused "BT" unit has tremendous potential as a building block for high-performance NFAs, and Y5 is a universal and efficient n-OS acceptor for OSC applications.
