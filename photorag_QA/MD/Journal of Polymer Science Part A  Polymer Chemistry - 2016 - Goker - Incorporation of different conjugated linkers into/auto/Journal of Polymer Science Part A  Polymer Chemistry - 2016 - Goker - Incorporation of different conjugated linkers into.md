Abstract: Four  new  2,1,3-benzooxadiazole-based  donoracceptor conjugated polymers, namely poly{9-(9-heptadecanyl)-9H-carbazole-alt-5,6-bis(octyloxy)-4,7-di(selenophen-2-yl)- benzo[c][1,2,5]oxadiazole)}(PSBSC), poly{9-(9-heptadecanyl)-9Hcarbazole-alt-5,6-bis(octyloxy)-4,7-di(furan-2-yl)benzo[c][1,2,5]- oxadiazole)}(PFBFC), poly{9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluorene-alt-5,6- bis(octyloxy)-4,7-di(selenophen-2-yl)benzo[c][1,2,5]oxadiazole)} (PSBSFL),  and  poly{9,9-dioctyl-9H-fluorene-alt-5,6-bis(octyloxy)-4,7-di(furan-2-yl)benzo[c][1,2,5]oxadiazole)}(PFBFFL), were synthesized via Stille polycondensation reaction. All polymers were found to be soluble in common organic solvents such as chloroform, tetrahydrofuran, and chlorobenzene. Their structures were verified by $^ 1 \mathsf { H }$ NMR and the molecular weights were determined by gel permeation chromatography (GPC). The polymer films exhibited broad absorption bands. Among all polymers, photovoltaic cells based on the device structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/PSBSC:PC;1BM(1:3, w/w)/LiF/AI revealed an open-circuit voltage of $0 . 6 2 \ \vee ,$ a short circuit current of 7.63 mA $\mathsf { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and a power conversion efficiency of $1 . 8 9 \%$ . This work demonstrates a good example for tuning absorption range, energy level, and photovoltaic properties of the polymers with different spacers and donor units can offer a simple and effective method to improve the efficiency of PSCs. $\circledcirc$ 2016 Wiley Periodicals, Inc. J. Polym. Sci., Part A: Polym. Chem. 2016, 54, 2459-2467

INTRopuction Research on organic photovoltaics has gained immense interest in recent years due to the increasing demand for sustainable clean energy. Bulk-heterojunction (BHJ) solar cells based on interpenetrating networks of electron-donating $\pi$ conjugated polymers and electronaccepting fullerene derivatives promise several advantages such as low cost, low weight, flexibility, and large-area manufacturing compatibility and the ease of materials processing.' Molecular design and device optimization played a significant role for a rapid development in the field of PSCs over the past decade.2,3 One of the most efficient strategies to achieve superior photovoltaic properties is the donoracceptor approach; Combination of electron-rich donor and electron deficient acceptor moieties in the polymer backbone. This strategy provides to modulate the energy levels and absorption properties by controlling the intermolecular charge transfer from the donor to the acceptor. Fluorene, carbazole, and benzodithiophene (BDT) are the frequently used D-type moieties to obtain a high open-circuit voltage in PSCs. Low-band-gap polymers have extended absorption in the longer wavelengths with an increased device efficiency. During the recent years, thieno[3,4-c]pyrrole-4,6-dione (TPD),7-11 diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP),12,13 2,1,3-benzodiathiazole (BT),14 quinoxaline,15 isoindigo (ID),16 1,2,3-benzotriazole (BTz),17,18 2,1,3-benzooxadiazole (B0),19-21 and 2,1,3-benzothiadiazole $\left( \mathrm { B T h } \right) ^ { 2 2 - 2 4 }$ are the most used acceptor moieties for tailoring the band gap of polymers. 2,1,3-benzooxadiazole (BO) is one of the strong electron withdrawing moieties widely used in PSCs due to its planar, rigid geometry, ease of preparation, excellent stability, and optoelectronic characteristics. Due to its capability to adopt the quinoid structure in the polymer, BO-based polymers have low-band gaps and coplanar structures. The good solubility of conducting polymers is very important for purification and high quality film formation in optoelectronic devices. Without alkyl chains, BO-based polymers have low molecular weights and poor solubility, therefore structural modification is required to get high performance active materials for PSCs and polymeric field effect transistors. 5,6-Bisalkoxy-substituted BO-based conjugated polymers reveal better solubility than that of their unsubstituted counterparts.25 As of late, 9,9-dialkylfluorenes are preferred as attractive donor candidates for organic photovoltaics since they possess good processability, high absorption coefficients, and significant $\mathtt { V _ { 0 C } }$ values. N-bridged carbazole moiety is fully aromatic, having high chemical and environmental stability. Poly(N-alkyl-2,7- carbazole) derivatives have been applied successfully in polymer light emitting diodes and organic field-effect transistors, demonstrating good p-type transport properties different from C-bridged fluorene counterpart.26-30 Conjugated linkers remarkably affect the interaction between donor and acceptor units and thus affect the optical, electrochemical, charge transport, and photovoltaic properties of the resulting D $\pi$ A conjugated copolymers. Selenophene lately draws attention as a novel class of donor unit in the conjugated materials. Incorporation of selenium atom in the polymer backbone results in high conductivity and hole mobility.31 Owing to its lower electronegativity and higher polarizability compared to sulfur atom, exchange of sulfur in backbone with selenium atom provides an opportunity to tune the band gap and optoelectronic properties of the polymers.32 In the meantime, furan-based polymers have lower highest occupied molecular orbital (Homo) energy level than thiophene counterparts due to high electronegativity of oxygen compared to sulfur atom. Furan-based polymers synthesized by Wang et al.33 resulted in higher solubility and slight difference in aromaticity. Furan-based co-monomers have better solubility, however the mechanism is not well understood. The difference in atomic radius and electronegativity difference between oxygen and sulfur atoms may affect solvent interactions, intermolecular interactions, and intramolecular steric interactions. Recently, furan-based materials have shown similar optical and electronic properties. Frechet et al. demonstrated that furan heterocyclics can be incorporated into conjugated polymer backbones without hindering their photovoltaic device performance.435 Previously, Cao et. al synthesized carbazole, fluorene, and 2,1,3-benzooxadiazole-based polymers comprising thiophene as the $\pi$ bridge. The polymers had very poor reversibility in cyclic voltammetry studies and the highest PCE for photovoltaic studies was reported as $1 . 3 1 \%$ 21,36 In this study, carbazole and fluorene units were used as the donor and 2,1,3-benzooxadiazole was used as the acceptor building blocks in the polymer chain bridged by different heteroaromatic rings as shown in Scheme 1. Selenophene and furan units were selected as the conjugated linkers. The effects of different conjugated linkers and donor moieties on electrochemical, optical, and photovoltaic properties were investigated.

# Electrochemical Studies

Cyclic voltammetry was used to investigate the electrochemical properties of the selenophene and furan bridged fluorene, carbazole/BO-based  copolymers. The onset oxidation and reduction potentials obtained from single scan cyclic voltammograms were used to determine the HOMO and LUMO energy levels, respectively. p-Doping potential of a polymer is the potential at which the polymer is oxidized at the anodic region and the reverse peak stands for the dedoping process for this case. n-Doping is the reduction peak at the cathodic region and the reverse peak is for the dedoping of this process. The oxidation (doping/dedoping) potentials were $1 . 3 1 \mathrm { ~ V } / 1 . 0 5 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for PSBSC, 1.40 V/0.97 V for PSBSFL, $1 . 1 0 \mathrm { ~ V } / 0 . 9 8 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for PFBFC and $1 . 2 6 \mathrm { ~ V } / 1 . 0 1 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for PFBFFL in the positive potential region. Furan substitution into a polymer backbone compare to selenophene, reduces the oxidation potential since oxygen-containing furan is richer in terms of electrons. Solubility, film morphology, and convenient band gap of the polymer are not the only crucial parameters for solar cell applications. The HOMO and the LUMO energy levels of the conducting polymers play an important role for fabrication of organic solar cell. To understand the electronic structures of the polymers, it is necessary to determine the energy levels of the conjugated polymers. HOMO energy levels were estimated from these values according to the vacuum level (4.75 eV). Electronic band gaps of the polymers were calculated from the difference between HOMO and LUMO energy levels and were found as $2 . 0 4 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for PSBSC, $2 . 1 8 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for PSBSFL, 2.15 eV for PFBFC, and $2 . 0 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for PFBFFL.

Copolymers based on carbazole, fluorene, and BO units were synthesized previously.21 They revealed very poor reversibility for redox processes. Wei and coworkers36 also synthesized copolymers having same donor and acceptor moieties but with different alkyl chains. The polymers possessed irreversible doping/dedoping (reduction/reoxidation) processes in the negative potential range. In this study, selenophene and furan bridges provide copolymers ambipolar (both p- and n-dopable) character. Selenophene has chemical and physical properties similar to those of thiophene; the relatively lower aromaticity of selenophene increases the ground-state quinoid resonance character of its resulting polymers with improved planarity, increased effective conjugation length, and lower band-gap energy.38 Selenophene comprising polymers have a lower band gap than corresponding thiophene-based polymers which were previously synthesized by Wei and coworkers. The electron-stabilizing effect of selenophene moieties due to larger size of Se atom than either an S or O atom resulted in narrowing of the band gap. On the other hand, furan and thiophene have similar energy levels with a comparable degree of aromaticity, hence furan-based copolymers possess also poor stability and similar oxidation potentials. Polymers having lower HOmO energy level can be achieved by introducing furan moiety into the polymer backbone due to high electronegativity of oxygen compare to sulfur atom. Wang et al. synthesized furan-based polymers as an alternative to thiophene-based ones and the results revealed increased solubility and slight difference in aromaticity.33 When furan was introduced to polymer backbone as a $\pi$ bridge, oxidation potential of the polymers was higher than expected hence higher HomO energy levels resulted in low oxidative stability and lower Voc. Higher HOMO energy levels were expected for carbazole-based polymers due to stronger electron donating ability of the N atom than that of C atom. However, HOmO energy levels and band gap of four polymers were very similar to each other.

# Spectroelectrochemical Studies

Spectroelectrochemistry studies were performed to explore the change in the optical properties of conducting polymer. These experiments disclose key properties of conjugated polymers such as band gap (Eg) and the intergap states that appear upon doping process. Spectroscopic measurements were performed in $0 . 1 \textbf { M }$ TBAPF6/acetonitrile (ACN) solvent medium with same electrodes used in electrochemical studies. For in situ spectroelectrochemical studies, spraycoated polymer films were stepwise oxidized and spectral responses were recorded using UV-Vis-NIR spectroscopy. Figure 2 displays the absorption spectra of PSBsc, PSBSFL, PFBFC, and PFBFFL recorded in a monomer free $0 . 1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ ACN/ $\mathrm { T B A P F } _ { 6 }$ solution.
