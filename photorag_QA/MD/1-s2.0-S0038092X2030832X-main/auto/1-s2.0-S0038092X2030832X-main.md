# ABSTRACT

Keywords: Organic solar cells Small molecule acceptors Halogen substituent Photovoltaic performance

Three small molecule acceptors (SMAs), named ZF, ZC and ZB, have been designed and synthesized for organic solar cells (OsCs) by introducing single F, Cl and Br atom on each terminal unit, respectively. The effects of different halogen substituent on molecular aggregation, photophysical and photovoltaic performance have been systematicall studied. Owing to its strong electronegativity of halogens, al the acceptors with monohalogenated terminal units possess red-shifted absorption spectra and deeper frontier energy levels compared to the corresponding acceptor without halogen substituents reported in the literature. Among the SMAs, ZB with brominated terminal units was found to show the higher molar absorption coefficient $( 2 . 3 1 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ , more orderly face-on $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking, higher electron mobility and more favorable morphology when blended with PM6. As a result, the PM6:ZB OSCs yielded a high power conversion efficiency (PCE) of up to $1 5 . 2 3 \%$ with a high shortcircuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ of $2 6 . 3 8 ~ \mathrm { m A ~ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , while the corresponding ZF- and zC-based devices showed the relatively inferior PCEs of $1 3 . 3 6 \%$ and $1 4 . 7 1 \%$ , respectively. These results demonstrated that the modulation of electron-withdrawing halogen substituents on terminal group provides a promising strategy to design and synthesize efficient SMAs for fabricating high-performance OSCs.

# 1. Introduction

In the context of rapidly increasing energy consumption, organic solar cells (OsCs) have attracted considerable attention due to the numerous advantages, such as flexibility and solution processability. To date, new design strategy of non-fullerene small molecule acceptors (SMAs) and device optimization have boosted up photovoltaic performance with the PCEs over $1 6 \%$ for single-junction OsCs (Cui et al., 2019; Fan et al., 2019; Song et al., 2019; Sun, H. et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2019).

Since ITIC based on fused aromatic rings as core was reported by Zhan in 2015 (Lin et al., 2015), OsCs have made remarkable advancements. In the past few years, extensiv,e efforts have been made to ehance the photovoltaic properties by modifying or varying the side chains, the core and terminal groups (Yan et al., 2018). As common sense, branched side chains are considered to be an effective way to endow them with great adjustability of miscibility with donors, intermolecular packing and solution processability (Chang et al., 2018; Dong et al., 2020; Fei et al., 2018; Li et al., 2019; Yan et al., 2017). And the fused aromatic heterocycles, such as the representative IDT (Lin et al., 2016) and 3TT (Li, T. et al., 2018), can efficiently extend the conjugated structure, promote $\pi$ electron delocalization, broaden the light absorption and enhance intermolecular $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking. Very recently, Zou et al. (Jiang et al., 2019; Yuan et al., 2019a; Yuan et al., 2019b) employed A-D-A-DA-type SMAs with an electron-deficient benzodithiazole unit fused with thienylthiophene unit (TPBT) as core and derivatives of indanone as terminal units for OSCs, which largely improved the PCEs of OSCs. Subsequently, Hou et al. (Cui et al., 2019) improved their UV-vis absorption spectra and intermolecular staking by using the same core and adjusting the terminal groups, which further enhanced the PCE, highlighting the importance of the terminal units. Simultaneously, introduction of other terminal groups (Suman and Singh, 2019), such as alkoxy chain substituted indanone (Zhu et al., 2017), benzene or thiophene of INIC (Feng et al., 2017), NINICN (Xie et al., 2017), was also proved to be efficient design strategy to improve the optical and electrochemical properties of SMAs.

In recent years, introduction of halogen substituents has been widely used to design high-performance organic photovoltaic materials owing to the high electronegativity and the positive effect of halogens in enhancing the intermolecular and intramolecular charge transfer, crystallinity and charge mobility (Tang and Bao, 2011; Ye et al., 2019). Furthermore, halogenated terminal groups result in reduced band-gaps, which have been proved to be one of the most effective molecular design strategies in SMAs and drive the field of OsCs to move forward (Yang et al., 2017). In general, the introduction of fluorine (F) atom (Sun et al., 2019; Yuan et al., 2019b; Zhang et al., 2017) can efficaciously regulate the delocalization of $\pi$ electrons without generating large steric hindrance for molecular packing because of the high electronegativity and small size along with the potentially formed multiple noncovalent conformational locks via F...H, F...S etc. (Li et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2017). In comparison, the molecules substituted by chlorine (Cl) possess large dipole moment which is beneficial for charge separation (Lu et al., 2020). Concurrently, more pronounced molecular stacking and prominently broadened absorption could be observed in chlorinated materials due to the enhanced intramolecular charge transfer effect (Cui et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2018). In addition, some brominated SMAs have been studied (Qu et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2018), and the monobrominated Y6 family acceptors with reduced energy loss and upshifted LUMO energy level attract widespread attention (Lai et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2020). Though a few halogenated smallmolecular acceptors have been recently reported, the effects of monohalogenated Y6 family acceptors on molecular aggregation and photoelectric properties have not been systematically studied.

Given the following two considerations, we designed and synthesized three mono-halogenated SMAs ZF, ZC and ZB (Fig. 1) by introducing single F, Cl and Br atom on each terminal unit respectively, and the effects of different halogen substituent on molecular aggregation, photophysical and photovoltaic performances have been carefully studied. On the one hand, compared with non-halogenated sMAs, the absorption spectra and energy levels of the monohalogenated ones could be efficiently tuned by introducing electron-withdrawing halogen substituents onto terminal groups. On the other hand, compared with dihalogen substituted acceptors, monohalogenated sMAs can avoid excessive intermolecular aggregation and obtain suitable microphase separation in active layer (Wan et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2018; Yang et al., 2017). The results demonstrated that all three SMAs have redshifted absorption spectra, deeper frontier energy levels, compared to the reported Y5 with no halogenation (Yuan et al., 2019a). Moreover, ZB showed stronger face-on - stacking, higher molar absorption coefficient and more favorable morphology when blended with PM6 than those of ZF and zC. As a result, the ZB-based device exhibited a remarkably high PCE of $1 5 . 2 3 \%$ .These results indicate that monobrominated substituent on terminal could be a promising approach to develop efficient SMAs for the fabrication of high-performance OSCs.

# 2.2. Optical and electrochemical properties

UV-vis absorption spectra of ZF, ZC and ZB in chloroform solution and thin film are shown in Fig. 2(a, b). In solution, all the molecules show absorption ranging from 500 to $7 8 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . ZF and ZC present the maximum absorption wavelength at 729 and $7 3 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ with the highest molar absorption coefficients of $1 . 8 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ and $1 . 9 8 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { \dot { c } m } ^ { - 1 }$ respectively, while ZB exhibits the maximum absorption wavelength at $7 3 5 \mathrm { n m }$ with the molar absorption coefficient of $2 . 3 \overset { \cdot } { 1 } \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ It is reasonable that bromine atoms suppress the spin prohibition of Q bands in ZB, which results in the highest molar absorption coefficient (Hayashi et al., 2014). Surely, higher molar absorption coefficient and red-shifted spectrum of ZB imply that it would yield a higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ (shortcircuit current density) for the OsC device. Compared with Y5 without halogens in the literature (Yuan et al., 2019a), the maximum absorption wavelengths of ZF, ZC, and ZB are red-shifted by 11, 12 and $1 7 \ \mathrm { n m }$ respectively. The inductive effect and extended $\pi$ -electron delocalization with halogens lead to an enhanced intramolecular charge transfer (Tang and Bao, 2011). Compared with their absorption in dilute solution, the maximum absorption wavelengths of ZF, zC, and ZB films are redshifted to 813, 815, and $8 1 5 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ , respectively, signifying much stronger intermolecular $\pi { - } \pi$ interactions in films. It is worth noting that ZB and ZF exhibit a absorption band in the range of $5 6 0 { - } 9 4 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ with the absorption onset located at $9 1 2 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } = 1 . 3 6 ~ \mathrm { e V } )$ and $9 0 8 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } =$ $1 . 3 7 ~ \mathrm { e V } )$ , respectively, while zC displays a stronger absorption in the relatively short wavelengths with the absorption band from 560 to 920 nm along with the absorption onset located at $9 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } = 1 . 3 8 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ The stronger absorption at shorter wavelengths of ZC might be ascribed to the more pronounced H-aggregation resulting from the introduction of chlorine (Ehrenreich et al., 2016; Zitzler-Kunkel et al., 2013). In addition, the three acceptor molecules exhibit complementary absorption spectra with that of polymeric donor PM6, which is conducive to capture more photons for a higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$

# 2.3.  Photovoltaic properties

The photovoltaic performances of ZF, ZC and ZB were evaluated by fabricating OsC devices with an inverted device structure of $\mathrm { I T O } / \mathrm { Z n O }$ $( 3 0 \mathrm { n m } )$ /active layer/ $\mathbf { M o O } _ { 3 }$ $\mathsf { 5 n m } ) / \mathsf { A l }$ $\cdot 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m } \cdot$ under the illumination of AM 1.5G $( 1 0 0 \mathrm { m W } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ , in which case the active layers were spincoated from chloroform solution (total concentration, D/A (wt/wt) $=$ 1:1) and thermally annealed at $1 1 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ . PM6 was chosen as a donor owing to its suitable energy levels and complementary absorption spectrum with these SMAs. The J-V curves are shown in Fig. 3 (a) and the corresponding photovoltaic parameters are summarized in Table 2. The ZF-, ZC- and ZB-based OSCs show approximate open circuit voltages $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ with the values of 0.89, 0.90, 0.90 v thought the LUMO energy levels of ZF, ZC and ZB are $- 4 . 0 7$ $- 4 . 1 1$ and $- 4 . 1 3 \ \mathrm { e V }$ . Usually, a higher LUMO energy level leads to a larger $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ . Moreover, less interface trap states and charge recombination also result in a larger $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ (Elumalai and Uddin, 2016). Therefore, it is inferred that the ZF-based blend film could possess more interface trap states and charge recombination but the ZC- and ZB-based blend films should possess less interface trap states and charge recombination. It could also be the reason that the ZF-based OSC shows the lowest fill factor (FF) of $6 1 . 8 8 \%$ , and ZC- and ZB-based OSCs show the FFs values of $6 5 . 0 9 \%$ and $6 4 . 1 6 \%$ . The ZF-, ZC- and ZBbased OSCs exhibit the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ values of 24.25, 25.11, and $2 6 . 3 8 \mathrm { m A c m ^ { - 2 } }$ The largest $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of the OSC based on ZB is due to the highest absorption coefficient and a more appropriate morphology in PM6:ZB blend films, whereas the smallest $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of the OSC based on ZF is attributed to the lowest absorption coefficient and an inferior morphology in PM6:zF. It is worth noting that the energy loss $( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } = E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { o p t } } - { \mathrm e } V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ of the PM6:ZB OSC is only $0 . 4 6 \mathrm { e V }$ , which is slightly lower than those of PM6:ZF and PM6:ZC (both of them are $0 . 4 8 \mathrm { e V } )$ and many non-fullerene OSCs, which could be attributed to well dispersed interpenetrating nanophase separation and less charge recombination in its blend film. # 2.4. Film morphologies and charge carrier mobilities

The surface morphologies of PM6:ZF, PM6:ZC and PM6:ZB blend films were investigated by atomic force microscopy (AFM), and the rootmean-squared (RMS) roughness of them were 1.20, 1.42 and $1 . 4 6 \ \mathrm { n m }$ respectively. As shown in the Fig. 4, the PM6:ZF and PM6:ZC films show large domain size. In comparison, PM6:ZB presents the smaller domain size and an appropriate morphology, indicating the excellent miscibility between ZB and PM6, which is expected to explain efficient charge separation and decreased charge recombination (Ye et al., 2018), so the ZB-based device possesses the highest $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ value.

To better understand the effects of halogen substituent on molecular aggregation, the molecular orientation and packing structures in both pristine and blend films were investigated by grazing incidence wide angle X-ray scattering (GIwAxs) measurements, and the corresponding 2D diffraction images along with the corresponding line-cuts are presented in Fig. 5. The ZF, ZC and ZB pristine films show an obvious (01 0) diffraction peak in the out-of-plane (Oop) direction at $q _ { \mathrm { z } } = - 1 . 7 1 \ \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ corresponding to the face-on $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking distance of ${ \sim } 3 . 6 7 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ Simultaneously, The ZF, ZC and ZB pristine films also show a clear (100) lamella scattering at $q _ { \mathrm { x y } } = 0 . 2 8 1$ , 0.290 and $0 . 2 9 9 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively, along the in-plane (IP) direction, indicating a dominant face-on orientation in all sMAs films, while the attribution of the second peak at $q _ { \mathrm { x y } } =$ 0.407, 0.413 and 0.416 $\mathring { \mathsf { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ respectively is unclear. Although three pristine films show a OOP (01 0) peak, the angle between the end point of hump-shaped scattering signal and $q _ { z }$ axis is larger for ZF $( 5 1 ^ { \circ } )$ relative to those of ZB and ZC $( 3 6 - 3 7 ^ { \circ } )$ , indicating that ZF has the most random orientation among three structures. These results mean that ZB possesses a more orderly face-on -t stacking, which is beneficial to charge transport in a vertical direction (Mai et al., 2018; Yao et al. 2019). The ZF, ZC and ZB blend films also display a pronounced (010) diffraction peak at $q _ { \mathrm { z } } = - 1 . 7 0 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ along the OOp direction, which can be attributed to the face-on $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking of the SMAs and PM6 (Song et al., 2019). Furthermore, these blend films show an obvious (100) diffraction peak at $q _ { \mathrm { x y } } = 0 . 2 9 \ \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ in the IP direction. These results suggest that all the blend films form a dominantly face-on morphology, indicating that the intermolecular orientation and packing structures of pristine donor (PM6) and acceptor films are not disturbed significantly by blending two components (Song et al., 2019). The charge transport properties of PM6:ZF, PM6:ZC and PM6:ZB blend films were investigated by the space-charge-limited current (SCLC) method (Zhang et al., 2020), and the corresponding results and $J { - } V$ characteristics are shown in Table 3 and Fig. S11. The structure of electron-only devices is $\scriptstyle \mathrm { I T O } / \mathrm { Z n O } ,$ active layer/LiF/Al, while the holeonly device configuration is ITO/PEDOT: PSS/active layer $\mathrm { { \ T M o O _ { 3 } / A l } }$ All the BHJ blend films display ambipolar charge transport, in which case the electron/hole mobility of PM6:ZF, PM6:ZC and PM6:ZB are $1 . 0 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } / 2 . 1 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ $1 . 5 4 \times { 1 0 ^ { - 4 } } / { 2 . 9 2 } \times { 1 0 ^ { - 4 } }$ and $2 . 5 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } /$ $3 . 3 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively. The charge mobilities increase along with the relative molecular mass of the halogen atoms, which can be attributed to better morphology, more orderly face-on - stacking and a probable heavy-atom effect' of bromine (Gan et al., 2018; Necedova et al., 2016). Accordingly, the ratio of the hole to electron mobility $\left( \mu _ { h } / \mu _ { e } \right)$ is 2.09 for PM6:ZF, 1.90 for PM6:ZC and 1.35 for PM6: ZB, respectively. The higher and more balanced mobilities of PM6:ZB is in line with its film morphology and molecular aggregation, which would bring about inhibited charge recombination and contribute to a higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF (Ye et al., 2018).

The dependence of $J _ { s c }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ versus light density $( P )$ was measured to probe the charge recombination in the devices, and displayed in Fig. S12 (a, b). The relationship between $J s c$ and $P$ can be discussed based on $J s c \propto P ^ { \propto }$ , and the exponential factor $\alpha$ represents the extent of the bimolecular recombination (Meng et al., 2019). When a value is more close to 1, it means a weaker bimolecular charge recombination in the devices (Liu et al., 2017). The $\alpha$ values of the OSCs based on ZF, ZC and ZB are 0.899, 0.955 and 0.970, respectively, demonstrating that the bimolecular charge recombination is suppressed more effectively in the OSCs based on ZB which shows more favorable morphology and the most orderly face-on $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking (Figs. 4 and 5) (Ye et al., 2018). Meanwhile, we also studied monomolecular recombination displayed in (Fig. S12(b)) via treating $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ as a function of $P$ The data were fitted to the linear law: $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } \propto \mathrm { I n } ( P )$ (Zhao et al., 2017). And the slopes of the fitting lines for the OSCs based on ZF, ZC and ZB are $1 . 0 8 k T / q , 1 . 0 5 k T /$ $q$ and $1 . 0 3 ~ k T / q$ , respectively. The smaller slope for ZB-based cells indicates lower monomolecular recombination. The morphological analysis and light intensity dependent $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ strongly support the highest $J _ { s c } ,$ FF, and PCE values of the ZB-based OSCs.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, three small molecule acceptors ZF, ZC and ZB with different monohalogenated terminal units were synthesized and applied in OsCs. The effects of different halogen substituents on molecular aggregation, photophysical and photovoltaic performance have been fully studied. These SMAs exhibit much more red-shifted absorption and deeper FMO energy levels compared with the unsubstituted reference Y5. In addition, ZB shows the most orderly face-on $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking, highest molar absorption coefficient $( 2 . 3 1 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \mathrm { ~ M } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { ~ c m } ^ { - 1 }$ at $7 3 5 ~ \mathrm { n m } )$ , best compatibility with PM6, which leads to the highest and much more balanced charge mobilities, the most efficient exciton dissociation and charge extraction. Lastly, the ZB-based OSCs obtained the highest PCE of $1 5 . 2 3 \%$ , and the ZF-, ZC-based devices displayed lower PCEs of $1 3 . 3 6 \%$ and $1 4 . 7 1 \%$ , respectively. The results demonstrated that appropriate monohalogenated substituent on terminal groups plays an important role in regulating molecular stacking, energy levels, absorption spectra and as such photovoltaic performances.
