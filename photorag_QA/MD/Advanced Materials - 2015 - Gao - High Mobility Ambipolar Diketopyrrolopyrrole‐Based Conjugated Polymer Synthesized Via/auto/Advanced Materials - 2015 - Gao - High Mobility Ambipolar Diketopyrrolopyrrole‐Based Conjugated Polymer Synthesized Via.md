# High Mobility Ambipolar Diketopyrrolopyrrole-Based Conjugated Polymer Synthesized Via Direct Arylation Polycondensation

Yao Gao, Xiaojie Zhang,\* Hongkun Tian,\* Jidong Zhang, Donghang Yan, Yanhou Geng,\* and Fosong Wang

High mobility $\pi$ conjugated polymers have attracted significant research attention for their application in solution processible organic thin film transistors (OTFTs).[1-4] Of those, diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP)-based conjugated polymers have been extensively investigated in recent years, and high mobility unipolar and ambipolar polymeric semiconductors have been developed via molecular engineering of either conjugated backbone or side chains.5-16] Nonetheless, these polymers are mainly prepared by transition metal-catalyzed Stille or Suzuki cross-coupling polycondensations. These approaches require tedious multistep prior preparation of high-purity bifunctional organometallic reagents such as organotin or organoboron compounds as monomers, and meanwhile bring a stoichiometric amount of byproducts. Some metal-containing wastes such as organotin compounds are toxic and environmentally risky. All these issues would be troublesome in the future commercialization of these conjugated polymers.

Recently, direct arylation polycondensation has emerged as a new method for the synthesis of conjugated polymers.[17-19] This protocol is more straightforward (fewer steps), atomeconomical, and more environmentally benign. Some DPPbased conjugated copolymers have also been synthesized by this method.20-25] In these studies, the thiophene-flanked DPP (DT-DPP) monomers often served as the hydrogenated monomer while phenyl-based (e.g., biphenyl, fluorene, carbazole, etc.) aryl bromides acted as the halogenated partners.[22] Nevertheless, the comonomers for DPP-based high mobility conjugated polymers are generally oligothiophene derivatives,[26] and this type of conjugated polymers have not been successfully synthesized via direct arylation polycondensation yet. Using thiophene-flanked DPP as aryl bromide monomer and oligothiophenes as hydrogenated monomer should be a more appropriate approach to synthesize this type of high mobility conjugated polymers. However, oligothiophenes often exhibit low reactivity for direct arylation, especially in the solvents with low polarity such as toluene,[27] which is considered as a better solvent in terms of the solubility of conjugated polymers. Meanwhile, the oligothiophene motifs have multiple $\mathrm { C - H }$ bonds (such as $\beta \ \mathrm { C - H }$ bonds), possibly leading to unwanted branched and cross-linked defects,[21,28-30] which are detrimental to semiconducting properties of the resulting polymers. In the current paper, we reported that the reactivity of (E)-1,2-bis(3,4-difluorothien-2-yl)ethene (4FTVT) for direct arylation is remarkably high in toluene, and consequently the first DPP-based high mobility conjugated polymer, i.e., poly[2,5-bis(2-decyltetradecyl)pyrrolo[3,4-c]pyrrole$1 , 4 ( 2 H , 5 H )$ -dione-alt- $^ { 5 , 5 ^ { \prime } }$ -di(thiophen-2-yl)- $^ { \cdot 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ (E)-1,2-bis(3,4- difluorothien-2-yl)ethene] (PDPP-4FTVT), was successfully synthesized via direct arylation polycondensation. PDPP-4FTVT exhibited ambipolar transport properties in air with hole and electron mobilities of 3.40 and $5 . 8 6 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively, as measured with bottom gate and top contact OTFTs. To the best of our knowledge, this performance is among the best for ambipolar conjugated polymers,[7,11,13-15] and the mobilities are the highest for conjugated polymers synthesized by direct arylation polycondensation.24,25,31-35]

4FTVT was chosen as the hydrogenated monomer because (1) (E)-2-(2-(thiophen-2-yl)vinyl)thiophene (TVT) is a promising building block for DPP-based high mobility conjugated polymers,5,10] and with which the polymers with hole mobility $> 1 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ have been demonstrated;[10] (2) the introduction of fluorine (F)-atoms in $\beta \mathrm { . }$ positions of thiophene rings not only evades the unwanted possible CH activation, but also can remarkably enhance the reactivity of the thiophene moieties toward direct arylation due to electron-withdrawing nature of F-atom;[36-40] (3) the incorporation of F-atoms may bring intramolecular noncovalent interactions so-called conformational locks, resulting in further improved planarity of the conjugated backbone.1241-44] Meanwhile, the strong electronegativity of F-atom induces a strong dipole along the CF bond, promoting molecular organization and crystallization;143,45,46) and (4) multifuorination can lower both the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels, thereby improving the stability of the polymer and facilitating electron injection from the electrodes in OTFT devices.[12]

The structure and synthesis of PDPP-4FTVT are outlined in Scheme 1, and synthetic details are provided in the Supporting Information. 2-Bromo-5-trimethylsilyl-3,4-difluorothiophene (1) was prepared according to literature.[45] Lithiation of 1 with 1 equiv. of $n$ BuLi followed by treatment with $N , N -$ dimethylformamide (DMF) produced 2 in a yield of $5 5 \%$ . The compound 3 was obtained in a yield of $7 5 \%$ via the McMurry reaction of 2 . It was treated with trifluoroacetic acid to afford monomer 4FTVT (M1) in a yield of $90 \%$ . The single crystals of 3 were obtained from recrystallization in ethanol for structure analysis. As shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information), two crystallographically independent molecules (form A and form B) were observed in the unit cell of 3. Both of them have a center of symmetry. The calculated mean standard deviation of the distance of each atom away from the least-squares planes of 4FTVT is $0 . 0 0 7 5 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ for form A and $0 . 0 1 2 3 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ for form B. The dihedral angle between 3,4-difluorothiophene and vinyl units is $1 ^ { \circ }$ for form A and $1 . 7 ^ { \circ }$ for form B. For comparison, the dihedral angle of about $2 . 2 ^ { \circ }$ between fluoro-substituted thiophenes was found in perfluoro $\alpha$ sexithiophene (PF-6T).[45] Above results indicate that the introduction of vinyl group can release steric hindrance, leading to a more planar conjugated structure.

The direct arylation polycondensation of M1 and M2 was conducted in toluene with three different catalyst systems, as shown in Table S1 (Supporting Information),[23,25,39,40,47] and the Herrmann's catalyst gave the best results probably due to its high thermal stability.[48] with this catalyst, the polymer PDPP-4FTVT was obtained in high yield $( 9 3 \% )$ in $1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ Compared to the previously reported direct arylation polycondensation,[20,23,25,47-53] the high reactivity of M1 is reflected in the much lower concentration (0.01 M) and the shorter reaction time needed. In a control polycondensation of TVT and M2, no polymeric product was produced, indicating that the introduction of fluorine atoms is crucial for the successful direct arylation polymerization of M1 (see Scheme S1, Supporting Information). To study the effect of fuorination on the properties of the resulting polymer, PDPP-TVT as a reference polymer was then synthesized by Stille polycondensation according to the literature.5l Number-average molecular weights $( M _ { \mathrm { n } } )$ of PDPP-4FTVT and PDPP-TVT are 59.9 and $5 3 . 0 \mathrm { \ k D a }$ , respectively, as measured by high temperature gel permeation chromatography (HT-GPC) with 1,2,4-trichlorobenzene (TCB) as eluent at $1 5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (Iable 1). In order to investigate the effects of fluorination on polymer configurations and intrinsic electronic structures, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were carried out on methyl-substituted trimer of DPP-4FTVT using Gaussian 09 with a hybrid B3LYP correlation functional and 6-31G (d) basis set. The optimized geometry and frontier molecular orbitals of the trimer are shown in Figure 1. The incorporation of fuorine atoms in PDPP-4FTVT does not cause an increase of the dihedral angle of polymer backbones. This is consistent with the single crystal analysis data of compound 3, in which the $\pi$ extended TVT moiety remains high coplanarity in spite of the fluorination (Figure S1, Supporting Information). The calculated $\mathrm { F } { - } S$ distance $( 2 . 9 6 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ } )$ is obviously shorter than the sum of van der Waals radii of F and S atoms (3.27 A). This implies the presence of weak intramolecular $\mathrm { F } { - } S$ interaction. Although the binding energy of $\mathrm { F } { - } S$ interaction is not sufficient to totally lock the conformation,[54-56] this weak interaction should be beneficial to the planarization of the polymer backbone and therefore promote intermolecular $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking. In addition, both the HOMO and LUMO of PDPP-4FTVT are highly delocalized along the polymer backbone. Accordingly, PDPP-4FTVT-based OTFTs are expected to exhibit excellent ambipolar behaviors.[7,11]

Figure 2a illustrates a comparison of the absorption spectra of fluorinated polymer PDPP-4FTVT and the reference PDPPTVT both in dilute solution and in thin film. The flm bandgap of PDPP-4FTVT estimated from the absorption onset is $1 . 4 1 \mathrm { e V } ,$ $0 . 1 1 \ \mathrm { e V }$ broader than that of PDPP-TVT. In both solution and film states, the intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) absorption band of PDPP-4FTVT red-shifts obviously compared to that of PDPP-TVT, and is featured with structured vibrational absorption peaks, which agrees with the improved planarity due to the presence of F-S noncovalent interactions. The electrochemical cyclic voltammograms of PDPP-TVT and PDPP-4FTVT as shown in Figure 2b were measured to estimate their HOMO and LUMO energy levels, and the data are outlined in Table 1. As expected, both HOMO and LUMO levels were noticeably lowered after the introduction of F-atoms, which are $- 5 . 3 6$ and $- 3 . 5 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for PDPP-4FTVT and $- 5 . 1 7$ and $- 3 . 3 5 \mathrm { ~ } \mathrm { e V }$ for PDPP-TVT.

Bottom-gate and top-contact OTFTs were fabricated to evaluate the semiconducting properties of PDPP-4FTVT, and those of PDPP-TVT were also prepared for comparison. The semiconductor layer was deposited by spin-casting a polymer solution in 1,2-dichlorobenzene (o-DCB) onto octadecyltrimethoxysilane (OTMS)-modified $\mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ $( 3 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m } ) / \mathrm { S i }$ substrate.

The OTMS self-assembled monolayer was deposited according to the method developed by Bao and co-workers.[57] Gold source and drain electrodes were finally deposited, and channel length $( L )$ and width (W) of the devices are $1 0 0 \mu \mathrm { m }$ and $3 \mathrm { m m }$ , respectively. The mobility of the devices was calculated in the saturation current regimes, and mobility $( \mu )$ and threshold voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { T } } )$ are summarized in Table 2 and Table S2 (Supporting Information). The reference polymer PDPP-TVT exhibited a p-type transport behavior. Its pristine film manifested a hole mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } \right)$ up to $0 . 8 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ . After annealing at $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ its maximum $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ was enhanced to $3 . 0 5 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ with an average $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ of $2 . 0 1 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ . This mobility is comparable to that in the previous report using the same device configuration.9l As expected, PDPP-4FTVT displayed ambipolar characteristics, consistent with its deep and delocalized LUMO level as aforementioned. OTFTs based on its pristine films showed hole mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } \right)$ up to $0 . 6 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and electron mobility $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ up to $0 . 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ (Figure S4 and Table S2, Supporting Information). The device performance was significantly improved after thermal annealing at $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ . Further elevating the annealing temperature to $2 5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ resulted in a degradation of device performance (Table S2, Supporting Information). Figure 3 shows the typical output and transfer characteristics of the devices based on PDPP-4FTVT film annealed at $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ . The output curves are characterized by the superposition of standard saturation behavior for one carrier at high $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ and a superlinear current increased at low $V _ { \mathrm { G } }$ and high $V _ { \mathrm { { D } } }$ due to injection of the opposite carrier. The transfer curves exhibited a V-shape. These characteristics are typical for ambipolar OTFTs. The lower current on/off ratio in the n-channel transfer curve is attributed to the high off-current engendered by the great hole conduction.11I The maximum $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ abstracted from the saturation regime are 3.40 and $5 . 8 6 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } ~ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively. To the best of our knowledge, these mobilities are comparable to high performance ambipolar polymers prepared by Stille polymerization,[7,11,13-15] and are the highest for the conjugated polymers synthesized by direct arylation polvcondensation.|24,25,31-35]

Film morphology and microstructures of PDPP-4FTVT were studied by 2D grazing incidence wide angle X-ray scattering (2D GIWAXS) and atomic force microscopy (AFM). As shown in Figure 4a, a strong (100) diffraction peak at $q _ { \mathrm { z } } = 2 . 9 4 ~ \mathrm { n m } ^ { - 1 }$ along with a weak (200) diffraction peak was observed for pristine film of PDPP-4FTVT. After thermal annealing at $2 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ (100) and (200) diffraction peaks became sharper and stronger along with the presence of (300) peak, although the absorption spectra before and after thermal annealing were similar (Figure S5, Supporting Information). The (100) peak was at $q _ { \mathrm { z } } = 2 . 8 9 \ \mathrm { n m } ^ { - 1 }$ corresponding to a lamellar $d$ spacing of $2 1 . 7 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$

Meanwhile, a weak (010) diffraction peak at $q _ { \mathrm { x y } } = 1 7 . 8 ~ \mathrm { n m } ^ { - 1 }$ corresponding to a  stacking distance of $3 . { \overset { \prime } { 5 } } 3 { \overset { \circ } { \mathrm { A } } } .$ was also observed (Figure 4b). These observations indicate that polymer backbones adopted an edge-on packing structures in thin film, and thermal annealing could noticeably improve the packing order of the polymer backbones in both in-plane and out-ofplane directions. In contrast, the film of the reference polymer PDPP-TVT only showed relatively weak (100) and (200) diffractions but not (010) peak even after thermal annealing (Figures S6 and S7, Supporting Information). This is consistent with our expectation that the introduction of F-atoms can enhance coplanarity of polymer backbone and therefore their packing order in film. The pristine film of PDPP-4FTVT was featured with "granular" morphology (Figure 4c). After thermal annealing at $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ fiber-like microstructures were observed (Figure 4d). This implies the re-organization of polymer backbones upon annealing. The highly uniform intertwined fibers can form interconnected polymer chain networks, therefore providing a more efficient channel for charge carrier transport. However, once increasing the annealing temperature to $2 5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ rod-like morphology with poorer continuity was observed (Figure S8, Supporting Information). All these morphology features are consistent with above observations on semiconducting properties of the polymers.

In conclusion, we have demonstrated the first DPP-based high mobility conjugated polymer, i.e., PDPP-4FTVT, which was synthesized via direct arylation polycondensation. The incorporation of F-atoms in $\beta$ positions of thiophene rings not only improved the efficiency of direct arylation, but also lowered HOMO and LUMO energy levels of the polymer. The resulting polymer can form ordered films with an edge-on alignment of polymer backbones. OTFTs based on PDPP-4FTVT exhibited ambipolar transport behavior in air with hole and electron mobilities up to 3.40 and $5 . 8 6 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively. This study suggests that direct arylation is a promising protocol for synthesizing high mobility polymeric semiconductors once the monomer was rationally designed.
