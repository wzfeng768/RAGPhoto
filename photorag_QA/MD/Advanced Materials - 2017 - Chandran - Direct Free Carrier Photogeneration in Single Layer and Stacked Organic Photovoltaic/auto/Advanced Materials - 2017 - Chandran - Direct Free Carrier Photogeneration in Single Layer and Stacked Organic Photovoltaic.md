# Direct Free Carrier Photogeneration in Single Layer and Stacked Organic Photovoltaic Devices

Hrisheekesh Thachoth Chandran, Tsz-Wai Ng, Yishu Foo, Ho-Wa Li, Jian Qing, Xiao-Ke Liu, Chiu-Yee Chan, Fu-Lung Wong, Juan Antonio Zapien, Sai-Wing Tsang, Ming-Fai Lo,\* and Chun-Sing Lee\*

High performance organic photovoltaic devices typically rely on type-II P/N junctions for assisting exciton dissociation. Heremans and co-workers recently reported a high efficiency device with a third organic layer which is spatially separated from the active P/N junction; but still contributes to the carrier generation by passing its energy to the $\mathsf { P } / \mathsf { N }$ junction via a long-range exciton energy transfer mechanism. In this study the authors show that there is an additional mechanism contributing to the high efficiency. Some bipolar materials (e.g., subnaphthalocyanine chloride (SubNc) and subphthalocyanine chloride (SubPc)) are observed to generate free carriers much more effectively than typical organic semiconductors upon photoexcitation. Single-layer devices with SubNc or SubPc sandwiched between two electrodes can give power conversion efficiencies 30 times higher than those of reported single-layer devices. In addition, internal quantum efficiencies (IQEs) of bilayer devices with opposite stacking sequences (i.e., SubNc/SubPc vs SubPc/SubNc) are found to be the sum of IQEs of single layer devices. These results confirm that SubNc and SubPc can directly generate free carriers upon photoexcitation without assistance from a P/N junction. These allow them to be stacked onto each other with reversible sequence or simply stacking onto another $\mathsf { P } / \mathsf { N }$ junction and contribute to the photocarrier generation.

Almost all the high performance organic photovoltaic (OPv) devices reported so far rely on $\mathrm { P } / \mathrm { N }$ junction for assisting excitons dissociation and photocharge generation.[1-15] To enhance the power conversion efficiency (PCE), some reports utilized three organic layers in OPV structure with cascading energy levels such that these three-layer stacks consist of two effective $\mathrm { P / N }$ junctions.16-19] $\mathrm { I n } ~ 2 0 1 4$ Heremans and co-workers reported a record-breaking PCE of $8 . 4 \%$ in a small molecule OPV device with a $\mathrm { P } / \mathrm { N } / \mathrm { N }$ stack of $\alpha$ sexithiophene $( \alpha { - } 6 \mathrm { T } )$ /subnaphthalocyanine chloride (SubNc)/subphthalocyanine chloride (SubPc).[20] Specifically, they propose a novel mechanism to harvest excitons generated in SubPc layer, which is spatially separated from the $\alpha { \mathrm { - } } 6 \mathrm { T / S u b N c }$ $\mathrm { P } / \mathrm { N }$ junction. In their device, excitons in the SubPc layer pass their energies to the neighboring SubNc layer via Forster resonance energy transfer. With this new design, exciton generated in SubPc can be harvested even though the distance from the $\mathrm { P } / \mathrm { N }$ junction is longer than the exciton diffusion length.[20,21]

In this work we observed that, in addition to the proposed "long-range exciton energy transfer" mechanism, there is in fact another mechanism contributing toward the high PCE. Our single-layer OPV devices show that, comparing with most organic semiconductors, SubNc and SubPc are capable of generating free charge carriers much more effectively without any assistance from a $\mathrm { P } / \mathrm { N }$ junction. For example, while PCEs of most single-layer OPV devices are extremely low $( \approx 0 . 0 1 \% )$ [22-25] PCEs of single-layer SubNc and SubPc devices are as high as $0 . 3 \%$ . It has to be understood that excitons in single-layer devices can be easily quenched by the neighboring electrodes and the Al deposition can severely damage the active layer in such single layer devices.[26,27] Therefore, unusual characteristics of the single-layer SubNc and SubPc devices do give a strong hint that these two materials have much higher photoexcitation yields of free charge carriers comparing to other organic semiconductors.

Based on this idea, we further exploit the SubNc/SubPc junction for OPV applications. It is noted that SubNc/SubPc junction is reported as a non-P/N junction.[28] we observed that devices consisting of SubNc/SubPc or SubPc/SubNc junctions are effectively functioning even if the sequence of active layers has reversed. More importantly, IQEs of bilayer devices estimated by a hybrid modeling/experimental approach are found to be the sum of IQEs of single layer devices. These results confirm that the charge dissociation process in these bilayer devices does not rely on the SubNc/SubPc or SubPc/SubNc interfaces. Our findings show that SubNc and SubPc are indeed special bipolar semiconductors which are capable of generating direct free carriers upon photoexcitation. They are thus able to freely stack onto each other with reversible sequence; or be stacked onto other suitable $\mathrm { P } / \mathrm { N }$ junctions[20,21] for enhancing photocharge generation.

Simple single-active layer devices with optimized thickness were fabricated with following structure: Indium tin oxide (ITO)/SubNc or SubPc $( 2 0 ~ \mathrm { \ n m } )$ /bathophenanthroline (BPhen) $( 8 \ \mathrm { n m } ) / \mathrm { A l }$ [SubNc/BPhen and SubPc/BPhen devices]. BPhen is used as a protecting layer that prevents hot Al penetration during metal deposition.[29,30] Figure 1a,b shows current density-voltage $\left( J - W \right)$ characteristics of the SubNc and SubPc devices under 1 Sun condition. Both SubNc and SubPc single-layer devices show $J { - } V$ curves with notable photovoltaic responses. The SubNc/BPhen device shows a short circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ , open circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ , and fill factor (FF) of $2 . 7 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ $1 . 0 2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ and 0.28, respectively which accounts for a PCE of $0 . 8 \%$ . Similarly, SubPc/BPhen device exhibited a PCE of $0 . 6 \%$ with a $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } ,$ $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and FF of $2 . 1 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ $1 . 1 0 \mathrm { V }$ and 0.23, respectively.

BPhen possesses a shallow lowest unoccupied molecular orbital level of $- 3 . 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and a low-lying highest occupied molecular orbital level of $- 6 . 4 \ \mathrm { e V } .$ It therefore forms a noninteractive junction with the neighboring SubNc or SubPc layer; and cannot contribute to any exciton dissociation.[31] To further confirm that SubNc and SubPc are indeed capable of generating free charge carriers upon photoexcitation, devices with structure of ITO/SubNc or SubPc $( 3 0 \ \mathrm { n m } ) / \mathrm { A l }$ was fabricated as a reference [SubNc only and SubPc only devices]. The illuminated $J { - } V$ curves of these simplified devices are included in Figure 1a,b. Surprisingly, reasonably high photocurrents are still observed in these simplified SubNc and SubPc devices with values of 1.6 and $1 . 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , respectively. This indicates a considerable amount of photo carrier generation in the single SubNc and SubPc layers.

Figure 1c,d shows the external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra of SubNc and SubPc devices with (empty symbols) and without (solid symbols) BPhen layer as well as their absorption spectra (lines without symbols). In all devices, EQE spectra match well with the absorption spectra of the corresponding active layer confirming photocharge generation from SubNc and SubPc layers. In Figure 1c, the maximum EQE value of SubNc device at $7 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ is ${ \approx } 2 6 \%$ (empty square). It is greatly reduced to $1 4 . 5 \%$ (solid square) when BPhen layer is removed. Similar EQE reduction is observed in SubPc devices (Figure 1d) at ${ \approx } 6 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , from a maximum of $\approx 3 0 \%$ (empty circle) to $14 \%$ (solid circle) after the removal of BPhen layer. These observed EQE reductions upon BPhen removal can be understood as the charge carrier quenching caused by the direct deposition of Al onto the active layers.[29] Nevertheless, it is worth noting that all the previous reports on single layer devices have a limited PCE of ${ < } 0 . 0 1 \%$ [22,24-27] It is thus of surprise to observe here that PCE in SubNc and the SubPc only devices are up to $0 . 3 \%$ which is more than 30 times higher than those of previously reported single layer devices. These results show that the photogenerated electron-hole pairs in the SubNc and SubPc single layers are much readily dissociated even in absence of typical D/A interface effect.

As intrinsic free carriers can be individually generated from the SubNc and SubPc films (Figure 1), we proceed to use either SubNc/SubPc $( 1 2 \mathrm { n m } / 1 5 \mathrm { n m } )$ or SubPc/SubNc ( $1 2 . 5 \mathrm { n m } / 1 5 \mathrm { n m }$ as the active layers in devices with a configuration of ITO/active layer/Al [SubNc/SubPc and SubPc/SubNc devices]. Figure 2a illustrates the log $J { - } V$ characteristics of the two devices under 1 Sun. Performances of these two devices are largely similar with $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } ,$ $V _ { \infty } ,$ and FF $\mathrm { ) f } { \approx } 3 \ \mathrm { m A } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ${ \approx } 0 . 5 5 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ and ${ \approx } 0 . 3 0$ , respectively, which accounts for a PCE of ${ \approx } 0 . 5 \%$ . Figure 2b shows the EQE spectra of the SubNc/SubPc (empty triangle) and the $\operatorname { S u b P c } /$ SubNc (solid triangle) devices. Both devices exhibit largely similar EQE spectra irrespective of the stacking sequence. Furthermore, to rule out any charge transfer reaction between SubNc and SubPc, we have measured the absorption spectra of SubNc-SubPc and SubPc-SubNc films (Figure S1, Supporting Information). Since there are no absorption peaks in the longer wavelength region $( > 8 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m } )$ , charge transfer reaction between SubNc and SubPc is considered to be limited.

It is well known that PV effects depend on two electric fields: (1) external field that is governed by the difference of the two electrodes work function, (2) internal field that is governed by the $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ junction. $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of the OPV cell is determined by the combined effects of these two fields.32,33] Polarity of the device may not change by reversing the D/A junction, if the external field is larger than the internal field. However, even if the polarity is not reversed, the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ will change considerably by reversing the D/A junction.34,35] In our experiment, reversing the organic junction has negligible influence on the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ (Figure 2a). This is consistent with the model that the organic junction of SubNc/SubPc or SubPc/SubNc does not work as a typical D/A junction.

To consolidate our discussion, a combination of measurement (i.e., complex indices of refraction $( \tilde { \boldsymbol { \mathrm { n } } } = \boldsymbol { n } - i \boldsymbol { k } )$ measured using spectroscopic ellipsometry) and optical modeling (i.e., transfer matrix modeling) are used to determine internal quantum efficiency (IQE) of single and bilayer devices. This method takes into account the interference of coherent reflected and transmitted waves at each interface in the stack and thereby provide a much better correction between modeled and measured absorptance, so as the IQE analysis.[36,37] Figure 3a,b shows the refractive index $( n )$ and extinction coefficient (k) values of SubNc, SubPc, CuPc, and $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ respectively. CuPc and $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 } ,$ the most commonly used photoactive materials are included as a reference. SubNc and SubPc exhibit significantly high n $( > 3 )$ and $k$ $( > 1 . 6 )$ values comparing to CuPc and $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ . These higher values of SubNc and SubPc are attributed to their molecular orientation and nonplanar cone shaped structures.[38]

By using the $n$ and $k$ values, transfer matrix modeling is further used to calculate the absorptance and average IQEs of single layer devices (Figure 4a) and bilayer devices (Figure 4b). In Figure 4, average IQEs for SubNc only, SubPc only, SubNc/ SubPc and SubPc/SubNc devices are estimated as $2 2 \%$ $2 6 \%$ $5 0 \%$ and $49 \%$ , respectively. EQE and absorption spectra are also shown here as a reference. Interestingly, average IQEs of bilayer devices $( \approx 5 0 \% )$ are almost the sum of average IQEs of single layer devices $( \approx 4 8 \% )$ . Furthermore, bilayer devices with opposite stacking sequences (i.e., SubNc/SubPc vs $\operatorname { S u b P c } /$ SubNc) have similar IQE values. These IQE results along with $J { - } V$ characteristics demonstrate that the interface (i.e., SubNc/ SubPc or SubPc/SubNc) is only having very limited influence on carrier dissociation. Limited interface influence on carrier dissociation serves as the direct evidence for free carrier generation in SubNc and SubPc materials.

To understand why SubNc and SubPc are generating free carriers more easily than typical organic semiconductors, the real part of dielectric constant $( \varepsilon _ { 1 } )$ is estimated from the complex refractive indices of the respective materials. SubNc and SubPc exhibit relatively higher $\varepsilon _ { 1 }$ values of 5.8 and 4.3, respectively, which are among the highest reported literature values for OPV materials (e.g., CuPc shows $\varepsilon _ { 1 }$ value of 3 only. See the Supporting Information).39-41] In most organic photoactive materials, the electron-hole pair created via photoexcitation must overcome the Coulomb attraction before separating into free carriers. A high $\varepsilon _ { 1 }$ in organic materials can show efficient screening effect and thus facilitate the generation of free carriers.42,43] In fact, $\mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ with a high $\varepsilon _ { 1 }$ of ${ \approx } 4 . 4$ has been demonstrated as a direct free carrier generator.[44,45]

To ascertain the influence of free carrier generation on device performance, we have optimized the bilayer devices for highest PCE. Bilayer device with structure ITO/SubNc $( 1 2 \mathrm { n m } ) /$ SubPc $\left( 1 5 \mathrm { n m } \right)$ /BPhen $( 8 \mathrm { n m } ) / \mathrm { A l }$ has shown the highest PCE of $3 . 2 \%$ with a $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } ,$ $\mathrm { V } _ { \mathrm { o c } } ,$ and FF of $6 . 6 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ $1 . 0 4 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ and $0 . 4 6 \%$ respectively (Figure 5a). It is also evident from the EQE spectrum (Figure 5b) that the BPhen layer has significantly reduced the carrier quenching and strengthened both SubNc and SubPc peaks. Moreover, PCE of this nonfullerene device is comparable to its fullerene counterpart, and thereby lend itself as a potential candidate among nonfullerene OPV devices.[46]

In summary, we show that single-layer SubNc and SubPc OPV devices exhibit much higher performance comparing to previously reported single-layer devices. It is demonstrated that their bilayer devices with reversing sequence (i.e., SubNc/ SubPc and SubPc/SubNc) show highly similar PV performance. More importantly, by using a hybrid modeling/experimental approach, average IQEs of bilayer devices are found to be the sum of average IQEs of single layer devices. This result further confirms that they do not work on the conventional $\mathrm { P } / \mathrm { N }$ junction mechanism. Combining with the results from the single. layer devices, we conclude that SubNc and SubPc are in fact bipolar semiconductors capable of generating free carriers upon photoexcitation. These characteristics allow them to be stacked on to each other with reversible sequence (the present work) or simply stacking onto another $\mathrm { P } / \mathrm { N }$ junction (the Heremans's work) and contribute to the photocarrier generation.

# Experimental Section

Patterned ITO-coated glass substrates with a sheet resistance of $7 5 \ \Omega$ square-' were routinely cleaned with Decon 90, rinsed in deionized water, dried in an oven, and finally treated with ultraviolet-ozone (UV-ozone) for $1 5 \mathrm { \ m i n }$ . Organic films were deposited through thermal evaporation at a deposition rate and a pressure of $1 - 2 \mathring { \mathsf { A } } \mathsf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $< 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 6 }$ Torr, respectively. The film thickness was calibrated using a variable angle spectroscopic ellipsometer (M-2000, J.A. Woollam Co.). All evaporating materials were used as received. The active device area is $0 . 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ which is defined by the overlapping region of the ITO anode and the Al cathode.47,48] Thicknesses of the organic layers in all devices are optimized for the highest PCE. The finishing devices were immediately encapsulated in a glove box. The $J { - } V$ characteristics of the devices were measured with a programmable Keithley model 237 power source under AM 1.5G illumination at $\mathsf { l o o } \mathsf { m w } \mathsf { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ (Newport Oriel solar simulator). EQE spectra were measured using an Oriel IQE 200 system.[49]

The complex index of refraction, $\tilde { n } = ( n , \ k )$ , was determined using Variable angle spectroscopic ellipsometry (J. A Woollam MDi2000). Organic thin films with different thickness (i.e., $5 0 { \mathrm { - } } 1 0 0 ~ { \mathsf { n m } } )$ were deposited on ultrasonic cleaned $\mathrm { S i } / \mathrm { S i O } _ { 2 }$ substrate. The spectroscopic ellipsometric angles $\Psi$ and $\Delta$ were measured in transmission and reflection mode with an incident angle of $4 5 ^ { \circ }$ $5 5 ^ { \circ }$ $6 5 ^ { \circ }$ ,and $7 5 ^ { \circ }$ respectively. A four-phase optical model consisting of ambient/intermix/ film/substrate and a parameterized dispersion model (i.e., Gaussian/ Lorentz/Tauc-Lorentz models) were used in the ftting process to obtain the complex index of refraction. Details on accurate determination on spectroscopic ellipsometry analysis can be found in the reference [50].
