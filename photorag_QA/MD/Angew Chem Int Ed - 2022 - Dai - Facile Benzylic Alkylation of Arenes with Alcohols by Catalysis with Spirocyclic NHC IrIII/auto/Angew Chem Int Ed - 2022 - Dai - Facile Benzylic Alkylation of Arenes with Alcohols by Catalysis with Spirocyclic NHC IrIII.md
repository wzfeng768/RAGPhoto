# Facile Benzylic Alkylation of Arenes with Alcohols by Catalysis with Spirocyclic NHC $\mathbf { I r } ^ { \bar { \mathbf { I I I } } }$ Pincer Complex

Kun-Long Dai, Qi-Long Chen, Wen-Ping Xie, Ka Lu, Zhi-Bo Yan, Meng Peng, Chang-Kun Li, Yong-Qiang Tu,\* and Tong-Mei Ding

Abstract: A facile benzylic alkylation of indenes and other arenes was developed from readily available primary and secondary alcohols using our newly investigated CCC pincer $\mathrm { I r } ^ { \mathrm { I I I } }$ catalyst (SNIr-H). Excellent regioselectivity and yield $( 8 9 \% )$ of the C3-alkylated indenes were obtained. Additionally, the challenging $\mathrm { s p } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { C }$ -alkylation was readily accomplished. This method could be utilized for the synthesis of the analogs of a histamine H1 receptor antagonist and the functional material template molecule, indeno[2,1-a]indene. A hemilabile $\mathrm { I r } ^ { \mathrm { I I I } }$ dihydride intermediate was proposed based on control experiments and previous density functional theory (DFT) calculations for the borrowing hydrogen mechanism and is key to the success of this $\mathrm { I r } ^ { \mathrm { I I I } }$ catalyst in the reduction of unactivated multisubstituted olefin intermediates.

Indene and other arene derivatives are found in many biologically active pharmaceutical molecules and functional materials. Although several synthetic methods have been developed, the direct benzylic alkylation of indenes from feedstock alcohols provides a straightforward route to indene derivatives. The C1- and C2-alkylation of indenes have been developed using Zr and Ru catalysts, respectively.l However, the C3-alkylation of indenes with alcohols has not been reported to the best of our knowledge. A possible route toward the C3-alkylation of indenes could incorporate the transition-metal-catalyzed borrowing hydrogen (BH) strategy. The BH strategy uses readily available alcohols, is an atom-economical and green alkylation method, and water is the only byproduct.3l A metalcatalyzed dehydrogenation, condensation, and hydrogenation sequence could realize the alkylation, in the presence of a base, of carbons that have acidic protons. The $\mathfrak { a }$ -carbons of the electron-withdrawing activation groups may be easily alkylated with alcohols under the conditions used for the BH.4I Nevertheless, the BH alkylation of less-activated indenes from alcohols has not been accomplishedl to date, possibly because of the bulkiness of the indenes (Scheme 1a).

However, under BH conditions, tetrasubstituted olefin intermediates from secondary alcohols and disubstituted nucleophilic carbons can be formed; the olefin intermediates are not easily reduced to the final products due to steric reasons. Admittedly, there are limited examples of the reduction of the olefin intermediates, using ester, amide, imine, and heterocyclic activation groups.l The reduction of the unactivated tetrasubstituted intermediates from bulky indenes remains a challenge (Scheme 1b). Therefore, the development of new catalysts for this type of reduction is highly desirable. Due to the successful $\mathrm { C - H }$ silylation and olefin isomerization reactions that were catalyzed by our recently  developed  spirocyclic $N .$ heterocyclic  carbene (SNHC)-based $\mathrm { I r } ^ { \mathrm { I I I } }$ CCC pincer complexes (SNIr-Cl),[8] we sought to explore the reactivities of the pincer complexes in BH reactions. Once the stable $\mathrm { I r } ^ { \mathrm { I I I } } { - \mathrm { H } }$ complex (SNIr-H) had been isolated, we began investigating the possible formation of $\mathrm { I r } ^ { \mathrm { I I I } }$ dihydride, which would accelerate the reduction of unactivated olefins via hydrogen transfer. Furthermore, we envision that the hemilabile[l nature of the pincer complex may provide sufficient space around the metal center, thereby enabling the coordination/activation of the sterically hindered olefin intermediates. Herein, we present the SNIr-X-catalyzed facile benzylic alkylation of unactivated indenes and other arenes with a variety of primary and secondary alcohols to solve the abovementioned challenges (Scheme 1c).

Having established the optimized conditions, we evaluated the reaction scope of alcohols with 1a (Table 2). Various primary alcohols reacted smoothly to afford C3- alkylated indenes in high yields $( 3 \mathbf { a } \mathbf { - } 3 \mathbf { q } )$ . The branched cyclopropyl and cyclohexyl groups were tolerated (3d and 3e). The basic tertiary amine and pyridine functional groups in 3i and 3k had little effect on the yield. When unsaturated 3-buten-1-ol was used, the $\mathrm { C } { \mathrm { - } } \mathrm { C }$ double bond was further reduced and 3b was obtained. However, the trisubstituted olefin in 3h did not undergo reduction. Surprisingly, the bisalkylation product 3r was detected when 2-phenylethanol was used under the same conditions. Approximately $7 4 \%$ of 3r was isolated when 2.5 equiv of 2r was added at $1 2 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ However, when secondary alcohols were used, a higher catalyst loading and temperature $3 \mathrm { m o l } \%$ Cat A and $1 2 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ were necessary to increase their reactivity (3s-3y). Both cyclic and acyclic secondary alcohols could be utilized in the formal C3-alkylation of indenes, although the reaction produced medium yields; the medium yields were probably due to the steric hindrance of the tetrasubstituted olefin intermediates.

We subsequently examined the scope of indenes using $n$ pentanol 2a (Table 3). The expected indene C3-alkylation products were isolated in good to high yields. The $\beta$ -aliphatic substituents had a small effect on the efficiency of the reactions (5a-5c). The $\beta$ -phenyl group of indene increased the reactivity markedly, and the bis-alkylation product 5d was obtained in high yields. Furthermore, the substitution effect on the aryl ring of indene was explored (5e-5k). The results indicated that using electron-donating groups (5e, 5f, 5h, 5k), as substituents, led to higher yields than those obtained when the substituents were electron-withdrawing groups (5g, 5i, 5j). Exclusive C3-selectivity was achieved in all cases. In addition to indenes, we also studied the alkylation of fluorenes because of their wide application in functional materials.[12] Fluorene and its derivatives reacted successfully, affording the expected products (51-5o) in high yields.

A further extension of the research focused on the alkylation of the relatively inert $\mathrm { s p } ^ { 3 } \mathrm { C - H }$ bonds in other arenes (Table 4). The reaction of xanthene (6a) and benzyl alcohol furnished the alkylation product $\mathbf { 7 a }$ in less than $2 0 \%$ yield under the optimized conditions used in this study. We anticipated that when less acidic substrates are used, condensation may be a challenge. Fortunately, the yield of 7a increased to $8 5 \%$ in the presence of Cat A $( 5 \mathrm { m o l } \%$ KO'Bu (1.5 equiv), and 18-crown-6 (1.0equiv) at $1 4 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ These conditions were used for the reactions of the S- or Ncontaining cyclic substrates (7b-7e). For the acyclic diaryl methanes, alkylation with benzyl alcohol or $n$ -pentanol effective in afforded 7f-7h in moderate to good yields. In addition, easily available methyl heteroarenes $\bf 6 i - 6 n$ could be alkylated to produce $7 \mathbf { i } \mathbf { - } 7 \mathbf { n }$ in good to high yields, under the given conditions. It is noteworthy that the direct alkylation of the above skeletons $( 7 \mathbf { a } , ^ { [ 1 3 \mathrm { a } ] } \ 7 \mathbf { h } , ^  [ 1 \mathrm $ 3d] and $\mathbf { 7 i } ^ { [ 1 3 \mathrm { f } ] }$ may be used for synthesizing pharmaceuticals, polymers, dyes and other functional materials.[13]

Two molecules were synthesized to demonstrate the potential applications of the newly accomplished indene C3- alkylation method (Scheme 2). The method was utilized in the synthesis of the analogs of the histamine H1 receptor antagonist 9, and did not require stoichiometric quantities of strong bases and toxic alkyl halides.[14] Under similar conditions, compound 11 could be easily prepared in $6 3 \%$ yield at a $1 \mathrm { m m o l }$ scale using 1a and bromobenzyl alcohol 10; compound 11 was further converted to indeno[2,1-a] indene 12 via the Herrmann-Beller catalysis[15] and is a versatile template for functional material molecules.16]

Control experiments were performed to investigate the mechanism of the reaction (Scheme 3). First, 1a reacts with ${ \bf 2 \ m }$ to generate diene $\bf { 3 m m }$ in $2 5 \%$ yield within $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ (Scheme 3a); diene $3 \mathbf { m } \mathbf { m }$ is transformed into the alkylated product $\mathbf { 3 m }$ under optimized conditions. Moreover, when 1a reacts with a more sterically hindered alcohol 13, a higher yield $( 7 5 \% )$ of diene 14 could be obtained; however, diene 14 was not easily reduced because of its steric hindrance (Scheme 3b). These results indicate that the reaction may proceed via a 1,3-diene intermediate. Deuterium-labeling experiments were performed under standard reaction conditions to establish the veracity of the proposed mechanism; compound $\mathbf { 3 } \mathbf { a } \mathbf { - } \mathbf { D }$ was isolated in $7 4 \%$ yield; with $7 \%$ deuterium being incorporated at the $\mathrm { s p } ^ { 3 } \mathrm { C } 1$ of indene and $8 4 \%$ retained at C1 of the alcohol-generated chain (Scheme 3c). This result indicates that either deuterium or the hydrogen originating from the alcohol was transferred to the C1-position of the indene moiety in the hydride intermediate (see IV in Figure 1).[17]

Based on the above experiments and our previous DFT calculations,8b] a plausible catalytic cycle is proposed in Figure 1. Initially, the hydrogen of the hydroxyl group is transferred to one of the phenyl groups of the catalyst, forming intermediate I that has a cleaved $\mathrm { P h { - } I r }$ bond. Hydrogen transfer from the $\mathbf { 0 - C - H }$ bonds of the alcohol to iridium form Ir-dihydride and release aldehydes. The 1,3- diene generated by the base-promoted condensation between indene and aldehyde coordinates with the lesscrowded Ir center-this is key for the reduction of multisubstituted olefins. The selective migratory insertion of the Ir-hydride bond to the exo double bond is favored and produces the allylic $\mathrm { I r } ^ { \mathrm { I I I } }$ intermediate IV. The reductive elimination of $\mathbf { I V }$ finally affords the C3-alkylated indene product 3 and releases the $\mathrm { I r } ^ { \mathrm { I } }$ species V. To complete the catalytic cycle, the $\mathrm { I r } ^ { \mathrm { I } }$ in $\mathbf { V }$ is re-oxidatively added into the phenyl $\mathrm { s p } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { C - H }$ to recover the catalyst.

In summary, we harnessed the hemilabile properties of our $\mathrm { S N I r { - } H }$ catalyst, and successfully developed a facile benzylic alkylation of various arenes (54 examples) with excellent regioselectivity and good to high yields (up to $8 9 \%$ ). Formal $\mathrm { s p } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { C } 3$ -alkylated indenes and other $\mathrm { s p } ^ { 3 } \ : \mathrm { C - H }$ alkylated arenes could be efficiently synthesized from easily available primary and secondary alcohols. This method was utilized for the preparation of biologically active molecules and functional material templates. The applicability of this SNM pincer complex to other challenging transformations is ongoing.
