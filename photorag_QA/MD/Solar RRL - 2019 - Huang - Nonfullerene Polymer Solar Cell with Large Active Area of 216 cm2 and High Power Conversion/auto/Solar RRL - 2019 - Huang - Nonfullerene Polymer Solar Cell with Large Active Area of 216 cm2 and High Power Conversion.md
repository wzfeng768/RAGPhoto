# Nonfullerene Polymer Solar Cell with Large Active Area of 216 cm? and High Power Conversion Efficiency of 7.7%

Kuan-Min Huang, Chih-Ming Lin, Szu-Han Chen, Cheng-Sian Li, Chen-Hsuan Hu, Yu Zhang, Hsin-Fei Meng,\* Chih-Yu Chang,\* Yu-Chiang Chao, Hsiao-Wen Zan,\* Lijun Huo, and Peichen Yu

A polymer solar cell involving a nonfullerene acceptor is made by blade coating. In the ternary bulk-heterojunction layer, the donor is poly[(2,6-(4,8-bis(5-(2-ethylhexyl)thiophen-2-yl)benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b'ldithiophene))-co-(1,3-di(5-thiophene-2-yl)- 5,7-bis(2-ethylhexyl)benzo[1,2-c:4,5-c'ldithiophene-4,8-dione)] (PBDB-T) and the acceptor is a mixture of 3,9-bis(2-methylene-(3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)-indanone))- 5,5,11,11-tetrakis(4-hexylphenyl)-dithieno[2,3-d:2',3'-d']-s-indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b'] dithiophene) (ITIC) and [6,6]-phenyl $\mathsf { C } _ { 7 1 }$ -butyric acid methyl ester $( P C _ { 7 1 } B M )$ . The device structure is an indium tin oxide (ITO)-coated glass substrate/PEDOT:PSS/ ternary active layer/interfacial layer/Al. For a small active area of $\scriptstyle 0 . 0 4 \ c m ^ { 2 }$ , the best power conversion efficiency is $9 . 8 \%$ with the LiF interfacial layer. For a large active area of $2 1 6 \ c m ^ { 2 }$ , the best efficiency is $7 . 7 \%$ with the ${ \sf z r o x }$ interfacial layer. After annealing at ${ \bf 8 5 } ^ { \circ } { \bf C }$ for 30 days, the large-area device keeps $75 \%$ of the initial efficiency. The efficiency of $4 . 9 \%$ is achieved for a large-area semi-transparent device.

# 1. Introduction

Polymer solar cells (PSCs) combine the advantages of simple solution fabrication, an absence of toxic heavy metals, relatively good stability, and the choice of semi-transparency. It is a promising green technology for application in the self-powered greenhouse and building-integrated photovoltaics. Power conversion efficiency (PCE) has been one shortcoming of PSCs. In recent years, the development of nonfullerene electron acceptor has opened a new direction for PSCs; the efficiency involving nonfullerene acceptor and polymer donor has reached over $1 3 \%$ for a single junction!1-4] In the fullerene acceptor, such as [6,6]-phenyl ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { 7 1 }$ -butyric acid methyl ester $( \mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M } )$ , there is little room for the tuning of the lower unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level by the molecular design. However, for the nonfullerene acceptor, the LUMO level can be raised to increase the open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ . The absorption of the visible is also stronger in the nonfullerene acceptor as its bandgap is lower than the fullerenes. The fused-ring core facilitates the carrier transport, and the end group can tune the electronic levels. The excessive aggregation is avoided by the bulky side groups.5] Studies have shown that fullerene materials will aggregate when subjected to high temperature, and the use of nonfullerene materials can greatly improve morphological stability in blend films.[6]

So far most reports on nonfullerene PsCs are for small areas below $1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } , ^ { \dag 1 - 4 , 7 - 1 0 } $ and spin-coating is used to deposit the active layer containing the polymer donor and nonfullerene acceptor. It is well-known that the microscopic morphology and phase separation of the active layer are sensitive to the film coating methods. Furthermore, the suitable level of phase separation between the donor and acceptor is critical to the exciton dissociation and the overall PCE. A PSC with fullerene acceptor has been shown to keep $7 0 \%$ of the small-area devices efficiency as the area is extended from $0 . 0 4 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ to over $5 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ [11-13] However, there is a fundamental difference in the molecular packing for the two types of acceptor as the fused-ring in the nonfullerene acceptor may cause a higher tendency of packing in the drying process. The details of the packing may be quite different for small area spin coating and other scalable coating methods suitable for large areas. To make the nonfullerene acceptors practical in applications, it is necessary that the high efficiency of the small area can be scaled up to the large area without much loss. There is so far only a few reports on this scaling.

In this work, we study a large-area nonfullerene PSC by the blade coating method,[13] which has been proved successful for nonfullerene cases. By careful control of the coating conditions on the glass substrate of $2 0 \mathrm { c m } \times 3 0 \mathrm { c m }$ (A4 size), we achieve a remarkable PCE of $7 . 6 9 \%$ for an active area of $2 1 6 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ . To our knowledge, this represents the highest reported efficiency for PSCs with the active area more than $\bar { 1 0 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ as shown in Figure 1c. We organize the progress for fullerene-based PSCs (active area larger than $1 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ [11-23] nonfullerene-based PSCs (active area larger than. $1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ 24-30] and ternary PSCs (active area larger than $1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } ) ^ { [ 3 1 , 3 2 ] }$ in Table 1. A ternary blend is used for the active layer, including a polymer donor poly[(2,6-(4,8- bis(5-(2-ethylhexyl)thiophen-2-yl)benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b'ldithiophene)) co-(1,3-di(5-thiophene-2-yl)-5,7-bis(2-ethylhexyl)benzo[1,2-c:4,5-c'] dithiophene-4,8-dione)] (PBDB-T), a nonfullerene acceptor 3,9- bis(2-methylene-(3-(1,1-dicyanomethylene)-indanone))-5,5,11,11- tetrakis(4-hexylphenyl)-dithieno[2,3-d:2',3'-d']-s-indaceno[1,2-b:5,6-b'] dithiophene) (ITIC), and the fullerene acceptor $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ . The fullerene acceptor is shown to improve the efficiency and stability (Supporting Information). For the small active area of $0 . 0 4 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ the efficiency is $9 . 6 3 \%$ by blade coating for the ternary blend. In other words, the large-area device keeps about $80 \%$ of the smallarea device efficiency. This demonstrates that the nonfullerene acceptor can be used for practical applications. The devices exhibit good long-term stability as well. The PCE still maintains $86 \%$ of the initial efficiency for 140 days at room temperature, $86 \%$ for 35 days at $6 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and $7 3 \%$ for 30 days at $8 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ thermal cycling test. This large-area ternary PSC has a good thermal stability and is also made as semi-transparent (ST).

# 2.3. Thermal Stability

Long-term stability is another key requirement for the commercial deployment of PsCs. We further investigated the thermal stability of the devices, which were tested with encapsulation in the ambient air on the hotplate with three different conditions room temperature, 65, and $8 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ thermal cycling as shown in the inset of Figure 1c. According to the consensus reached by the consortia of the international summit on PsC stability, several procedures for testing equipment stability have been established.137,38] In this work, our methods are in accordance with international summit on OPV stability-thermal cycling test (ISOs-T-1) testing protocols.(37] The devices were tested with encapsulation in the ambient air on a hotplate with three different conditions, room temperature, 65, and $8 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ thermal cycling. The thermal cycling here means a test at a fixed temperature. The ambient conditions are defined as $2 7 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and $65 \%$ relative humidity. Figure 5a-c shows the change of small-area opaque devices' normalized PCE as a function of the thermal cycling lifetime. After 130 days room temperature lifetime, Device $\mathsf { A } _ { 1 }$ (with LiF) dropped to about $7 2 \%$ of the initial PCE. However, Device $\mathtt { B } _ { 1 }$ (with Liq) and Device $\mathrm { C _ { 1 } }$ (with $\mathrm { Z r O x } )$ retained $90 \%$ and $100 \%$ of their initial PCE, respectively, as shown in Figure 5a. As we go further into the thermal stability test of these devices, the thermal cycling temperature was increased to 65 and $8 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ .After 42 days, Device $\mathtt { B } _ { 1 }$ and Device $\mathrm { C _ { 1 } }$ retained $84 \%$ and $8 8 \%$ of their initial PCE, but Device $\mathsf { A } _ { 1 }$ dropped to about $2 5 \%$ of the initial PCE at $6 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ thermal cycling as shown in Figure 5b. In sharp contrast, the PCE of Device $\mathsf { A } _ { 1 }$ showed a significant drop (drop to about $6 2 \%$ of the initial PCE) at the initial stage after $^ { 4 \mathrm { h } }$ and did not work after 8 days at $8 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ thermal cycling. The ternary blend materials include PBDB-T, ITIC, and $\mathsf { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathsf { B M }$ The PBDB-T:ITIC: $\mathsf { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathsf { B M }$ based ternary devices have been reported for inverted $\mathsf { P S C S } ^ { [ 4 4 ] }$ and for normal (conventional) ${ \mathsf { P S C S } } ^ { }$ , both show good photovoltaic performance. Such ternary concepts have been widely reported for two-acceptor ${ \mathsf { D } } { : } { \mathsf { A } } _ { 1 } { : } { \mathsf { A } } _ { 2 }$ type and two-donor $\mathsf { D } _ { 1 } { : } \mathsf { D } _ { 2 } { : } \mathsf { A }$ type. Compared to binary PSCs, the ternary PSCs has broader absorption spectrum and exhibit better photovoltaic parameters are shown in Figure S5 and Table S3, Supporting Information. We also find that this ternary PSCs show better stability than binary PSCs at room temperature lifetime.

The interfacial layer between the active layer and the electrode is also one of the main factors affecting efficiency and stability. By using a suitable interfacial layer, the formation of the ohmic contact is facilitated and the charge accumulation is effectively reduced at the interface. The polar interfacial layer reduces the work function of the upper electrode. In addition to improving the electrical characteristics of the device, stability can also be significantly improved. There are many studies on the interfacial layer materials, such as LiF, ${ \sf C s } _ { 2 } { \sf C O } _ { 3 }$ , CsF and titanium chelate (TIPD).[50-52] In this work, we employed three different interfacial layer materials LiF, 8-hydroxyquinoline lithium (Liq) and  Zirconium acetylacetonate (ZrAcac, prepared ZrAcac thin layer is named as ZrOx layer). The material Liq is often used as electron transport and hole blocking material in OLED.53] The ZrAcac is successfully employed as an electron extraction layer in perovskite solar cells and Psc.54-56 It has an advantage of solution processability using protic solvents such as isopropanol (IPA) and has the proper energy level is good for forming a good ohmic contact with minimal resistance.

Large-Area Device Design: The $R _ { S }$ of ITO causes the horizontal voltage drop as the current flow through ITO. The horizontal voltage drop dV and the vertical current density $J$ are related by ${ \mathsf { d } } V = \mathsf { R } _ { \mathsf { S } } J L ^ { 2 }$ , where $L$ is the width of the cell.57] For a typical PSC current density of $\mathsf { 1 5 \mathsf { m A } c m } ^ { - 2 }$ dV is $0 . 0 5 3 \vee$ with a width of 1 cm. Such voltage drop is much smaller than the typical $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ around $_ { 0 . 6 \vee }$ of the solar cell, so the energy loss is acceptable. On the contrary, for the width of $_ { 2 \mathsf { c m } }$ dV becomes $0 . 2 1 2 \vee$ , which is comparable for the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and the loss due to ITO $R _ { S }$ is detrimental. This is the reason we choose the optimal cell width to be around $\mathsf { 1 c m \ ( 0 . 9 c m ) }$ . For an even smaller cell width, the areas occupied by the inactive cell-gaps around $\mathsf { 1 } \mathsf { m m }$ width will cause energy loss due to aperture ratio. More details on the effect of the cell width design has been reported before.58] The large-area device design has also been reported before.l13] The top view and the side view of the schematic diagrams for the large-area device design are shown in Figure 4d,e. There are 16 cell stripes connected in series on the $2 8 0 \mathsf { m m } \times 2 0 0 \mathsf { m m }$ prepatterned ITO glass substrate. The gap between each stripe (width of $1 2 m m$ and a length of $1 7 5 \ : \mathsf { m m }$ is $2 \mathsf { m m }$ . The cathode of the left cell is connected to the ITO of the right cell to form a series connection. This large-area device has a total active area of $2 7 6 \mathsf { c m } ^ { 2 }$ . When connected in a series, current flows through each cell, and each cell is critical to the performance of the entire device. Therefore, to ensure that each cell connected in series performs well, flm uniformity is the most important requirement.
