S energy harvesting technologies.-+ In one embodiment, polymer solar cells (PsCs) are fabricated by blending a ptype (electron-donor) polymer and an n-type (electronacceptor) small-molecule semiconductor to create a bulkheterojunction (BHJ) photoactive layer.4 For more than a decade, the search for new donor polymers and/or fine-tuning of promising backbones has dominated strategies for advancing the field,? whereas the superiority of fullerenes as acceptors seemed unassailable.3 However, nonfullerene acceptors (NFAs) have recently enabled PSCs with power conversion efficiencies $\left( \mathrm { P C E s } \right) ^ { 5 - 7 }$ as high as $1 6 \% ^ { 1 }$ via mechanisms that are not entirely understood.-14

During the past 5 years, many hundreds of successful and unsuccessful NAs hve been reported $5 , 7 , 1 2 - 1 5$ Among them, indacenodithienothiophene (IDTT)7 and perylenediimide $\mathrm { ( P D I ) } ^ { 1 6 , 1 7 }$ acceptors frequently outperform fullerenes' and many other NFAs.13 Compared to IDTTs, which are only accessible via complex, multistep syntheses18,19 and possess labile end-groups,?2 PDIs are readily synthesized robust alternatives (the cores are used in automotive paints) that exhibit efficient and stable electron transport in ambient,20,21 have unique photophysical and spintronic properties,22,23 and intense, high-energy visible range optical absorption. The latter is attractive because the majority of polymers developed for fullerene-based PSCs have absorption complementary to that of PDIs. Despite these attractions, PDI-based PSCs are far less investigated than IDTT acceptors, with only a handful enabling PCEs $\geq 9 \%$ 6,17,24-28 While these empirical results demonstrate that synthetic modulation of PDIs can significantly enhance solar cell performance, the fundamentals underlying PSC differences between PDIs are not well understood. While only one system has provided insight into PDI energetics and photophysics by probing exciton and charge dynamics via photoinduced electron transfer,17 broader analysis of a BHJ blend nanostructure and its effects on these parameters is lacking. BHJ morphology remains a major challenge for PDIbased PSC development, especially considering the pronounced tendency of PDIs to aggregate and form large crystalline domains, which risks excessive BHJ phase segregation, reduced charge mobilities, and lower performance.9-31 Although previous studies have adressed this ssue using twisted geometry PDIs intended to suppress aggregation and optimize nanoscale BHJ morphology,26,32 such strategies risk impure BHJ domains that inhibit phase segregation and reduce carrier generation and mobility." 24,29,31 Therefore, it is imperative to explore alternatives to optimizing PDI-based BHJ active layer morphology.

Fluorinating semiconducting polymers effectively lowers frontier molecular orbital (FMO) energies, enhances environmental stability, increases backbone planarity, promotes denser packing and hence crystallinity and mobility, and enhances self-assembly through intermolecular CF...H, F..S, and $\operatorname { F } { \cdots } \pi$ interactions.3-36 While such donor polymer fuorination effects have proven promising in modifying BHJ fullereneand IDTT-based PSC performance, the application to PDI PSCs has been barely explored with respect to BHJ film morphology, charge carrier dynamics, and device performance.33

Here we investigate fluorination effects on BHJ performance by comparing/contrasting the fluorine-poor donor polymer PBDTT-FTTE with the fluorine-rich analogue PBDTTFFTTE (Figure 1A), paired for the first time with a twisted PDI acceptor, $\mathbf { P h ( P D I ) } _ { 3 }$ (Figure 1A).26 The present systems are characterized experimentally by optical absorption spectroscopy, cyclic voltammetry, ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS), photovoltaic response, space-charge-limited current (SCLC), AFM, TEM, GIWAXS, and fs/ns transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy and theoretically with groundstate and excited-state electronic structure to probe local differences arising from backbone fluorination. The data reveal that additional fluorination dramatically modifies BHJ morphology and semiconductor phase separation, leading to higher electron mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } \right)$ , longer free charge (FC) lifetimes, higher FC yields, and lower FC bimolecular recombination (BR) in PBDTTF-FTTE: $\mathbf { P h } ( \mathbf { P D I } ) _ { 3 }$ blends, affording significantly enhanced PSC performance. Moreover, the computed dipole differences between the ground and excited state, $p _ { \mathrm { e g } }$ is known to be associated with lower exciton yields.Here our computed nterpreation of polymer binding energies and hence greater FC lifetimes and $p _ { \mathrm { e g } }$ trends requires systems larger than dimers, and the dipolar orientation angle between the two moments provides a key measure of polarization forces acting against the Coulomb potential,40 especially when polymer backbone conformations are nearly identical. We find that PBDTTF-FTTE has overall larger dipole moments due to the heavier fluorination and also exhibits antiparallel directionality between the excited- and ground-state dipoles--an arrangement that should afford lower exciton binding energies and higher FC lifetimes due to less recombination, in agreement with experimental results.

Charge photogeneration and recombination are central limits to PSC efficiency and remain unresolved in most NFA-based PSCs,59-61 especially in PD1s.,17,61 Femtosecond transient absorption (fsTA) and nanosecond transient absorption (nsTA) spectroscopies in vacuo along with light intensity dependence experiments were employed here to probe differences in charge photogeneration yields and recombination mechanisms for the first time in PDI-based PSCs.17 Charge separation was monitored by TA of the $\mathbf { P h } ( \mathbf { P D I } ) _ { 3 }$ bleach at $5 2 5 ~ \mathrm { { n m } }$ following BHJ blend excitation at $7 0 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure 4A,B). Because $\mathbf { P h ( P D I ) } _ { 3 }$ does not absorb at $7 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { \ n m } }$ charge generation upon photoexcitation at this wavelength must occur purely by electron transfer (polymer $\mathbf { P h } ( \mathbf { P D I } ) _ { 3 } )$ , with negligible probability of Forster resonant energy transfer. Full blend data sets were globally fit at selected wavelengths to two successive first-order decays and a longlived bimolecular process attributed to FC recombination (see the SI). Multiple decay components are common in PSC systems because disordered morphologies can have several populations.60 Ultrafast electron transfer is observed in each blend, with the fastest component having a time constant $<$ 500 fs, far faster than previously reported (3 ps).17 Additionally, both the PBDTT-FTTE: $\mathbf { P h } ( \mathbf { P D I } ) _ { 3 }$ and PBDTTFFTTE: $\mathbf { P h } ( \mathbf { P D I } ) _ { 3 }$ blends behave similarly at early times, indicated in the full fsTA data sets (Figures S24-S30). The internal polarization arising from changes in excited- and ground-state dipole moments was computed because the degree of intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) and, hence, the exciton binding energy are related to the difference between the ground- $\left( { p _ { \mathrm { g } } } \right)$ and excited-state $\left( \boldsymbol { p } _ { \mathrm { e } } \right)$ dipole moments $( \Delta p _ { \mathrm { g e } } )$ 37,38,64 The expectation is that the larger $\Delta p _ { \mathrm { g e } }$ correlates with local charge separation and therefore leads to a greater likelihood of charge generation. While previous studies focused on monomers or dimers,38,6S here the dipole moments for an oligomer series, monomertetramer, $N = 1 { - } 4 _ { }$ were computed because larger oligomers (trimers/tetramers) were found necessary for meaningful dipole comparisons. Additionally, determining differences in dipole orientation between $p _ { \mathrm { g } }$ and $p _ { \mathrm { e } }$ was found critical in elucidating $\Delta p _ { \mathrm { g e } }$ trends. Trends for the PBDTT-FTTE and PBDTTF-FTTE $N = 1 { - } 4$ oligomers are compiled in Table 2 (see geometries for each in Figures S35-S38). Note the abrupt rise in $\Delta p _ { \mathrm { g e } }$ and change in dipole orientation difference between ground and excited states $( \theta _ { \mathrm { e g } } )$ for $N \ > \ 2$ We attribute this change in dipole orientation to the backbone curvature of the cis inward-oriented FTTE units (Figure 5). Interestingly, the PBDTTF-FTTE trimer and tetramer show antiparallel ground- and excited-state dipole orientations, ideal for exciton scission. As with previous dipole calculations that compare the performance of fluorinated and nonfluorinated acceptors and polymers $^ { 3 7 , 3 8 , 6 4 , 6 5 }$ the magnitude of $\Delta p _ { \mathrm { g e } }$ is also greater for PBDTTF-FTTE compared to that of PBDTTFTTE due to the overall larger dipole moments of both $p _ { \mathrm { g } }$ and $p _ { \mathrm { e } }$ (see Table 2 for comparison). Yet, even if only the magnitude of $\Delta p _ { \mathrm { g e } }$ mattered for the efficacy of exciton separation in a given polymer (which for the 1D exciton transport polarization direction certainly does matter), in order for PBDTT-FTTE to have the same $\Delta p _ { \mathrm { g e } }$ as PBDTTF-FTTE, it would require a ${ \sim } 2 . 5 \times$ larger $p _ { \mathrm { g } }$ or $p _ { \mathrm { e } }$ The fluorination of the thiophene side-chains in this study changes the dipole magnitudes of $p _ { \mathrm { g } }$ or $p _ { \mathrm { e } }$ by at most only $1 . 5 \times$ The cis configuration is the minimum-energy configuration for both polymers (Figures S43-S46), with the PBDTTF-FTTE oligomer demonstrating slightly greater stability. Pronounced curvature in polymer backbones is known to depress hole mobility due to suboptimal flm morphologies and increased disordert6,67 This may explain the modest PBDTTFFTTE: $\mathbf { P h } ( \mathbf { P D I } ) _ { 3 }$ $\mathrm { \bf ~ D I } ) _ { 3 } \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } = 1 3 . 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { \bf ~ V } ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 }$ versus PBDTT-FTTE $: \mathbf { p h } ( \mathbf { p D I } ) _ { 3 } ~ \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } = 3 0 . 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } ~ \mathrm { c m ^ { 2 } ~ V ^ { - 1 } ~ s ^ { - 1 } }$ In contrast, modest curvatures afford more favorable domain dimensions and hence smaller BR values.67 In a solid film environment, there will certainly be a distribution of torsion angles with some conformations of torsions in the trans orientation, i.e., the FTTE group alternating up and down.

This will lead to a dispersion in dipole moments,68 but when including interchain exciton diffusion, there will be pathways to avoid trans domains.69,70

We next investigated exciton delocalization for each oligomer to probe the degree of electron-hole charge separation within the exciton. The electron-hole $( R _ { \mathrm { e h } } ^ { \mathrm { ~ \tiny ~ \cdot ~ } } )$ separation distance is ${ \sim } 2 0 \%$ greater for $N > 2$ PBDTTFFTTE (see the SI for details). Together with the greater dipole difference between the ground state (higher $\Delta p _ { \mathrm { g e } }$ and $\theta _ { \mathrm { e g } }$ in Table 2) in PBDTTF-FTTE affording reduced geminate recombination, this analysis provides a plausible explanation for the higher FC yield and greater $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ in the PBDTTFFTTE: $\mathbf { P h ( \bar { P } D I ) } _ { 3 }$ films.

In summary, two polymer donors, fluorine-poor PBDTTFTTE and fluorine-rich PBDTTF-FTTE, are blended with the acceptor $\mathbf { P h } ( \mathbf { P D I } ) _ { 3 }$ to investigate donor backbone fluorination effects in PDI-based BHJ PSCs. An unexpected, remarkable PCE increase from $5 . 9 3 \%$ for PBDTT-FTTE: $\mathbf { P h } ( \mathbf { P D I } ) _ { 3 }$ to $9 . 1 0 \%$ for PBDTTF-FTTE: $\mathbf { P h } ( \mathbf { P D I } ) _ { 3 }$ is achieved, while such a PCE increase is not observed in the analogous pairs, PBDTTFTTE:ITIC-Th vs PBDTTF-FTTE:ITIC-Th (8.02 vs $7 . 9 0 \%$ respectively)43 and PBDTT-FTTE: $\mathbf { P C _ { 7 1 } B M } ^ { 4 3 , 4 4 }$ vs PBDTTFFTTE: $\mathbf { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathbf { B M }$ (8.21 vs $6 . 4 8 \%$ respectively). Detailed film morphological, carrier photogeneration, recombination, and transport analysis reveals that the latter material has more suitable semiconductor phase separation, larger $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } } ,$ longer-lived and higher yields of FC carriers, and slower bimolecular recombination than PBDTT-FTTE: $\mathbf { P h } ( \mathbf { P D I } ) _ { 3 }$ . Moreover, the lower-lying PBDTTF-FTTE HOMO affords a larger $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and a significantly lower $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of $0 . 5 9 ~ \mathrm { e V } ,$ among the lowest recorded for PsCs. Ground- and excited-state dipole orientation computation shows that fluorination alters the dipole orientation and reduces exciton binding energies. The PBDTTF-FTTE oligomers support nearly antiparallel groundand excited-state dipole moments, explaining the increased FC lifetimes and yields. Thus, pairing PDIs with highly fluorinated donor polymers is promising for enhancing PDI-based PSC performance.
