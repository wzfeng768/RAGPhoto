# Boosting Highly Effcient Hydrocarbon Solvent-Processed AllPolymer-Based Organic Solar Cells by Modulating Thin-Film Morphology

ABSTRACT: Many highly efficient all-polymer-based organic solar cells (OsCs) have been achieved owing to material design and device engineering. However, most of them were achieved by using halogenated solvents to process the active layers, being not beneficial to its nature of green energy technology. In this work, we compared chloroform- and toluene-processed PM6:PY-IT-based all-polymer devices with the same blend solution recipe, same film formation speed, and same postcast treatment. The film cast from toluene exhibited weaker crystallinity. For device performance, toluene enabled a better power conversion efficiency (PCE) of $1 5 . 5 1 \%$ , outperforming that of chloroform $\left( 1 5 . 0 0 \% \right)$ , and it is the highest value for non-halogenated solvent-cast all-polymer-based OSCs to date. Toluene's morphology tuning effect was characterized to increase and balance the charge transport and then suppres the exciton recombination and improve the charge extraction, considered to be the reason for eficiency enhancement. Besides, the toluene-cast active layer-based devices showed slightly better photostability than the chloroform-driven ones. This work provided a new direction for building low-toxicity solvent-treated all polymer OSCs with cutting-edge performance.

# INTRODUCTION

Solution-processed organic solar cells (OsCs) possess great advantages of low-cost production, flexibility, color tunability, and semitransparency, endowing them with promising prospects in the future energy market as a novel third-generation photovoltaic (PV) technology.-1 For the future energy market, competitiveness of an OsC depends primarily on its power conversion effciency (PCE) and stability1-13 At present, polymer donor $\left( P _ { \mathrm { D } } \right) { \mathrm { - s m a l l } }$ molecular acceptor (SMA)-based solution-processed OSCs have presented multiple cases of over $1 8 \%$ PCE values, approaching other types of PVs, but the poor stability (operationally or mechanically) strongly undermines their marketing prospects.14-22 In contrast, OSCs with active layers composed of $\mathrm { \Delta } P _ { \mathrm { D } }$ and polymer acceptor $\left( P _ { \mathrm { A } } \right)$ are stable and efficient (although not as high as $1 8 \%$ for PCE) and have rapidly become the field's new hot topic.23-28 To date, over $1 6 \%$ efficiency has been achieved for $P _ { \mathrm { D } } { : } P _ { \mathrm { A } }$ based OSCs, indicative of this type of solar cells great potential.29-32

On the other hand, current cutting-edge PCEs for $P _ { \mathrm { D } } { : } S \mathbf { M } \mathbf { A } { - }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { D } } { : } P _ { \mathrm { A } }$ -based OSCs are rarely enabled by non-halogenated solution-processed active layers but mostly by those cast from chloroform (CF) and chlorobenzene (CB). To date, the best

PCE as high as $1 4 . 2 8 \%$ has been enabled by $^ o$ -xylene and a ternary system PBDB-T:PJ1:PJ2, whereas the second highest PCE of $1 3 . 8 \%$ for all-polymer-based OSCs processed by a halogen-free solvent, 2-methyltetrahydrofuran (2-MeTHF), was enabled by PTzBI-OF as Pp and PS1 as PA 334 In comparison with SMA-based counterparts treated with hydrocarbon solvents with PCE approaching $1 8 \%$ $P _ { \mathrm { D } } { : } P _ { \mathrm { A } }$ type solar cells still cannot achieve more than $1 5 \%$ PCE, although $1 6 \%$ efficiency has been reported for CF, which is an unsatisfactory status quo.35-42 Once a cutting-edge PCE can be achieved for all-polymer-based OSCs by low-toxicity halogen-free solvents, the future of application would be much brighter.

In this work, we chose a previously reported groundbreaking all-polymer system of PM6:PY-IT with over $1 5 \%$ PCE processed by CF as the target because PM6 performs well in hydrocarbon solvents such as toluene, $^ o$ -xylene, and 1,2,4-trimethylbenzene and PY-IT is well soluble in these solvents as well.43 Hence, we selected toluene as our target solvent and prepared a corresponding blend solution with the same recipe as that of

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The structures of PM6 and PY-IT are shown in Figure 1a. These two materials we used in this work are the same batch produced in a previous study. With the help of previous understanding in constructing high-performance OSCs with a high-boiling-point solvent, we hereby chose to cast from a toluene blend solution when it was heated at $9 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and the substrates were also preheated at this temperature, imitating the film formation of CF-cast ones. The ultraviolet-visible-near-infrared (UV-vis-- NIR) absorption profiles with normalized intensity films cast from CF and toluene are illustrated in Figure 1b. Compared with CF, toluene induces a slightly blue-shifted edge and lower peak at 808 nm, implying that the aggregation of PY-IT is weaker.4,45

To further reveal the molecular packing change due to toluene's effect, we conducted grazing-incidence wide-angle Xray scattering (GIWAXS) experiments upon films from CF and toluene: Figure 1c lists 2D patterns and Figure 1d provides corresponding line-cut intensity profiles, and the parameters are summarized in Tables S1 and $S 2 . ^ { 4 6 - 4 8 }$ The lamellar peaks alongside in-plane (IP) direction of both CF- and toluene-cast layers locate at $q \sim 0 . 3 0 \mathring \mathrm { A } ^ { - 1 } .$ , corresponding to an identical dspacing value of 20.9 A. The CF-cast film demonstrates a 54.3 A crystal correlation length (CCL), while that of the toluene film is $4 2 . 1 \mathring \mathrm { A }$ for this peak. The $\pi - \pi$ stacking peaks of both active layers appear at $q \ \sim \ 1 . 6 7$ A alongside the out-of-plane (OOP) direction, simultaneously, with CCLs of 24.2 and $2 1 . 1 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ for CF and toluene, respectively. These results imply that the film cast from toluene has weaker crystallinity due to the morphology tuning effect of the solvent, which could result in better device performance.

To confirm the morphology change (crystallinity and aggregation) and gain a clearer insight, atomic force spectroscopy (AFM) measurements were carried out. In Figure 2, the AFM height image of the CF-cast film delivers a $1 . 5 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ root mean square (RMS), while that of the toluene-cast film is 1.23 nm, a slightly lower value. Furthermore, we can interpret that phase separation induced by aggregation is suppressed in toluene film compared with the CF coat one, which is consistent with the conclusions based on absorption and GIWAXS tests. Smoother surface of the toluene film might lead to more efficient charge extraction at interface.

After figuring out the morphology change brought about by changing the solvent from CF to toluene, we fabricated a series of devices to screen the performance variation, whose structure is ITO/PEDOT:PSS/PM6:PY-IT/PNDIT-F3N/Ag,49 as shown in Figure 3a. Then, we used a sequence of photophysical characterizations to reveal the reason for performance improvement and confirm the conjecture proposed based on the morphology analysis above. The hole and electron mobilities $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } \mathsf { a n d } \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ of the active layers were evaluated by measuring hole-only and electron-only devices, respectively, and fitting the data based on the Mott-- Gurney model. Their $J { - } V$ curves are illustrated in Figure $^ { 4 a , \mathrm { b } }$ The $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ values of CF and toluene films are 8.31 and $9 . \bar { 2 } 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ while their $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ values are 4.76 and $5 . 5 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ $\mathrm { { V } ^ { - 1 } } \ : s ^ { - 1 } ;$ respectively, Accordingly, their effective mobilities are 6.05 and $6 . \dot { 9 } 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ respectively.57 Then, we can say that the toluene-processed active layer shows enhanced charge transport, although its thin-film crystallinity is weakened. Notably, it is usually claimed that crystallinity is positively correlated with mobilities, but the truth is that comprehensive morphology is the dominator, including not only crystallinity

but also other factors, e.g., phase-separation length scale, domain purity, and domain size's diversity. To figure out the exact reason, we used a transmission electron microscope (TEM) to observe the active layers cast from CF and toluene. The images in Figure S1 show that the toluene-processed film contains fewer. large phases but purer, which contributes to charge transport enhancement. Next, we calculated the charge dissociation and collection efficiencies ( $\chi _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $\eta _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } )$ of each through figuring $( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \mathrm { - } V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ $^ { 5 8 - 6 0 } J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$   
illumination $\left( J _ { \mathrm { L } } \right)$ minus the dark current density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { D } } \right)$ , and $V _ { 0 }$ refers to the voltage value when $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } = 0$ Accordingly, $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } = V _ { 0 } -$ $V _ { \mathrm { a p p l } }$ where $V _ { \mathrm { a p p l } }$ represents the applied voltage, has a clear meaning. Importantly, when $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ reaches a high value $\scriptstyle ( > 2 \lor )$ , it is normally believed that the generated excitons are fully collected, in which $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ is equal to saturated current density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { { s a t } } } \right)$ . Then, we can calculate $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { { m a x } } } / J _ { \mathrm { { s a t } } }$ to describe efficiencies $\eta _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $\eta _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } . J _ { \mathrm { m a x } }$ is the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ at the maximal output point. The $( \eta _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } , \eta _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } ) s$ for CF and toluene devices are $\left( 9 1 . 6 \% , 7 9 . 4 \% \right)$ and $( 9 2 . 6 \% )$ $8 1 . 8 \%$ , respectively: a better charge extraction takes place in the toluene-processed device.

Then, the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ vs $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ vs. $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ relationships were analyzed to reveal the exciton recombination change in devices $\scriptstyle P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ refers to the incident light intensity) and are visualized in Figure 4d,e.1-63 The ratio of the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ in logarithm is equal to $n k T / q { : } q$ is the elementary charge, $k$ is the Boltzmann constant, and $T$ is the thermodynamic temperature. The linear slope S of $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ s and logarithmic $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ determines the degree of bimolecular recombination $\left( R _ { \mathrm { b m } } \right)$ : the closer the value of S to 1, the weaker the $R _ { \mathrm { { b m } } }$ The fitted $\boldsymbol { S } \boldsymbol { s }$ are 0.96 and 0.98 for CF- and toluene-processed active layer-based devices, respectively, indicative of slightly reduced $R _ { \mathrm { b m } } ,$ thanks to films cast from toluene. Usually, the $n$ value lies between 1 and 2. An $n$ value approaching 1 indicates that $R _ { \mathrm { { b m } } }$ takes the dominant position while trap state recombination $\left( R _ { \mathrm { t } , s } \right)$ is very weak and vice versa when n comes close to 2. The fitted $n$ values for devices cast from CF and toluene are 1.20 and 1.14, respectively, meaning $R _ { \mathrm { t } , \mathrm { s } }$ takes a less dominant position in toluene film, although $R _ { \mathrm { { b m } } }$ is more suppressed. These results suggest that toluene devices contain a weaker exciton recombination, which is supportive of the increased $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF. In addition, we investigated the operational stability (under 1 sun light) of devices of this work (Figure 4f).64-66 The toluene-cast device exhibits improved stability under standard illumination in air compared with the CF-processed device.

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we applied toluene, a typical hydrocarbon solvent, to substitute the widely used solvent CF with higher toxicity and carcinogenicity, to process the all-polymer system PM6:PY-IT, which successfully improved the efficiency from 15.00 to $1 5 . 5 1 \%$ , enabled by enhanced $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF, which were the results of finely tuned morphology-induced better charge transport and extraction and suppressed exciton recombination. Our system attains the highest reported efficiency for nonhalogenated solvent-processed all-polymer-based OSCs without sacrificing operational stability, which is of great significance to this PV technology's application prospects. Besides, it is also instructive to future development of device engineering: trying more solvents brings more possibilities.

# EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES

UV-vis absorption spectra were measured using a Shimadzu UV-2500 recording spectrophotometer. AFM measurements were obtained using a Dimension Icon AFM (Bruker) in tapping mode. The grazingincidence X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) measurement was carried out with a Xeuss 2.0 SAXS/WAXS laboratory beamline using a Cu X-ray source $\left( 8 . 0 5 \mathrm { k e V } , 1 . 5 4 \mathring \mathrm { A } \right)$ and a Pilatus3R 300K detector. The incident angle was $0 . 2 ^ { \circ }$ The samples for GIWAXS/GISAXS measurements were fabricated on silicon substrates using the same recipe for the devices. Contact angle tests were achieved by an Attension Theta Flex meter.
