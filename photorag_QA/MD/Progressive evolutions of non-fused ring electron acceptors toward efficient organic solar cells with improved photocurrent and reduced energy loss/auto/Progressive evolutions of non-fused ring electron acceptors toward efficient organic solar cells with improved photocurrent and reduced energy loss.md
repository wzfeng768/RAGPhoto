Resolving competitive counterbalance between high external quantum efficiency (EQE) response and low energy 1oss is critical for achieving efficient organic solar cells (oscs), including those based on low-cost non-fused-ring electron acceptor (NFREA). In this work, we performed a comparable study by utilizing four NFREAs with progressive evolutions in molecular structures, energetic levels, spectral coverages, crystallinity, etc, to pair with polymer donor J52. By regulating the terminal and side chains of NFREAs, the enhanced intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) effect allows O-PC-EH possessing the broader spectral coverage for higher photocurrent, the reduced energetic offset enables O-PC-EH-based device owning higher luminescence property for lower energy loss, the strengthened crystallinity endows O-PC-EH-based blend better charge transport property for less charge recombination. With those comprehensive managements from molecule to blend properties, the J52:O-PC-EHbased OsCs achieved the best efficiency of $1 4 . 4 \%$ (certified: $1 4 . 1 \% )$ with benefits in all the three device parameters. This work reveals the importance of fine-tuning molecular structures of NFREAs according to paired polymer donor for maximizing the device performances.

# 1. Introduction

Since the emergence of narrow bandgap small molecule acceptors (SMAs), the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of organic solar cells (OSCs) have developed rapidly in recent years [1-9]. At present, the PCEs of single-junction OsCs have exceeded $1 9 \%$ ,reflecting the immense potential of OsCs for photovoltaic applications [10-19]. To achieve high performance, resolving competitive counterbalance among high external quantum efficiency (EQE) response, low energy loss and efficient charge transport is the key to handle with [20-23]. However, the current champion efficiencies were achieved based on fused-ring series SMAs, which require complicated synthetic steps, making it difficult for large-scale production. As counterparts, non-fused-ring electron acceptors (NFREAs) with broken fused-ring structures and easier synthesis procedures also catch the attention of researchers [24-34], though the efficiencies of NFREAs still lag behind the fusedring electron acceptors (FREAs). To drive the development of NFREAs, it's necessary to figure out how fine-tuning of molecular structures of NFREAs affects the relevant optoelectronic properties, which enables synergistic optimization in all the three device parameters, thus boosting the device efficiencies.

Early in 2018, our group demonstrated one of initial NFREAs by introducing noncovalent intramolecular interactions, e.g., F..H interactions, instead of the fused structure, yielding DF-PCIC [27]. Such molecular design simplified the synthetic procedure, but reserved planar geometry and comparable properties, i.e., absorption, energy levels, etc., to FREA of ITIC. As a result, over $1 0 \%$ efficiency was achieved in the PBDB-T:DF-PCIC-based OSCs with excellent thermal stability. However, the absorption coverage of DF-PCIC (onset: $7 8 0 \mathrm { n m }$ is still not enough to effectively utilize the photon in the near-infrared region, thus limiting the photocurrent of DF-PCIC-based device. Hence, further modifications of DF-PCIC are required to address the shortages of DF-PCIC for better photovoltaic performance.

Among various molecular modifications of acceptor-donor-acceptor (A-D-A) type SMAs, both terminal modification and side chain engineering are found to be effective in fine-tuning the optoelectronic properties [26,35]. For terminal modification, introducing halogen atoms, e.g., F, Cl, to the end-group is commonly used for enhancing the intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) effect and intermolecular noncovalent interactions, e.g., F..H, S..F, etc., which is conducive to extending the absorption, improving the crystallinity and facilitating charge transport [36-38]. The effectiveness of halogen atoms on the terminals has been certified in many works. For example, by fluorinating of ITIC on the terminals, the obtained IT-4F got enhancement in intermolecular electronic coupling for better charge transport and red-shifted absorption for higher photocurrent [6,39]. Also, Zou et al. attached fluorine atoms to non-halogenated Y5, the resulting Y6 got enhancement in crystallinity, thus enabling broad thickness tolerance [7,40].

In addition, side chain plays a critical role in affecting the molecular packing behavior and modulating the planarity of molecular skeleton [8,31,41-48]. One of typical examples is alkoxy chains, which not only can improve the planarity of molecular backbone by forming O..S or $\mathrm { O } { \cdot } { \cdot } \mathrm { H }$ noncovalent interaction, but also enhance the ICT effect through the electron-donating property of oxygen, thus being a good choice for extending spectral coverage [24,28,49-57]. Taking IEIcO as an example, when the inward alkyl chain on the thiophene ring of IEIC was replaced with alkoxy chain, the absorption edge was significantly redshifted from $8 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ for IEIC to $9 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ for IEICO by lifting the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOmO) level [58,59]. In another example, Yan et al. replaced one outward alkyl chain with alkoxy, yielding asymmetric Y6-1O, the interaction between alkoxy side chain and terminal unit strengthened the intermolecular packing, thus benefiting electron mobility for higher fill factor (FF), but the absorption is blue-shifted a little bit [60]. Anyway, alkoxy is a good tool for tuning the spectral coverage and aggregation property, but different orientation or position may bring different influences.

Based on the above backgrounds, we selected J52 as the matching polymer donor [61,62], then performed progressive evolutions of NFREAs by terminal and side chain engineering, including fluorination of terminal units from DF-PCIC to HF-PCIC, introduction of alkoxy from HF-PCIC to O-PC-OC, and branching of alkyl chain from O-PC-OC to OPC-EH. Above molecular engineering leads to gradually modulations in energetic levels, spectral coverage, and crystallinity properties, resulting

O-PC-EH as the optimal molecule to pair with J52, in which narrowed energetic offset reduced energy loss, extended absorption increased photocurrent, and enhanced crystallinity improved charge transport. As a result, the J52:O-PC-EH-based OSC possessed superiorities in all the three device parameters, leading to a best PCE of $1 4 . 4 3 \%$ (certified: $1 4 . 1 \%$ . This work demonstrates the importance of fine-tuning molecular structures of NFREAs according to one specific polymer donor, and competitive counterbalance between high photocurrent generation and low energy loss can be addressed to maximize the device performance, thus providing some guidance about the design of efficient NFREAs.

# 2. Results and discussion

# 2.1. Molecular design and quantum chemistry calculations

The chemical structures of four studied NFREAs (DF-PCIC, HF-PCIC, O-PC-OC, O-PC-EH) are presented in Fig. 1a. DF-PCIC and HF-PCIC were synthesized according to our previous reports [27,63], while the synthetic routes and detailed synthetic descriptions of O-PC-OC and O-PCEH can be found in the supplementary information (Fig. S1-s4). As depicted in Fig. 1a, HF-PCIC was obtained by fluorinating the end groups of DF-PCIC, and O-PC-X series NFREAs was designed and synthesized by further replacing the fluorine atoms on the core of HF-PCIC with different alkoxy groups: linear alkyl chain for O-Pc-OC and branched alkyl chain for O-PC-EH. In order to obtain the optimal geometry of molecules, density functional theory (DFT) theoretical calculations were performed at the B3LYP/6-31 G(d,p) level. As shown in Fig. 1b, all the molecules bear planar configurations with relatively small dihedral angles due to the formation of intramolecular F..H or O...S noncovalent interactions, which help lock the molecular geometry, and are identified in previous reported single crystal structures [26,51,64]. In addition, the electrostatic potential (EsP) distributions of molecules were also calculated (Fig. 1b), and the positive ESP distributions suggest the electron-withdrawing property of these NFREAs [65].

Then, external quantum efficiency (EQE) tests were carried out to cross-check the photocurrent generation, and the results are shown in Fig. 2e and Table 1. The EQE response range of HF-PCIC-based OSC is a bit broader than DF-PCIC-based OSC, consistent with the higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of HF-PCIC-based OSC than DF-PCIC-based one. Apparently, devices based on O-PC-X series NFREAs showed the broadest photo-response ranging from 300 to $9 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , beneficial for the utilization of absorption in the near-infrared region. Besides, higher photon-to-electron response in the range of $5 0 0 { - } 8 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ was presented in the O-PC-EH-based OSC, relative to O-PC-OC-based one, thus leading to a further increase in photocurrent. The integrated $J _ { \mathrm { c a l } }$ values from the EQE curves were 17.42, 19.07, 22.54 and $2 3 . 8 6 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for the OSCs based on J52:DF-PCIC, J52:HFPCIC, J52:O-PC-OC and J52:O-PC-EH, respectively, in accordance with those derived from their $J { - } V$ curves.

Photoluminescence (PL) quenching experiments are also employed to evaluate the charge transfer ability. Laser with wavelength of $6 9 0 \mathrm { n m }$ was utilized to selectively excite the acceptors. As shown in Fig. S8, J52: HF-PCIC blend exhibited the most efficient and complete quenching, which mainly due to the largest HOMO offset between donor and acceptor. What's more, J52:O-PC-EH blend exhibited much more efficient quenching than J52:O-PC-OC, which may be owing to the better crystallinity and molecular orientation that will be discussed later.

To figure out which type of charge recombination was the weakness for limiting the performance, the dependence of $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ on light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ were tested to explore the charge recombination process in devices (Fig. 2h and Fig. S9). The relationship between $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ can be described as $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \propto P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } ^ { \alpha } ,$ and the closer $\alpha$ value is to 1, the less component of bimolecular recombination exists. It turned out that all the three devices exhibited similar $\alpha$ values close to 1, implying the little extent of bimolecular recombination. The slopes $( n )$ obtained from the equation of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } \propto n k T / q \mathrm { l n } ( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ can be used as the indicator for identifying recombination behavior, larger $n$ represents the more proportional monomolecular recombination. It was found that the n of J52:OPC-EH blend is much lower than that of J52:DF-PCIC blend and J52:HFPCIC blend, indicating the effective suppression of monomolecular recombination, which may be owing to the formation of appropriate phase separation size and interpenetrating networks that will be discussed in the following blend morphology characterization.

To further explore the photon-to-electron process for understanding the differences in the device parameters, we studied the relationship between the photocurrent density $( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } )$ and effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ of the OSCs (Fig. 2f) to explore the bias-dependent exciton-dissociation and charge-collection behavior. $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ versus $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ curves were used to evaluate the exciton-dissociation efficiencies $( P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } )$ and charge-collection efficiencies $( P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } )$ in OSCs [69]. The results showed that the $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ of the J52:DF-PCIC-based device were the lowest among all the devices $( 9 0 . 6 6 \%$ and $6 7 . 3 6 \%$ , respectively), representing the inefficient charge generation and transfer progress, thus the lowest $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF. Compared with DF-PCIC-based OSC, the device based on HF-PCIC owns relatively higher $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ $9 4 . 1 6 \%$ and $7 3 . 9 6 \%$ , respectively), implying the fluorinated terminal benefits both charge separation and collection. Besides, further improvements in $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ were observed in the O

PC-X-based devices, in accordance with the higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF values of the corresponding devices, and the J52:O-PC-EH-based device performed the best with $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ of $9 6 . 0 3 \%$ and $8 1 . 8 6 \%$ , respectively, indicating the most efficient charge generation and transfer process, thus the best efficiency. Above results tell the truth that fine-tuning of NFREAs' molecular structures matter a lot for carrier dynamics management, in purpose of pursuing higher efficiencies.

# 2.3. Charge transfer and energy loss analysis

In the following parts, O-PC-EH with the better performance was selected as the representative of O-PC-X series molecules to compare with DF-PCIC and HF-PCIC, aiming to figure out how molecular evolutions of NFREAs affect the charge transport property, energy loss and morphological characteristics.

In order to investigate the charge-transport properties of the blend films, space-charge-limited current (SCLC) tests were conducted in holeonly devices (ITO/PEDOT:PSS/active layer/ $\mathrm { { M o O } _ { 3 } / A g }$ and electrononly devices (ITO/ZnO/active layer/PDINN/Ag) to measure the hole and electron mobilities (Fig. S10 and Table S3). All the three blend films exhibited similar hole mobilities $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } \right)$ of $4 . 5 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 }$ $4 . 1 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace s ^ { - 1 }$ and $5 . 2 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 }$ for J52:DF-PCIC, J52:HF-PCIC and J52:O-PC-EH blends, respectively. When it comes to electron mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } \right)$ , J52:DF-PCIC blend exhibited the lowest $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of $5 . 8 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 6 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \dot { \mathrm { V } } ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 }$ , resulting in a least balanced $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ ratio of 78.31, and J52:HF-PCIC blend exhibited a $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of $1 . 9 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 }$ $\mathsf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , giving a $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ ratio of 21.56. In contrast, J52:O-PC-EH blend possessed the highest $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of $1 . 1 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , resulting in the most balanced $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ ratio. We also measured and compared the electron mobilities of pristine acceptor films (Fig. S10 and Table S3), and O-PCEH possesses the highest value, but not a big advantage over DF-PCIC and HF-PCIC. The significantly better electron mobility in J52:O-PCEH blend could be strongly correlated with the crystallinity and molecular orientation, as discussed below.

We further conducted electrical characterizations in order to explore the mechanism of $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ amplification. The impedance spectroscopy measurement was performed in dark with the frequency ranging from 100 Hz to $2 \ : \mathrm { M H z }$ (Fig. S11). All the data was fitted using the circuit model shown in the inset, where $R _ { s }$ represents the series resistance from a number of contacting interfaces, while $R _ { \mathrm { t r a n s } }$ and $R _ { \mathrm { r e c } }$ are the transport and recombination resistances of the active layer. The device capacitor (C) accounts for the dielectric behavior of solar cells. Since OsCs follow nonideal behaviors due to the dispersive charge transport, a capacitorlike element named constant phase element (CPE) was introduced into the circuit model instead of the chemical capacitor to describe the imperfect physical process. Smaller $R _ { \mathrm { t r a n s } }$ and larger $R _ { \mathrm { r e c } }$ indicates better charge transport properties and less recombination. As shown in Table S4, J52:O-PC-EH blend exhibited the smallest $R _ { \mathrm { t r a n s } }$ and largest $R _ { \mathrm { r e c } } ,$ which is conducive to charge transfer, thus J52:O-PC-EH blend exhibiting the highest charge mobility. The results of impedance spectroscopy are consistent with the charge-transport properties demonstrated above.

The energy loss of OSCs based on J52:DF-PCIC, J52:HF-PCIC and J52:O-PC-EH were analyzed and the classifications are shown in Fig. 3a. The detailed energy loss data are summarized in Table 2. The optical bandgaps $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } )$ of all the blend films have been determined by the intersection between the normalized absorption and emission spectra, as shown in Fig. S12 [70]. The $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ values of J52:DF-PCIC, J52:HF-PCIC and J52:O-PC-EH blends were determined to be 1.661, 1.601, and $1 . 4 9 0 \mathrm { e V }$ respectively. Then according to the measured $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ values (denoted as $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ^ { \mathrm { m e a s } } )$ values, 0.867, 0.763 and 0.811 V for J52:DF-PCIC-, J52:HF-PCICand J52:O-PC-EH-based devices, respectively, the total voltage loss were obtained as $0 . 7 9 4 \mathrm { V }$ for J52:DF-PCIC-based device, 0.838 V for J52:HFPCIC-based device, and $0 . 6 7 9 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for J52:O-PC-EH-based device. To further analyze the composition of energy loss, the highly sensitive EQE (sEQE) of the solar cells was measured using a highly sensitive setup to determine $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { C T } } ,$ which is the energetic difference between the singlet excited state and charge transfer (CT) state, also known as the energetic offset. The sEQE curves and normalized EL spectra of devices were used to derive $E _ { \mathrm { C T } }$ (Fig. 3b-d). $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { C T } }$ values for J52:DF-PCIC-, J52:HF-PCICand J52:O-PC-EH-based devices were calculated as 0.151, 0.191 and $0 . 0 9 0 \ \mathrm { e V } _ { : }$ respectively. The smaller $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { C T } }$ value is suggestive of the smaller voltage loss related to the charge transfer process. Typically, the voltage loss of a solar cell can be divided into radiative recombination voltage loss $( \Delta V _ { \mathrm { r a d } } )$ and $\Delta V _ { \mathrm { n o n - r a d } }$ [70]. $\Delta V _ { \mathrm { n o n - r a d } }$ is a key factor in determining the final $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of the device, which can be calculated via the following equation: $\begin{array} { r } { \Delta V _ { \mathrm { n o n - r } a d } = \frac { k T } { q } \ln \left( \frac { 1 } { E Q E _ { E L } } \right) } \end{array}$ [71]. The electroluminescence quantum efficiency $( \mathrm { E Q E } _ { \mathrm { E L } } )$ experiments were also performed, as shown in Fig. 3e, device based on O-PC-EH exhibited the highest $\mathtt { E Q E } _ { \mathrm { E L } }$ value of $8 . 4 9 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 6 }$ , much higher than those of DF-PCIC-based device $( 1 . 7 5 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 6 } )$ and HF-PCIC-based device $( 3 . 1 8 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 7 } )$ , thus resulting in the lowest $\Delta V _ { \mathrm { n o n - r a d } }$ of 0.301 V. Above results indicate that reducing energetic offsets is beneficial for obtaining higher luminescence property and lower non-radiative loss, thus leading to a lower energy loss. And synergistic modifications via side chain and terminal engineering are effective approaches for energetic levels modulation to well balance reduced energetic offsets and efficient charge transfer.

We also carried out atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements to investigate the top surface morphological properties of the blend films (Fig. 5). From the height images, we could find out that J52:DF-PCIC blend film possessed the smoothest surface with the smallest meansquare surface roughness $( R _ { \mathrm { q } } )$ value of $1 . 5 4 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , while J52:HF-PCIC blend film possessed the roughest surface with the largest $R _ { \mathfrak { q } }$ value of $2 . 4 3 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , and J52:O-PC-EH blend film was in-between. Nonetheless, J52:DF-PCIC blend film exhibited large phase separation size, which will lead to severe monomolecular recombination. This is consistent with the lowest $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ of J52:DF-PCIC blend and explains the reason for the low $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF of J52:DF-PCIC-based OSC to some extent. For J52:HF-PCIC blend film, an obvious nanoscale phase-separation structure could be observed, implying stronger intermolecular aggregation. When it comes to J52:O-PC-EH blend film, fiber-like domains with appropriate size could be observed, indicating the formation of interpenetrating network, which is consistent with the highest $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ of J52:O-PCEH blend. Compared with J52:O-PC-EH blend film, the phase separation in the J52:DF-PCIC and J52:HF-PCIC blend film may be too large for mitigating monomolecular recombination and assisting exciton dissociation, which is consistent with the exciton dissociation and charge recombination demonstrated above, thus resulting in the low $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF for the corresponding devices. From the above results, we could deduce that O-PC-EH could form suitable phase separation with J52, which was conducive to exciton dissociation and charge-transfer process, thus resulting in the best device performance.

# 3. Conclusions

In conclusion, with J52 as the standard polymer donor, we explored how progressive evolutions of NFREAs affect the device performance, to figure out the matching criterion. In molecular design, it was found that energetic levels of NFREAs could be well managed by turning the electron-donating ability on the molecular core and electronwithdrawing ability on the terminal, the spectral coverage would be significantly extended by enhancing the ICT effect, and the crystallinity could be modulated by introducing steric hindrance groups. In device engineering, it was unveiled that narrowing energetic offset benefited a reduced non-radiative loss, thus a lower energy loss, and a wellconstructed fibril blend network with suitable phase separation benefited efficient charge generation, thus a higher photocurrent. Among four NFREAs, O-PC-EH achieved well balance in abovementioned issues, resolving the competitive counterbalance between high photo-response and low energy loss, thus leading to a best PCE of $1 4 . 4 3 \%$ (certified: $1 4 . 1 \%$ with optimized values in all the three device parameters. This work demonstrates that fine-tuning of NFREAs' molecular structure according to the properties of relevant polymer donors is critical for comprehensive carrier management, thus boosting the device performance.
