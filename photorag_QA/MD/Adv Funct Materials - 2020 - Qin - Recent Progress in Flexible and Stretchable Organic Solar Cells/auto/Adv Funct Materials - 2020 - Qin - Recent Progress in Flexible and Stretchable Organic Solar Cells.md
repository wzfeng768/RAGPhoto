Consequently, many high-performance flexible OsCs have been fabricated as shown in Table 1.

These high-performance flexible OsCs have demonstrated the potential in portable applications. However, functional textiles or wearable devices demand for flexibility as well as stretchability.[10] There are two common strategies used for fabricating stretchable OsCs. The first method is to adjust the structure of the devices, such as buckling (by adhering the active layers to the prestretched deformable substrate) or textiles, which is not intrinsically stretchable and mechanically compliant.[11] The limitation with this strategy is that the PCE of these devices based on buckling is still low. The second method focuses on utilizing different functional semiconducting materials in the active layer toward intrinsically stretchable OsCs. Bao and co-workers described intrinsically stretchable OSCs based on the two conjugated polymers blending with [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester $( \mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M } )$ for the first time, and the devices permit reversible stretchability.[10] Based on fullerene or other conjugated polymers, there are many outstanding works to explore intrinsically stretchable OsCs.[12]

Herein, we focus on the recent unprecedented growth in the use of flexible and stretchable OsCs. Firstly, a general overview of the development of the commonly used flexible electrodes and the performance of the flexible OsCs is explored. Secondly, two common strategies to fabricate stretchable OsCs based on nonintrinsic and intrinsic methods are discussed. Finally, the opportunities and challenges associated with these flexible and stretchable OsCs are discussed.

# 2. Flexible Organic Solar Cells

The main challenge associated with flexible OsCs is the inability to find excellent FTE. The following features are associated with the high-performance FTE: i) superior transparency which is needed to allow the photons to pass through and be absorbed by active layer; ii) high conductivity to reduce the series resistance of the device; iii) excellent mechanical flexibility to keep its performance after bending; iv) low surface roughness which is essential to avoid the leakage; and v) suitable work function to ensure efficient charge extraction.26] In the past decades, various efficient FTEs have been reported and developed, such as conventional ITO, conducting polymers, carbon materials, metal nanowires, and metal grids.

# 2.2. Flexible Organic Solar Cells Based on Conducting Polymers

The conducting polymer poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):- poly(styrene sulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) is another popular candidate for FTE due to its high conductivity $( 1 0 ^ { - 3 } { \mathrm { - } } 4 \times 1 0 ^ { 3 } { \mathrm { ~ S ~ c m } } ^ { - 1 } )$ excellent optical transparency $( > 9 0 \%$ at $5 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , tunable work function $( 4 . 8 \mathrm { - } 5 . 4 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ , low surface roughness, and superior flexibility.[43] As shown in Figure 3a, PEDOT:PSS is composed of conducting conjugated PEDOT and insulating PSS.[44] The conductivity of PEDOT:PSS can be changed by tuning the ratio of PEDOT to PSS. In OSCs, the PEDOT:PSS aqueous solutions can be used as to prepared hole transport layer (Clevios PVP AI 4083, with a PEDOT to PSS ratio of 1:6) and electrode (Clevi0s PH500, PH510, and PH1000, with a PEDOT to PSS ratio of 1:2.5).[45]

In 2002, Zhang et al. firstly replaced the ITO with PEDOT:PSS and modified PEDOT:PSS (doping with glycerol and sorbitol) to fabricate the rigid OsCs.[46] These results demonstrate the possibility of using PEDOT:PSS as electrode for OSCs. Later, Aernouts et al. deposited PEDOT:PSS on PET substrates to fabricate FTE for $\mathrm { O S C s . } ^ { [ 4 7 ] }$ The flexible device showed a low short-circuit current $( \boldsymbol { I _ { \mathrm { s c } } } )$ of $5 . 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ A. After the introduction of a metallic $\mathrm { A g }$ grid between PET and PEDOT:PSS, the device exhibited an enhanced $I _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $1 . 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 }$ A due to the improved conductivity of the electrode. This result indicates that the low conductivity of PEDOT:PSS electrode severely limits the performance of the device. To improve the conductivity of PEDOT:PSS electrodes, many efforts have been made.

Kim's group firstly fabricated high conductivity PEDOT:PSS (PH500) electrode on glass and PET substrates by doping $5 \%$ dimethyl sulfoxide (DMsO).[48] Compared with pure PH500 $( < 1 \mathrm { ~ \ : S ~ } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ , the modified PH500 showed a relatively high conductivity $( 5 5 0 ~ \mathrm { { S } ~ c m ^ { - 1 } } )$ . When prepared on glass and PET substrate, it exhibited a PCE of $3 . 2 7 \%$ and $2 . 8 \%$ , respectively, which could be comparable to the ITO-based devices $( 3 . 6 6 \%$ and $2 . 9 \%$ for glass and PET substrates, respectively). And the modified PH5o0-based flexible device exhibited superior mechanical robustness than that of ITO-based one, of which efficiency remained nearly constant after 300 bending cycles with a radius of $8 \ \mathrm { m m }$ . # 2.3. Flexible Organic Solar Cells Based on Carbon Materials

Recently, carbon materials have attracted enormous interests at flexible OsCs due to its high conductivity, superior transparency, excellent stability, and good flexibility. The commonly used carbon materials FTE for flexible OsCs include one dimensional carbon nanotubes (CNTs) and 2D graphene.

# 2.3.1. Graphene

In 2004, Novoselov was the first to report 2D graphene material using mechanical exfoliation (repeated peeling) method.[62] Graphene has a 2D structure of $\mathsf { s p } ^ { 2 }$ -hybridized carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal honeycomb lattice. Graphene is an excellent candidate for FTE due to its high electron mobility $( 2 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ , high electrical conductivity $( 1 0 ^ { 4 } \ \mathrm { S \ c m ^ { - 1 } } )$ excellent optical transparency $( 9 7 . 7 \% )$ , and remarkable mechanical properties (with Young's modulus and intrinsic strength of 1100 and $1 2 5 \ \mathrm { G P a }$ , respectively).[63] Graphene can be fabricated using several techniques, such as mechanical exfoliation, sonication-induced exfoliation, laser-induced exfoliation, chemical vapor deposition (CVD), and reduction of graphene oxide (rGO).[64] Considering the mass production, CVD and rGO are widely used.

Zhou's group prepared graphene films using a CVD method and then transferred onto transparent substrates (glass and PET) (Figure 4a).[65] The graphene electrodes showed low surface roughness $( 0 . 9 \ \mathrm { n m } )$ and controllable sheet resistance and transparency (between $2 3 0 \Omega \ \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 }$ at $7 2 \%$ transparency and

However, CVD graphene-based flexible device could withstand bending angle up to $1 3 8 ^ { \circ }$ while the ITO-based device exhibited cracks at bending angle of $6 0 ^ { \circ }$ . Mutilayer stacking and doping are effective methods to improve the conductivity of graphene films. Lee et al. reported high-performance graphene electrodes using CVD-grown multilayer graphene (MLG) electrodes doped with $\mathrm { H N O } _ { 3 }$ or $\mathrm { S O C l } _ { 2 }$ [66] The sheet resistance of MLG electrodes reduced from 850 to $4 5 0 ~ \Omega ~ \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 }$ after doping, while the transmittance kept unchanged $( 9 0 \% )$ in the visible light range. # 2.3.2. Carbon Nanotubes

In 1991, Iijima firstly reported the needle-like CNTs, which can be considered as a hollow cylinder winded using graphene sheets. It can be divided into single-walled CNTs (SwCNTs), double-walled CNTs (DWCNTs), and multi-walled CNTs (MWCNTs) according to the number of the layers (Figure 4e).[77) The percolation network CNTs have been widely used as FTE for flexible OsCs due to its relatively low sheet resistance, high optical transmittance, good mechanical fexibility, and remarkable stability.[63] Over the past decade, CNTs as FTE have been widely reported in flexible OSCs.

In 2006, Rowell et al. fabricated FTE by printing SWCNTs network flms on PET substrate.78] The prepared swCNTs electrodes exhibited a sheet resistance of $2 0 0 ~ \Omega ~ \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 }$ and light transmittance of $8 5 \%$ at $5 5 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . The relevant flexible device based on poly $[ 2$ -methoxy-5- $^ { 3 ^ { \prime } , 7 ^ { \prime } }$ -dimethyloctyloxy)-1,4- phenylenevinylene] (MDMO-PPV) $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ and $\mathrm { P 3 H T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ showed a PCE of $1 . 2 \%$ and $2 . 5 \%$ , respectively. The flexibility of the PET/SwCNTs-based device was much greater than the PET/ ITO-based device. The PET/SwCNTs-based device showed no degradation and $2 0 { - } 2 5 \%$ loss in PCE with a bending radius of 5 and $1 ~ \mathrm { m m }$ , respectively. To further improve its performance, many efforts have been made for CNTs electrodes. Salvatierra et al. fabricated polyanilines:CNTs (PANI:CNTs) interfacial thin films.79] To further improve its conductivity, the PANI:CNTs film was doped with camphorsulfonic acid and exposed to metalcresol vapors. The sheet resistance of PANI:CNTs film was dramatically decreased from $8 5 ~ \mathrm { k } \Omega ~ \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 }$ to $2 9 5 ~ \Omega ~ \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 }$ with an unchanged light transmittance of $89 \%$ at $5 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . Moreover, the PET/PANI:CNTs electrodes exhibited excellent mechanical stability during the bending and stretching tests. The flexible electrode showed no change in resistance after 100 bending cycles and could be stretched to at least $50 \%$ of its original dimensions without conductivity loss. The flexible OsCs were built using PET/PANI:CNTs as an electrode and poly[9,9'-dioctyl-fluorenecobithiophene] $( \mathrm { F } 8 \mathrm { T } 2 ) / \mathrm { C } _ { 6 0 }$ as the active layer, yielding a higher PCE $( 2 . 2 7 \% )$ compare to the ITO-based device $( 1 . 2 0 \% )$ . Later, Jeon et al. prepared direct and dry deposited SwCN'Ts doped with $\mathsf { M o O } _ { x }$ on both PET and PI substrates as FTE (Figure 4f).80] The SwCNTs film showed enhanced transmittance after doping with $\mathrm { M o O } _ { x }$ The PET-based flexible device with $\mathrm { P T B 7 { : } P C _ { 7 1 } B M }$ as the photoactive layer showed a PCE of $3 . 9 1 \%$ , which retained a comparable PCE value after ten flexing cycles with a radius of $5 \mathrm { m m }$

Apart from the commonly used solution-based coating and dry deposition method, new methods have been proposed. Kim and co-workers developed the CNTs electrodes on PET substrates using a simple brush-painting method.[81] The brush-painted CNTs electrodes exhibited low sheet resistance $( 2 8 6 \Omega \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 } )$ , high optical transmittance $( 7 8 . 4 5 \% )$ , low surface roughness. Moreover, the brush-painted CNTs electrodes showed excellent flexibility in bending and twisting test. Both inner and outer bending tests results indicated there was no change in resistance after $1 0 ~ 0 0 0$ cycles at a radius of $1 0 \ \mathrm { m m }$ . Meanwhile, the CNTs electrodes showed a very small change in the resistance even twisting angle up to $8 0 ^ { \circ }$ . The CNTs-based flexible device with $\mathrm { P 3 H T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ as the active layer showed a PCE of $1 . 6 3 \%$

In summary, graphene and CNTs materials have been proved to be good candidate for FTE and widely used in flexible OSCs. However, they still have challenges. As FTE, the conduc. tivity and transmittance of carbon materials need to be further improved until they can be comparable to ITO electrode.

# 2.4. Flexible Organic Solar Cells Based on Metal Nanowires

Metal nanowires, such as Ag NWs and copper nanowires (Cu NWs), have been used as FTE due to its obvious merits in conductivity, flexibility, and transparency. Furthermore, the metal nanowire networks are also applicable for large-area production and solution-based processing preparation techniques.

# 2.4.1. Ag Nanowires

Currently, Ag NWs are also the most commonly used due to the excellent physical property of Ag. In 2008, Peumans' group firstly reported the Ag NWs electrode in OsCs, which was fabricated at the entire solution process and low-temperature annealing.[82] Compared with the ITO-based device, the device based on Ag NWs showed a $1 9 \%$ higher photocurrent. Subsequently, Yang et al. fabricated PET/Ag NWs as FTE in flexible conventional OSCs.83] The flexible PET/Ag NWs electrodes showed low sheet resistance $( 3 0 . 8 \Omega \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 } )$ and high transparency $8 0 \%$ from 400 to $2 0 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . Consequently, the flexible device with poly[benzo[1,2-b:4,5. $. b ^ { \prime } ]$ -dithiophene-alt-5,6-difluoro-4,7- dithien-2-yl-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole] (PBnDT-DTffBT) $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ as the active layer showed a PCE of $2 . 5 \%$ which retained $2 . 3 \%$ after ten bending cycles with a bending angle/radius of $1 2 0 ^ { \circ } / 7 . 2$ mm (Figure 5a). These results indicate Ag NWs electrode is a promising flexible electrode for OSCs.

Despite its significant advantages, Ag NWs films still suffer from several shortcomings, such as high surface roughness, easy oxidation, and poor adhesion to the substrates. In 2011, Peumans' group reported effective FTE by embedding Ag NWs into PEDOT:PSS, which exhibited low roughness.84] The sheet resistance of the FTE was slightly increased from 17 to $1 8 \Omega$ sq-1 at a bending radius of $1 . 2 \mathrm { m m }$ and a strain of $3 . 5 \%$ (Figure 5b). Lam et al. firstly developed an efficient by coupling Ag NWs and bio-based polyethylene furandicarboxylate (PEF) substrate.[94] Compared with the commonly used PFT, the PEF substrate possessed enhanced durability against oxygen, moisture, and thermal annealing. Furthermore, PEF substrates showed a high light transmittance in the visible light range and intense adhesion to Ag NWs. The flexible OSCs device with PEF/Ag NWs as electrode and PTB7-Thi $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ as the active layer exhibited a PCE of $6 . 7 \%$ which was higher than the device based on PEN/ITO. In 2019, Chen's group successfully synthesized grid-like FTE using water suspension of Ag NWs and polyelectrolyte PSSNa, which showed high transparency, low surface roughness, and excellent flexibility.[19] Then, the FTE was employed to fabricate flexible OsCs devices with different donors and acceptors, of which performance could be comparable to the rigid ITObased device. The PCE of the devices based on flexible tandem and single junction achieved $1 6 . 5 5 \%$ and $1 3 . 1 5 \%$ , respectively. # 2.4.2. Cu Nanowires

Although Cu is $6 \%$ less conductive than Ag, it is 100 times cheaper and 1o0o times more abundant. Thus, the replacement of Ag NWs by Cu NWs will greatly reduce cost.[95] $\mathrm { I n } 2 0 1 3$ , Leo's group synthesized high aspect ratio Cu NWs using hydrazinefree hydrothermal method.[96] Then, the Cu NWs were deposited on glass and PET foils using the spray coating method. After pressing and annealing, a high-performance Cu NWs electrode was obtained (Figure 5f), which could be comparable to the Ag NWs electrode. The small molecule OSCs based on Cu NWs as electrode and $\mathrm { F _ { 4 } Z n P c : C _ { 6 0 } }$ as the photoactive layer exhibited a PCE of $3 . 0 8 \%$ , which was comparable to the ITObased device $( 3 . 9 9 \% )$

Despite the low cost of Cu NWs, easy oxidation and high surface roughness are the main drawbacks of its application in FTE. To address these issues, Chen et al. fabricated the $\mathrm { C u }$ NWs-based FTE by embedding Cu NWs into pre-prepared PEDOT:PSS on the PET.97] The prepared Cu NWs-based FTE exhibited excellent antioxidant stability, low surface roughness $( 7 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ in height), high conductivity $( 1 5 \Omega \ \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 } )$ , and good transparency $( 7 6 \%$ at $\lambda = 5 5 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . In addition, the $\mathrm { C u }$ NW-based electrode displayed excellent mechanical flexibility, which showed no significant change in the resistance after 1000 bending cycles with a bending angle of $1 2 0 ^ { \circ }$ . The resulting flexible device based on $\mathrm { P 3 H T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ showed a PCE of $1 . 4 \%$ . In 2017, Sun's group reported a simple method to synthesize Cu $\mathrm { N W - T i O } _ { 2 }$ -polyacrylate composite FTE at room temperature.[98] The composite FTE showed high transparency $( 8 2 \% )$ , low sheet resistances $( 1 3 \Omega \ \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 } )$ and surface roughness. Consequently, the $\mathrm { C u }$ -NW-based inverted flexible device with $\mathrm { P 3 H T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ as the photoactive layer revealed a PCE of $3 . 1 1 \%$ , which remained $90 \%$ after 500 bending cycles with a bending frequency of $0 . 5 \ : \mathrm { H z }$ and bending radius of $3 \mathrm { m m }$

As mentioned above, the FTE based on metal nanowires exhibits many advantages such as high conductivity, excellent transparency, superior flexibility, and scalable solution processability. However, some drawbacks need to be further improved, including high surface roughness, weak adhesion to the substrate, and poor stability to moisture and oxygen. Also, much more attention should be paid to Cu NWs because $\mathrm { C u }$ is much more abundant and less expensive than Ag.

# 2.5.1. Ultrathin Metal Films (UTMFs)

UTMFs have been considered as a promising candidate for FET due to its high conductivity, excellent flexibility, superior ductility, and good transparency in the visible light range. In 2008, Guo's group fabricated high transparency metal electrodes (Au, Ag, and Cu) for rigid OsCs device via nanoimprint lithography method.[99] The metal electrodes exhibit high conductivity and excellent transparency in the visible range, and the corresponding devices showed similar efficiency to the ITObased device.

# 2.5.2. Metal Grids

Recently, ultrathin metal electrodes have been widely used in fexible OsCs. However, the high-quality metal films are really difficult to obtain. Meanwhile, metal grid-based flexible electrodes have been introduced in flexible OsCs. The metal grid electrodes can be patterned with different geometries. The electrical and optical properties of metal grids can be optimized by tuning the grid parameters, including aspect ratio, line width, depth, and pitch size.116] The technique for fabricating metal grids is diverse. However, the surface roughness and thickness of the flexible electrode, especially for OsCs with a photoactive layer about hundreds of nanometers, should be taken seriously during fabrication. To overcome the roughness concern, many efforts have been taken. Tvingstedt and Inganas firstly fabricated the $\mathrm { A g }$ grids ( $W = 4 0 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } }$ $S = 6 0 0 ~ \mu \mathrm { m } )$ using a soft lithographic metal deposition method.122] Then, a high conductive diethylene glycol-PEDOT:PSS layer was covered on the top of the Ag grids to reduce the roughness of Ag grids. The PEDOT:PSS/Ag grid composite electrodes showed lower series resistance $( 7 0 ~ \Omega )$ than the PEDOT:PSS electrode $( 3 5 0 ~ \Omega )$ . The flexible device with PEDOT:PSS/Ag grid as an electrode and APFO-Green $5 { : } \mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ as the photoactive layer showed a PCE of $1 . 0 \%$ . This work proved that covering with PEDOT:PSS could effectively reduce the surface roughness of Ag grids. Later, Li et al. patterned a high-resolution hexagonal $\mathrm { A g }$ grid $( W = 3 \mu \mathrm { m } )$ on PET (Figure 6c), which showed low sheet resistance $( 0 . 5 \Omega \ \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 } )$ and high transmittance $( 8 5 \% )$ [123] The composite electrode PET/Ag grid/PH1000 was fabricated by coating PH1000 on PET/Ag grid. The composite electrode exhibited a lower sheet resistance of $6 . 1 \Omega \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 }$ compared to the PET/Ag grid electrode $( 2 0 0 ~ \Omega ~ \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 } )$ . The large-area $( 1 . 2 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ fexible device based on PET/Ag grid/PH1000 showed a PCE of $1 . 3 6 \%$ . Later, Li's group fabricated flexible composite electrode PET/Ag mesh/PH1000:Ag NWs by doping $2 0 \ \mathrm { \ w t \% }$ Ag NWs into PH1000.[24] The prepared electrode showed a smooth surface, low sheet resistance $( 6 \Omega \thinspace \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 } )$ and high transparency $8 6 \%$ at $5 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ . The relevant device with PBDB-T-2F:IT-4F as the active layer exhibited a PCE of $1 2 . 0 7 \%$ , which could be comparable to the rigid ITO-based device $( 1 2 . 9 4 \% )$ . Recently, Han et al. fabricated a hexagonal $\sf A g / \sf C u$ grid with a width of $3 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } }$ using nanoimprinting and electrodeposition method.23] To further smoothen the surface of $\sf A g / \sf C u$ grid, a high conductive PEDOT:PSS (E100) was coated on the top of $\sf A g / \sf C u$ grid. Then, a series of flexible devices with different photoactive layers were fabricated using $\mathrm { P E T / A g / C u / }$ E100 as an electrode. The large-area $( 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ flexible devices with ${ \mathrm { N F } } 3 0 0 0 { \mathrm { - P } } { \mathrm { : N F } } 3 0 0 0 { \mathrm { - N } }$ as the photoactive layer showed a high PCE of $1 2 . 2 6 \%$ and a certified PCE of $1 1 . 4 5 \%$ . Furthermore, the flexible device based on $\mathrm { P E T / A g / C u / E 1 0 0 }$ electrode exhibited long-term stability and superior mechanical durability, and could maintained $9 5 \%$ of the initial efficiency after 2000 bending cycles at a radius of $7 . 5 \mathrm { m m }$

Apart from conductive polymer PEDOT:PSS, carbon materials were combined with $\mathrm { A g }$ grids to fabricate high conductivity FTE for OSCs. Moreover, the combined electrodes exhibited excellent mechanical flexibility, which did not show any increase in the resistance after 500 bending cycles at a radius of $4 ~ \mathrm { m m }$ . The $\mathrm { A g }$ grid-based flexible OSCs exhibited a PCE of $2 . 9 \%$ . In summary, the metal-based flexible electrodes have been widely used in the flexible OsCs due to its high conductivity, excellent flexibility, and superior ductility. However, they are still some issues need to be addressed. For UTMFs, it is vital to explore new techniques to fabricate high-performance ultrathin films. For the metal grids, a new metal grids-based composite electrode with high conductivity, excellent transparency, superior flexibility, and low surface roughness need to be developed. Alternatively, new techniques with low cost, precise control, and simplified procedures need to be explored to adapt to largearea and large-scale production.

# 3. Stretchable Organic Solar Cells

These high-performance flexible OsCs have demonstrated the potential in a portable application. However, the flexible capability of these devices is not enough to allow such OsCs to be incorporated effectively onto textiles or other wearable devices, which require flexibility as well as stretchability.[10] Moreover, to cope with the environment for next-generation wearable electronic devices, buckle-based nonintrinsically stretchable OSCs and intrinsically stretchable OsCs come into being. Therefore, to enhance the mechanical durability of OsCs appropriate for the next-generation wearable electronic devices, it is highly demanding to develop high-performance stretchable OSCs.

# 3.1. Buckle-Based Nonintrinsically Stretchable Organic Solar Cells

One of the most impressive works of stretchable OsCs has been reported in 2011 by Bao and co-workers [11a] In this work, the authors utilized mechanical buckling to produce the stretchable OsCs, which can survive under the strains of $2 7 \%$ Meanwhile, when PDMS-based devices were stretched to $1 8 . 5 \%$ after 12 cycles, the devices exhibited PCE of $1 . 2 \%$ which is comparable to the ITO-based devices' PCE of $2 \%$ .The buckling strategy provided a new method to fabricate stretchable organic electronic devices which are inelastic. However, the power efficiency of these devices was still lower. Also, the devices produced cracks in the active layer when it is stretched beyond $2 2 . 2 \%$ . To further improve the electronic performance and understand the mechanical properties of the devices. In 2012, Bauer and coworkers fabricated an ultrathin fexible OsCs from a $2 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ thick blend of P3HT with $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ [11b] By adhering the device to a prestretched substrate PET, the device can reversibly withstand extreme mechanical deformation with equal PCE to their glassbased counterparts, performing with PCE of $4 . 2 \%$ . Meanwhile, the authors cycled the device from $0 \%$ to $5 0 \%$ compression and back more than 20 times, showing a gradual decrease in the $I _ { \mathrm { s c } } ,$ the FF, and the output power, resulting in a $2 7 \%$ decrease in power. These ultrathin solar cells exhibited considerable power efficiency and unique mechanical resilience.

In 2017, Sun and co-workers developed highly efficient and stretchable OsCs based on a random buckling process.[1c] To further improve its mechanical robustness, an NPB sacrificial layer was deposited on the cathode to avoid direct contact between the OSCs and elastomeric substrate. Its PCE of $5 . 8 \%$ under $70 \%$ tensile strain was the largest to date among the reported OSCs. Meanwhile, the stretchable OSCs exhibited small variations in performance after 400 stretching-releasing cycles. Similarly, Chen and co-workers fabricated an efficient, stretchable inverted OsC based on a similar strategy.[129] The devices exhibited PCE of $5 . 6 1 \%$ under $0 \%$ compression, and remained $7 1 \%$ efficiency under $30 \%$ compression, more importantly, after 50-cycle compression from $0 \%$ to $30 \%$ it still maintained $6 4 . 3 \%$ efficiency. According to the exhaustive characterization, the authors introduced that PCE has the same decreasing trend as the photocurrent, in the compressed state, further shrinkage of the active area due to the formation of wrinkles, which will lead to less effective light-harvesting. Certainly, the linkage between the decrease in efficiency and degradation of the structure due to morphological variation, detached interfacial contact, and crack formation is particularly useful, to realize mechanical robustness, it is necessary to examine the compression-stretching endurance of OSC.

In 2018, Sun and co-workers applied stencil-pattern transferring technology to stretchable OsCs to realize an ordered buckling profile.130] In this work, the authors introduced a low-cost stencil-pattern transferring technique, realizing periodic-buckled structure with stretch-release cycles; the stretchable OsCs could remain certain efficiency after 12 000 cycles, which is two orders of magnitude higher than the random buckle-based stretchable OsCs. More importantly, due to its compatibility with various materials, structures, and fabrication processes for devices fabrication, the technology exhibits great potential solution for various stretchable electronics.

Apart from utilizing buckling to increase mechanical properties in OsCs, developing fiber-like OsCs that are woven into a textile is another approach. The fiber-shaped solar cells can greatly improve their flexibility with low-cost metal wire in a simple helical twisting structure.[131] Peng and co-workers deposited photoactive materials onto the spring-like electrode, these fiber-shaped OSCs exhibited PCE of $1 . 2 3 \%$ , and remained about $90 \%$ efficiency after being bent for 1o0o times and under stretching at a strain of above $30 \%$ [132] Despite low efficiency, the fiber-shaped OsCs could be woven into the textiles, and further utilized into the electronic products due to its lightweight and stability. Later, the authors further designed an interlaced structure of cathode and anode fibers using fiber-shaped OSCs, the textiles may deform in three dimensions, the devices exhibited $1 . 6 2 \%$ efficiency in the organic textiles.[133] After $1 5 { \mathrm { ~ d ~ } }$ in air, the organic photovoltaic textile retained over $8 5 \%$ PCE of its initial value. Interestingly, electronic performance hardly decreased after 1000 bending cycles. The fabrication of these textiles could be easily scaled up in the factory, which significantly promotes the commercialization of the organic photovoltaic textiles.

# 3.2. Intrinsically Stretchable Organic Solar Cells

The buckle-based or fiber-shaped structure is used to achieve the stretchability of the devices and to avoid the functional layer material itself from being broken using tensile fracture. However, this buckle-based or fiber-shaped structure complicated processes, high cost, and poor structural stability serve as a drawback for this device. Moreover, those devices were stretched in uniaxial direction, which limits its practical application.[11a,130] It is, therefore, necessary to develop intrinsically stretchable OSCs to eliminate the drawbacks. # 3.2.1. Stretchable Electrodes

To fabricate intrinsically stretchable OsCs, it is required that all layers in the device configuration can accommodate the strains when stretched. Most stretchable OsCs have been fabricated on elastomer substrates, such as PDMS or polyurethane.[10,12d] These substrates provide adequate deformability and mechanical stability, assisting the devices functioning well at large strains. However, the unbalanced mechanical capabilities between substrates and other layers will cause the tensile failure of the devices. Thus, to further develop intrinsically stretchable OSCs, it is necessary to improve the mechanical capabilities of other layers or cohesion between different layers.

Similar to flexible OsCs, the electrode is one of the critical factors for realizing efficient intrinsically stretchable OsCs.[11a] The common used electrodes in stretchable OsCs are liquid metals (EGaIn) or ductile metals as cathodes, and PEDOT:PSS as anodes. However, the commercial PEDOT:Pss electrodes present some limitations, such as relatively low conductivity and low adhesion on hydrophobic substrates, causing performance failure of the device at large strains. These properties can be improved by using different kinds of additives. Bao and co-workers added $1 \%$ Zonyl fluorosurfactant (Zonyl) to the PEDOT:PSS solution, treating with ultraviolet/ ozone $\left( \mathrm { U V } / \mathrm { O } _ { 3 } \right)$ to improve the adhesion between PDMS and PEDOT:PSS.[134] The resistance of optimized PEDOT:PSS film increased by a factor of only two at $50 \%$ strain and it retained significant conductivity up to $18 8 \%$ strain, which derived from the PEDOT:PSS grains reorient in the direction of the strain. Similarly, to reduce the surface tension of the PEDOT:PSS and enable PEDOT:PSS coated on hydrophobic substrates, Jeong and co-workers added a small amount of a nonionic surfactant (Triton X-100) to commercial PEDOT:PSS solutions.[135] The treated PEDOT:PSs films showed excellent electrical conductivity of $8 3 0 ~ \mathrm { S ~ c m ^ { - 1 } }$ , low sheet resistance of $2 5 0 ~ \Omega ~ \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 }$ at $9 6 \%$ transmittance (at $5 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ and high electrical stability at strain of $1 0 . 3 \%$ . To further exploit the effect on mechanical properties of common additives, Lipomi and co-workers fabricated PEDOT films containing different content of DMsO and Zonyl and poly(ethyleneimine) (PE1).(136] Containing $5 \%$ DMSO and $10 \%$ Zonyl, the films exhibited excellent elasticity and ductility. Further results showed that concentration of DMsO had little effect on the mechanical properties, differently, the addition of Zonyl significantly improved the mechanical compliance while retained good electrical properties due to the increasing free volume and weak intermolecular forces between the polymer chains. This work provided a feasible method to fabricate stretchable electrodes. Besides, the metal thin films that widely used in flexible OsCs exhibit weakness in resisting strain for the application in stretchable OsCs. To solve the problem, Sun and co-workers proposed the "sesame candy bar' structure of CuNWs, in which CuNWs was decorated by Ag nanoparticles.137] CuNWs electrodes achieved a low sheet resistance of 47 $\Omega ~ \mathrm { { s q } ^ { - 1 } }$ , a high transmittance of $8 9 . 1 \%$ and showed negligible change within $1 5 0 \%$ strain. Chen and co-workers fabricated a stretchable semi-embedded CuNWs electrode by electroless depositing Cu on the electrospun poly(4-vinylpyridine) polymer template semi-embedded in PDMS, which showed low sheet resistance of $1 5 . 6 ~ \Omega ~ \mathrm { s q } ^ { - 1 }$ at $8 2 \%$ transmittance and no obvious sheet resistance change after ${ > } 2 0 0 0$ stretching cycles at a strain of $2 5 \%$ [138] The metal thin films can effectively accommodate the strain by reversible phase separation. For instance, a hybrid Ag electrode retained $100 \%$ of the original conductivity after 50 cycles of stretching under a $20 \%$ strain due to the nanogranular-like morphology formed in Ag.[139] Also, CNTs and graphene have been exploited as stretchable electrode candidates due to their excellent electrical conductivity and mechanical properties.[81,140]

# 3.2.2. Stretchable Active Layers

Bao and co-workers described intrinsically stretchable OSCs based on the two conjugated polymers P3HT, DPPT-TT blending with $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ (Figure 7a) for the first time in 2012,[10] and the devices also permitted reversible stretchability. According to this work, the authors introduced that the different conjugated polymers significantly exhibit distinguished mechanical properties. Blending with $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ in bulk heterojunction (BHJ) films, $\mathrm { P 3 H T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ demonstrated greater elastic modulus than $\mathrm { D P P T - T T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ , which was related to a greater tendency of $\mathrm { P 3 H T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ to fracture under strain. Interestingly, the cracks responded to strain dominated the evolution of photovoltaic figures of merit. However, the power efficiency of the devices was low, the $\mathrm { P 3 H T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ and DPPT$\mathrm { T T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ exhibited PCE of $0 . 4 7 \%$ and $0 . 4 2 \%$ respectively. To further identify the factors that influenced the electronic performance under strain and how to improve the mechanical robustness.

In 2013, Brendan et al. described how the alteration in the film morphology affects not only the OsCs electronic performance but also the mechanical properties of the devices.[12a] The methods used for this investigation based on $\mathrm { P 3 H T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ with different processing methods: solution, spin speed and post cost treatment. Interestingly, the elastic modulus of the neat P3HT and $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ films was $0 . 2 2 \pm \ : 0 . 0 3$ GPa and $3 . 0 6 ~ \pm ~ 0 . 1 7$ GPa, respectively. Meanwhile, the different $\mathrm { P 3 H T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ blend films showed varying elastic modulus attributed to variation in the solid-state order and magnitude of the negative volume of mixing. Moreover, power efficiency around $3 . 7 \%$ was achieved with a large elastic modulus. And those devices with high PCE showed deleterious effects on mechanical properties, i.e., increasing the tensile modulus and lowering the crack onset strain. To achieve robust and stretchable OsCs, it is, therefore, significant to further understand how the morphology of the active layer relates to both electronic and mechanical behavior.

The variation of active materials will determine the different morphology of the film. Lipomi and co-workers have performed impressive and significant works in the fields of stretchable OSCs in the last decade.12,141] Firstly, this group exploited a series of P3ATs blending with $\mathrm { P C } _ { 6 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ to examine the effect of length of the side chain on the tensile modulus and brittleness.12d] With the length of the side chain increasing, the tensile modulus decreased from 1.87 to 0.16 GPa (Figure 7b). Subsequently, cracks appeared at $6 \%$ strain for P3BT and ${ > } 6 0 \%$ for P3DDT, which was attributed to the different adhesion of P3ATs. Interestingly, the $\mathrm { P 3 A T s { : } P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ blends films showed greater elastic modulus than pure poly mers by factors 2-4. To further determine the effects of the conjugated polymers, this group tested four polymer samples, i.e., a physical blend of equal parts P3HT and poly(3-octylthiophene) (P3OT), a block copolymer (P3HT $\mathbf { \nabla } _ { \cdot } b$ -P3OT), a random copolymer (P3HT-co-P3OT), and poly(3-heptylthiophene) (P3HpT). Similarly, P3HpT exhibited maximum compliance of $7 0 \pm 1 0$ MPa and PCE of $2 . 1 6 \%$ that was similar to $\mathrm { P 3 H T : P C _ { 6 1 } B M }$ , showing that $\mathrm { P 3 H p T }$ has the great potential to achieve mechanical robust and stretchable OsCs (Figure 7c).141a] Moreover, compared to pure films, the decreased compliance of the active layer was attributed to the solubility of the fullerene in the amorphous areas and fullerenerich phases. The concentration of fullerene makes the BHJ films stiffer and more brittle, and fracture.142] Furthermore, the films exhibited unequal moduli by changing the extents of molecular mixing, suggesting that a large extent of the polymer determined the stiffness of the blend.[141b]

Recently, Kim et al. enhanced and tuned the mechanical properties of the P $\mathrm { T B } 7 { : } \mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ BHJ films through different composition mixing ratios and addition of 1,8-diiodoctane (DIO) as additive.142] Interestingly, with $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M }$ content above $5 0 \%$ the addition of DIO increased the tensile strength and decreased the crack onset strain compared to the films without DIO (Figure 7d). Usually, DIO has been used as an additive to tune the morphology, improving the power efficiency of the films. Meanwhile, the variation in morphology may attribute to the transitions of the mechanical properties. Unconventionally, Verduzco and co-workers improved the mechanical robustness of the films though incorporating an internal elastic network,[143] using reactive small molecular additives to prepare the stretchable OsCs, where additives are rapidly crosslinked through the thiol-ene coupling. Interestingly, the crack onset strain increased with the increasing concentration of the thiol-ene. When the concentration of thiol-ene was $2 0 ~ \mathrm { w t \% }$ , there were no cracks appear until to $2 6 \%$ strained. However, the increasing additives also decreased the power efficiency of the films. There was a balance between electronic performances and mechanical properties, the films with $2 0 ~ \mathrm { w t \% }$ additives exhibited high power efficiency compared to other conditions. Furthermore, the network-stabilized OSCs with $2 0 ~ \mathrm { w t \% }$ additives with little loss in PCE. This demonstrated that the incorporation of an internal network is useful for improving mechanical properties without deleterious degradation for power efficiency.

Furthermore, with more amorphous regions forming, the films remained $89 \%$ initial PCE after 1000 cycles under $45 \%$ compression and bending radius of $1 0 \ \mathrm { m m }$ (Figure 9d). This significant improvement in power efficiency and mechanical properties show that the strategy of introducing the third component is an effective way to fabricate the electronically and mechanically robust solar cells.

Moreover, the proper definition of device areas is vital for the accurate measurement of PCEs during stretching. Since the device areas vary when stretched, it is necessary to derive the dependence of device area on strain in the calculation.

Assuming the effective area is elliptical with area $A \ = \ \pi a _ { \mathrm { L } } a _ { \mathrm { T } } ,$ where $a _ { \mathrm { L } }$ is half of the longitudinal axis (parallel to the strain) and $a _ { \mathrm { T } }$ is half the transverse axis (perpendicular to the strain) (Figure 7a). Then the varied area can be calculated through Equation (1)[10,147]

$$
A \left( \varepsilon \right) = \pi a _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { L } } \left( 1 + \varepsilon ^ { \prime } \right) a _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { T } } \left( 1 - \nu _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { s } } \varepsilon ^ { \prime } \right)
$$

where $\varepsilon ^ { \prime } = \varepsilon / 1 0 0 \%$ , and $\nu _ { \mathrm { s } }$ is the Poisson ratio of the substrate. The assumption that $\nu _ { \mathrm { s } }$ does not change for strains ${ \leq } 2 0 \%$ is accurate for strains of about $10 \%$ , but deviates by only $10 \%$ at $20 \%$ strain.134 Besides, considering the practical applications, the power output per weight might be a more suitable metric in the circumstances, where the payload is an important parameter in the fields of wearable devices, aircraft or spacecraft.[11b,148]

# 4. Conclusions and Outlook

The development of flexible OsCs and stretchable OsCs has been discussed extensively. The features of the commonly used FTE, which include transparent conducting oxides, conducting polymers, carbon materials, metal nanowires, metal grids, and the performance of the relevant flexible OsCs have been discussed. More so, two general strategies for the fabrication of stretchable OsCs have also been discussed. Consequently, the PCE of the device based on flexible OSCs and stretchable OSCs have reached $1 5 . 2 1 \%$ - $1 6 . 5 5 \%$ for flexible tandem OSCs) and $1 3 \%$ respectively. However, there is still much room for improvement.

As for flexible OsCs, the key factors are the photovoltaic materials and FTEs. The high-quality FTEs should possess high electrical conductivity, optical transmittance, mechanical fexibility, long-term stability, and low surface roughness as well. Based on the analysis, the following suggestions are considered for future development: i) the efficient nonfullerenebased photoactive blend systems should be applied in flexible OSCs. ii) The high-quality and low-cost FTE needs to be further developed especially for composite electrodes. iii) Large areas and large-scale techniques need to be developed to adapt to industrial production.

Besides, there is also the introduction of the recent advances toward the stretchable OsCs. Contrary to donor polymer/ fullerene blending, all-polymers systems have shown greater potential to fabricate stretchable OsCs due to the stronger entanglement between donors and acceptors in networks. Despite more and more functional materials having been developed to fabricate stretchable OsCs, there remain many challenges to be emphasized to achieve intrinsic stretchability, such as, how to maintain the co-existent of photovoltaic performance and mechanical properties is still a critical issue. These issues can be addressed by designing conjugated semiconductors with reversible noncovalent bonds, so that the strained energy can be dissipated as the noncovalent bonds break. Furthermore, the technologies from supramolecular chemistry can also be borrowed to achieve intrinsically stretchable OSCs. These synergetic developments in mechanically robust OSCs will revolutionize the consumer electronics, biomedical devices, and devices for fieldwork and contribute to the next generation of wearable power sources.
