ABSTRACT: Polythiophenes (PTs) are promising donor materials for the industrialization of polymer solar cells (PSCs) due to the merits of easy synthesis, low cost, and large-scale producibility. The rapid progress of non-fullerene acceptors requires the development of new PTs for use in non-fullerene PSCs. In this work, we present a set of PTs with different degrees of backbone fluorination (P6T-FO0, P6TF50, P6T-F75, and P6T-F100) to investigate the effect of fluorination on the photovoltaic properties of PTs in nonfullerene PSCs. Upon increasing fluorine content, the PTs tend to have higher crystallinity, higher absorption coefficients, and enhanced relative dielectric constants. # 1. INTRODUCTION

Among numerous semiconducting conjugated polymers, polythiophenes (PTs) are one of the most promising candidates for many types of optoelectronic devices such as organic photovoltaics' and organic field-effect transistors (OFETs)? because of their easy synthesis, excellent charge carrier mobility, and compatibility with large area processing. Among various PTs, poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT) is the most extensively investigated donor material and played a crucial role in the development of polymer solar cells (PSCs).4 In the early stage of PSCs, much effort has been devoted to the morphology optimization of the active layer composed of the P3HT and [6,6]-phenyl- $\mathrm { \cdot { C } } _ { 6 0 }$ -butyl acid methyl ester (PCBM) blend, which finally contributed to a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of ${ \sim } 5 \%$ 5 The main restraints for these devices are their low open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ and shortcircuit current density $\left( J _ { s c } \right)$ due to the high-lying highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level and large optical bandgap $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } )$ of P3HT. In recent years, non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs) have led to great progress of PSCs due to their broad and strong light absorption, tunable energy levels, and capability to allow for small energy loss in the $\mathrm { P S C s } , ^ { 6 - 8 }$ which have revived interest in PT-based materials for PSCs.9\~18 For example, McCulloch et al. reported two NFAs named O-IDTBR and EH-IDTBR based on the indacenodithiophene core, which afforded power conversion efficiencies (PCE) of $6 . 4 \%$ and $6 . 0 \%$ when blended with P3HT, respectively. Afterward, they incorporated a wide bandgap non-fullerene acceptor IDFBR into the P3HT:O-IDTBR blend, and the resulting ternary devices yielded a maximum PCE of $7 . 7 \%$ 10 Very recently, the same group further boosted the PCE to $7 . 0 \%$ and $6 . 3 \%$ for the binary solar cells of

Among the various molecular design principles reported in the literature, fluorine (F) substitution on the backbone of conjugated polymer is particularly effective to improve the performance of PSCs due to several prominent features of the fluorine atom21-23 First, the van der Waals radius of the fluorine atom is $1 . 3 5 \ \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ,$ only slightly larger than that of the hydrogen atom (1.2 A), which can effectively avoid the steric hindrance caused by the introduction of $\mathrm { F }$ atoms.24 Second, the fluorine atom has strong electron-withdrawing ability with the highest Pauling electronegativity of 4.0, which can lower the HOMO levels and thereby enhance the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of PSCs.25 Third, the intramolecular and intermolecular interactions can be enhanced in fluorinated molecules due to the noncovalent interactions such as F...S, F...C-H...F, and $\mathrm { C - F } { \cdots } \pi _ { \mathrm { F } } ,$ which can promote the molecule coplanarity, crystallinity, and charge transport property.26 In addition, fluorination can reduce the exciton binding energy and increase the lifetime of charge carrier by promoting the relative dielectric constants $\left( \varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } } \right)$ of the resulting materials.7-29 However, there is a big difference in the main cause for the PCE improvement in different systems upon fluorination. The elevated $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ is the primary element for some systems, while the increase of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and/or FF is the main cause for others.30-37 Moreover, fuorination has negative impacts such as poorer solubility and serious aggregation for the resulting polymers.38,39 Therefore, the fluorination degree of conjugated polymers should be carefully optimized to obtain high-performance PSCs.

In this work, we report a series of new polythiophenes (P6T$\mathrm { F } x \mathrm { \overbrace { } }$ ) with varied fluorination degree by changing the feed ratios between the $^ { 3 , 3 ^ { \prime } }$ -difluoro- $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ bithiophene (2F-TT) unit and the $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -bithiophene (TT) unit in polymerization, where $_ x$ stands for the percentage of 2F-TT unit relative to the total units of 2F-TT and TT. The molar percentages of the 2F-TT unit were 0, $5 0 \%$ $7 5 \%$ and $1 0 0 \%$ for P6T-F00, P6T-F50, P6TF75, and P6T-F100, respectively. The chemical structures of the polymers are shown in Scheme 1a. The device performance of $\mathrm { P } 6 \mathrm { T } { \cdot } \mathrm { F } x$ in PSCs has been evaluated in combination with a non-fullerene acceptor EH-IDTBR (Scheme 1b). Upon increase of the fluorination degree, the resulting polymers exhibit improved backbone coplanarity, enhanced aggregation in solutions, higher absorption coefficients, and increased crystallinity. Moreover, the relative dielectric constant $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ has been increased for both pure $\mathrm { P } 6 \mathrm { T } { \cdot } \mathrm { F } x$ films and P6T-Fx:EHIDTBR blend films along with increasing fluorination degree. We also observed a finer phase separation P6T-Fx:EH-IDTBR blend flms as the increase of fluorination degree. These positive factors collectively contribute to improved charge transfer, suppressed recombination losses, and enhanced overall device performance in the resulting PSCs. Moreover, the highest PCE of $7 . 3 \%$ has been achieved for P6T-F100, which is higher than that of the champion binary solar cells composed of P3HT and non-fullerene acceptor.1 These results demonstrate the prospect of PTs for the fabrication of high-performance non-fullerene PSCs via backbone fluorination.

2.5. Charge Generation, Transport, and Recombination. The relative dielectric constants $\left( \varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } } \right)$ of the pristine P6T$\operatorname { F } x$ polymers and the blend films were tested via capacitance (C)-voltage $( V )$ measurements with a device structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/sample/Ca/Al and frequency range of $1 0 ^ { 3 } \mathrm { - } 1 0 ^ { 5 } ~ \mathrm { H z }$ . The $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ is estimated from the following equation: $~ C ~ = ~ \varepsilon _ { 0 } \varepsilon _ { \mathrm { { r } } } S / d ,$ where $\varepsilon _ { 0 } , \ S ,$ and $d$ refer to the vacuum permittivity, device area $\left( 0 . 0 5 1 6 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \right)$ , and thickness of the tested film, respectively. The relative dielectric constants of the polymers and blends are presented in Figure S12 and Table S7. From Figure Sa, it is clear that the $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ values increase gradually for both the neat polymer flms and blend flms upon increasing fluorination degree. The increased $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ can be ascribed to the dipole moment of the $\mathrm { C - F }$ bond due to the large electronegativity of fluorine.29 It is believed that a higher $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ could reduce the binding energy of charge transfer (CT) state and thus promote exciton dissociation.8,49,50 The exciton binding energies of charge transfer states can be estimated from the equation $E _ { \mathrm { B } } ^ { \mathrm { { { C T } } } } ~ = ~ q ^ { 2 } / ( 4 \pi \varepsilon _ { 0 } \varepsilon _ { \mathrm { { r } } } r )$ where $q$ and $r$ represent the elementary charge and radius of the charge transfer state exciton, respectively.28 The results are displayed in Figure Sb and Table S7. The exciton binding energy reduces significantly with the increase of fluorine content, which is 0.40, 0.28, 0.26, and $0 . 2 4 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for the blend based on P6T-F00, P6T-F50, P6T-F75, and P6T-F100, respectively. The smaller exciton binding energy can greatly promote charge separation and suppress geminate recombination and thereby contribute to the improvement of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF. The photoluminescence (PL) quenching efficiencies were further used to investigate the charge-transfer process between the P6T- $\mathrm { . F } x$ polymers and EH-IDTBR. The original PL spectra of the pure polymers and relevant blends are displayed in Figure S13. The PL quenching efficiencies of the blends based on various P6T $\neg \mathrm { F } x$ polymers are plotted in Figure 5c, which are $8 4 \%$ $8 9 \%$ $9 5 \%$ and $9 7 \%$ for P6T-F00, P6T-F50, P6T-F75, and P6T-F100, respectively. A higher PL quenching efficiency suggests more efficient charge transfer in the active layer, which is consistent with the reduced exciton binding energy upon backbone fluorination of the donor polymers.

F } x$ :EH-IDTBR blends were measured by using the space-charge-limited-current (sCLC) method in single carrier devices. The current-voltage $( J ^ { 1 / 2 } - V )$ characteristics are presented in Figure S14, and the mobility data are listed in Table S7. In Figure 5d, we plot the hole and electron mobility as a function of fluorination degree of the P6T- $\mathrm { . F } x$ polymers. In neat films, the hole mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } \right)$ is enhanced from $8 . 6 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ s ^ { - 1 }$ for P6T-F00 to $2 . 8 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ $\mathrm { ~ V ~ } ^ { - 1 } \ s ^ { - 1 }$ for P6T-F1o0. For the blend flms, both the hole mobility and electron mobility $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } \right)$ increase along with increasing the fluorine content in $\mathrm { P } 6 \mathrm { T } { \cdot } \mathrm { F } x$ polymers, which is beneficial to suppressing charge recombination and improving $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF in resulting PSCs.

The charge recombination behaviors of the devices were investigated by analyzing the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ as a function of light intensity $\left( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } \right)$ . The relevant characteristics are plotted in Figure 6a,b. in general, the correlation between $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ can be expressed as $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \propto P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } { } ^ { \alpha } ,$ where $\alpha$ is an exponential factor that will approach unity when the bimolecular recombination is negligible.1 The $\alpha$ values are 0.95, 0.97, 0.99, and 1.00 for the devices of P6T-F00, P6T-F50, P6T-F75, and P6T-F100, respectively, indicating that fluorination of donor polymers can help to reduce bimolecular recombination. Additionally, the slope of $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ versus the natural logarithm of $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ gives the value of $k _ { \mathrm { B } } T / q ,$ where $k _ { \mathrm { B } } , T ,$ and $q$ represent the Boltzmann constant, temperature in kelvin, and elementary charge, respectively.52 When the slope value is greater than 1.0 $k _ { \mathrm { B } } T / q$ more trap-assisted or monomolecular recombination occurs in the PSCs. The slope for the devices of P6T-F00, P6T-F50, P6T-F75, and P6T-F100 is calculated to be 1.93, 1.68, 1.52, and $1 . 3 4 k _ { \mathrm { B } } T / q ,$ respectively, suggesting that trapassisted and monomolecular recombination is suppressed by increasing the fluorination degree of the polymers.

Transient photocurrent (TPC) and transient photovoltage (TPV) measurements were employed to evaluate charge extraction time and carrier lifetime in PSCs. These measurements help to probe the competition between charge recombination and charge sweep-out process. Shorter charge extraction time and longer carrier lifetime indicate slower recombination rates.354As shown in Figure 6, the charge extraction time of the solar cells based on P6T-F100 is $0 . 0 9 \mu s ,$ which is much shorter than those of the other devices $( 0 . 1 5 \mu s$ for P6T-F00, $0 . 1 3 ~ \mu s$ for P6T-F50, and $0 . 1 4 \mu s$ for P6T-F75). Furthermore, the TPV results (Figure 6d) show that the charge carrier lifetime increases along with the fluorination degree, which is 8.79, 12.68, 21.50, and $4 9 . 1 0 \mu s$ for the blends based on P6T-F00, P6T-F50, P6T-F75, and P6T-F100, respectively. These results suggest that the charge recombination is suppressed by adding more fluorine atoms into the polymer backbone.

2.6. Morphology Characterization. To understand the correlation between the fluorine content and the photovoltaic performance difference, grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIwAxs) characterization was conducted to define the molecular packing and orientation of the neat polymer films as well as of the blends. As shown in Figure 7, with increasing the fluorination degree in backbone, multiple lamellar stacking peaks of $\left( h 0 0 \right)$ in the out-of-plane direction came into even more distinct. The edge-on (010) $\pi \cdot$ stacking crystallite coherence length $\scriptstyle \left( \mathbf { C C L } _ { 0 1 0 } \right)$ values that calculated from the Scherrer equation increased gradually in the following order: P6T-F00 (33.31 A), P6T-F50 (35.01 A), P6T-F75 (40.27 A), and P6T-F100 (46.08 A), which suggests the improved microstructural ordering and enhanced crystalline structure induced by the higher degree of backbone fluorination, which are consistent with the \*relative aggregation strength' results mentioned above. In particular, the P6T-F100 crystallites featured a mixed edge-on and face-on orientations, a so-called 3-D texture. Upon blending the $\mathrm { P } 6 \mathrm { T } { \cdot } \mathrm { F } x$ polymers with EH-IDTBR, all of the P6T- $\mathrm { . F } x$ :EH-IDTBR films exhibited dominant face-on orientations with well-defined lamellar structure retained in the fluorine-containing system. Note that the (010) $\pi \cdot$ face-on diffraction positions and corresponding $\mathrm { C C L } _ { 0 1 0 }$ values presented the following order: P6TF00:EH-IDTBR $( 1 . 6 \bar { 9 } \ \mathring { \mathrm { A } } ^ { - 1 }$ 19.88 A) $<$ P6T-F50:EH-IDTBR (1.72 A-1, 20.97 A) $<$ P6T-F100:EH-IDTBR $\mathit { \Omega } ^ { \prime } 1 . 7 6 \mathrm { \AA } ^ { - 1 }$ 29.93 $\begin{array} { r l r } { \hat { \mathrm { A } } ) } & { { } < } & { \mathrm { P } 6 \mathrm { T } { \cdot } \mathrm { F } 7 5 { \cdot } \mathrm { E } \mathrm { H } { \cdot } \mathrm { I } \mathrm { D } \mathrm { T } \mathrm { B } \mathrm { R } } \end{array}$ $\left( 1 . 7 7 \mathrm { ~ \normalfont ~ \AA ^ { - 1 } ~ } \right.$ 38.24 A). The parameters indicate the formation of much higher crystalline and denser molecular packing nanostructures in the fluorinecontaining blends than that of fluorine-free blend, accounting for their significantly improved charge transport properties.5,8

The top surface and bulk morphology of the blend films were further investigated by atomic force microscopy (AFM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM), respectively. The AFM height and phase images (size: 5 $\mu \mathrm { m } \times 5 \ \mu \mathrm { m } )$ are shown in Figure 8a,b. All blend films exhibit smooth surfaces with root-mean-square (rms) roughness of $1 . 4 2 { - } 2 . 1 1 \ \mathrm { \ n m } _ { }$ suggesting excellent flm-forming property of the P6T- $\mathrm { . F } x$ polymers. A more distinct difference can be observed in the TEM images. As shown in Figure 8c, the P6T-F00:EH-IDTBR blend film is quite homogeneous without distinct phase separation, indicative of very good miscibility between P6TF00 and EH-IDTBR. This intimately mixed morphology is not beneficial for charge transport and can lead to considerable geminate recombination. The P6T-F50:EH-IDTBR blend film shows slightly improved phase separation. The P6T-F75:EHIDTBR and P6T-F1O0:EH-IDTBR blend films are further phase separated while granular aggregates become visible in the P6T-F75:EH-IDTBR blend, which can act as a trap or barrier for efficient charge generation and transport. In contrast, well-dispersed fibrillar structure with bicontinuous interpenetrating networks is obtained in P6T-F100:EHIDTBR blend, contributing to more efficient exciton dissociation and charge transport and thus resulting in higher $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF in solar cell.7-60 These morphological features are attributable to the solubility and aggregation property of the polymers. With increasing fluorine content, the solubility of the polymer is decreased and finer fibrils can be formed.61

# 3. CONCLUSION

We have developed a set of new polythiophenes (P6T-F00, P6T-F50, P6T-F75, and P6T-F100) with different fluorine contents for application in non-fullerene PSCs. The crystallinity, absorption coefficient, hole transport mobility, and relative dielectric constant $\varepsilon _ { \mathrm { r } }$ of the polythiophenes improve significantly with increasing fluorination degree. The increase of fluorination degree leads to reduced exciton binding energy, enhanced PL quenching efficiency, suppressed charge recombination, and extended charge carrier lifetime when the polymers blended with a non-fullerene acceptor EH-IDTBR. Furthermore, the morphology of blend films is more phase separated accompanied by the formation of finer fibrillar nanostructures upon increasing fluorination degree. As a result, the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF of the PSCs enhance dramatically from P6T-F00 to P6T-Fi00. The device based on the most fluorinated polymer P6T-F100 offered a champion PCE of $7 . 3 \%$ which is superior to the device based on P3HT:EH-IDTBR and P3HT:O-IDTBR binary blends. Our results suggest that backbone fluorination is an effective strategy to boost the photovoltaic properties of PTs in non-fullerene PSCs. We believe polythiophenes will play a more important role in highperformance PSCs via rational molecular design.
