# Recent Advances in Single-Junction Organic Solar Cells

Huifeng Yao and Jianhui Hou\*

![](images/2a6950b9bb9d20fd46480a308630402c3c02e3d8298703b9df335e45d2d9ab75.jpg)

Abstract: Single-junction organic solar cells (OsCs) have made significant progress in recent years. Innovations in material design and device optimization have improved the power conversion efficiencies to over $1 9 \%$ . In this Minireview, based on recent advances, we discuss molecular design strategies to tune the absorption spectrum, energy level, and intermolecular aggregation as well as highlight the role of molecular electrostatic potential in decreasing energy loss. Then, we introduce the latest progress in four types of OSCs composed of different donoracceptor combinations: polymer donor:small-molecule acceptor, all-polymer, all-small-molecule, and small-molecule donor: polymer acceptor. Finally, the challenges of OSCs in practical applications, including material cost, stability, and multifunction integration, are discussed.

# 1. Introduction

Organic solar cells (OsCs) are a next-generation photovoltaic technology that uses organic semiconductors to convert photons into electricity; which has potential advantages of low cost, flexibility, and semi-transparency.ll In the past few years, owing to innovations in materials, understanding of the working mechanism, and device optimization, OsCs have achieved significant progress with a rapid increase in power conversion efficiency (PCE) (Figure 1). At present, cutting-edge OSC devices can output PCEs of $\approx 1 9 \%$ under the standard one-sun condition (AM 1.5G),2-4] showing a significantly narrowed gap compared to perovskite or silicon solar cells and demonstrating the considerable potential for some practical applications.

Organic semiconductors are $\pi$ -bonded small molecules or polymers that are made up of carbon, hydrogen, and heteroatoms such as nitrogen, sulfur, and oxygen, which have many differences from their inorganic counterparts.5] On one hand, the dielectric constants of organic semiconductors are much lower than that of inorganic photovoltaic materials. Light absorption generates tightly bonded Frenkel excitons (hole-electron pairs) instead of free charges, where a heterojunction formed by a donor and an acceptor is required to facilitate charge dissociation.6,7] On the other hand, when compared to inorganic photovoltaic materials that possess highly ordered molecular structures, OSC materials are usually amorphous or less crystalline. The hierarchical morphology generally has a pronounced influence on  charge  generation,  transport, and recombination.8,9]

The development of photoactive materials plays a crucial role in increasing the PCEs of OSCs. Fullerene derivatives such as [6,6]-phenyl-C61/C71-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) have been the dominant acceptors for approximately two decades. During this period, designing donors, including small molecules and polymers, was the main focus of related research. Poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT) was widely used as a donor in early studies, and this type of polymer remains one of the most important OsC materials to this day.10] To decrease the band gap, the donor-acceptor alternating concept was introduced into the design of polymer donors.1 Owing to the enhanced intramolecular charge transfer (ICT) effect, the absorption spectra are largely redshifted.12,13] Polymers such as $\mathrm { P T B 7 - T h } ^ { [ 1 4 ] }$ and PffBT-4T[15,16] have achieved PCEs of approximately $1 1 \ \%$ Since 2015, non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs) have emerged as the most popular materials and have brought new prosperity to the research field.[17,18]

In this Minireview, we discuss recent advances in singlejunction OsCs. Due to space limitations, progress in electron/hole-transporting materials and multi-junction OSCs is not included. In the first part, based on recent progress, we discuss molecular design strategies for highly efficient materials. We then highlight the best results for four types of devices. Finally, we discuss the further development of single-junction OSCs.

# 2. Rational Material Design Strategy

To obtain outstanding photovoltaic parameters of the shortcircuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ , open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } )$ , and fill factor (FF), the absorption spectrum, molecular energy level, and intermolecular aggregation should be carefully tuned when designing OsC materials. Currently, there is an urgent need to identify a rational method to decrease energy loss $( E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } )$

# 2.1. Absorption Spectrum and Molecular Energy Level

Light absorption is the first step in photon-electron conversion in OsCs. The optical absorption of the donoracceptor blend should match well with the solar radiative spectrum, which is a prerequisite for obtaining a high $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ In addition, the maximum achievable $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ is suggested to scale with the energy gap between the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level of the donor and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level of the acceptor. Furthermore, the differences in the frontier orbitals (AHOMO and ALUMO) have a significant impact on charge generation. Therefore, a suitable energy level alignment is the key factor for achieving efficient charge dissociation with minimum $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ [19]

In the past few years, numerous NFAs with acceptordonor-acceptor (A-D-A) structures have been developed (Figure 2a). The absorption spectra and energy levels (Figure 2b) can be changed greatly by modulating the ICT effect. In 2015, Zhan et al.20 reported an NFA ITIC and achieved a very good PCE of $7 . 5 2 \%$ , thus starting a hot research topic in OSCs. The ICT effect can be enhanced by introducing halogen atoms such as fluorine and chlorine into the terminal groups or by adding oxygen atoms to the donor part.21] Conversely, the ICT effect can be suppressed by decreasing the electron-withdrawing properties of the terminal group.22] Through these molecular design strategies, the absorption spectra of ITIC derivatives can cover the entire range of ${ \approx } 4 0 0$ to $1 0 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ with HOMO/LUMO energy levels that are tuned accordingly.

# 2.2. Intermolecular Packing, Charge Transport, and Processability

Alkyl chain engineering can efficiently tune the intermolecular packing, getting a balance between the charge transport and solution processability. Since 2019, the development of the NFA $\mathbf { Y } 6 ^ { [ 2 3 ] }$ has rapidly increased PCEs, which contains two types of alkyl chains: an inner alkyl chain on the pyrrole unit and an outer side chain on the bithiophene (Figure 2c). We previously conducted a two-step alkyl engineering on the chlorinated Y6 (BTP-4Cl). First, we optimized the 2- ethylhexyl (2-EH) alkyl chain of pyrrole using longer alkyl chains of 2-butyloctyl (2-BO) and 2-hexyldecyl (2-HD).[24) Owing to its superior morphology, the BTP-4Cl-12-based device exhibited an optimal PCE of $1 7 \%$ . More importantly, the uniform morphology could be maintained in bladecoating processing technology, which has a significant advantage in fabricating large-area devices. Yan and Zou groups!25] performed detailed studies on the branching positions of alkyl chains and achieved very good results.

Then, we further tuned the outer alkyl chains of the bithiophene moiety, where the original $n$ -undecyl (C11) was shortened to $n$ -nonyl (C9) and then to $n$ -heptyl (C7).[26] The resulting NFA BTP-eC9 could yield $1 8 \%$ PCEs in OSCs. Recently, Sun and co-workers[27] changed the linear alkyl chain (C11) of Y6 with branched ones and tuned the length from 2-BO to 2-HD, and then to 2-octyldodecyl (2-OD). They found that the use of branched alkyl chains can decrease the void sizes in corresponding single crystals (Figure 2d), enhancing the structural order and facilitating charge transport. As a result, the NFA L8-BO with 2-BO alkyl chains achieved a high PCE of $1 8 . 3 2 \%$ in OSCs.

# 2.3. Molecular Electrostatic Potential and Energy Loss

$E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ from the band gap $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } )$ of the material to $\mathrm { e } V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ determines the maximum PCE of a photovoltaic cell (Figure 3a). Theoretically, $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ originates from the charge generation and recombination processes (Figure 3b).28] The decrease in $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ is one of the main reasons for obtaining higher PCEs. For example, the best-performing OSCs based on fullerene derivatives have $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of approximately $0 . 8 \mathrm { e V }$ ${ \mathrm { { } } } E _ { \mathrm { { g } } }$ is evaluated by the intersection point of the absorption and luminescence spectra), thus limiting the PCEs to approximately $1 1 \ \%$ [15,2] For ITIC-like NFAs, the $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ values are decreased to ${ \approx } 0 . 7 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and the PCEs increase to $\approx 1 5 \%$ [30] The Y6-based devices commonly have $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ of approximately $0 . 5 { \mathrm { - } } 0 . 6 \ \mathrm { e V }$ and can yield high PCEs of over $1 8 \%$ . This decreased $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ is closely related to its high electroluminescence quantum efficiency (usually ${ > } 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ ) and thus low non-radiative recombination loss. The electrostatic potential (EsP) is a surface property that expresses the charge distribution of each nucleus and every electron in the molecular system.32] For organic semiconductors, the ESP is a very good indicator for the delocalization of $\pi$ -electrons. Both the asymmetric chemical reaction and noncovalent interaction are highly related to the ESP distributions. In our previous studies, we systematically studied the ESP of commonly seen OSC molecules and their effects on $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ [335] We found that the donor and acceptor have significantly different ESP distributions. By contrast, the NFAs have much higher ESP values than the fullerenes, which facilitates charge generation under low $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ conditions. We then further studied the influence of ESP on the non-radiative $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ using an NFA system (BTP$x \mathrm { F }$ $x = 0$ 2, 4, 6) with gradually increasing ESP (Figure 3c).[36 These results suggested that a higher ESP difference between the donor and acceptor will increase the charge transfer (CT) ratio in its hybridization with the local exciton state (Figure 3d). However, at the same time, the calculated oscillator (Figure 3e) and measured electroluminescence quantum efficiency $( \mathrm { E O E } _ { \mathrm { E L } } )$ suffered a corresponding decrease (Figure 3f). Therefore, we can conclude that finely tuning the ESP is the key for designing OSC materials with low $E _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ In addition to the examples discussed above, many other design methods have been developed and have achieved significant success.37,38]

# 3. Cutting-Edge Single-Junction OSCs

OSC materials can be divided into four categories: polymer donors, small-molecule donors, polymer acceptors, and small-molecule acceptors. Based on the available combinations of the donor and acceptor, there is a total of four types of OsCs, all of which have achieved great advances in the past few years.

# 3.1. Polymer Donor:Small-Molecule Acceptor OSCs

The development of polymer donors plays an essential role in improving PCEs.39] In our previous studies, we designed and developed many benzothiophene (BDT)-based polymers that are widely used to fabricate high-efficiency OSCs.40] At present, most of the top PCEs were obtained using 2D-conjugated BDT materials (Figure 4a). In 2012, we reported the synthesis of the polymer donor PBDB-T via the copolymerization of BDT  and benzo[1,2-c:4,5- $c ^ { \prime } ]$ dithiophene-4,8-dione.41] The absorption spectrum of PBDB-T in the solution state shows a temperature dependence, suggesting a strong pre-aggregation effect (Figure 4b). When blended with NFAs, the aggregation becomes crucial as it offers an opportunity to control the growth of the NFAs and thus obtain an optimal blend morphology with high phase purity. At present, most high-efficiency OSCs with PCEs of over $1 8 \%$ use Y6-type NFAs. The best binary OSCs consisting of one polymer donor and one acceptor have achieved PCEs of $\approx 1 8 . 5 \%$ [47] To get more efficient utilization of sunlight or enhanced charge transport, multiple-component blending is an effective way to obtain higher photovoltaic parameters compared with those of binary OSCs. Note that adding one or two additional materials into the binary blend results in increased complexity of the morphology control. The use of compatible NFAs with similar structures can partially avoid this issue. For example, we recently constructed a ternary system based on a polymer donor (PBQx-TF) and two NFAs (F-BTA3 and eC9-2Cl).2) Owing to the optimized light absorption, energy level alignment, and intermolecular packing, we got a maximum PCE of $1 9 \%$ in the devices. Liu and Sun et al.4l designed a double-fibril network morphology using two polymers (Figure 4c), PBDB-TF and D18, which led to an enhanced exciton diffusion length and reduced recombination rate in the OSC, resulting in the highest PCE reported to date.4]

# 3.2. All-Polymer OSCs

All-polymer OsCs have advantages in terms of inherent mechanical flexibility and thermal stability, and many studies have focused on improving their PCEs. Perylene diimide (PDI) and naphthalene diimide (NDI) are excellent electron-accepting units with large conjugated planes that perform very well in designing polymer acceptors. For example, Zhan and co-workers[49] reported the alternating copolymer PDI-DTT (Figure 5a) and obtained a PCE of over $1 \%$ in 2007. The NDI-based polymer N2200 has gained considerable attention owing to its excellent photovoltaic performance.50] Using siloxane-functionalized side chains, Huang et al.51] designed a polymer donor named PtzBI-Si and obtained PCEs of over $1 1 \ \%$ in N2200-based OSCs. In addition, many other polymer electron acceptors containing B-N,[52] bithiophene imide,[53] and cyanobenzothiadiazole[4)] units have achieved significant advances in all-polymer OSCs.

In 2017, Zhang and Li et al.15] developed a molecular strategy for the design of polymer acceptors, i.e., polymerizing small-molecule acceptor (PSMA) (Figure 5b), which has become very popular and achieved great success.56 In their work, they used brominated terminal groups in IDIC-C16, which provided reaction positions for the Stille polymerization. The resulting polymer PZ1 (the number-average molecular weight $M _ { \mathfrak { n } }$ is $3 3 . 6 5 \mathrm { k D a }$ and the polydispersity index PDI is 2.19) showed a red-shift in the absorption spectrum of $5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ compared to that of IDIC-C16. As a result, the all-polymer OSCs achieved a PCE of $9 . 1 6 \%$

Later, numerous polymer acceptors were reported using the PSMA strategy. The absorption spectrum, molecular energy level, and charge mobility can be effectively tuned by changing the core unit, terminal group, alkyl chain, or linkage units. For IDIC-type polymer acceptors, the OSCs have yielded PCEs of approximately $1 2 \%$ . When the PSMA strategy was used for Y6 molecules,57-62] the PCEs increased to over $1 8 \%$ [63] Note that the $M _ { \mathrm { n } } \mathrm { s }$ are usually lower than $1 5 \mathrm { k D a }$ for Y6-based highly efficient PSMA acceptors, corresponding to a repeat unit number of ${ \approx } 1 0$ . The impact of molecular weight, PDI, and chain conformation on photovoltaic performance should be carefully studied. By matching new polymer donors and/or optimizing the blend morphology, further improvement in the PCEs of allpolymer OSCs can be expected.

# 3.3. All-Small-Molecule OSCs

Although polymers can achieve very high PCEs in different types of OsCs, batch-to-batch variation is a major problem. At present, it remains a significant challenge to control the molecular weight and polydispersity during polymerization, which limits large-scale production. In addition, the structural defects formed in the reaction also have uncertain effects on the photovoltaic performance. Therefore, studying the OsCs made of all-small molecules with definite molecular structures is very important.

With the development of materials and device optimization methods, all-small-molecule OsCs have achieved much progress. In the past few years, the PCEs of all-smallmolecule OsCs have experienced a rapid increase using the NFAs. For example, Zhu et al.[64] designed an NFA named NITI and blended it with a BDT-based donor called BTR[65] to fabricate devices. The binary OsC only yielded a moderate PCE of $6 . 8 2 \%$ . They then added PCBM as the third component to tune the morphology and charge transport. As a result, the optimal all-small-molecule OsCs achieved a maximum PCE of $1 3 . 6 3 \%$ .At present, the phenyl-substituted BTR named B1 recorded the highest PCE of $1 7 \%$ in the ternary all-small-molecule OsCs; two compatible NFAs, BO-2Cl and BO-4Cl, were used.[66] It should be noted that the morphology control is particularly important because the small molecules usually have higher crystalline properties and a faster film-forming rate compared with the polymers. In addition to routine device optimization methods such as the use of solvent additives and thermal annealing, solvent vapor annealing is usually necessary to get the optimal phase separation and bicontinuous charge transport network. It is still urgent to develop efficient and easy methods to further improve the PCEs of the all-small molecule OSCs.

# 3.4. Small-Molecule Donor:Polymer Acceptor OSCs

Compared to the other three OsC combinations, smallmolecule donor:polymer acceptor OsCs have shown much more sluggish development.167,68] To date, the best-performing devices have realized PCEs of only $\approx 1 0 \%$ ,which is much lower than that of the polymer donor:small-molecule acceptor-type OsCs. Because both combinations consist of one small molecule and one polymer, it is interesting to explore the reasons for this large efficiency gap. On one hand, the development of efficient polymer acceptors has been a major issue. Liu et al.52] developed a series of $\mathbf { B } { \gets } \mathbf { N }$ polymer acceptors with very good applications. The highest PCE of $9 . 5 1 \%$ was recorded for a $\mathbf { B } { \gets } \mathbf { N }$ polymer acceptor (PBN-15) combined with a small-molecule donor (BD3T).[69] With the development of PSMAs, PCEs are expected to increase rapidly in the near future. On the other hand, small-molecule donors usually have a higher crystallinity than small-molecule NFAs because of their more planar structures, which may cause overlarge phase domains and thus limit the charge generation. Therefore, it is necessary to design new donors that can form desirable morphologies with the polymer acceptors. Although the PCEs are not sufficiently high, this type of OSC has exhibited outstanding thermal stability in some reports, which may be attributed to the phase stability of the polymer and the high crystallinity of the small-molecule donor. This advantage is extremely attractive because it is an urgent requirement for other types of high-efficiency OSCs.

# 4. Further Development of Single-Junction OSCs

In the past decade, OsCs have experienced the transition from fullerenes to NFAs and have achieved great progress. As the efficiency gap has been significantly narrowed compared with other photovoltaic technologies, OsCs have demonstrated great potential for practical applications. To make OsCs more commercially accessible, the following issues beyond efficiency should be considered.

# 4.1. Material Cost

Although state-of-the-art materials show very high PCEs, it should be noted that their chemical structures become increasingly complex, resulting in increased material cost. Therefore, the development of highly efficient materials with simple structures and easy syntheses is an important topic. For polymer donors such as PBDB-TF and D18, tedious synthetic procedures and low-yield reactions are the main reasons for their high cost. In contrast, polythiophenes are classic polymer donors with very simple structures (Figure 6). Taking P3HT as an example, by optimizing the NFAs and the processing method, the best P3HT-based OSCs can achieve a PCE of $\approx 1 0 \%$ [70] To downshift the energy level and obtain a controlled morphology simultaneously, electron-withdrawing functional groups such as fluorine and cyano groups were used. We designed estersubstituted polythiophene PDCBT and its fluoride, PDCBT2F, which achieved very good PCEs.[71] Duan etal.[72] combined fluorination and cyano-substitution to design P5TCN-Fx and obtained PCEs of over $1 7 \%$ . In a recent study, we designed the ester-substituted poly(thiophene vinylene) PTVT-T and obtained PCEs of over $1 6 \%$ [73] Li et al.47] developed a polymer donor, PTQ10, consisting of two simple units, i.e., thiophene and difluoride quinoxaline, which achieved a very high PCE of $1 8 . 5 5 \%$ in a recent report.

ITIC- and Y6-like NFAs have multi-ring fused ladder cores, which increase the synthetic complexity and thus lead to high costs of these materials.74] In 2019, by removing the $\mathrm { s p } ^ { 3 } { \cdot } \mathrm { C }$ , Li et al.175] broke the fused structure to design several non-fused NFAs. With the assistance of non-covalent interactions, the non-fused NFA PTIC could achieve PCEs of over $1 0 \%$ . To achieve efficient charge transport, the molecular design of non-fused NFAs should carefully tune their intermolecular packing. In our recent study, we used bis-triisopropylphenyl-substituted bithiophene as the core structure to design NFA A4T-16[76] Owing to the 3Dinterpenetrated charge transport, a high PCE of $1 5 . 2 \%$ was recorded in OSCs. Many other non-fused NFAs have been reported,[77,78] and we believe that higher PCEs can be realized by optimizing the material or processing methods. Note that the molecular design of low-cost materials is relatively independent, it is important to develop matchable donor and acceptor combinations in future studies.

# 4.2. Stability

In the past few years, the stability of OsCs has attracted increasing attention, as this is one of the biggest obstacles preventing their practical application. The instability originates mainly from two degradation models. First, the active materials or interfaces are likely to undergo chemical decomposition, particularly in the presence of external factors such as light, water, and oxygen. For example, Li et al.79] found that the volume-conserving photo-isomerization of exocyclic vinyl groups is one of the main decomposition steps of A-D-A-type NFAs. Second, morphological rearrangement is the other most prevalent degradation mode, which is closely related to intermolecular interactions. Recently, Ade etal.[80] revealed that the morphological evolution in state-of-the-art systems is strongly dependent on the diffusion of NFAs. The relatively low glass transition temperatures of Y6-like NFAs result in a high diffusion coefficient, which may be a major cause of their unstable morphology.81] In addition, to obtain an accurate evaluation and fair comparison, stability testing should follow standard protocols,8] which are of great significance for the long-term development of OsCs.

# 4.3. Multiple-Function Integration

To make OsCs more competitive, it is necessary to fully utilize their unique features. For example, the great tunability of the light-response range has enabled some promising applications, such as semi-transparent devices, nearinfrared (NIR) organic photodetectors (OPDs), and indoor photovoltaic cells. Semitransparent photovoltaic cells should have high transmittance in the visible light range. The utilization of solar photons in the NIR region has become increasingly important.[83]

Using the low-band gap NFA IEICO-4Cl, we fabricated efficient semi-transparent OsCs with controllable colors (Figure 7a).[21) Yip et al.[84) demonstrated that a heat-insulation function could be effectively integrated into semitransparent OSCs (Figure 7b). In addition, low-band gap materials are promising for NIR OPD applications. For instance, Zhu et al.85] developed a series of NFAs with absorption up to $1 \mu \mathrm { m }$ and achieved an excellent detectivity of over $1 0 ^ { 1 3 }$ Jones in OPDs (Figure 7c). Organic photovoltaic cells can also yield very good PCEs under the illumination of indoor light sources, such as light-emitting diodes (LEDs) and fluorescent lamps (Figure 7d). By designing wide-band gap materials, $1 0 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ organic photovoltaic cells have obtained a high PCE of $2 3 \%$ (Figure 7e).[86] Recently, we demonstrated that the integration of photovoltaic and lightemitting functions could be achieved in a single device (Figure 7f).[87] In addition to the applications mentioned above, it is of great importance to develop more functional integrations of OsCs by taking advantage of non-toxicity, light weight, and flexibility.

# 5. Summary and Outlook

In the past few years, significant progress has been made in the field of OsCs. With the development of Y6-type materials, the PCEs of single-junction OSCs have surpassed $1 9 \%$ , showing great potential for practical applications. In this Minireview, based on recent advances, we discuss rational molecular design strategies for optimizing the absorption spectrum, energy level, intermolecular aggregation, and energy loss. The development of new-generation materials with enhanced luminescent properties is a feasible method for achieving the next breakthrough. The latest progress in the four types of OsCs, including polymer donor:small-molecule acceptor, all-polymer, all-small-molecule, and small-molecule donor:polymer acceptor OsCs, is also discussed. We also highlight a few research directions that could make OsCs more commercially accessible.
