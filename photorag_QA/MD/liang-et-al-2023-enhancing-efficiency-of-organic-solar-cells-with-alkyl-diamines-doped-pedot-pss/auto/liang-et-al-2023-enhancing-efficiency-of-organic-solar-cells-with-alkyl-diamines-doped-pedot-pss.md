# Supporting Information

ABSTRACT: Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene): poly(styrene-sulfonate) (PEDOT: PSS) has been the most popular conductive polymer as a hole transport material in organic solar cells (OsCs). Modification of PEDOT: PSS can improve its various properties to realize high performance in OSCs. The conductivity of PEDOT: PSS is an essential factor in determining device performance. In this work, PEDOT: PSS-BA, PEDOT: PSS-HA, and PEDOT: PSS-OA are prepared via facile doping of corresponding alkyl diamines into PEDOT: PSS, considering the amino ion can interact with redundant sulfonic acid groups of PSS. All the modified products exhibit enhanced conductivities due to their more ordered molecular packing comparing PEDOT: PSS. Using PTQ10 and m-BTP-PhC6 as the binary active layer, the OSC devices based on PEDOT: PSS-BA yield a superior power conversion efficiency of $1 8 . 4 4 \%$ , which ranks as one of the highest values in the binary OSCs.

rganic solar cells (OsCs) have become a hot research topic in the materials science and engineering field because of their ability to fabricate low-cost, lightweight, flexible, and semitransparent devices via solution processing.1-5 With numerous efforts dedicated to the development of materials and device engineering,6- 6-10 the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of OSCs have achieved over $1 9 \% , ^ { 1 1 - 1 7 }$ but still need further improvement to be more competitive for their commercialization. Aside from the reasonable design of active materials,s suitable interfacial layers are also of high significance in improving the efficiency and stability of OsCs.1923 On the one hand, the interfacial layers contribute to the enhancement of charge extraction by tuning the work function (wF) to form an ohmic contact between active layers and electrodes.2425 On the other hand, the recombination of interfacial charge can be reduced with the selectivity to transport the holes (or electrons) and block the electrons (or holes) by choosing the right hole transport layer (or electron transport layer).26,27

The famous conductive polymer, poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene): poly(styrene-sulfonate) (PEDOT: PSS), has been widely applied as a hole transport material (HTM) owing to its good transparency, solution processing, and thermostability.28-31 One of the key factors in determining device performance is the conductivity of the conductive polymer.32 However, the rapid development of solar cells raises higher requirements for the properties of PEDOT: PSs.33-36 For instance, it is necessary to enhance the WF of PEDOT: PSS to match the deepening HOMO level of emerging donors.24,37

More importantly, PEDOT: PSS's shortcomings including the relatively low conductivity as well as the electrical and structural inhomogeneities brought by the redundant sulfonic acid groups of Pss will affect the performance of devices.38,39 Doping strategy is an efficient method to modulate these properties for the requirement of practical applications.40-44 Various polar organic solvents,5-48 ionic liquids,49-51 and surfactants2-s4 have been added into PEDOT: PSS and successfully improved its conductivity. However, the traditional post-treatment modification method is not only relatively cumbersome in the process of solvent removal, but also at the expense of the smoothness and uniformity of the surface morphologys-s7 In previous work, our group developed phenylethylamine derivatives as dopants to achieve the enhancement of work function and conductivity.58,59 It is worth noting that the sulfonic acid groups of PSS can interact with phenylethylamine, which is beneficial to the more ordered packing of conjugated backbones, resulting in the improvement of device performances. Therefore, it is of great significance to develop a simple and efficient method to modify PEDOT: PSS.

The effect of aromatic amino derivatives with one amino group on the properties and device performances of dopedPEDOT: PSS has been studied in our previous work.58,59 In this work, three alkyl diamines with two amino groups and different lengths of alkyl chain including 1,4-butanediamine hydrochloride (BA-HCl), 1,6-hexanediamine hydrochloride (HA-HCl) and 1,8-octanediamine (OA) were proposed to dope PEDOT: PSS, respectively. These alkyl diamines possess two active functional groups to react with the redundant sulfonic acid groups of Pss, then enhancing the packing interaction of PEDOT backbone (Figure 1). By mixing the dopant with PEDOT: PSS solution directly, the corresponding HTMs named PEDOT: PSS-BA, PEDOT: PSS-HA, and PEDOT: PSS-OA were prepared. With the advantages of enhanced work function and conductivity, these HTMs were further applied for OsCs to investigate their performances. Due to the enhanced carrier extraction and reduced charge recombination, the PEDOT: PSS-BA-based devices with PTQ10:m-BTP-PhC6 as active layer achieved the best PCE of $1 8 . 4 4 \%$ , which is higher than that of devices with PEDOT: PSS $\left( 1 7 . 7 6 \% \right)$ . This work demonstrates the doping of alkyl diamines into PEDOT: PSS is a simple and efficient method to improve its performance for high-efficiency OSCs.

The ultraviolet-visible-near-infrared (UV-vis-NIR) absorption and transparence spectra of these HTMs were investigated to study the effect of doping on absorption. The absorption spectra of PEDOT: PSS doped by alkyl diamines almost overlapped with the pristine PEDOT: PSS in solution and flm (Figure S1). Meanwhile, the transmittance of PEDOT: PSS flm before and after doping remained nearly constant (Figure S1). These results suggest that the addition of alkyl diamines did not obviously affect the optical properties of PEDOT: PSS, which is beneficial to the light absorption of the active layer.

The cyclic voltammetry (CV) test was conducted to probe the oxidation process of four HTMs and the result was shown in Figure S2. In the CV curves of PEDOT: PSS, PEDOT: PSSBA and PEDOT: PSS-HA, none of the obvious oxidation peak was observed, while the HOMO level was determined to be $5 . 3 8 \mathrm { e V }$ for PEDOT: PSS-OA. After scanning for 15 cycles, the oxidation current of modified PEDOT: PSS increased with increasing scanning cycles, indicating their higher electrochemical oxidation stability. Furthermore, the photoelectron spectroscopy in air (PESA) test was carried out to verify the WF change of four HTMs with their coated film (Figure $2 \mathrm { a } - 2 \mathrm { d } )$ . The WFs of PEDOT: PSS-BA and PEDOT: PSS-HA were estimated to be 5.44 and $5 . 4 6 ~ \mathrm { e V } $ respectively, which are much higher than $5 . 3 6 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ of PEDOT: PSS (Table 1). Due to the relatively poor water solubility, the dopant OA did not significantly change the arrangement of PEDOT in aqueous solution, resulting in a WF of $5 . 3 7 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for PEDOT: PSS-OA, which is close to the original value. An increased WF is desirable to have a better match with the emerging donors with deep HOMO levels. To comprehend the reason for the enhancement of WF, the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels of these three dopants were measured by the cyclic voltammetry method (Figure S3). The HOMO energy levels of BA-HCl, HA-HCl, and OA are calculated to be 5.53, 5.55, and $5 . 0 9 \ \mathrm { e V } $ respectively. These values except OA are well above the well-acknowledged WF of $5 . 1 ~ \mathrm { { \ e V } }$ for PEDOT: PSS. It is worth noting that these results are in good agreement with the changing trend of the WFs of doped PEDOT: PSS films.

The conductivity of four HTMs was measured by the fourpoint probe method (Table 1). Consequently, the conductivities of PEDOT: PSS-BA, PEDOT: PSS-HA, and PEDOT: PSS-OA were determined as $2 . 8 3 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 }$ $3 . 1 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 }$ and $8 . 6 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { ~ S ~ c m ^ { - 1 } }$ which are much higher than that of pristine PEDOT: PSS film $( 6 . 7 1 \ \times \ 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { ~ S ~ } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ . The improvement of conductivity can be attributed to the more regular packing of PEDOT backbone due to the interaction between superfluous sulfonic acid groups and the anion ion of alkyl diamines leading to stronger intermolecular stacking of the PSS segments. Furthermore, the $\mathrm { p H }$ value of modified PEDOT: PSS exhibited a minor improvement compared with strongly acidic PEDOT: PSS by adding a small amount of alkyl diamines (Table S1).

The electron spin resonance (EsR) test was applied to investigate the radical character of four HTMs in both solution and powder under the same condition (Figure 2e-2f). Compared with the original PEDOT: PSS, three doped samples solution exhibited a stronger ESR intensity. In addition, the ESR signal increase of doped samples was more remarkable than that of PEDOT: PSS when testing the same mass of dry powder. The results show that the paramagnetism could be raised by doping of alkyl diamines due to the enhanced packing of PEDOT enabled by the strong interchain interaction between PSS and the dopants,60-62 thereby improving the ESR signal and conductivity of alkyl diaminedoped PEDOT: PSS.

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) was employed to investigate the surface morphology of these HTMs (Figure 3a-3d). The root-mean-square (RMs) roughness values of PEDOT: PSS, PEDOT: PSS-BA, PEDOT: PSS-HA and PEDOT: PSS-OA film were 2.94, 2.66, 1.92, and $5 . 4 5 \ \mathrm { n m } ,$ respectively, which suggests that all these HTMs, except PEDOT: PSS-OA, exhibited a quite smooth surface morphology. This can be attributed to the difference in the water solubility of the dopants. Both BA-HCl and HA-HCl containing hydrochloride are easily dissolved in PEDOT: PSS aqueous solution, allowing a more efficient reaction between the dopant and PEDOT: PSS, thus improving the surface morphology (Figure S4). On the contrary, the OA with relatively low water solubility inclines to self-aggregation in the PEDOT: PSS, then enlarging the surface roughness. The smoother and more uniform film is beneficial to hole extraction and charge transport.

To evaluate the practicability of PEDOT: PSS modified by alkyl diamines on the device performances, the OsCs were fabricated with the photoactive materials of PTQ10 as polymer donor and m-BTP-PhC6 as small molecule acceptor that we reported in previous work.64 The chemical structures and device structure are shown in Figure 4a and Figure 4b, respectively. The relevant current density-voltage $\left( J - V \right)$ curves are portrayed in Figure 4c, and the photovoltaic performances are summarized in Table 2. For the control device based on PEDOT: PSS, the optimal PCE of $1 7 . 7 6 \%$ was achieved, which is accordant with our previous report.64 The performance improvements arose in devices with all three types of doped HTMs, among which the PCE of those using PEDOT: PSS-BA was the highest up to $1 8 . 4 4 \%$ with the open circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ of $0 . 8 9 1 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } _ { ; }$ short circuit current density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \right)$ of $2 5 . 9 7 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and fill factor (FF) of $7 9 . 7 \%$ which is also one of the highest value in the binary OSCs reported to date.65-69 The PCE enhancement results from the simultaneously increased $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF. In addition, the device with PEDOT: PSS-HA and PEDOT: PSS-OA as HTM exhibited the optimal PCE of $1 8 . 1 1 \%$ and $1 7 . 9 1 \%$ ,respectively. In previous work, the morphological tuning effect of HTM on active layer is popular and important to device performance, and the source of this effect usually comes from the change of surface energy of HTM.70,71 However, this change is not obvious in this work. Alkyl diamines can effectively adjust the internal structure of PEDOT: PSS, but it seems difficult to change its surfaces,s9 Therefore, the PCE improvement of devices is mainly attributed to the enhanced conductivity of modified PEDOT: PSS.

The external quantum efficiency (EQE) of the OSCs was further measured to verify the accuracy of $J { - } V$ results (Figure 4d). The integrated $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ from the EQE spectra exhibited good consistency with the experimental values from the $J { - } V$ measurements within a $3 \%$ mismatch. Furthermore, the devices based on PEDOT: PSS-BA displayed an enhanced photoelectrical response than that with PEDOT: PSS in the wavelength range of $5 0 0 { - } 8 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ indicating more efficient charge extraction from the active layer. Figure 4b shows the distribution of device efficiencies, which means the doping method is reliable and repeatable.

To gain insight into the exciton dissociation and charge collection behavior, the corresponding curves of photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ versus effective voltage $\left( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } \right)$ were studied (Figure 5a and Table S4). $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ is equal to the current density under illumination $\left( J _ { \mathrm { L } } \right)$ minus that in the dark $\left( J _ { \mathrm { D } } \right)$ , and $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ is defined as the voltage value at $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } = 0$ $\left( V _ { 0 } \right)$ subtracts from the applied voltage $( \bar { V } _ { \mathrm { a p p l } } )$ . Then, the exciton dissociation probability $\left( P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } \right)$ and charge collection probability $\left( P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } \right)$ of the OsCs were calculated according to the formula of $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } =$ $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } / \mathrm { J } _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } ~ = ~ J _ { \mathrm { m a x } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } ,$ respectively, where $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ is the saturated photocurrent density and $J _ { \mathrm { m a x } }$ is the $J _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ at maximum power output point. Both $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ of devices could be enhanced by doping alkyl diamines into PEDOT: PSS, among which the highest $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ of $9 9 . 6 \%$ was obtained for those with PEDOT: PSS-OA, and the $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ peaked at $9 2 . 4 \%$ for those with PEDOT: PSS-BA. These results demonstrate the modification of PEDOT: PSS can efficiently boost carrier extraction and reduce charge recombination in the devices. Meanwhile, the dark $J { - } V$ curves of these devices were depicted in Figure 5b. The devices with doped PEDOT: PSS displayed lower dark current than that with pristine counterpart, indicative of an improved electron blocking process and more efficient suppression of bimolecular recombination.

Figure 5. (a) Dark $J { - } V$ curves (b) and $J _ { \mathbf { p h } }$ versus $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ curves of the OSC devices with PTQ10:m-BTP-PhC6 system based on PEDOT:PSS, PEDOT: PSS-BA, PEDOT: PSS-HA, and PEDOT: PSS-OA, respectively.

In summary, a facile method of PEDOT: PSS modification by the doping of alkyl diamines was provided to improve its multiple properties by the interaction between the amino ion and the redundant sulfonate groups to improve the stacking of PEDOT. The incorporation of alkyl diamine could endow PEDOT: PSS with enhanced work function, conductivity and surface morphology. Among three kinds of modified products, the PTQ10:m-BTP-PhC6-based device with PEDOT: PSS-BA offered the largest PCE enhancement, which increased from $1 7 . 7 6 \%$ to $1 8 . { \dot { 4 } } 4 \%$ . The higher efficiency resulted from the improved carrier extraction and suppressed charge recombination. Overall, this work paves a way for facile and efficient modification of PEDOT: PSS to enhance the performance of organic electronic devices.
