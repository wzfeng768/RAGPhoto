# Diffusion-Limited Accepter Alloy Enables Highly Efficient and Stable Organic Solar Cells

Cen Zhang, Ming Zhang, Qiuju Zhou, Shanshan Chen,\* Seoyoung Kim, Jia Yao, Ze Zhang, Yang Bai, Qi Chen, Bowen Chang, Hongyuan Fu, Lingwei Xue, Haiqiao Wang, Changduk Yang, and Zhi-Guo Zhang\*

Organic solar cells (OsCs) are designed based on a blend of polymer donor and small molecular acceptor whereby the thermodynamic relaxation of the morphology raises the concerns related to operational stability. Herein, it is demonstrated that the classical Y6-based binary device can be stabilized by using its derivative of ZCCF3 as the third component, which is designed with the replacing of the thiadiazole group on Y6 with the trifluoromethyl substituted diazepine unit. ZCCF3 delivers not only higher glass transition temperature $( T _ { \mathfrak { g } } )$ than Y6 but also have hyper-miscibility with Y6, contributing to a favorable diffusion-limited Y6:ZCCF3 alloy when blended with polymer donor. Consequently, a champion power conversion efficiency of $1 8 . 5 4 \%$ is achieved in the optimal PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 devices, which can retain their $80 \%$ initial efficiency of up to $3 6 0 \text{‰}$ . This study highlights the importance of high $\tau _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of the third component and its derived hyper-miscible accepter alloys in achieving highly efficient and stable OSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Organic solar cell (OsC) is a promising renewable energy technology with the advantages of being lightweight, low-cost, and flexible, which has attracted considerable research attention.[1-7]

Rapid progress in material design of polymer donor and donor-acceptor (D-A) constructed small molecular acceptors (SMAs) as well as device engineering with new interlayers has boosted the power conversion efficiency (PCE) upward the threshold value in various practical applications.[8-18] However, with the solution deposition process in OsCs, the polymer donors and SMAs construct bulk heterojunction (BHJ), most of which are kinetically quenched to be a thermodynamically metastable or non-equilibrium state in the solid.[19-23] Once exposed to thermal stress, heating-induced molecular relations and excessively self-aggregation inevitably lead to a sharp decline in PCEs, which is a challenging issue in realizing a long-term operational stability of OsCs.[24,25]

To minimize the trade-off between device efficiency and stability in OsCs, great efforts have been devoted to investigating effective strategies that can suppress the diffusion-enabled demixing of the morphology under thermal stress. For example, the non-fullerene SMAs with weak crystallinity, i.e., feature-less thermally phase transition, retained $7 5 \%$ of its initial PCE after thermal treatment at $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 1 6 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ [26] The utilization of an extra UV crosslinker was also verified to be a feasible method for confining the segment relations of the photovoltaic materials to lock BHJ morphology upon thermal treatment.27-29] Moreover, due to the intrinsic characteristic of high-temperature resistance, insulating polymeric matrices, i.e., poly(aryl ether) and polyacenaphthylene, were introduced to elevate thermal stability in device.[30,31] Nevertheless, such component regulations are normally detrimental to the charge carrier transport accompanied by the attenuation of PCEs as sacrificial merit. Thus, it's urgent to explore acceptor semiconductors with an intrinsic higher glass transition temperature $( T _ { \mathrm { g } } )$ to approach diffusion-limited crystallization in the matrix against long-term thermal stress. Recently, ternary strategy, adding an additional SMAs into conventional binary polymer donor: SMA blend, such as the classical PM6:Y6 blend, to tune the absorption, morphology and energy loss, has attracted great interest in the field of OsCs.32-35] Despite the advantage in achieving record high efficiency, fewer effects has been focused on the device stability within this promising approach.36]

To address the challenge, here, based on the structure of A-DA"D-A type SMA (typical Y6 and its derivative),[37] we designed ZCCF3 (Figure 1) with a diazepine unit in the $\mathrm { A } ^ { \prime \prime } ~ \pi \cdot$ core as the third component in the binary PM6: Y6 device. Owing to the strong non-covalent interactions along with weak electron-withdrawing capacity of the $\mathsf { A } ^ { \prime }$ core, the trifluoromethyl substituted diazepine in ZCCF3 enables a high $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of $1 1 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and suitable energy levels in comparison with that of typical Y6 acceptor (Figure 1b). Moreover, the estimated Flory-Huggins interaction parameter $\left( \chi \right)$ between ZCCF3 and Y6 is as low as 0.03, which inclines to form a glass-forming acceptor alloy for their blend (Figure 1d).34,38] In the given PM6:Y6:ZCCF3 ternary platform, a champion PCE of $1 8 . 5 4 \%$ was achieved with a decent open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ of $0 . 8 6 \mathrm { V }$ and short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } )$ of $2 6 . 9 5 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . Especially, greatly enhanced thermal stability was realized in the PM6:Y6:ZCCF3 device. Through investigating the morphology evolution under thermal stress, we highlight that the ZCCF3 featuring higher $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ can sever as an anchor to prevent the diffusion of Y6 in its polymer blend. These findings provide insight to reconsider the underlying vitrification mechanism for developing intrinsic thermally stable OsCs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

# 2.1. Material Design and Synthesis

In light of the "like dissolves like" principle, a new Y6-derived SMA with a benzodiazepine electron-deficient unit was designed and synthesized. Note that the diazepine group in ZCCF3 is a seven-membered heterocyclic unit containing two imides, and features a relatively weaker electron-withdrawing capacity in comparison with the benzothiadiazole group in Y6. The noncoplanar scaffold of diazepine is known to alleviate the quinoid resonance of the fused-ring core when the $\pi$ electrons are delocalized. The additional trifluoromethyl group $\left( \mathrm { C F } _ { 3 } \right)$ at the 2,4-position of the diazepine unit is expected to provide a halogen bonding effect, which can enhance the noncovalent bonding with Y6 in the form of F...S and $\mathrm { C - H \cdot \cdot \cdot F }$ interactions.[39,40]

The synthetic route of ZCCF3 is shown in Figure 1a. The commercially available precursor 1 was treated with lithium aluminum hydride to afford diamine compound 2. Without further purification, compound 2 was directly cyclo-condensated with hexafluoroacetyl acetone in the presence of boron trifluoride diethyl etherate $\left( \mathrm { B F } _ { 3 } \cdot \mathrm { E t } _ { 2 } \right)$ as Lewis acid-based catalyst to obtain the diazepine-based compound 3. Compound 4 was then obtained via the Vilsmeier Haack reaction using dry N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF), and phosphorus oxychloride $\left( \mathrm { P O C l } _ { 3 } \right)$ . Finally, our newly reported Lewis acid-catalyzed Knoevenagel condensation reaction was utilized to synthesize the target product ZCCF3.[41] The chemical structure of ZCCF3 was verified by mass spectrometry and proton nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy in Figures S1-S4 (Supporting Information).

Upon mixing with ZCCF3, the $\lambda _ { \operatorname* { m a x } }$ of Y6 exhibits a blue shift to $8 1 3 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . The film absorption coefficients of acceptors were provided in Figure S5a (Supporting Information), showing a higher value of ZCCF3 film relative to that of Y6. The results suggest that the structural torsion of ZCCF3 leads to a different electronic effect in both neat and dual acceptor blend, and its diazepine group can raise LUMO energy level to help boost the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ output in OSCs,[43] consistent with the proposed molecular geometry aforementioned.

Moreover, the new diazepine group functionalized ZCCF3 further impels us to investigate their molecular orientation and packing behavior in solids.[44,45] As shown in Figure 2c,d, the neat Y6 film presents a predominant $\pi$ face-on orientation with a coherence length (CCL) value of $3 3 . 0 8 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ for the (010) lattice face along the out-of-plane direction (Table S1, Supporting Information). Once blended with ZCCF3, the dual acceptor blend switches to a predominant $\pi$ edge-on orientation with a slightly loose packing for the $d$ spacing (3.70 A). Despite that, it shows a greatly enhanced aggregation, showing the in-plane $\mathrm { C C L } _ { ( 0 1 0 ) }$ value of $6 2 . 8 5 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ along with an extra (100) lattice face at $0 . { \dot { 3 } } 8 { \overset { \cdot } { \mathrm { A } } } ^ { - 1 }$ in the out-of-plane direction. The result indicates a favorable molecular ordering in the blend. Such morphological evolution in solid suggests the formation of acceptor alloy, which is probably correlated with the molecular association between Y6 and ZCCF3 as verified by $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR and $^ { 1 9 } \mathrm { F }$ NMR diffusion-ordered spectroscopy (DOSY).

As shown in Figure S6a (Supporting Information), in the presence of Y6, all the chemical shifts $( \delta ~ 9 . 2 \mathrm { - } 7 . 6 ~ \mathrm { p p m } )$ of ZCCF3 move to a lower frequency relative to its chemical shift in $\mathrm { C D C l } _ { 3 }$ (Figure S6a, Supporting Information), which is ascribed to the aromatic solvent-induced shifting and suggests the formation of ZCCF3: Y6 complex.46] This hypothesis is further verified by the different diffusion coefficients between neat ZCCF3 and ZCCF3: Y6 sets. The DOSY technique allows the diffusion coefficient of a chemical specie to be correlated with its molecular size and hydrodynamic volume, which is particularly appropriate for characterizing host-guest supramolecular association.147,48] As ZCCF3 has characteristic $^ { 1 9 } \mathrm { F }$ peak from its $\mathrm { C F } _ { 3 }$ group, it was used to conduct the $^ { 1 9 } \mathrm { F }$ DOSY. It can be seen that ZCCF3 exhibits the diffusion coefficient $( D )$ value of $5 . 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 1 0 } \mathrm { ~ m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { ~ s } ^ { - 1 }$ . Upon blending with Y6, a significantly decreased $D$ value of $5 . 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 1 2 } \ \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ was recorded (Figure $\$ 6 b,4$ Supporting Information), suggesting the formation of a complex between ZCCF3 and Y6 in solution.

Recently, it is discovered that the diffusion of SMAs into the donor polymer exhibits Arrhenius behavior and that the diffusion coefficients in blends decrease exponentially with an increasing glass transition temperature $( T _ { \mathrm { g } } )$ of the SMAs.[49] Here, based on the UV-vis deviation metric (DMT) results (Figure S7, Supporting Information),[50] it's interesting to note that the estimated $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ (Figure 2e) of Y6, Y6: ZCCF3 and ZCCF3 specimens are 101, 110, and $1 1 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , respectively, which implies that the ZCCF3 component can enable a relatively diffusionlimited demixing of the morphology when it blended with polymer donor, and thus benefits for realizing thermal stable OSCs toward operational application (Table 1).

# 2.3. Photovoltaic Performance

Conventional OSCs based on the architecture of ITO/PEDOT: PSS (poly (3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene): poly(styrene-sulfonate))/ PM6: acceptor /PDINN (aliphatic amine-functionalized perylenediimide)[51,52]/Ag were fabricated to elucidate the effect of ZCCF3 on photovoltaic performance of the ternary device. The weight ratio of the donor and acceptor components was kept at $1 ; 1 . 2$ in blend. The current density versus voltage $\left( J \cdot W \right)$ curves of the optimal devices are shown in Figure 3a, and the corresponding device parameters are summarized in Table 2. The PM6: Y6 based device exhibits a PCE of $1 6 . 7 9 \%$ with a $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 4 \mathrm { V } ,$ $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 5 . 8 3 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , and fill factor (FF) of $7 7 . 4 0 \%$ . The binary PM6: ZCCF3 based device presents a low PCE of only $6 . 9 9 \%$ (a $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 8 \mathrm { V } , J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $1 2 . 4 0 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and FF of $6 4 . 2 0 \%$ with 1-CN $( 0 . 7 \%$ V/V) as additive, while the PCE can be further improved to $9 . 8 9 \%$ when DIO $( 0 . 5 \%$ V/V) was used (Table S2 and Figure S8, Supporting Information). While for the PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 system, simultaneously higher device parameters (0.86 v for $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } ,$ $2 6 . 9 5 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ for $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ $8 0 . 0 1 \%$ for FF) deliver a superior PCE value of $1 8 . 5 4 \%$ in devices. The histogram of the PCEs for OSCs is provided in Figure 3b, exhibiting average values of $1 6 . 5 9 \%$ for the PM6: Y6 system and $1 8 . 3 8 \%$ for the PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 system. Notably, the impressive PCE is the highest values to date for PM6: Y6 cells. (Figure 3c; Table S3, Supporting Information). Figure 3d presents the corresponding external quantum efficiency (EQE) spectra with an integrated current density of $2 6 . 2 2 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ , which matches well with the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ values obtained in $J { - } V$ measurement within a mismatch of $3 \%$ It's demonstrated the introduction of ZCCF3 greatly enhances the EQE response in the wavelength range of $3 6 0 { - } 9 3 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , contributing to a higher $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ output in devices; and its upshifted LUMO energy level facilitates the alignment of energy levels in the matrix to reduce energy loss and offer a higher $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ output in devices.[53]

Moreover, compared with the $\mathrm { C C L } _ { ( 0 1 0 ) }$ value $( 2 3 . 3 4 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ } )$ of PM6: Y6 blend, a nearly 2-fold $\mathrm { C C L } _ { ( 0 1 0 ) }$ value up to $4 3 . 0 2 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ is obtained in the PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 blend (Figure 2c,d). The significantly enhanced $\pi$ face-on ordering is beneficial for carrier transport in vertically oriented devices. As illustrated in Figure S9 (Supporting Information) and Table 2, for the PM6: Y6 system, the hole mobility $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } )$ and electron mobility $( \mu _ { e } )$ values are estimated to be $2 . 1 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ and $2 . 0 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } ;$ while for the PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 system, the $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ values are increased to $4 . 6 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ and $4 . 4 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively. The synergetic higher and balanced carrier mobilities in PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 blend concur well with its molecular packing behavior aforementioned and favors a high FF and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ generation in the OSCs.[54-57]

To further investigate the impact of the ZCCF3 component on the exciton dissociation in ternary devices, we performed the saturation current density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } \right)$ and charge dissociation efficiency $( P \ ( E , \ T ) )$ measurements. Figure 3e presents the photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ versus the effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ curves of the devices. Here, the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ is defined as $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } = J _ { \mathrm { L } } - J _ { \mathrm { D } }$ where $J _ { L }$ and $J _ { D }$ stand for the current densities under illumination and in the dark, respectively. $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ is given by $V _ { \mathrm { { e f f } } } = \ : V _ { 0 } { - } V _ { \mathrm { { b i a s } } } .$ where $V _ { 0 }$ is the voltage at which $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ is zero and $V _ { \mathrm { b i a s } }$ is the applied bias voltage.[58] A larger $\mathrm { V _ { e f f } }$ can provide a larger internal electric field for exciton dissociation. Based on the plots of $\dot { J } _ { \mathrm { { p h } } }$ versus $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } ,$ the exciton dissociation probability $( P ( E , \ T ) )$ was estimated by the value of $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } ,$ where $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ is the saturated photocurrent density. Under short circuit conditions, the $P ( E , \ T )$ values are $9 8 . 6 \%$ and $9 8 . 2 \%$ for PM6: Y6 and PM6: Y6: ZCCF3, respectively, indicating the slightly improved exciton dissociation in the ZCCF3- based device. Moreover, the corresponding charge recombination behavior was investigated by measuring the dependence of $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ outputs on light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ . In general, the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ follow the relationship of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \propto P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } \ : \stackrel { \alpha } { }$ where $\alpha$ expresses the degree of bimolecular recombination.59] The value of $\alpha$ approaches 1 if the carriers are entirely extracted by the electrodes, while $\alpha < 1$ indicates some extent of bimolecular recombination. As shown in Figure 3f, the extraction $\alpha$ value of the device based on the PM6: Y6 system is 0.975, while the $\alpha$ value of the PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 system is promoted to 0.995. This suggests that the bimolecular recombination is effectively inhibited under short-circuit conditions with the addition of ZCCF3 component. Besides, the dependence of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ on $P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ is utilized to investigate the recombination probability in devices. The slope between $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and ln $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ can be expressed as $n k T / q$ where $n$ is the ideality factor, $k$ is the Boltzmann constant, $T$ is the Kelvin temperature and $q$ is the basic charge. The smaller $n$ value suggests less trap-assisted recombination existing in the devices.60] As shown in Figure 3f, the n value of the PM6: Y6 system is 1.206 and then sharply drops to 1.030 upon adding ZCCF3 in blend. The result confirms the addition of ZCCF3 effectively reduces the degree of trap composition. The simultaneously enhanced exciton dissociation and suppressed charge recombination are the key factors for the superior photovoltaic properties observed in the PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 device.

# 2.4. Thermal Stability

Long-term thermal stability was evaluated to unveil the impact of ZCCF3 on the bulk morphology evolution under light stress. Normalized efficiency decays are shown in Figure 4a, with the full device characteristics detailed in Figure S10 and Table S4 (Supporting Information). The period to retain $80 \%$ initial efficiency in devices is termed as $T _ { 8 0 }$ lifetime. As shown in Figure 4a, the PM6: Y6 system exhibits a $T _ { 8 0 }$ lifetime of only $^ { 4 0 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ aging, while the PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 system exhibits much better thermal stability with a prolonged $T _ { 8 0 }$ lifetime of up to $3 6 0 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ The stability difference emphasizes that the intrinsic thermodynamically unstable PM6: Y6 system can be effectively suppressed with the addition of ZCCF3 as third component. The surface texture of blend films before and after aging was further probed by atomic force microscopy (AFM) characterization. As shown in Figure S11 (Supporting Information), the roughness of PM6: Y6 film increases by $2 1 . 8 \%$ from 0.692 to $0 . 8 4 3 \ \mathrm { n m }$ after aging, whereas the PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 film still maintains finer phase separation with a smooth surface (0.775 Vs $0 . 8 5 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . The severe phase segregation with coarsening domains in PM6: Y6 is detrimental to its charge transport process in OsCs. Instead, the relative glassy state observed in PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 film delivers excellent operation stability under thermal stress.

To understand the mechanism of ZCCF3 improving bulk thermal stability, we conducted the contact angle characterization and calculated the corresponding Flory-Huggins interaction parameter $\left( \chi \right)$ . As shown in Table S5 and Figure S12 (Supporting Information), the surface tensions for PM6, Y6 and ZCCF3 are 19.94. 26.75 and $2 8 . 5 1 ~ \mathrm { m N } ~ \mathrm { m } ^ { - 1 }$ respectively. Both Y6 and ZCCF3 exhibited larger surface tension than PM6 due to the high polarity of the terminal group attached in SMAs. The hyper-miscibility between Y6 and ZCCF3 is associated with their molecular association as suggested by the $^ { 1 9 } \mathrm { F }$ DOSY and further confirmed by the extra small $\chi$ parameter of 0.04. Yet, the $\chi$ parameters of PM6: Y6 and PM6: ZCCF3 pairs are 0.50 and 0.76, respectively, suggesting that the PM6: ZCCF3 combination is more hypo-miscible in their blend. It's reported that the PM6: Y6 system is kinetically quenched near the percolation threshold to offer decent photovoltaic performance, but the morphology is far from the thermodynamic equilibrium state and associated with severe burn-in device degradation. In terms of the Ade-O'Connor-Ghasemi stability theory,[49] the higher $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of a component generally requires higher activation energy for diffusion, yielding a lower diffusion coefficient in the matrix. Thus, in the given hyper-miscible Y6: ZCCF3 alloy, the ZCCF3 featuring higher $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ can serve as an anchor to effectively impede the diffusion of Y6 forming burly aggregates in the matrix (Figure 4b-c). Above all, we can infer that the ZCCF3-assisted vitrification system with higher phase transition temperature is an effective model for delivering simultaneously higher PCE and thermal stability.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, a highly fluorinated SMA of ZCCF3 was designed and synthesized by substitution the thiadiazole group on Y6 with a novel diazepine group. The synergistic non-coplanar scaffold and halogen bonding effect contributes to an upshifted LUMO energy level and enhanced $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ The appearance of Y6: ZCCF3 molecular association is revealed by the $^ { 1 9 } \mathrm { F }$ DOSY spectra in solution, such intermolecular behavior is correlated with the formation of acceptor alloy in solid. Owing to proper aligned energy level, dense molecular packing, and diffusion-limited vitrification, a superior efficiency of $1 8 . 5 4 \%$ with a prolonged $T _ { 8 0 }$ lifetime of up to $3 6 0 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ (under light stress) was achieved in the PM6: Y6: ZCCF3 combination. The

ZCCF3-assisted structure-property-stability framework demonstrates a new guideline for developing highly efficient and stable OSCs towards practical applications, and can also be employed to optimize other thermally stable organic electronics.
