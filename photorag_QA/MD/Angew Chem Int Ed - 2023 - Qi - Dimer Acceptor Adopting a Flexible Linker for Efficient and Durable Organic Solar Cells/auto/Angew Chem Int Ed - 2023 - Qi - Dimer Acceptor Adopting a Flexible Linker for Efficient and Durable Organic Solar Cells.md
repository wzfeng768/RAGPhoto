# Dimer Acceptor Adopting a Flexible Linker for Efficient and Durable Organic Solar Cells

Feng $\mathcal { Q } i ^ { + }$ Yanxun $L i ^ { + }$ , Rui Zhang, Francis R. Lin, Kaikai Liu, Qunping Fan, and Alex K.-Y. Jen\*

Abstract: Organic solar cells (OsCs) have advanced rapidly due to the development of new photovoltaic materials. However, the long-term stability of OsCs still poses a severe challenge for their commercial deployment. To address this issue, a dimer acceptor (dT9TBO) with flexible linker is developed for incorporation into small-molecule acceptors to form molecular alloy with enhanced intermolecular packing and suppressed molecular diffusion to stabilize active layer morphology. Consequently, the PM6:Y6:dT9TBO-based  device displays an improved power conversion efficiency (PCE) of $1 8 . 4 1 \%$ with excellent thermal stability and negligible decay after being aged at $6 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 8 0 0 \mathrm { h }$ Moreover, the PM6:Y6:dT9TBO-based flexible OSC also exhibits excellent mechanical durability, maintaining $9 5 \%$ of its initial PCE after being bended repetitively for 1500 cycles. This work provides a simple and effective way to fine-tune the molecular packing with stabilized morphology to overcome the trade-off between OSC efficiency and stability.

Organic solar cells (OsCs) acting as an emerging photovoltaic technology attract significant attention due to their solution processability facilitates large-scale production and flexible form factors that can be used for versatile sensors and wearable electronics.l In an OsC, the active layer consisting of polymer donor and small-molecule acceptors (SMAs) governs its power conversion efficiency (PCE).2l In the past few years, the development of SMAs has advanced rapidly through delicate molecular engineering of their chemical structures to enable efficient near-infrared absorption and tunable energy levels.3] Among the SMAs, Y6 - $( 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } { - } ( ( 2 Z , 2 ^ { \prime } Z )$ -((12,13-bis(2ethylhexyl)-3,9-diundecyl-12,13- dihydro-[1,2,5] thiadiazolo[3,4-e]thieno[2, ${ } ^ { 6 } 3 ^ { \prime \prime } { : } 4 ^ { \prime } { , } 5 ^ { \prime } ]$ thieno $[ 2 ^ { \prime } , 3 ^ { \prime } { : } 4 , 5 ]$ pyrrolo[3,2-g]thieno $[ 2 ^ { \prime } , 3 ^ { \prime } ; 4 , 5 ]$ thieno [3,2- bJindole-2,10-diyl)bis(methanylylidene))bis(5,6-difluoro-3- oxo-2,3-dihydro-1H-indene-2,1-diylidene))dimalononitrile)) and its derivatives are widely used in fabricating highefficiency OsCs, as their crescent-shaped molecular geometries can induce dense packing to form a 3D network,[4] resulting in efficient exciton delocalization and charge carrier transport.] As a result, the OsCs based on Y-series SMAs have achieved a PCE over $1 9 \%$ by adopting the ternary strategy.l However, the long-term stability of these devices remains a great challenge for their commercialization due to the molecular diffusion and self-aggregation of SMAs under external stresses, leading to a meta-stable morphology.8]

To address the OsC operational stability, oligomeric acceptors linking two or three SMAs directly or through thienyl linkers have been exploited to stabilize the morphology of active layer. The employment of these oligomeric acceptors has helped inhibit molecular diffusion in the blend films and improved their photostability and thermostability. For example, He et al. reported a series of oligomerized SMAs with excellent photostability.[9c] However, the relatively long and twisted conjugated backbone of the oligomers they used deteriorates the originally well-packed pristine SMAs in condensed phase, undermining the highly crystalline domains needed for charge carriers generation and transport. This leads to lower PCEs than those based on pure SMAs.10] The incorporation of flexible alkyl linker in the molecule is an effective way to tune the crystalline behavior and morphology of active layer[1] because the flexible linker can lessen certain degree of backbone rigidity to enhance the intermixing between donor and acceptor. This has been shown to improve the long-term stability and mechanical properties of all-polymer solar cells.[12] Furthermore, the flexible alkyl linker has also been applied to the design of double-cable polymers, which can facilitate proper donor-acceptor nanophase separation to improve device performance and mechanical properties.[13] However, the introduction of flexible alkyl linker into oligomers has not been explored yet.

In this work, we introduce a flexible alkyl linker into the dimer acceptor to stabilize the active layer morphology and enhance the stability in OSC under bias, heat, and mechanical deformation. The Y6-derivative T9TBO and 1,6-di(thiophen-2-yl) hexane possessing a flexible alkyl linker were chosen as the monomer to synthesize the dimer acceptor dT9TBO via Stille coupling. Benefiting from the morphological optimization enabled by the flexible alkyl linker, the champion ternary OSC (PM6 (Poly[(2,6-(4,8- bis(5-(2-ethylhexyl-3-fluoro)thiophen-2-yl)-benzo[1,2-b:4,5- b]dithiophene))-alt-(5,5-( $^ { 1 ^ { \prime } , 3 ^ { \prime } }$ -di-2-thienyl- $\cdot 5 ^ { \prime } , 7 ^ { \prime }$ -bis(2- ethylhexyl)benzo $[ 1 ^ { \prime } , 2 ^ { \prime } { \bf - c } : 4 ^ { \prime } , 5 ^ { \prime } { \bf - c } ^ { \prime } ]$ dithiophene-4,8-dione)]):Y6:dT9TBO) exhibits a high PCE of $1 8 . 4 1 \%$ with a high open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } , 0 . 8 8 \mathrm { V } )$ , short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } , 2 7 . 1 7 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ) and fill factor (FF) $( 7 7 . 2 6 \% )$ Meanwhile, the PM6:Y6- and PM6:Y6:T9TBO-based OSCs only display a $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ of 0.86 and $0 . 8 7 \mathrm { V }$ ,respectively, indicating the dimer acceptor can be effective in enhancing the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ and PCE, which can be attributed to the up-shifted lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy level and efficient charge transport induced by the higher crystallinity of the blend as verified by grazing-incident wide-angle $\mathbf { X }$ -ray scattering (GIWAXS) characterization.

Due to the higher glass-transition temperature $( T _ { \mathrm { g } } )$ of Y6:dT9TBO blend, the molecular diffusion can be suppressed.d] Besides, the good miscibility of Y6 and dT9TBO enables the formation of a molecular alloy with denser stacking. Therefore, molecular packing in the bulk can be further corroborated by the dimer acceptor linked through the flexible alkyl chain, of which the motion of the monomer is restrained by the adjacent unit. Consequently, the PM6: Y6:dT9TBO film exhibits a more stable morphology under aging and external stresses. As a result, the lifetime of PM6: Y6:dT9TBO based devices under thermal aging and maximum-power-point (MPP) tracking can also be simultaneously enhanced. More importantly, the incorporation of flexible alkyl linker further enhances the mechanical properties of flexible OsC, which can maintain $9 5 \%$ of its initial PCE after 1500 bending cycles. The low dosage of dimer acceptor incorporated in our ternary material system not only improves the device efficiency, but also enhances their durability over heat, light, bias, and mechanical stresses. Therefore, this work provides a simple and efficient way for simultaneously enhancing the efficiency and stability of ternary OSCs.

T9TBO and dT9TBO were synthesized as described in Scheme S1. The molecular structures of T9TBO, dT9TBO and commonly used Y6 are exhibited in Figure 1a. The optical properties of dT9TBO and T9TBO in solution and thin film were characterized by the ultraviolet-visible (UV-- Vis) spectrometry and the related parameters are summarized in Table S1. As shown in Figure S1, dT9TBO displays a maximum absorption peak at $7 2 3 \mathrm { n m }$ in solution, showing a $1 0 \mathrm { n m }$ red-shift compared to that of T9TBO. This can be attributed to the pre-aggregation of dT9TBO induced by the flexible alkyl linker. To further verify this phenomenon, we have also measured the absorption spectrum of dT9TBO at two different concentrations (Figure S1). In thin film, both acceptors exhibit a similar absorption peak at $\approx 7 9 0 \mathrm { n m }$ corresponding to a band gap of ${ \approx } 1 . 4 5 \mathrm { e V }$ calculated from the absorption onset (Figure 1b). Besides, the absorption spectra of different ratios of Y6:dT9TBO in thin film are displayed in Figure S1. It can be found that the absorption peak blue shifted with the ratio of dT9TBO is increased in the blend film. Y6 and dT9TBO are complementary in terms of their optical absorption profiles, which provides the potential for fabricating high-efficiency ternary devices by optimizing the dimer acceptor content in the active layer.

To investigate the electrochemical properties of dT9TBO and T9TBO, cyclic voltammetry (CV) was employed to assess their energy levels (Figure S2). As shown in Figure 1c and Table S1, the LUMO and the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) levels of dT9TBO were calculated as $- 3 . 7 2 / - 5 . 5 5 \mathrm { e V }$ , which are higher than the LUMO/ HOMO $( - 3 . 7 5 / - 5 . 6 0 \ : \mathrm { e V } )$ of T9TBO. This can be attributed to the electron-rich thienyl units in the linker of dT9TBO. The density functional theory (DFT) calculations were further performed to understand the electronic structure of the two acceptors (Figure S3). The results of DFT calculation agree well with the experimental LUMO/HOMO values, indicating the incorporation of linker affects the electronic properties of the chromophore minimally.

To understand the phase change of acceptors that is strongly associated with OsC stability, we have further performed the differential scanning calorimetry (DsC). Due to the cold crystallization temperature $( T _ { \mathrm { c } } )$ of SMAs can be more easily identified than $T _ { \mathrm { g } } , \ T _ { \mathrm { c } }$ was used in the DSC characterization to investigate the relationship between nucleation rate and crystallization kinetics of the blends.[14] As shown in Figure S4, Y6 and monomer T9TBO exhibit a clear $T _ { \mathrm { c } }$ of $1 3 7 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and $1 6 4 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ respectively. On the other hand, no obvious $T _ { \mathrm { c } }$ could be observed in the dT9TBO and Y6:dT9TBO blend, indicating that the dimer acceptor is less prone to crystallization and has an amorphous morphology.5 In addition, the $T _ { \mathrm { g } } \mathrm { s }$ of the acceptors have also been measured by analyzing the deviation metric from temperature-dependent absorption spectra performed in thin film (Figure S5) to assess their thermal tolerance.[16] The deviation metric is extracted by accumulating the square of absorbance difference at the range of $7 0 0 { - } 8 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ , which has a clear change under increasing temperature. By linear fitting, the $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of Y6, T9TBO, and dT9TBO are 80, 112, and $8 8 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ respectively (Figure S6). Although T9TBO has a much higher $T _ { \mathrm { g } } .$ the Y6: T9TBO blend displays a lower $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ than that $( 9 3 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ of Y6:dT9TBO. These results indicate dT9TBO can be well intermixed with Y6 to form an alloy,7] meanwhile the enhanced molecular packing can help immobilize the motion and diffusion of molecules to increase $T _ { \mathrm { g } } , ^ { [ 1 8 ] }$ lowering the diffusion coefficient of molecules in the alloy[9a] Moreover, the PM6:Y6:dT9TBO blend exhibits a higher $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of $9 3 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ than that $( 6 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ of PM6:Y6 film. This also illustrates that dT9TBO may play an important role in improving OsC stability.

All the integrated photocurrent densities are consistent with the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \mathbf { s }$ obtained from the $J { - } V$ measurements. This demonstrates that our molecular design strategy can be generally applicable to different material systems.

The charge extraction and recombination processes in OSCs were further investigated by transient photocurrent (TPC) and transient photovoltage (TPV) measurements. As shown in Figure S10, the PM6:Y6:dT9TBO-based device exhibited a shorter charge extraction time than that of PM6: Y6- and PM6: Y6: T9TBO-based OSCs, indicating the incorporation of dT9TBO can optimize the morphology to improve charge extraction through better molecular packing and enhanced charge transport. Besides, the longer carrier recombination lifetime in the PM6:Y6:dT9TBO-based device leads to reduced recombination in ternary OSCs containing dimer acceptor. This shows the incorporation of dT9TBO can further inhibit carrier recombination and improve device efficiency through matched energy level, leading to a cascade energy alignment to improve charge transport and reduce energy loss.19] To further understand the charge recombination behavior of the OsCs, the dependence of $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ on light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ were examined (Figure S10c-d). Based on the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } { - } P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ dependency profiles, we found the slopes of all devices are close to unity, indicating the extent of bimolecular recombination is small and similar in all devices. In the $V _ { \mathrm { o c } } { \mathrm { - } } P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } }$ dependency profiles (Figure S10d), the PM6: Y6:dT9TBO-based device exhibits an ideal factor of 1.13, which is smaller than the ideal factors of PM6:dT9TBO (1.17), PM6:Y6:T9TBO (1.18) and PM6: Y6 (1.18) devices. This result demonstrates less trap-assisted recombination in the dT9TBO-based ternary OsCs, which should contribute to the enhanced device performance.

Furthermore, atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements were performed to clarify the effect of dT9TBO on device stability from a morphological perspective. As shown in AFM height images (Figure S12-13), all fresh and aged films exhibited similar mean-square roughness $( R _ { \mathrm { q } } )$ , indicating the surface morphology do not change significantly during the thermal aging process. However, the aged PM6:Y6 (Figure 4a-b) and PM6:Y6:T9TBO (Figure S13) films exhibited enlarged phase separation without similar fiber-like structure of the fresh films, resulting in deteriorated device stability. In contrast, both fresh and aged PM6: Y6:dT9TBO films could maintain the well-organized fiber-like structure and similar phase separation (Figure 4a-- d), which enabled the aged device to maintain a comparable PCE with the fresh devices. This shows that thermal induced molecular diffusion in annealed PM6: Y6 and PM6: Y6: T9T9TBO blends facilitates the growth of crystalline SMA grains, resulting in severe phase separation. However, the dimer acceptor-based ternary system can effectively stabilize the blend morphology to improve device stability.

In summary, a dimer acceptor (dT9TBO) was synthesized by coupling a slightly larger band gap T9TBO SMA with the 1,6-di(thiophen-2-yl) hexane flexible linker. The incorporation of dT9TBO in PM6:Y6 as the low-dosage third component enabled a high PCE of $1 8 . 4 1 \%$ in the ternary devices due to enhanced $V _ { \mathrm { o c } }$ The general applicability of using dT9TBO as the third component in ternary devices has also been demonstrated in PMHJ devices to deliver a high PCE of $1 8 . 5 7 \%$ in the D18/BTP-eC9:dT9TTBO-based devices. Due to the higher $T _ { \mathrm { g } }$ of the Y6:dT9TBO blend with enhanced molecular packing, not only the molecular diffusion can be suppressed, but also the motion of the covalently bonded monomers can be confined by the flexible alkyl linker to result in more stable morphology and better stability under the MPP tracking and thermal aging. Moreover, the more flexible dT9TBO can also be used to alleviate the mechanical stresses applied during bending tests for improving the mechanical durability of PM6:Y6:dT9TBO-based flexible device. This work provides a simple and effective way to use dimer acceptor with flexible linker in ternary blends to significantly improve the stability and mechanical properties of OSCs.
