# INTRODUCTION

Solar cells are developed as a renewable clean energy technology to overcome the energy and environmental problems. Solution-processed bulk heterojunction (BHJ) organic solar cells (OsCs), as a cost-effective alternative for utilizing solar energy, possess advantages, such as light weight, low cost, flexibility, and semitransparency, and have attracted considerable attention in the past two decades.1-4 Conventional OsCs are based on blends of donor and fullerene acceptor materials that form BHJ structure. For a long time, fullerene derivatives have occupied the predominant position in electron acceptor materials; power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of up to $1 1 - 1 2 \%$ have been achieved for the devices based on fullerene acceptors.-8 However, some shortcomings of fullerene derivatives, such as limited tunability of electronic properties, weak absorption in the visible region, and morphology instability, constrain further development of OSCs.9,10

In 2015, we reported fused-ring electron acceptors (FREAs) (e.g., ITIC) with acceptor-donor-acceptor (A-D-A) structure, which are based on indacenodithiophene or indacenodithienothiophene as electron-donating core and 1,1-dicyanomethylene-3-indanone as electron-accepting endgroups. s.11,12 Now the A-D-A type FREAs have been a research focus in the field of OsCs, and considerable efforts have been dedicated to modifications of the electron-donating cores 1-20 the electron-withdrawing end-groups21-27 and the side-chains.8-33 Relative to traditional fullerene acceptors, FREAs present strong absorption in the visible and nearinfrared (NIR) region, tunable energy levels fitting various high-performance donor materials, and facile synthesis.3435 Furthermore, FREA-based OSCs have shown higher PCEs with better device stability than fullerene-based control devices.36-44

Recently, we reported naphtho[1,2-b:5,6-b']dithiophene (NDT)-based FREAs, such as IOIC2 (Figure 1a). IOIC2 has highly planar structure, strong molecular packing, and high electron mobility, and OsCs based on wide bandgap donor FTAZ:IOIC2 blends show PCEs of ca. $1 2 \%$ 45 However, the absorption edge of IOIC2 is around $8 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m } ,$ which restricts the utilization of long-wavelength sunlight and the enhancement of short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } )$ in solar cells.

Here, to extend absorption of IOIC2 into the NIR region, we used hexyloxy groups to replace hexyl substituents on NDT and synthesized a new FREA, IOIC3 (Figure 1a). Change in the two analogues influences their electronic, optical, charge

# CONCLUSIONS

In summary, we synthesized IOIC3 via replacing hexyl substituents on NDT in IOIC2 with hexyloxy groups. Compared with IOIC2, IOIC3 exhibits a narrower bandgap, red-shifted absorption spectra, and higher electron mobility. The OSCs based on the PTB7-Th:IOIC3 blend show significantly higher $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } ,$ FF, and PCE than the PTB7- Th:IOIC2-based devices. OSCs based on the PTB7-Th:IOIC3 blend yield PCEs of up to $1 3 . 1 \%$ $4 0 \%$ higher than that of PTB7-Th:IOIC2-based devices $( 9 . 3 3 \% )$ . ST-OSCs based on the PTB7-Th:IOIC3 blend afford PCEs of up to $1 0 . 8 \%$ with an AVT of $1 6 . 4 \%$ which is the highest value reported for STOSCs so far. These results demonstrate that introduction of alkoxy side-chains in FREAs appears to be a promising approach to enhance absorption and electron mobility and finally efficiencies in OSCs.
