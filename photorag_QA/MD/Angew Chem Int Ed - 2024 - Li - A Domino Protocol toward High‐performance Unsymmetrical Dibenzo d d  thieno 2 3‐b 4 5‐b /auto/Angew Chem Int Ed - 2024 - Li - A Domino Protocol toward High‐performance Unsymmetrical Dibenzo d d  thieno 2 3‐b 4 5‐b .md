# A Domino Protocol toward High-performance Unsymmetrical Dibenzo $^ { d , d ^ { \prime } }$ ]thieno[2,3-b;4,5- $\mathbfcal { { b } ^ { \prime } }$ Idithiophenes Semiconductors

Jiahui $L i ^ { + }$ $P u$ Wang, Jiaxuan Dong, Ziyi Xie, Xiangyu Tan, Lu Zhou, Liankun Ai, Baolin $L i , ^ { * }$ Yang Wang,\* and Huanli Dong\*

Abstract: Unsymmetric organic semiconductors have many advantages such as good solubility, rich intermolecular interactions for potential various optoelectronic applications. However, their synthesis is more challenging due to intricate structures thus normally suffering tedious synthesis. Herein, we report a trisulfur radical anion $( \mathsf { S } _ { 3 } ^ { \bullet - } )$ triggered domino thienannulation strategy for the synthesis of dibenzo $[ d , d ^ { \prime } ]$ thieno[2,3-b;4,5- $b ^ { \prime }$ ]dithiophenes (DBTDTs) using readily available 1- halo-2-ethynylbenzenes as starting materials. This domino protocol features no metal catalyst and the formation of six C-S and one $\mathrm { C - C }$ bonds in a one-pot reaction. Mechanistic study revealed a unique domino radical anion pathway. Single crystal structure analysis of unsymmetric DBTDT shows that its unique unsymmetric structure endows rich and multiple weak SS interactions between molecules, which enables the large intermolecular transfer integrals of $8 6 \mathrm { m e V }$ and efficient charge transport performance with a carrier mobility of $1 . 5 2 \ : \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ : \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ : \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ . This study provides a facile and highly efficient synthetic strategy for more high-performance unsymmetric organic semiconductors.

# Introduction

Organic semiconductors have attracted much attention from both basic research and industrial applications due to their unique and prominent properties, such as mechanical flexibility, designable structure and solution processability.1-3] There have been much efforts devoted into the development of organic semiconductors, and a variety of organic semiconductors have been exploited and applied to various devices, especially for organic field-effect transistors (OFETs), organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs) and organic photovoltaics (OPVs), bringing a revolutionary development in the fields of optical communication, clean energy, and health monitor.!421] In general, organic semiconductors can be simply classified into symmetrical and unsymmetrical materials. The symmetric materials have been extensively studied with the advantages of relatively facile synthesis and a highly ordered molecular arrangement in the past decades.22] Compared to symmetric materials, unsymmetric materials have their own unique superiority of better solubility and richer intermolecular interactions.23-27] More importantly, unsymmetric structures can not only introduce nonequivalent intermolecular interactions that are able to induce unique molecular packing, but also introduce specific functional groups for accurate induction/detection and synergetic modulation, which are appealing to various research fields such as organic optoelectronic devices, biomedicine, and anti-counterfeit sensing.28-29] However, unsymmetric materials are rarely investigated, which could be attributed to their intricate structures and difficulty of synthesis.[6,26,30-35]

Thienoacenes possess similar electronic structure to the classical acene analogues and have become one of the most important types of organic semiconductors owing to their good air stability and the unique intermolecular sulfur-sulfur interactions.3641] In the past decades, abundant symmetric thienoacenes have been synthesized and demonstrated to possess excellent carrier mobilities.4247] Hu and Miyata et al. respectively synthesized symmetric dibenzo[d,d']thieno[3,2- $b ; 4 , 5 { - } b ^ { \prime } ]$ dithiophenes (symmetric DBTDTs).48-51 Takimiya group  synthesized[1]benzothieno[3,2-b]benzothiophenes (BTBTs).52-53] Oktamoto group synthesized V-shaped dinaphtho[2,3-b:2',3'-d]thiophenes (DNT-Vs) semiconductors.47] Inspired by these superior symmetric thienoacene semiconductors, a natural question is how about their unsymmetric derivatives? However, as discussed in the previous paragraph, the synthesis of unsymmetric materials is more challenging due to their intricate structures, thus most cases normally suffer tedious and cumbersome synthetic methods. Therefore, unsymmetric thienoacenes are rarely explored to date.bos4sl Bao group synthesized unsymmetric tetraceno[2,3-b]thiophene and anthra[2,3-b]thiophene via five step reactions with mobilities lower than $0 . 5 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { \mathbf { V } } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { \mathbf { S } } ^ { - 1 }$ [54] Jiang et al. synthesizedl unsymmetric [1]benzothieno[3,2-b]thieno[2,3-f]- [1]benzothiophene isomers with $\pi$ -extended BTBT structure via three step reactions, which exhibited moderate mobilities.30 Wang group synthesized unsymmetric boattype heptathienoacenes (DBHTs) via around ten step reactions.[55]

The typical synthesis method for thienoacenes, which involves multi-step synthesis including coupling and other reactions, usually suffer low synthesis efficiency and high cost. For instance, Hu et al. synthesized symmetric DBTDT parent compound via three-step reactions starting from benzo $[ b ]$ thiophene in $1 5 \%$ total yield,[51] and the synthesis of its dihexyl-substituted derivative needs even more reaction steps.48 49] Thus, a better synthesis method would be a domino reaction, frequently also described as a tandem or cascade reaction, i.e., a process involving two or more consecutive reactions in which subsequent reactions result as a consequence of the functionality formed by bond formation or fragmentation in the previous step.159-61] The advantage of domino reactions lies in their associated step efficiency, which can streamline an approach towards a target molecule, and in the increase of structural complexity.

However, the reports on the synthesis of organic semiconductors using domino reactions are rare, we found only a few examples concerning the synthesis of thienoacenes.62-65] Takimiya group reported a domino synthesis of BTBT and dinaphtho $[ 2 , 3 - b { : } 2 ^ { \prime } , 3 ^ { \prime }$ -f|thieno[3,2-b]thiophene (DNTT) from $o$ -chlorobenzaldehyde and 3-chloro-2-naphthaldehyde respectively in modest yields (Scheme 1a).65] Itami group reported a method for the formation of thiophene-fused $\pi \cdot$ systems by heating arylethynyl-substituted polycyclic arenes in $N , N$ -dimethylformamide (DMF) in the presence of $\mathrm { \Delta S _ { 8 } }$ (Scheme 1b).[64] Recently, a novel ${ \mathrm { S } _ { 3 } } ^ { \bullet - }$ -triggered domino stitching thienannulation approach for the rapid construction of rylene diimides (RDI)-fused, largely $\pi$ -extended thienoacenes from ethyne-, butadiyne-, and hexatriynelinked $\pi$ systems was reported by Wen group (Scheme 1c).[63] More recently, Song group reported that benzo[b]thieno [2,3-d]thiophene (TBT) derivatives were synthesized from readily accessible 1-bromo-2-(3-(trifluoromethyl)but-3-en-1- yn-1-yl)benzenes and $\mathrm { S } _ { 8 }$ (Scheme 1d).[62] Despite the successful examples mentioned above, the synthesis of thienoacenes via highly efficient domino reactions remains a prominent challenge. Hence, it is urgently needed to develop a class of high-performance unsymmetric thienoacenes via facile domino synthesis.

# Results and Discussion

Herein, we report a facile and highly efficient domino thienannulation protocol via ${ \mathrm { S } _ { 3 } } ^ { \bullet - }$ intermediate (Scheme 1e), in which readily available 1-halo-2-ethynylbenzene derivatives were heated in $N$ methylpyrrolidone (NMP) in the presence of ${ \bf S } _ { 8 }$ and ${ \bf K } _ { 2 } { \bf C } { \bf O } _ { 3 }$ to afford the corresponding unsymmetric  dibenzo $[ d , d ^ { \prime } ]$ thieno[2,3-b;4,5-b']dithiophenes (DBTDTs). This protocol is simple yet effective and versatile, featured by a metal catalyst-free method, simple operations under air atmosphere, readily available starting materials and very high step efficiency (formation of seven new $\mathrm { C - S / C - C }$ bonds and three new fused thiophene rings in good yields, up to $6 1 \%$ ). Moreover, the hole mobility of DBTDT parent compound-based single crystal organic field-effect transistors (SC-OFETs) is up to $1 . 5 2 \ : \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ : \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ : \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ indicating that DBTDTs have high potential application in organic optoelectronic devices. The development of this efficient domino synthesis method plays an important role for promoting high-efficiency and low-cost synthesis and related applications of high-performance thienoacene materials.

The discovery of this domino thienannulation reaction was a serendipity. During the attempted synthesis of benzo$[ b ]$ thiophene via treating 1-chloro-2-ethynylbenzene (1a) with ${ \bf S } _ { 8 }$ in DMF,[] surprisingly, we got $6 \%$ yield of unsymmetric DBTDT $( \mathbf { 2 } \mathbf { a } )$ instead (entry 1, Table1). We then optimized the reaction conditions, as summarized in Table 1. No to a trace amount of 2a was detected by using other solvents such as NMP, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMsO), pyridine, and 1,4-dioxane (entries 2-5, Table 1). Excitingly, $4 8 \%$ and $5 1 \%$ yield of 2a was obtained with the addition of 1.0 equiv. of ${ \bf K } _ { 2 } { \bf C O } _ { 3 }$ in DMF and $N , N$ -dimethylacetamide (DMAc) (entries 6-7, Table 1). This remarkable improvement is attributed to the production of ${ \mathrm { S } _ { 3 } } ^ { \bullet - }$ from the reaction between $\mathrm { S } _ { 8 }$ and the base.[62] When DMF was used as solvent, we found that $N , N .$ -dimethyl-2-phenylethanethioamide was formed as a byproduct.[66] To avoid this side reaction, we chose to use NMP as the solvent, as expected, the yield of 2a was raised to $6 1 \%$ (entry 8, Table 1). We also tried the reaction under ${ \bf N } _ { 2 }$ (entry 9, Table 1), but the isolated yield $( 5 5 \% )$ is slightly lower than that under air condition. We further screened different inorganic and organic bases (entries 10-21, Table 1), however, the yield had not been further improved. Different bases have different basicity and solubility, thus affording different yields. Notably, despite its $\pi$ extended fused-ring structure, 2a possesses remarkable solubility in common solvents such as tetrahydrofuran $( \sim 1 0 \mathrm { m g \ m L ^ { - 1 } ) }$ , chloroform $( \sim 7 ~ \mathrm { m g ~ m L ^ { - 1 } } )$ and dicloromethane $( \mathrm { { \sim } 6 ~ m g ~ \ m L ^ { - 1 } } ) _ { \mathrm { { \ell } } }$ at room temperature thanks to its unsymmetric nature, which is beneficial for solution processing in optoelectronic devices.

With the optimized reaction conditions (entry 8, Table 1) in hand, we examined the substrate scope and the results were summarized in Scheme 2. Gratifyingly, besides chlorine, bromine and fluorine on the ortho-position of the aromatic rings were all reacted under the optimized conditions. However, 2a was obtained in a much lower yield $( 1 4 \% )$ when using 1-ethynyl-2-fluorobenzene as substrate. It is perhaps due to the much higher dissociation energy of $\mathrm { C - F }$ bond compared with $\mathrm { C - C l }$ bond, which makes it harder for the fluorine to be substituted. The highly coplanar structure of 2a was unambiguously verified by X-ray single crystal analysis (CCDC No. 2205141, Scheme2). When weak electron-donating methyl groups were introduced at the para-position of ethynyl group on the phenyl rings, the desired product (2b) was obtained in good yield $( 5 2 \% )$ Nevertheless, the corresponding products (2c-2d) were obtained in different yields when the methyl groups were introduced at the ortho-position of ethynyl group or orthoposition of halogen on the phenyl rings, due to the different steric hindrance of methyl groups at different positions.

In particular, halogens (Cl and F) on the phenyl rings could be retained under the optimized conditions with modest isolation yields $2 \mathbf { e } \ 3 3 \ \%$ ,2f $2 0 \%$ ). Reactions could also be promoted when moderate electron-withdrawing substituents $\left( \mathrm { C F } _ { 3 } \right)$ or electron-donating substituents $\mathrm { ( O C F _ { 3 } ) }$ were introduced to the phenyl rings $( \bf { 2 } \tt { g } \mathrm { - } \bf { 2 } \ddot { 1 } )$ . Unfortunately, no target products were formed when a strong electronwithdrawing nitro group (2j) or cyano group (2k) was introduced into the substrate, which is probably because both nitro group and cyano group were easily attacked by ${ \mathrm { S } _ { 3 } } ^ { \bullet - }$ . In addition, introduction of a strong electron-donating methoxy group into the substrate produced only a trace amount of desired 21, probably because the terminal ethynyl carbon is too electron-rich to be attacked by ${ \mathrm { S } _ { 3 } } ^ { \bullet - }$ due to the $\mathbf { n } { - } \pi$ conjugation effect between the methoxy group and ethynyl group through the phenyl linker. To further explore the potential application of this thienannulation strategy, 2- chloro-3-ethynylpyridine containing a heterocycle was examined as the substrate, and the corresponding fused product $( 2 \mathbf { m } )$ was isolated in $1 4 \%$ yield.

The kinetic isotope effect (KIE) was also measured to understand this transformation.68] A KIE of 2.67 was observed from two parallel reactions (Scheme 3d and Figure S4), suggesting that $\mathrm { C - H }$ bond cleavage may not be a rate-determining step. The experimental results showed that $\mathbf { G }$ was formed much quicker than 2a, thus the last cyclization step (G to 2a) is considered as the rate-determining step (Scheme 4).

Based on the above results as well as reported work,[69] a plausible reaction mechanism was proposed (Scheme 4). Firstly, ${ \mathrm { S } _ { 3 } } ^ { \bullet - }$ radical anion is generated in situ from $\mathrm { S } _ { 8 }$ and ${ \bf K } _ { 2 } { \bf C O } _ { 3 }$ in NMP. Then the reaction proceeds through ${ \mathrm { S } _ { 3 } } ^ { \bullet - }$ attack at the end of the acetylene of 1a to obtain radical intermediate A, which adds to another molecular 1a to furnish intermediate B. Intermediate $\mathbf { B }$ combines with ${ \mathrm { S } _ { 3 } } ^ { \bullet - }$ to afford dianionic intermediate C by intermolecular radical coupling. Intermediate C immediately undergoes the first intramolecular cyclization to give anionic intermediate $\mathbf { D }$ which was attacked by ${ \mathrm { S } _ { 3 } } ^ { \bullet - }$ to obtain intermediate E. Subsequently, intermediate $\mathbf { E }$ yields dianionic intermediate $\mathbf { F }$ by dehydrogenative aromatization.[62] Intermediate $\mathbf { F }$ undergoes the second intramolecular cyclization to obtain intermediate $\mathbf { G }$ , which eventually affords the target compound 2a by the third intramolecular cyclization.

To demonstrate the potential applications of this domino thienannulation reaction, we tried a gram-scale reaction, satisfyingly, treatment of 10 mmol 1a with 5 mmol ${ \bf S } _ { 8 }$ under the standard conditions, the desired product 2a was isolated in $5 8 \%$ yield (Scheme 5). Furthermore, the diverse transformations of product 2a were also investigated (Scheme 6). Firstly, we had realized a selective monobromination of 2a to give 3, which offers feasibility for further modification of structures using coupling reactions. And, fluorescent compound 4 could be obtained in $6 6 \%$ yield by sequential tribromination and Suzuki coupling reactions. Secondly, compound 5 with an acyl substituent, a precursor of potential semiconductor material of OFETs, could be synthesized in a high yield $( 8 0 \% )$ by Friedel-Crafts acylation reaction. Finally, 2a underwent ring-opening reactions under different temperatures to give unsymmetric bis-benzo[b]thiophene 6 and its derivative 7 in quantitative or $5 4 \%$ yield respectively.

Density function theory calculations indicate that the electron clouds delocalize throughout the whole backbone (Figure 1b), indicating effective conjugation and charge transport.7 Not unexpectedly, the dipole moment of 2a is 0.6287 Debye, which is slightly smaller than that of symmetric DBTDT (0.8536 Debye); this result is in good agreement with the slightly lower polarity of 2a in thin layer chromatography analysis. We found the slightly different dipole moment has little effect on the molecular packing structure, both symmetric DBTDT and 2a show similar herringbone structure. Multiple intermolecular interactions induced by sulfur atoms are observed in 2a single crystals (Figure S6). In particular, as shown in Figure 2c, large-area face-to-face overlap with a small distance of $3 . 3 7 3 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ between adjacent molecules along $\pi - \pi$ direction is desirable for efficient charge transport.M71-72] Figure 1d displays the herringbone packing mode of 2a single crystals with herringbone angle of $1 1 3 ^ { \circ }$ and the distance of $5 . 5 \mathrm { \AA }$ and $1 9 . 8 \mathrm { \AA }$ between adjacent molecules, respectively. High quality single crystals of 2a are prepared via physical vapor transport (PVT) method. As shown in Figure 2a and Figure S5, a typical high-quality 2a single crystal shows regular and one-dimensional shape, and thickness of around $7 4 \mathrm { n m }$ The selected area electron diffraction (SAED) images of the single crystal prove its excellent quality (Figure 2b) and the single crystal grows along the dominant $\pi { - } \pi$ direction (Figure 2c), which is beneficial to charge transport. To investigate the charge transport property of 2a single crystals, we fabricate bottom-gate topcontact OFETs as shown in Figure 2d. The transfer and output curves are displayed in Figure 2e and 2f. As a result, the mobility distribution of fifteen effective devices shows an average mobility of $1 . 1 3 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathbf { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathbf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and the maximum mobility of $1 . 5 2 \ : \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ : \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ : \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ (Figure S7a). # Conclusion

In summary, we have synthesized DBTDTs for the first time via ${ \mathrm { S } _ { 3 } } ^ { \bullet - }$ triggered domino thienannulation reactions, featured by a metal catalyst-free and the formation of seven new $\mathrm { C - S / C - C }$ bonds in a domino reaction. This study also expands the application of ${ \mathrm { S } _ { 3 } } ^ { \bullet - }$ in organic synthesis. A unique mechanism has been proposed by control experiments, intermediate trapping experiments as well as kinetic experiments. Furthermore, the practical utility of this reaction is demonstrated by the gram-scale synthesis and diverse transformations of $\mathbf { 2 4 }$ . In addition, large charge transfer integral of $8 8 . 6 \mathrm { m e V }$ is obtained for 2a single crystals along the $\pi { - } \pi$ packing direction, and a high charge carrier mobility of $1 . 5 2 \ : \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ : \mathbf { V } ^ { - 1 } \ : \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ is obtained in 2a based SC-OFETs, indicating that DBTDTs have high potential application in organic optoelectronic devices. The development of this highly efficient domino synthesis method would play an important role for promoting high-efficiency and low-cost synthesis and related applications of high-performance thienoacene materials.
