# Biobased Thermoset Substrate for Flexible and Sustainable Organic Photovoltaics

Jingfu Tian, Yang You, Han Zhou, Haojie Li, Lin Hu, Yaozhu Tian, Haibo Xie,\* Yuanpeng Xie,\* and Xiaotian Hu\*

Toward a sustainable development of the flexible electronics industry, the design and facile preparation of sustainable flexible substrates with satisfactory mechanical properties and transparency is still a challenge. Herein, taking structural advantages of biobased eugenol and vanillin, a polymerizable aromatic carbonate monomer with ${ \pmb { \alpha } } , { \pmb { \omega } }$ -diene functionality (2E), and a robust aromatic acetal monomer with ${ \pmb { \alpha } } , { \pmb { \omega } }$ -tetraene functionality (4E), are designed and prepared. Then, they are cross-linked with pentaerythritol tetrakis(2-mercaptoacetate) (4SH) via thiol-ene click reaction to generate a series of ternary poly(thioether carbonate acetal) thermosets. It is found that the introduction of 4E allows the production of thiol-ene thermosets with higher crosslink density, which can balance their mechanical, optical, and surface properties. In addition, the thermoset shows good resistance against various natural environmental conditions and can be decomposed into recyclable small molecules under harsher conditions (3 m NaOH, ${ \bf 8 0 } ^ { \circ } { \bf C } )$ . As a result, the optimal thermoset is well-suited for the fabrication of flexible organic solar cells (OsCs), yielding a power conversion efficiency of $1 5 . 4 7 \%$ - which is superior to polyethylene terephthalate-based Osc. The results provided significant insights for the design and preparation of sustainable polymer substrates for OsCs, even flexible electronics.

# 1. Introduction

Compared to other inorganic photovoltaics, flexibility is one of the most significant features of organic solar cells (OsCs).[1]

Flexible OsCs have the merits of lightweight, mechanical flexibility, and notable specific power, which show great potential in terms of wearable and portable electronic devices.2,3] Recently, the synergistic efforts of optimizing transparent electrodes,[4,5] photoactive  materials,[6-10] and device structures have pushed the power conversion efficiency (PCE) to $1 4 - 1 7 \%$ for flexible small-area OSCs.[11-19]

Flexible polymer substrates are one of important components in fexible OsCs, and they are divided into two types of semi-crystalline polymers and amorphous polymers.[20 The widely used polyethylene terephthalate (PET) and polyethylene naphthalate (PEN) are semi-crystalline polymers,[21,22] while amorphous polymers include polycarbonate (PC), polyethersulphone (PES), and polyimide (PI).[23-26] These plastic substrates have made remarkable progress in the field of flexible OSCs,[27] as it allows rational trade-off among optical transmittance, surface quality, and mechanical fexibility. However, these plastics are nonrenewable petroleum-based resources synthetic polymers, which do not meet the needs for sustainable development and green society. Although the great advances have been made in biomass-based ethylene glycol and terephthalic acid for producing PET, they are still suffering from high-cost of feedstock, catalyst and low atom economy.[28] To date, the monomers of PET polymerization is still derived from fossil fuels.[29] Therefore, there is increasing demand for the design and preparation of synthetic polymers by using renewable resources, due to the depletion of petroleum resources.

Biobased substrates have been identified as a promising alternative to petroleum-based substrates for flexible electronics.[30] For example, Hu et al. reported a highly crystalline paper substrate prepared from the aqueous dispersion of cellulose nanofiber (CNF).[31] Due to the diameter of CNF is much less than the wavelength of visible light, the flexible substrate was highly-transparent with maximum transmittance over $90 \%$ Zhou et al. developed a cellulose nanocrystal (CNC) paper substrate for flexible and recyclable OsCs.32] The average transmittance and surface quality of CNC substrate could be comparable to the commercial plastic substrate. Although much effort has been devoted to the development of biomass-based fexible substrates, their resultant OsCs presented a PCE of far below $10 \%$ . On the one hand, cellulose based substrates are usually extremely brittle with a maximum fracture strains less than $10 \%$ [33,34] On the other hand, cellulosed based substrate usually processed from aqueous solution and can be redispersed into water.[35] These drawbacks will be unable to resist external force and solvent erosion during the operation processes.

Lignin is the most abundant natural aromatic polymer resources on earth, and the catalytic biorefinery of lignin produced a large spectrum of aromatic chemicals, such as vanillin, vanillic acid, guaiacol, and eugenol.136-38] Vanillin is a natural phenolic aromatic compound from the pods of an orchid, considering as one of the most important flavors around the world.l39] Now, the vanillin is even produced from lignin at an industrial scale.[40] Eugenol is an aromatic phenolic compound also can be obtained from the natural essential oils of clove plants, which has been widely used in the field of cosmetology, medicine, and pharmacology, due to its antibacterial, antiviral, anticancer, and antioxidant characteristics.41] Their unique aromatic structure feature and versatile functional groups (e.g., reactive aldehyde, phenolic, $\alpha , \omega$ monoene groups) provided significant potential for the design and preparation of sustainable polymerizable monomers, thus aromatic polymers with good thermal and mechanical properties.

Herein, taking structural advantages of biobased eugenol and vanillin, a polymerizable aromatic carbonate monomer with $\alpha , \omega$ -diene functionality (2E), and a robust aromatic acetal monomer with $\alpha , \omega$ -tetraene functionality (4E), were designed. Then, they was applicable in established thiol-ene polymerization with a tetrathiol monomer (4sH), yielding a series of flexible poly(thioether carbonate acetal) thermosets. It was found that the incorporation of 4E monomer to 2E monomer enables to promote the crosslink density of flexible thermosets and thus improving its mechanical properties, but slightly losing its optical transmittance. When the 4E contents was fixed at $30 \%$ (molar ratio of alkenyls), the thermosets obtained optimal properties with an excellent tensile strength of $5 5 ~ \mathrm { M P a }$ , average transmittance of over $90 \%$ in the range of $4 0 0 { - } 8 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , and uniform surface morphology with root-mean-square of ${ \approx } 3 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . Furthermore, the optimal thermoset as flexible substrate was applied in OsCs, the resultant device achieved a best PCE of $1 5 . 4 1 \%$ when using PM6:BTP-eC9 as the active layer, which is higher than the value of $1 4 . 5 3 \%$ of PET based OSCs. More importantly, the as-prepared flexible thermoset possesses the merits of stability in natural environment and good degradability under harsher conditions $( 3 \mathrm { ~ \textbf ~ { ~ M ~ } ~ N a O H }$ aqueous and $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ due to the presence of carbonate, ester, and acetal bond functional moieties in the polymer backbone. Our study provided significant insights for the design and preparation of sustainable and recyclable polymer substrates for OsCs, even flexible electronics.

# 2.1. Design and Synthesis of the Biobased Thermosets via Thiol-Ene Click Chemistry

As shown in Scheme S1 (Supporting Information), eugenol is featured with a phenolic hydroxyl and $\alpha , \omega$ -ene functionality, which provide the possibility to react with triphosgene to produce a polymerizable aromatic carbonate monomer with $\alpha , \omega \cdot$ diene functionality (2E). The chemical structure of 2E was confirmed using nuclear magnetic resonance $( { } ^ { 1 } \mathrm { H } \mathrm { N M R } )$ and carbon13 nuclear magnetic resonance $( { } ^ { 1 3 } \mathrm { C } \ \mathrm { N M R } )$ analysis (Figure S1, Supporting Information), while the functional groups of 2E were verified using Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra. The appearance of a new signal at 1780 and $1 6 3 0 ~ \mathrm { c m ^ { - 1 } }$ indicated the presence of functional groups of $\mathrm { C } { = } \mathrm { O }$ and $\mathrm { C } { = } \mathrm { C }$ (Figure S2, Supporting Information).

Vanillin is featured with a phenolic hydroxyl and one aldehyde group, making it possible to be converted to a robust aromatic acetal monomer with $\alpha , \omega$ tetraene functionality (4E) by a range of reactions of williamson ether synthesis, Claisen rearrangement, and acetalization (Scheme S2, Supporting Information). $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ and $^ { 1 3 } \mathrm { C }$ NMR were used to monitor the chemical structure of intermediate products (Figures S3-s5, Supporting Information). As shown in $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ and $^ { 1 3 } \mathrm { C }$ NMR spectra (Figure S6, Supporting Information), the target 4E molecule was successfully synthesized with the appearance of a new signal at 5.0-5.5 and $3 . 5 { - } 4 . 0 ~ \mathrm { p p m }$ . In addition, the emergence of a characteristic peak at $1 7 3 7 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ $( { \mathrm { C } } { = } { \mathrm { C } } )$ accompanied by the disappearance of peaks at $3 2 8 0 ~ \mathrm { c m ^ { - 1 } }$ $( \mathrm { - O H } )$ and $1 6 8 0 ~ \mathrm { c m ^ { - 1 } }$ $( { \mathrm { C } } { = } 0 )$ in FT-IR spectra also proves the 4E monomer was successfully synthesized (Figure S7, Supporting Information). Thus, we have successfully designed and prepared a robust biobased polymerizable monomer with high alkenyl functionalities, high rigidity, and degradable acetal moiety under mild conditions.

Figure 1a shows the chemical structures of eugenol, vanillin, 2E, and 4E, while pentaerythritol tetrakis(2-mercaptoacetate) (4SH) is a widely used monomer in the field of coating industry, plasticizers, surfactants, and medicine. The $\alpha , \omega$ -diene functionality in 2E, $\alpha , \omega$ -tetraene functionality in 4E, and sulfhydryl in 4SH provide active sites for thiol-ene click polymerization. It was reported that the cross-linking density and the rigidity of the monomers significantly correlated with the mechanical properties of the thermoset prepared by the thiol-ene click polymerization[42] Therefore, 2E and 4E are mixed with 4SH in different proportions (the molar ratio of total alkenyl and thiol is 1:1), in order to adjust the material properties by using the robust 4E monomer with high alkenyl functionalities and high rigidity. To our delight, a series of robust transparent thermosets were achieved via thiol-ene click reaction under solventfree conditions when the mixture was melted at $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ in a mold and then cured under ultraviolet light (Scheme S3, Supporting Information). Figure 1b illustrates representative schematic diagrams of poly(thioether carbonate) thermosets (binary 2E4SH), poly(thioether carbonate acetal) thermosets (ternary 2E4SH-4E), and poly(thioether acetal) thermosets(binary 4E-4SH). For binary 2E-4SH thermoset, the crosslinking points are far from each other which resulted in low crosslinking density, owing to the insufficient ene functionality of 2E. In contrast, for binary 4E-4SH thermoset, the crosslinking points are close and the crosslinking density is much higher. As for the ternary thermoset of 2E-4SH-4E, it includes distant crosslinking points of 2E-4SH and close crosslinking points of 4E-4SH, and their corresponding proportions are tunable by varying the 4E content.

# 2.4. Stability and Degradability of Thiol-Ene Thermosets

The environmental stability of flexible substrates is vitally important to prolong the life of flexible electronic devices. Therefore, the stability of flexible substrate under a natural environment is studied. To simulate the various states of the fexible substrate in outdoor applications (sun, rain, and acid/alkali corrosion), the flexible thermosets were immersed in the acidic $\left( \mathrm { p H } \ 2 \right)$ , neutral (pH 7), and alkaline $\mathrm { ( p H 1 4 ) }$ aqueous solutions, respectively, and exposure under light intensity of $1 ~ \mathrm { K W ~ m } ^ { - 2 }$ $( \mathsf { A M } 1 . 5 \mathsf { G } )$ . An infrared camera was used to record the temperature of the substrate under sun irradiation. As shown in Figure S13 (Supporting Information), after undergoing $1 2 0 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ of continuous illumination, the maximum temperatures of the solution are $3 2 { \mathrm { - } } 3 6 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and all samples have no obvious decomposition trail. FT-IR result further proves that the thiol-ene substrate is quite stable under a natural environment (Figure S14, Supporting Information).

In addition to excellent stability, the thiol-ene thermoset shows good degradability in the presence of strong alkali and high temperature (3 mol $\mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ NaOH, $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ , caused by the hydrolysis of ester and acetal bond of the thermoset. Figure $_ { 4 \mathrm { a - c } }$ shows the time-resolved degradation of ternary thiol-ene thermosets with $0 \%$ $30 \%$ , and $100 \%$ 4E. The thiol-ene thermoset was immersed in NaOH aqueous solution with a temperature of $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ These thiol-ene thermoset was completely decomposed within $8 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ to obtain a uniform solution. Assuming that the hydrolysis of ester bonds and acetal bonds in polymers was complete, we proposed the degradation mechanism of ternary thiol-ene thermosets and their corresponding decomposition products. As shown in Figure S15 (Supporting Information), the decomposition products are pentaerythritol (D1) and phenolic compound (D2) for the binary 2E-4SH thermoset $( 0 \%$ 4E). For the binary 4E4SH thermoset ( $100 \%$ 4E), the decomposition products are composed of D1 and an aldehyde compound (D3). Figure 4d shows the chemical structure of a ternary thiol-ene thermoset, its decomposition products contain D1, D2, and D3.

To confirm this prediction, the residues were collected and characterized by $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR (Figure 4e,f) and FT-IR spectrum (Figure S16, Supporting Information). The detailed isolation method and analysis are shown in Supporting Information. Among these degradation products, D1 is widely used in the coating industry to produce alkyd resin, as well as the synthesis of plasticizers, surfactants, and medicine. Despite the commercial PET also contains ester bonds, it hardly degrades under $3 \mathrm { m o l } \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ NaOH or HCl aqueous solution at $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (Figure S17, Supporting Information). Previous work suggested that the degradation of PET occurs under high pressure $( 2 \mathrm { - } 3 \ \mathrm { M P a } )$ , high temperature $( > 2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ , and strong alkali conditions.43-45] These results indicate the thiol-ene thermoset has advanced in degradability compared to petroleum-based plastic flexible substrates.

# 2.5. Device Performance of Flexible Organic Solar Cells

From the viewpoints of optical transmittance, mechanical property, and surface quality, the thiol-ene thermoset is a promising alternative to commercial flexible plastic substrates. Therefore, the optimal ternary thiol-ene thermoset $3 0 \%$ 4E) was used to fabricate flexible OsCs. The device architecture of flexible OsC is displayed in Figure 5a, where the optimal ternary thermoset is used as flexible substrate, highly conductive PEDOT:PSS (PH1000, ${ \approx } 1 2 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ is selected as bottom transparent anode, the widely used PM6:BTP-eC9 $\left[ 1 1 0 \mathrm { n m } \right]$ blend act as active layer, PEDOT:PSS (Al 4083, $4 0 \mathrm { n m }$ ) and PNDIT-F3N $( 5 \mathrm { n m } )$ act as anode and cathode interface layer, respectively. The detailed chemical structures of PM6, BTP-eC9, PEDOT:PSS, and PNDIT-F3N are shown in Figure 5b. Additional details of device fabrication are shown in the Supporting Information. In order to facilitate comparison, the PET-based flexible OSCs as reference devices were also fabricated. The current density-voltage $\left( J - W \right)$ curves of the flexible OsCs based on the ternary thiol-ene thermoset and PET are presented in Figure 5c. The reference device yielded a PCE of $1 4 . 5 3 \%$ , with an open-voltage $ { ( \mathrm { V _ { o c } } ) }$ of $0 . 8 3 \mathrm { ~ V } ,$ a fill factor (FF) of $7 0 . 4 7 \%$ , and a short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathrm { s c } } )$ of $2 4 . 8 2 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . This value is comparable to previous work with the same active layer.[47] The thiol-ene thermosetbased OSC shows a PCE of $1 5 . 4 1 \%$ with higher $\mathrm { V _ { o c } }$ and FF, possibly attributed to the more uniform surface of the thiol-ene thermoset. The rigid OSC using glass/indium tin oxide (ITO) as substrate is provided in Figure S20 (Supporting Information), exhibiting a PCE of $1 7 . 8 0 \%$ with $\mathrm { V _ { o c } }$ of $0 . 8 6 2 \mathrm { V } ,$ FF of $7 8 . 5 \%$ , and $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ of $2 6 . 3 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . Compared to rigid OsCs, flexible OSC exhibit simultaneously reduced device parameters, which could be ascribed to the deficient conductivity of hc-PEDOT:PSs.[48] The corresponding EQE spectrum is depicted in Figure 5d, showing integral current densities of 24.15 and $2 4 . 8 1 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for PET and thiol-ene thermoset-based OsCs, which agrees well with the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ from $J { - } V$ results. Finally, the bending experimental measurements were performed to evaluate the mechanical stability of the flexible devices. As shown in Figure S21 (Supporting Information), after 1o00 bending cycles with a curvature radius of $5 \mathrm { m m }$ the PCE of the PET and ternary thermoset-based device remained at $9 2 . 0 \%$ and $9 6 . 2 \%$ of the initial value, respectively. This demonstrates that the biobased flexible substrate has a high flexural endurance.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, by taking the structural advantages of biobased eugenol and vanillin, 2E and 4E monomers with different functionality and rigidity were successfully designed and prepared, which was used in the preparation of a series of robust biobased thermosets via thiol-ene polymerization with a tetra thiol monomer. The addition of 4E monomer to 2E monomer to form a ternary cross-linking strategy can significantly adjust the properties of the thermosets, thus optimizing and offering material merits to meet the requirement of flexible substrate in OSCs. The findings indicated that the high-density alkenyl and high rigidity structure of 4E enables to increase in the crosslinking density of the thermosets and thereby improves the mechanical property of flexible substrate. When the 4E contents were fixed at $30 \%$ , the ternary thermosets achieved optimal properties with tensile strength of $5 5 ~ \mathrm { M P a }$ , high average transmittance of over $90 \%$ , and uniform surface. As a result, using the optimized thermosets to fabricate flexible OsCs yielded a power conversion efficiency of $1 5 . 4 1 \%$ superior to PET-based flexible OSCs. In addition, due to the presence of abundant carbonate, ester, and acetal groups in the thermoset's backbone, the biobased substrate shows excellent stability under common environmental conditions. In contrast, under harsher conditions-NaOH ( $3 \textbf { M }$ , aqueous) and $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ -the thermosets can be decomposed into defined products, which might be useful in the preparation of new materials. Our results provide a feasible way to optimize the property of biobased flexible substrates and demonstrate that biobased monomers have great potential in the design and preparation of sustainable flexible substrates for flexible electronics.
