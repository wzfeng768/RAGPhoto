# High-Performance Ternary Organic Solar Cells with Morphology-Modulated Hole Transfer and Improved Ultraviolet Photostability

Kang-Ning Zhang, Zhi-Nan Jiang, Tong Wang, Meng-Si Niu, Lin Feng, Chao-Chao Qin, Shu-Kong So, and Xiao-Tao Hao\*

A ternary bulk-heterojunction (BHJ) strategy that synergistically combines the merits of fullerene and nonfullerene acceptors has been regarded as a promising approach to enhance the power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of organic solar cells (OsCs). Herein, the fullerene derivative ICBA as the morphology regulator is incorporated into a nonfullerene-based PBDB-T-2F:BTP-4CI (PM6:BTP-4Cl) system to fabricate the high-performance ternary OsCs. The amorphous ICBA prefers to homogeneously distribute in the BTP-4Cl phase to form the well-mixed acceptor domains due to their better miscibility, which distinctly reduces the exciton decay loss driven by the unfavorable phase separation and enhances BHJ morphology stability of ternary blends. The appropriate addition of ICBA induces the efficient long-range Forster resonance energy transfer to BTP-4Cl and facilitates the ultrafast hole transfer process from BTP-4Cl to PM6, thereby contributing to charge carrier generation in the actual devices. Ultimately, the optimal ternary OsCs not only yield an average PCE higher than $1 6 . 5 \%$ but also show the superior ultraviolet photostability relative to binary control devices due to the increased harvesting of ultraviolet photons, boosted charge transfer, more balanced charge transport, and more stable nanostructural morphology. The results provide new insights to enable the simultaneously improved device performance and ultraviolet durability in state-of-the-art ternary OsCs.

# 1. Introduction

Over the past few years, nonfullerene organic solar cells (OSCs) have achieved considerable progress, benefiting from the synergistic developments in the design and synthesis of new photoactive materials,1-3] device structure optimization,46 morphology control7,8] and interfacial modification.[9,10]

Currently, over $1 7 \%$ power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) have been successively reported in single-junction nonfullerene OSCs.11-16] Continuous inspiring emergence of novel nonfullerene acceptors has markedly promoted the development of organic photovoltaic (Opv) field.[17] Notably, the nonfullerene acceptors exhibit. high molar extinction coefficients and tunable energy levels, which demonstrate that their rational combination with wide bandgap polymer donors can yield abundant absorption of photons in the visible and near-infrared (NIR) regions.18] Nonetheless, the limited light-harvesting capabilities of binary nonfullerene OSCs in the near ultraviolet (UV) region restrict the further generation of photocurrent. As a simple and effective strategy, ternary OSCs can realize the sufficient spectral response over the whole solar spectrum, which is mainly by incorporating the additional counterpart to result in the complementary absorption of host binary blend.[19,20] Moreover, the addition of the third component also plays a vital role in the optimization of photoactive layer morphology.[21-23] In comparison with nonfullerene acceptors, the conventional fullerene derivatives have their unique advantages, such as the strong light absorption in the wavelength range from 300 to $5 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ , isotropic electron transport characteristics, and so on.24,25] Accordingly, the synergistic utilization of fullerene derivatives and nonfullerene acceptors will facilitate the improvement in photovoltaic performance in ternary OsCs.[26]

The efficient charge generation through interfacial hole transfer process has the eventful contribution to device performance in nonfullerene OSCs, which cannot be neglected due to the strong photon absorption ability of nonfullerene acceptors.!27,28] In parallel with the driving force, the corresponding hole transfer rate is dependent on the film morphology as well.[29] Consequently, the introduction of fullerene derivatives into the nonfullerene binary systems to optimize the bulk-heterojunction (BHJ) morphology may be a practical approach to boost the photoinduced hole transfer from host acceptor to donor. In addition, photostability is also a decisive factor in the commercialization of OsCs because those successful OPV products should simultaneously exhibit high PCEs and superior operational lifetime. Several previous studies have demonstrated that the UV radiation has a noticeable impact on the degradation process of OsCs.30,31] In this regard, it is necessary to inhibit and even overcome the UV instability. Very recently, even though numerous high-performance ternary OPV devices based on fullerene and nonfullerene acceptors have been successfully prepared,[32,33] the relevant research devoted to morphology-modulated hole transfer and UV photostability has been rarely reported. Hence, a comprehensive and in-depth research about the role of fullerene derivatives on these fundamental mechanisms might be helpful for achieving highefficiency and high UV photostability ternary OSCs.

In this work, we use the amorphous ICBA as the guest acceptor to simultaneously strengthen the device performance and UV durability of host binary system based on a polymer donor PBDB-T-2F (PM6) and a nonfullerene acceptor BTP-4Cl. The best-performance ternary device exhibits an increased PCE of $1 6 . 7 6 \%$ relative to $1 5 . 9 8 \%$ for the binary control Osc, along with the improved $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 7 6 \mathrm { V }$ $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 6 . 1 3 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and fill factor (FF) of $7 3 . 2 3 \%$ . It is worth noting that the presence of ICBA can enhance the photo-response of ternary blend at short wavelength region. The time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL) and femtosecond transient absorption (TA) spectra reveal the coexistence of Forster resonance energy transfer (FRET) from ICBA to BTP-4Cl and more efficient hole transfer from BTP-4Cl to PM6, which correlates well with the increased $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ values in ternary devices. According to the experimental analysis of morphology characterization, the incorporated ICBA appreciably facilitates better molecular packing, enlarges the well-mixed acceptor domains, and induces the well-optimized phase separation, leading to more balanced charge transport and lower charge carrier recombination. More crucially, the added ICBA also works as a morphology stabilizer to reduce the damage to the devices from UV radiation. Our results highlight the beneficial role of ICBA as the third component on the growing device performance and tolerance to UV light in highly efficient ternary OSCs.

# 2.2. Exciton Dissociation, Charge Carrier Transport, and Collection Behavior

To evaluate the influence of introducing ICBA on photocarrier dynamic mechanisms, we first performed the photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ versus effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ measurements on the fabricated OPV devices, as shown in Figure 2c. Generally, the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \ ( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } = J _ { \mathrm { L } ^ { - } } J _ { \mathrm { D } }$ where $J _ { \mathrm { L } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { D } }$ represent the illuminated and dark current densities, respectively) can reach the saturated state $( J _ { \mathrm { { s a t } } } )$ at the larger $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ value, which means that almost the maximum number of excitons can be sufficiently dissociated into free charges and extracted by the corresponding electrodes. Thus, the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ values under short-circuit and maximal power output conditions can separately characterize the exciton dissociation probability $\left( P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } } \right)$ and charge collection efficiency $( P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } } )$ of binary and ternary OsCs.3,35] The determined $P _ { \mathrm { d i s s } }$ and $P _ { \mathrm { c o l l } }$ Of the ternary PM6:BTP-4Cl:ICBA (1:1:0.2, wt/wt) device are $9 6 . 8 9 \%$ and $8 8 . 1 0 \%$ (Table S1, Supporting Information), which are a little higher than $9 6 . 7 0 \%$ and $8 5 . 5 5 \%$ for the binary PM6:BTP-4Cl OSC, respectively. The results demonstrate the incorporated ICBA has a positive effect on exciton dissociation and charge collection processes, which can finely account for the increased $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values in the optimized ternary OSCs.

Then, to discern the charge transport properties, the space charge limited current (SCLC) method with single carrier devices was used to estimate the hole $\left( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } \right)$ and electron $( \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } )$ mobilities (Figure 2d and S1, and Table S2, Supporting Information). The $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of host binary PM6:BTP-4Cl devices were calculated to be $2 . 8 3 \times { { 1 0 } ^ { - 4 } }$ and $\dot { 2 } . 4 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ 'erespectively, together with a $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ ratio of 1.14. For the ternary PM6:BTP-4Cl: ICBA (1:1:0.2, wt/wt) devices, the corresponding $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ are slightly increased to $3 . 2 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ and $3 . 0 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ respectively, which leads to a lower $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ ratio of 1.08. The more balanced charge carrier mobility can agree well with the higher FF of $7 3 . 2 3 \%$ in the optimal ternary OsC. As the further increase in ICBA proportion over $3 0 \mathrm { w t \% }$ $( 3 0 - 5 0 \mathrm { w t } \% )$ , the corresponding OPV devices present the decreased and unbalanced charge carrier mobility, which are attributed to the disruption of charge transport channels. Therefore, the introduction of $2 0 \mathrm { w t } \%$ ICBA into host PM6:BTP-4Cl system can apparently improve charge transport and reduce charge carrier recombination.

# 2.3. Morphology Characteristics with Multiple Length Scales

The multi-length scale morphologies with the ordered molecular packing and optimized phase separation are greatly beneficial for obtaining high device efficiency. Herein, the morphological features have been carefully considered in the donor/acceptor blends. Grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) technique was utilized to clarify the effects of ICBA on the molecular packing and crystallinity properties of host photoactive layers, as shown in Figure 2 and 3. The pristine PM6 and BTP-4Cl films show the more pronounced $( 0 1 0 ) \pi - \pi$ stacking peaks in the out-of-plane (OOp) direction at 17.08 and $1 8 . 0 \bar { 3 } \mathrm { { \bar { n } m } ^ { - 1 } }$ (Table S3, Supporting Information) with their relevant $d$ spacing of 0.368 and $0 . 3 4 8 \mathrm { n m }$ $( d { \cdot } \mathrm { s p a c i n g } = 2 \pi / q )$ , respectively, than those in the in-plane (IP) direction, demonstrating the preferential face-on orientation of host donor and acceptor.3l The ICBA film exhibits an isotropic ring in the $q$ region from 12 to $1 6 \mathrm { { n m } ^ { - 1 } }$ suggesting its amorphous feature.8 In addition, we also calculated the crystalline coherence length (CCL) to quantitatively compare the strength of crystallinity using the following equation

$$
\mathrm { C C L } = \frac { 2 \pi k } { \mathrm { F W H M } }
$$

here, $k$ and FwHM are the shape factor with a typical value of $0 . 9 ^ { [ 2 5 ] }$ and full width at half-maximum for the (010) $\pi - \pi$ stacking peaks, respectively. The BTP-4Cl acceptor shows the larger CCL of $3 . 7 0 \mathrm { n m }$ than that of PM6 $( 2 . 2 3 \mathrm { n m } )$ , which signifies the relatively strong crystallinity of BTP-4Cl molecules. As for the host PM6:BTP-4Cl blend, the dominant face-on orientation is well maintained with a distinct (010) $\pi - \pi$ stacking peak at $1 7 . 5 1 \mathrm { n m } ^ { - 1 }$ (Figure 3a), which originates from the overlapping of diffraction signals of PM6 donor and BTP-4Cl acceptor. With loading 10 and $2 0 \mathrm { w t \% }$ ICBA into host binary blend, the monotonously enhanced $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking features are observed in Figure 3b,c, further confirmed by the slightly increased CCL $( 2 . 7 1 \mathrm { n m }$ for $1 0 \mathrm { w t } \%$ ICBA and $2 . 8 1 \mathrm { n m }$ for $2 0 \mathrm { w t \% }$ ICBA) relative to that of binary PM6:BTP-4Cl film $( 2 . 6 3 \mathrm { n m } )$ . As the proper content of ICBA is evenly distributed in the BTP-4Cl phase due to the better compatibility between these two acceptors confirmed by contact-angle measurements (Figure S3, Supporting Information), the strengthened crystallinity primarily derives from the improvement of crystallized regions of BTP-4Cl acceptor. It makes more sense that the enhanced crystallinity is favorable for facilitating charge transport in ternary OPV devices, keeping up with the increased charge carrier mobility. As the ICBA content further increased from 30 to $5 0 \mathrm { w t \% }$ , these (010) $\pi - \pi$ stacking signals reduce significantly (Figure 3d-f), implying the gradually decreased crystallinity. In this stage, the excessive amount of ICBA can gradually demix from the well-mixed acceptor phase and further form their own domains and charge percolation pathway, which results in the disruption of multi-length-scaled morphology framework, especially yielding the decreased crystallinity and intermolecular interaction between PM6 and BTP-4Cl in ternary blends. Again, the analysis of GIWAXs data is consistent with the surface morphology properties obtained by atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements (Figure S4, Supporting Information).

# 2.5. Morphology-Modulated Hole Transfer Dynamics

The femtosecond TA spectroscopy was specifically used to research the underlying impact of ICBA on photoinduced hole transfer dynamics from BTP-4Cl to PM6 in ternary blend film, which was principally relevant to the favorable BHJ morphology. To avoid the possible influence of electron and energy transfer, we utilized the low-energy light of $8 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ to pump selectively BTP-4Cl acceptor without exciting PM6 donor and ICBA acceptor. Figure 6a,b show the 2D TA maps of binary PM6:BTP-4Cl and ternary PM6:BTP-4Cl:ICBA (1:1:0.2, wt/wt) blends in the visible range after $8 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ excitation. The appeared ground-state bleaching (GsB) signals originating from PM6 donor can be clearly observed in the wavelength range from 550 to $6 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ which basically validates the efficient hole transfer processes from BTP-4Cl to PM6 in both binary and ternary blends.45,46] The GSB dynamic curves of PM6 donor probed at $6 3 0 \mathrm { n m }$ of these two blends in a normalized scale are shown in Figure 6c. The ternary PM6:BTP-4Cl:ICBA (1:1:0.2, wt/wt) film presents the faster rising trace than that of binary PM6:BTP-4Cl blend, indicating that ICBA as a morphology regulator can facilitate the faster and more efficient hole transfer from host BTP-4Cl acceptor to PM6 donor. These positive signals can be attributed to the features of charge-separated (CS) state of free carriers generated from photo-induced hole transfer. The kinetic curve monitored at $9 7 0 \mathrm { n m }$ in ternary blend shows the faster decay rate relative to binary control film in Figure 6f, which is well matched with the results of GSB dynamics of PM6 donor. Given that the used exciting energy is relatively low to pump PM6 donor and ICBA acceptor, the positive effects of ICBA on hole transfer rate from host acceptor to donor are mainly morphologymodulated and strongly associated with the optimized phase separation in ternary blend film. The increased interfacial contacts between PM6 and BTP-4Cl should be responsible for the more efficient hole transfer process at the host donor/ acceptor interface, supporting the higher $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ values in the ternary PM6:BTP-4Cl:ICBA (1:1:0.2, wt/wt) OSCs.

# 2.6. UV Photostability

The photostability of OsCs is a critically significant factor for influencing photovoltaic stability, which closely depends on a multitude of aspects including the intrinsic properties of the organic materials, the stability of BHJ morphology, the application of the appropriate interface layers, etc. Generally speaking, the number of UV photons in the solar spectrum is relatively small, but they exhibit ultrahigh radiation energy, indicating the negative impacts caused by UV light on nanostructural morphologies and/or intrinsic materials features may be the dominant degradation mechanisms of Opv devices. Considering that the added ICBA indeed makes contribution to the intensifying crystallinity and intermolecular interaction, we further determine the durability of the encapsulated host binary and the optimized ternary OsCs under the continuous illumination of UV light-emitting diodes (LEDs) with an emission peak at $3 6 5 \mathrm { n m }$ in ambient air. The evolution results of device parameters under UV accelerated aging test are shown in Figure 7. The binary PM6:BTP-4Cl control devices suffer from the fast degradation and retain about $45 \%$ of the initial PCE. As for the ternary PM6:BTP-4Cl:ICBA (1:1:0.2, wt/wt) OSCs, the relatively slower decline behaviors are observed and their corresponding PCEs are down to around $60 \%$ of the original values. The results corroborate that the integration of $2 0 \mathrm { w t \% }$ ICBA in binary PM6:BTP-4Cl blend can moderately enhance the tolerance to UV radiation, which is mainly attributed to the more stable BHJ morphology verified by the relatively lower $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF loss (Figure 7c,d).[47,48] The enhanced durability under UV light demonstrates the synergistic effects of intermolecular interactions among all functional materials in favorable morphology can effectively restrain the inevitably optical degradation behavior of OsCs, highlighting the importance of microstructure stability in BHJ blends!49,50

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we successfully fabricated a series of ternary OSCs based the polymer donor PM6, nonfullerene acceptor BTP-4Cl, and fullerene acceptor ICBA. The optimized ternary devices exhibit the simultaneous improvement in photovoltaic efficiency and UV photostability. The extra introduction of amorphous ICBA in binary PM6:BTP-4Cl system can enhance photon harvesting in the short-wavelength region. The incorporated ICBA is mainly distributed in the BTP-4Cl domain, yielding the enlarged acceptor-rich phase closely associated with the efficient FRET from ICBA to BTP-4Cl and ultrafast hole transfer from BTP-4Cl to PM6. Furthermore, the improved crystallinity and molecular arrangement are beneficial to strengthening charge transport and morphological stability, which provides the favorable evidence for the enhanced charge carrier mobility and durability on UV radiation in ternary OSCs. The resulting ternary devices present the average PCEs above $1 6 . 5 \%$ , which is higher than those of host PM6:BTP-4Cl devices. Overall, this work provides the rational guidance to select the feasible fullerene derivative as the dualfunction solid additive to enhance the efficiency and photostability of ternary OSCs.
