# Easily Available High-Performance Organic Solar Cells by Regulating Phenylalkyl Side Groups of Non-Fused Ring Electron Acceptors

Junjie Wang, Qingrui Luan, Pengchao Wang, Chenyu Han, Fuzhen Bi, Chunming Yang, Yonghai Li,\* and Xichang Bao\*

Although non-fused ring electron acceptors (NFREAs) have received increasing attention due to their relatively low synthetic costs, the achievement of high efficiencies strongly depends on tedious pre- or/and posttreatments to refine the active layers, which in turn greatly increase fabrication complexity and expense of organic solar cells (OsCs). Nowadays most of the available as-cast devices based on NFREAs are below $12 \%$ efficiencies. Herein, phenylalkyl category side groups $\scriptstyle ( \mathsf { C n P h } )$ are employed to construct new NFREAs named BOR-CnPh $( n = 3$ , 4, and 6), which exhibit inherently decent molecular aggregation and thus exclude additional treatments from device fabrication. The modified alkyl spacers of CnPh side groups not only trigger different aggregation of the acceptors, but also regulate the interaction conformations of donor (D) and acceptor (A), and thus $\mathsf { D } / \mathsf { A }$ interactions. Encouragingly, the pristine PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph blend delivers intrinsic fibrous networks with dominating face-on orientation, which yields an optimal efficiency up to $1 3 . 1 2 \%$ and ranks as the highest value among as-cast OsCs based on NFREAs. This research provides a practical strategy to control molecular aggregations, interactions, and pristine heterojunction morphologies for easily available and high-performance organic photovoltaics.

1. Introduction Solution-processed organic solar cells (OSCs) have been regarded as one of the next-generation photovoltaics owing to their advantages such as light-weight, low-cost, flexibility, and translucence.1-6] Beneficial from the innovative photoactive materials and device engineering, power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of OSCs have reached over $1 9 \%$ ,demonstrating bright future for commercialization.7-11] The landmark breakthrough is mainly attributed to the blossom of fused-ring electron acceptors (FREAs), which feature with modular conjugated backbones and high planarity Amone them, the most repderivatives.12-14] Although the intrinsic differences in molecular skeletons and side chains, the accesses of these FREAs usually require multiple-step ring-fusing reactions to covalently interlock aromatic units, which finally create $\pi$ conjugated planar backbones with 5-9 membered fused rings.15,16] The unique electronic structures facilitate intramolecular $\pi$ orbitals delocalization and intermolecular $\pi$ orbitals coupling, affording decent absorption, energy level, and charge transport properties. On the other hand, the multistep syntheses of fused-ring molecular backbones somehow limit the availability due to relatively high synthetic complexity and costs.

With regard to FREAs, non-fused ring electron acceptors (NFREAs) provide an alternative approach to reduce synthetic complexity by avoiding chemically ring-fusing reactions.[7-22] The NFREAs could maintain advantageous planar conformations locked by the multifarious intramolecular noncovalent interactions (O...S, O...H, S...N, et. al.) between $\pi$ skeletons and side groups, and thus NFREAs were also called supramolecular fused-ring acceptors.23-26] Encouragingly, OSCs based on NFREAs have surpassed $1 5 \%$ in some cases.[21,27-31] Developing new molecular backbones of NFREAs, and paring with more efficient polymer donors to further boosts device efficiency could be expected. However, the expenditure in fabricating solar cells requires carefully considered in the future commercialization. The primary cost variables in fabricating OsCs lie in the operational complexity of morphological pre- or /and post-treatments in that the preparation of other components of OsCs have been basically modularized. At current stage, most of the high-performance OsCs strongly depend on device optimization, with a variety of pre- (additive, guest usage, or layer-by-layer) or /and post-treatments (solvent polishing, base preheating, thermal annealing, solvent-vapor annealing, et. al.) employed to refine the heterojunction morphologies of active layers.32-36] Taking the TBFT-TR:Y6 system for example, the thermal optimized device gave high PCE up to $1 4 . 0 3 \%$ forming striking contrast with the extremely low PCE of $0 . 1 9 \%$ of its as-cast solar cell.137] The additional procedures could inevitably increase energy consumption and process complexity, and some additives may raise the risk of environmental pollution. And unfortunately, even after such complex morphology optimization, both the donor (D) and acceptor (A) molecules might still migrate and rearrange into a thermodynamic equilibrium state during long-time utilization. Hence, developing efficient as-cast OsCs free of any pre- and post-treatments would be the most preferred candidates.

Generally, casting from blend solution usually results in nonideal morphology with either insufficient or excessive phase separation scale, which is the internal cause for additional treatments. In essence, the initial morphologies of active layers are determined by the pre-aggregation behaviors of photoactive materials in solutions, which are governed by complicated interactions between like and unlike components. Intensive reports have confirmed that the pre- or/and post-treatments for active layers evidently enhanced the interactions and crystallinity of donor or/and acceptor.38,39] This result suggests the insufficient molecular stacking of the materials involved, but from another perspective, may provide a practicable approach to exclude the treatments for active layers, that is, strengthening the inherent crystallinity of relevant materials. The interactions between donor and acceptor control the interfacial properties, and help to disperse the two phases for better blending morphology.30,40 43] From this point of view, rationally manipulating the D/A interactions would promote the integration and dispersion of donor and acceptor phases for more appropriate phaseseparation without any additional treatments. These principles may support us to develop practicable NFREAs with collective achievements of satisfactory efficiencies, long-lifetime, lowcost, and easy-operation.

Compared to the tedious backbone modifications of NFREAs, side-chain engineering[44 47] is versatile and cost-effective to modulate the molecular stacking and microstructures of active layers. As described above, the current NFREAs often have decent molecular planarity fastened by multiple intramolecular non-covalent interactions, which could enable strong selfaggregation behaviors and be detrimental to phase-separation. Therefore, long branched alkyl side chains, with good solubilization effect, are favored by most of the NFREAs.48-50] However, the bulky side groups adjacent to $\pi$ backbones fatally disturb intermolecular stacking by reducing $\pi$ orbitals coupling, which generate unfavorable hypsochromic absorption spectra and reduced charge transport capacity of pristine acceptors. Under these circumstance, developing or employing alternative side groups is desperately needed. Phenylalkyl category side groups, which we developed to balance the crystallinity and miscibility of materials for refined blending networks,[51] could be promising in NFREAs owing to their unique electronic structures and versatile functions. The alkyl section attached to $\pi$ backbone ensures ordered molecular packing for preferred charge transport. The bulky phenyl away from skeleton provides moderate stereo-hindrance for raised solubility. Consequently, the phenylalkyl-FREA based as-cast device received ${ \approx } 7 7 \%$ fill factor (FF), largely surpassing those of alkyl$( 6 9 \% )$ and alkylphenyl- $( 5 6 \% )$ based counterparts. Subsequent studies suggested that the phenyl terminals in phenylalkyl could function as extra bases to interact with adjacent acceptor or/and donor molecules, leaving improved molecular packing in acceptor phases and interfacial D/A interactions.[52,53] These findings strongly encourage us to further explore the application of phenylalkyl in NFREAs for cost-effective as-cast OSCs.

Herein, by employing phenylalkyl side groups, we design three NFREAs named BOR-CnPh $( \mathrm { n } = 3$ , 4, and 6) with subtly different alkyl spacers. Compared to routine branched alkyls, the phenylalkyl side groups significantly improve solid-state aggregation of BOR-CnPh, offering possibility for highperformance pristine solar cells. Yet it is worth noting that the delicate differences of CnPh side chain bring out greatly distinguishing aggregation properties, that is, strong aggregation but disordered orientations for BOR-C3Ph, strong aggregation and face-on orientations for BOR-C4Ph, and weak aggregation and disordered orientations for BOR-C6Ph. Meanwhile, the differences in alkyl spacer regulate D/A spatial arrangement, affording additional $\pi \mathfrak { - } \pi$ interaction contributions between the phenyl terminal of $\mathrm { C 4 P h } /$ C6Ph and donor (PBDB-T) $\pi$ backbone. Therefore, the PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph and PBDBT:BOR-C6Ph pairs receive stronger $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ intermolecular interactions, $\pi$ orbital coupling, and charge transfer capacity. These modulations collectively contribute to the polarization of as-cast heterojunction morphologies, with large aggregates/chaotic orientations in PBDB-T:BOR-C3Ph blend and fibrous morphology/face-on orientation in PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph blend, when casted from chlorobenzene (CB) solutions. The as-cast devices based on BOR-C3Ph, BOR-C4Ph, and BOR-C6Ph deliver optimal PCE of $4 . 1 6 \%$ - $1 0 . 3 6 \%$ in chloroform (CF)), $1 3 . 1 2 \%$ , and $1 1 . 6 1 \%$ , respectively. The PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph as-cast device also receives the most favorable thermal stability among the three solar cells. Furthermore, the efficiency of PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph ranks the best among NFREAs based as-cast solar cells as far as we know. This study provides a feasible approach for low-cost as-cast efficient OSCs by rationally employing versatile phenylalkyl side groups in NFREAs.

# 2. Results and Discussion

The synthetic procedure of the three acceptors BOR-CnPh $[ \mathrm { n } = 3$ , 4, 6) are displayed in Figure 1a. The synthesis details are shown in Scheme S1 (Supporting Information). Different from conventional alkyl side chains, the BOR-CnPh acceptors carry phenylalkyl side groups in the dithiophene cyclopentadiene (DTC) moieties, which was realized by incorporating phenylalkyl bromide under alkaline condition. All the intermediates and target NFREAs were fully characterized by NMR spectra. We then measured the absorption properties as shown in Figure 1b and the relevant data in Table 1. In dilute solutions, the three BOR-CnPh acceptors exhibit nearly overlapped and broad absorption bands ranging from 500 to $8 3 0 \mathrm { n m }$ with peaks centered at ${ \approx } 7 4 3 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , which are almost identical with that of the branched alkyl modified analog DOC2C6-2F in literature.[24] However, the differences are visually enlarged in films. Compared to solution states, notable bathochromic shifts of over $8 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ were found in BOR-CnPh films, which are significantly larger than that of DOC2C6-2F with less than $4 0 \mathrm { n m }$ offset. This indicates the markedly enhanced aggregation of BOR-CnPh, attributed to the better aligned phenylalkyl side groups over branched alkyls. Accordingly, the optical bandgaps $( E _ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { \mathrm { { o p t } } } )$ of BOR-CnPh (1.37-1.38 eV) are smaller than that of DOC2C6-2F $( 1 . 4 2 \ \mathrm { e V } )$ , determined from the absorption onset $( \lambda _ { \mathrm { o n s e t } } )$ . The bathochromic absorptions would help to harvest more photons at lower-energy regions, and the improved molecular packing could enhance charge transport of free carries following photon-exciton dissociation. Besides, regarding BOR-CnPh, the subtle variations of methylene numbers in the alkyl spacer of CnPh triggers differences in molecular assemblies. With the increase of alkyl spacer, the maximum absorption peaks mildly and gradually red shift, however, the absorption onsets move conversely. The highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest occupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels were calculated based on cyclic voltammetry (CV) (Figure S1, Supporting Information). As Table 1 shows, the three NFREAs of BOR-CnPh have comparable molecular frontier energy levels. This result is reasonable given the minor effect of nonconjugated side chains on $\pi$ electron densities. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations at the B3LYP/6-31G(d) level were conducted to study the molecular geometries (Figure S2, Supporting Information). To reduce simulation complexity, the long alkyl chains in central benzene cores are simplified to methyl, while the characteristic $\mathrm { C n P h }$ side groups in DTC moieties fully maintain. All the three molecules possess good molecular planarity with dihedral angles of $1 0 { - } 1 2 ^ { \circ }$ between the central phenyl and adjacent DTC motifs, attributed to the lock effect of multiple $\boldsymbol { \mathrm { O } } \cdot \cdot \cdot \boldsymbol { \mathrm { S } }$ noncovalent interactions. The calculated energy levels (Figure S3, Supporting Information) further support the negligible disturbance of CnPh side groups on molecular orbitals, which is conducive to investigating the impacts of molecular stacking intently. Thus, these results sug. gest that the delicate phenylalkyl side chains can finely tune optical properties and aggregation behaviors, and the preferred molecular stacking of BOR-CnPh acceptors could ensure passable inherent charge transport and be particularly suitable for as-cast OSCs.

Differential scanning calorimetry (DsC) was employed to roughly study the crystallization behaviors of the three NFREAs. As given in Figure S4 (Supporting Information), BOR-C4Ph exhibits one endothermic peak at $2 8 9 . 9 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ with a large melting enthalpy of $6 7 . 1 3 \mathrm { ~ J ~ g ^ { - 1 } }$ . In comparison, the BOR-C6Ph gives a relatively smaller melting enthalpy of $5 6 . 7 8 \ \mathrm { ~ J ~ } \ \mathrm { g } ^ { - 1 }$ at $2 7 9 . 8 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } .$ The greater melting temperature and larger enthalpy change suggest more orderly molecular self-assembly and stronger crystallinity of BOR-C4Ph compared to those of BOR-C6Ph. Notably, there are no obvious endothermic or exothermic transitions for BOR-C3Ph during this temperature range, which probably suggests disordered molecular stacking of BOR-C3Ph molecules. Grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) studies were further performed to investigate molecular stacking and orientations. The line-cut profiles in in-plane (IP) and out-of-plane (OOP) azimuths are shown in Figure 1c,d, with corresponding 2D patterns collected in Figure 1e-g. It is apparent that BOR-C3Ph has series of diffraction rings and dense diffraction plots in both the IP and OOP directions, suggesting decent molecular packing but extremely disordered stacking orientations. Meanwhile, the long-range (h00) diffraction plots in OOP direction, and larger laminar stacking coherence length (CCL) up to $3 6 . 9 6 \ \mathrm { n m }$ in IP direction confirm strong self-aggregation of BOR-C3Ph. However, the disordered stacking orientations are against vertical charge transport of OsCs. It is amazing that, with only one methylene difference, the BOR-C4Ph affords completely different stacking properties, that is, typical face-on orientation with dominating (010) diffraction spot in OOP direction (Figure 1f). Based on

Scherrer's equation, the $\pi \mathfrak { - } \pi$ stacking $\operatorname { C C L } _ { \pi \cdot \pi }$ of BOR-C4Ph was calculated to be $4 . 9 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , with distance spacing $( d _ { \pi \cdot \pi } )$ of $0 . 3 4 \ \mathrm { n m }$ . According to previous reports, the oriented face-on molecular stacking is highly preferred by OsCs to transport free carries more effectively.[8,9] BOR-C6Ph again displays different diffraction patterns with less diffraction plots and faint $\pi \mathfrak { - } \pi$ stacking signal (Figure 1g). Nevertheless, all the three BOR-CnPh have more compact $\pi \mathfrak { - } \pi$ stacking and greater $\operatorname { C C L } _ { \pi }$ $\pi$ than those of their analog DOc2C6-2F (Table 1). The greatly shortened laminar distance spacing of BOR-CnPh again supports the superiority of structured phenylalkyl as side groups over the long and branched alkyls. AFM images of neat BOR-CnPh films (Figure 1e-g) suggest BOR-C3Ph has stronger aggregation behavior considering the slightly larger root-mean square roughness $( R \mathsf { q } )$ of $0 . 9 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ , compared to those of BOR-C4Ph $( 0 . 7 2 \ \mathrm { n m } )$ and BOR-C6Ph $( 0 . 7 0 \ \mathrm { n m } )$ . However, these aggregates in BOR-C3Ph film should be intricately arranged, judging from its GIWAXS pattern. The smaller domains in BOR-C6Ph again support its low crystallinity. We then determined the internal reorganization energy $( \lambda _ { \mathrm { i n t } } )$ of BOR-CnPh molecules, which is a metrics of the energetic cost for structural relaxation after ionization of the molecule. The results of electron and hole reorganization energy $( \lambda _ { \mathrm { e } }$ and $\lambda _ { \mathrm { h } } )$ are collected in Table 1. Apparently that BOR-C4Ph shows a smaller $\lambda _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of $1 6 3 ~ \mathrm { m e V }$ than those of BOR-C3Ph $( 1 6 8 ~ \mathrm { m e V } )$ and BOR-C6Ph $( 1 6 4 ~ \mathrm { m e V } )$ , and similar situation for their $\lambda _ { \mathrm { h } }$ values. The smaller $\lambda _ { \mathrm { e } }$ as $\lambda _ { \mathrm { h } }$ suggests more advantageous electron transport instead of hole transport. Especially for BOR-C4Ph, the lowest $\lambda _ { \mathrm { e } }$ among the three molecules manifests the most competitive electron transport capacity. Promoted by the strong crystallinity with preferential orientation, BOR-C4Ph could have satisfactory intrinsic charge transport, which is desired for NFREAs based low-cost OSCs free of any additional pre- or/and treatments.

Photovoltaic devices were fabricated with a conventional structure of ITO/PEDOT:PSS/active layer/PDINN/Ag (Figure 2a). PDINN, an aliphatic amine-functionalized perylene-diimide, was used as electron transport layer. The lowcost polymer PBDB-T (relative to frequently used fluorinated PM6) was employed as donor to pair the BOR-CnPh series NFREAs. The detailed fabrication and characterization of OSCs were described in Supporting Information. All the best D/A weight ratios of the three as-cast devices processed in CB were determined to be 1:1 (Table S1, Supporting Information). The optimal $J { - } V$ plots are displayed in Figure 2b and Table 2. Among the three as-cast solar cells, PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph device generates the most competitive PCE up to $1 3 . 1 2 \%$ , with opencircuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ of 0.827 V, short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathsf { S C } } )$ of $2 2 . 6 7 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ , and FF of $7 0 . 0 3 \%$ The further thermal annealed devices result in a slightly decrease efficiency of $1 2 . 4 4 \%$ , suggesting the approaching saturated exciton/charge management at as-cast situation. Notably, the as-cast efficiency of BOR-C4Ph largely surpasses that of alkyl modified analog DOC2C6-2F (PBDB-T as donor) with optimal PCE of $1 1 . 6 9 \%$ Besides, the subtle modification of CnPh side chains produces distinguishing photovoltaic efficiencies for BOR-CnPh community. The devices of PBDB-T:BOR-C6Ph afford a relatively lower PCE of $1 1 . 6 1 \%$ , with $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 2 4 \mathrm { V } ,$ collectively decreased $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 0 . 8 9 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and FF of $6 7 . 4 6 \%$ . Perhaps more surprisingly is the hugely reduced efficiency recorded from BOR-C3Ph processed in CB, with the PCE of $4 . 1 6 \%$ low $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 7 8 \mathrm { V } ,$ $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $1 4 . 6 7 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ and FF of $3 6 . 3 8 \%$ . Considering the decent efficiencies from BOR-C4Ph and BOR-C6Ph based devices, and the similar energy levels of the three NFREAs, the inefficient PCE of PBDB-T:BOR-C3Ph is probably ascribed to the extremely serious phase-separation with a large $R _ { \mathfrak { q } }$ up to $1 8 . 4 \ \mathrm { n m }$ (Figure S5, Supporting Information). The solubilities were then measured to help understand this rough surface. As shown in Figure S6 (Supporting Information), all the three NFREAs exhibited good solubility in CB and CF solvents at concentrations of $1 0 \ \mathrm { \ m g \ m L ^ { - 1 } }$ The solubility values further suggest that BOR-C3Ph has a relatively smaller solubility in CB $( 1 3 . 0 7 ~ \mathrm { { m g } ~ \mathrm { { m L ^ { - 1 } } ) } }$ and CF $2 2 . 5 6 ~ \mathrm { m g ~ m L ^ { - 1 } }$ , compared to the other two acceptors (Table S2, Supporting Information). Therefore, CF was employed to cast PBDBT:BOR $. \mathrm { C } 3 \mathrm { P h }$ blend, which yields an increased PCE of $1 0 . 3 6 \%$ with reasonable $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of $0 . 8 2 0 \mathrm { V } , J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ of $2 1 . 0 7 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and FF of $5 9 . 9 1 \%$ . PCE histograms (Figure 2c) based on sufficient devices again confirm the sig. nificant impacts of finely modified phenylalkyl of NFREAs on as-cast PCEs. More encouragingly, as suggested in Figure 2d and Table S3 (Supporting Information), the efficiency of PBDBT:BOR-C4Ph ranks the highest value among as-cast OSCs based on low-cost NFREAs, which declares the great potential for cost-effective practical application.

Charge transfer kinetics was studied by time-resolved photoluminescence (TRPL), with decay curves shown in Figure 3a and relevant data summarized in Table S4 (Supporting Information). The PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph blend has an accelerated emission decay with fluorescence lifetime $\varTheta$ calculated to be

0.46 ns, which is smaller than those of PBDB-T:BOR-C3Ph (0.69 ns) and PBDB-T:BOR-C6Ph (0.58 ns), and suggests faster charge transfer at D/A interfaces. Likewise, steady-- state PL confirms the most efficient charge transfer inside PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph system, where the quenching efficiencies were calculated to be $91 \%$ $9 8 \%$ and $9 5 \%$ for the BOR-C3Ph, BOR-C4Ph and BOR-C6Ph-based as-cast blends, respectively (Figure S7, Supporting Information). The advantageous charge transfer property between PBDB-T and BOR-C4Ph is to some extent attributed to their well-matched molecular conformations in that both materials possess dominating face-on orientations (Figure 1f; Figure S8, Supporting Information). In other words, the extremely disordered molecular orientations of BOR-C3Ph would reduce the $\pi$ orbital coupling with donor segments and therefore hinder charge transfer between them. Exciton dissociation and charge generation were investigated by measuring photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ versus effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ (Figure 3b).[54] Generally, most of the photogenerated excitons of OsCs could be dissociated into free charge carriers and swept out to the electrodes when $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ approaches $2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ or more, and hence, $J _ { \mathrm { p h } }$ could reach saturation $\left( J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } \right)$ . Contrastingly, the charge dissociation probabilities P(E,T) (a function of electric field E and temperature T) were calculated to be $9 2 \%$ $9 7 \%$ and $9 5 \%$ for BOR-C3Ph, BOR-C4Ph and BOR-C6Ph-based ascast devices, respectively. The result highlights the satisfactory exciton dissociation of PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph system, in spite of the very smaller HOMO offset.

According to the Wu model, surface energies () of the neat PBDB-T, BOR-C3Ph, BOR-C4Ph, and BOR-C6Ph films are estimated to be 39.05, 40.89, 43.82, and $4 4 . 0 3 \mathrm { m N } \mathrm { m } ^ { - 1 }$ respectively. The corresponding dispersion $( \gamma ^ { \mathsf { d } } )$ and polar components $( \mathcal { P } )$ of the surface energies were summarized in Table S5 (Supporting Information). The interfacial energy between two materials can be obtained according to the formula of $\gamma _ { ( A : B ) } = \gamma _ { A } + \gamma _ { B } - 4 ( \frac { \gamma _ { A } ^ { d } \gamma _ { B } ^ { d } } { \gamma _ { A } ^ { d } + \gamma _ { B } ^ { d } } + \frac { \gamma _ { A } ^ { p } \gamma _ { B } ^ { p } } { \gamma _ { A } ^ { p } + \gamma _ { B } ^ { p } } )$ where 7(A:B) denotes the interfacial energy between different materials, and $\gamma _ { \mathrm { A } }$ and $\gamma _ { \mathrm { { B } } }$ denote the surface energies of A and B materials, respectively.[57] Generally, small interfacial energies between two materials would increase their compatibility, which is essential to shape favorable BHJ morphologies for active layers. As illustrated in Figure 5b, the YPBDb-TBoR-c3Ph is up to 0.92 mN m-1, which is much larger than that of YPaDB-T:BoR-c4Ph $( 0 . 3 3 \mathrm { ~ m N ~ m ^ { - 1 } }$ andYPBDB-T:BOR-C6Ph $( 0 . 4 2 \mathrm { ~ m N ~ m ^ { - 1 } } )$ . In comparison, the moderate interfacial energies in the other two systems profitably account for sound phase-separations (Figure 4e,f).

To figure out the D/A interfacial characteristics in-depth, we carried out theoretical simulations for the three D/A systems based on the B3LYPD3/def2-SVP level of theory. The independent gradient model based on Hirshfeld partition (IGMH) isosurface analysis was employed to visually map interactions between the two molecules. Figure 5c-e give the corresponding IGMH isosurfaces of the three PBDB-T:BOR-CnPh pairs, where the green regions represent van der Waals interactions. It is apparently that the interactions between D/A $\pi$ conjugated skeletons dominate the interfacial interactions. However, we can discover that the phenyl terminals of CnPh side chains of the three acceptors receive distinguishing interaction conformations with donor skeleton. The $\mathrm { C } 3 \mathrm { P h }$ exhibits limited $\mathrm { C } { \cdot } \mathrm { H } \cdots \pi$ interactions between phenyl and PBDB-T backbone, owing to the short alkyl spacer in side chains and resultant sig. nificant steric hindrance for conformation rearrangement of the phenyl group. The extension of alkyl spacer increases the freedom of phenyl terminal. As a result, the BOR-C4Ph and BOR-C6Ph demonstrate comparable interaction conformations between their side groups with donor skeleton. More precisely, the phenyls of BOR-C4Ph and BOR-C6Ph favor to overlap with the benzodithiophene-4,8-dione (BDD) plane of PBDB-T, resulting in increased orbital coupling with $\mathrm { d } _ { \pi \cdot \pi } \mathrm { o f } \approx 3 . 2 5$ A. The additional $\pi \mathfrak { - } \pi$ interactions of phenyl/BDD in PBDB-T:BORC4Ph and PBDB-T:BOR-C6Ph not only facilitate charge transfer at $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ surfaces (consistent with TRPL studies, Figure 3a), but also further enhance D/A interactions. Attributed to the distinct spatial side group arrangements and resultant interaction contributions, the PBDB-T:BOR-C3Ph, PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph, and PBDB-T:BOR-C6Ph deliver D/A binding energy $( E _ { \mathrm { b } } )$ of 52.10, $- 6 3 . 8 0$ , and $- 6 9 . 9 6 \ \mathrm { k c a l \ m o l ^ { - 1 } }$ , respectively. The smaller $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ interactions between PBDB-T and BOR-C3Ph reduce the driving force for homogeneous dispersion of the donor and acceptor phases, which could be another origination of the excessive phase-separation in CB-processed blend (Figure S5, Supporting Information). Combined with the disordered molecular orientations of BOR-C3Ph, the overall schematic diagram of molecular packing in as-cast blend film can be illustrated in Figure 5f. Besides, the C3Ph side chains related spatial hindrance slightly enlarge the stacking distance (3.40 A) between dicycanovinylindan1-one (DCI) of BOR-C3Ph and benzo[1,2-b:4,5-b'ldithiophene (BDT) of PBDB-T, which is disadvantageous to charge transfer. In comparison, the closer $d _ { \pi \cdot \pi }$ in PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph and PBDB-T:BOR-C6Ph assemblies would enhance $\pi$ orbital coupling and charge transfer at D/A interfaces. Moreover, the stronger interactions facilitate exciton dissociation and charge generation, but also provide increased opportunity to form D/A interfaces and thus promote compatibility. Compared to BOR-C6Ph, the preferred higher crystallinity with more prominent face-on orientation of BOR-C4Ph further optimize charge transport channels, giving more appropriate molecular packing and phase-separation (Figure 5g,h). Therefore, these results highlight the elaborate regulation of phenylalkyl side groups on D/A conformations, interactions, and phase-separations of pristine active layers, so as to get rid of the bondage of multitudinous additional pre- or/and post-treatments and providing approach to cost-effective organic photovoltaics.

# 3. Conclusions

To conclude, this study reports a kind of NFREAs named BORCnPh $( \mathrm { n } = 3$ , 4, 6), which carry elaborately modified phenylalkyl side groups and receive high performance in cost-effective ascast OsCs. Compared to intensively employed branched alkyl chains, the well-aligned phenylalkyl dictate improved solid-state stacking, and thus reduce requirements of tedious pre- or/and post-treatments. More intriguingly, the subtle variations in CnPh chains trigger remarkable regulation on aggregation and photovoltaic performance. The BOR-C3Ph receives disorderly orientated strong self-aggregation property, which can be effectively transformed into superior well-organized face-on orientations for BOR-C4Ph. However, the crystallinity and stacking regularity are markedly weakened by further extending the alky spacer of phenylalkyl. Noticeably that the subtly changed alkyl spacer alters the spatial arrangement of phenyl terminal at interfaces, resulting in distinguishing D/A conformations and interactions. PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph and PBDB-T:BOR-C6Ph pairs exhibit additional $\pi$ orbital interactions between the phenyl terminals of phenylalkyl and PBDB-T backbones, which enhance the D/A miscibility and afford appropriate phase-separation in the two pristine blends. With CB as solvent, the as-cast solar cells based on BOR-C3Ph, BOR-C4Ph, and BOR-C6Ph demonstrate PCE of $4 . 1 6 \%$ $1 3 . 1 2 \%$ and $1 1 . 6 1 \%$ , respectively. Notably, the efficiency of PBDB-T:BOR-C4Ph ranks the highest among NFREAs based as-cast OsCs. Therefore, this study highlights the great potential of phenylalkyl side groups in high-performance NFREAs, but also provides a promising strategy to regulate the intrinsic molecular stacking and morphologies for cost-effective OsCs for future industrialization. Besides, considering the distinguishing efficiencies of BOR-C3Ph-based devices processed from different solvents, regulating the solvent effects from solubility and volatilization rate views should be viable to further refine the film-forming kinetics, BHJ morphologies, and exciton/charge behaviors for boosting PCEs of as-cast solar cells.
