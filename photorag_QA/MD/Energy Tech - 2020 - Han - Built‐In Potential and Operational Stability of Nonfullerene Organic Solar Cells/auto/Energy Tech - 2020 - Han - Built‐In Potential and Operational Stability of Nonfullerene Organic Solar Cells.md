# Built-In Potential and Operational Stability of Nonfullerene Organic Solar Cells

Jiayin Han, Yiwen Wang, and Furong Zhu\*

Many progresses have been made in the advancement of solution-processable high-performance organic solar cells (OsCs), offering an encouraging alternative photovoltaic (PV) technology option to traditional solar cells. The solution processing capability provides OsCs with great fabrication flexibility for new device concepts, cell architectures, and applications including lightweight large. area transparent and flexible PV modules. The unique transparency and flexible features also add an attractive artistic aspect to emerging OsCs for use on curved surfaces, portable electronics, and indoor appliances. However, the stability of the OsCs is still less than satisfactory compared with the conventional inorganic semiconductor solar cells. This work discusses the improvement of the operational stability of OsCs through analyses of the important issues of 1) unbalanced charge mobility, 2) vertical stratification in the bulk heterojunction, and 3) built-in potential across the photoactive layer, and retaining a stable builtin potential for enhancing the operational stability of OsCs.

# 1. Introduction

Solar energy is the abundant sustainable energy source in nature. There are intense research activities on organic solar cells (OSCs) for exploring efficient renewable energy sources to reduce our dependence on fossil fuel. Compared with the conventional silicon and other inorganic semiconductor solar cell technologies, the emerging OsCs offer an attractive alternative photovoltaic (PV) technology due to the solution fabrication capabilities, huge variety of material choices, and more competitive short payback time for cost reduction. OsCs made with low-bandgap polymers[1-3] and nonfullerene acceptors have many advantages, improved miscibility between the acceptor and donor in the photoactive blend layers, higher absorption extending to the long wavelength range in the solar spectrum, and tunable optical and electronic properties. Many exciting advancements have been developed for attaining high power conversion efficiency (PCE) OSCs through the emergence of new polymer donors with tailored energy levels,[46] nonfullerene acceptors,7,8] materials processing,[9-11] and new device fabrication technologies.[3,12] A PCE of ${ > } 1 7 \%$ has been achieved for solution-processable OsCs.[13] The high-quality functional nonfullerene acceptors and polymer donors can be prepared by different solution-fabrication processes, including inkjet printing for large-scale OsCs at a low cost. Compared with inorganic solar cells, energy payback time of the OSCs is shorter. OSCs also have a higher PCE under low light intensity, making them very suitable for indoor energy harvesting devices. OsCs can be prepared in different colors, made flexible, light weight, and transparent, adding an attractive artistic aspect to the OsC panel for applications in tension membrane, greenhouses, power-generating windowpanes, mobile electronics, and different outdoor appliances. The development of stable and high-efficiency OsCs holds a great promise as a source of clean energy, constituting the enormous potential for the next major step in PV field.

In parallel to the exciting achievements in the synthesizing highly efficient low bandgap photoactive materials, much effort has been conducted to the development of high-performance OSCs. Different approaches, including morphological analysis, material structural analysis, and photophysics measurement,1416] have been used to study the issues hampering the stability of OsCs. Various degradation paths, e.g., poor miscibility between the donors and acceptors in the bulk heterojunction (BHJ),[15) oxidation occurred at the cathode/organic interface,[17] localized failure in BHJ due to the moisture encroachment, and unbalanced charge mobility in the photoactive region in the $\mathrm { O S C s ^ { [ 1 6 ] } }$ have been identified. The existence of the undesired exciton dissociation processes occurred at the metal/organic interface in regular configuration OsC is not favorable for charge collection, due to the compensation of the photogenerated charges in the OSCs. The charge extraction probability and reproducibility of the cell performance can be greatly improved upon removal of undesired charge compensation.18,191 A continuous stratification in a BHJ occurs in the nonfullerene OSCs during the aging period, leading to an acceptor-rich zone toward the bottom part and a polymer-rich region appeared on the top surface in the BHJ.[19,20] An incessant reduction in the built-in potential $( V _ { 0 } )$ was observed in the 3,9-bis(2-methylene-(3- (1,1-dicyanomethylene)-indanone))-5,5,11,11-tetrakis(4-hexylphenyl)-dithieno $[ 2 , 3 { \cdot } \mathrm { d } ; 2 ^ { \prime } , 3 ^ { \prime } { \cdot } \mathrm { d } ^ { \prime } ]$ -s-indaceno[1 $, 2 \cdot b : 5 , 6 \cdot b ^ { \prime } ]$ dithiophene (ITIC)-based nonfullerene OsCs, due to an investable charge transfer between the poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) hole extraction layer (HEL) and 2-(3-oxo-2, 3-dihydroinden-1-ylidene)malononitrile (INCN) moieties in ITIC, leading to a gradual reduction in charge collection and a poor cell operation stability. Suppression of the interfacial reaction between PEDOT:PSS and ITIC, e.g., through interfacial engineering and modification for attaining a stable $V _ { 0 }$ across the BHJ, is crucial for attaining the high-performance OsCs.[19,20]

Apart from efficiency and cost competitiveness, stability is another important factor with practical implications if OsCs are to be a feasible option for a sustainable green energy source. Much research effort has devoted to improving the OSCs performance.21] The improved acceptor/donor miscibility, energy-level alignment of the functional layers, and morphology control are closely associated with the stability of nonfullerene OsCs.[22] The use of the cross-linkable polymers helps improving the cell stability through suppression of phase separation in BHJ, e.g., the photoactive materials with high glass transition temperature.23-26] The polymer layers with ordered molecular packing are favorable for efficient operation of the OsCs, showing a less burn-in degradation upon the illumination,27] as cells are less influenced by energetic disorder.28,29]

Nonfullerene acceptor with a relatively high lying lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level helps to suppress energy losses and results in a large open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ in corresponding devices. However, a deep highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level favors the efficient charge transfer in BHJ. A combination of a deep HOmO level and a high LUMO level acceptor is helpful to enhance shelf lifetime of the OsCs.[22] Nonfullerene OsCs possessed a much higher efficiency;[30-32] however, the fundamental understanding of the impacts of the vertical stratification and morphology of BHJ on charge transport properties, PCE, and lifetime of nonfullerene OSCs is not fully understood yet. OsCs can be prepared using a regular and an inverted configuration. For regular OsCs, the front transparent electrode acts as the anode, and rear opaque electrode, such as Al, Ag, and low work function metal alloy, acts as the cathode. For reverse configuration cell, the metal electrode is the anode, whereas the front transparent electrode is the cathode. The photogenerated charges in the organic photoactive layer are drifted toward the respective electrodes under an effective internal electric field $\left( E _ { \mathrm { e f f } } \right)$ , which is a function of the external bias $( V _ { \mathrm { a } } )$ and $V _ { 0 }$ across the BHJ. In addition to the materials, the cell configuration and process conditions also affect the charge generation, charge extraction, charge transport, and thereby the performance of the OsCs. The recent progresses reveal that the reverse config. uration is more favorable for high-performing nonfullerene.32] However, the related fundamental physics on enhanced charge extraction through suppressing bimolecular recombination and retaining of a high $V _ { 0 }$ in the nonfullerene OsCs, is still far from adequate.

The use of the appropriate interlayer in OSCs is also important to the stable operation of OsCs.33] The insertion of a buffer layer not only enhances the charge collection in devices, but also eliminates the unfavorable interfacial exciton dissociation.[15,18] The use of an appropriate interlayer in OsCs helps forming an Ohmic and thermal stable contact at the active layer/contact interfaces,21] aiding in additional advantage for retarding the moisture and oxygen encroachment to the active layer.[34] This work discusses the improvement of the operational stability of OsCs through analyses of the following important issues: 1) unbalanced charge mobility and moisture encroachment, 2) vertical stratification and space charge buildup in a BHJ, and 3) built-in potential across the BHJ. The recent research effort on retaining a high built-in potential and its impact on charge transport and stability of nonfullerene OsCs are also presented.

# 2. Unbalanced Charge Mobility and Moisture Encroachment

The charge transport properties are critical to the cell performance. The photogenerated charge carriers in the solar cell are drifted toward the respective anode and cathode under internal electric field. The imperfections formed in the photoactive and functional layers, e.g, impurity-induced charge traps, defects formed by the domains in the active layers, vertical stratification in the BHJ, and change in the morphological properties of the blend layers, are responsible for the performance.[16] Apart from the intrinsic deterioration in the material properties, the charge transport behaviors in the donor/acceptor blend layer are also closely associated with the film morphology, process conditions, the polarity of the organic solvents used in the formulations, postdeposition treatment, stratification of the acceptor and acceptor in the BHJ, and charge accumulation in the BHJ in OSCs.[17]

Different photophysics techniques, e.g., steady-state photoinduced absorption (PA), time-of-flight photoconductivity technique, transient photocurrent (TPC), and photoinduced charge extraction by linearly increasing voltage (PhotoCELIV), have been developed to study the dynamics of the charge carrier mobility, charge density, and lifetime. The steady-state charge density and lifetime in an OSC were investigated by the transient photovoltage measurements.35 Devices with charge injection contacts are used in the TPC measurements, providing the polarity of the photogenerated charge carriers drifted under the effective internal electrical field.36 It is well known that photoexcitation on polymer films produces neutral excitons and the subsequent relaxation of the excitons into charge carriers has its unique spectral profile. The photoexcited states in the polymer film can be analyzed using pump-probed PA measurements. Earlier work on PA mostly concerned with intrinsic polymer films. The understanding on the dynamics of exciton dissociation at the acceptor/ donor interface and the subsequent charge transport properties in the photoactive blend layer is critical for efficient operation of OSCs.37,38 It shows that charge separation and charge recombination processes occurring at the interface of acceptor and donor is very complex and the relaxation process is over the time scale from femtosecond to microsecond.39] The vertical phase separation, variation of the morphology of the BHJ, relaxation dynamics, and carrier transport properties are the functions of the formulations and process conditions, e.g., new syntheses route!40] and postdeposition annealing[41] treatments.

The anisotropy of the organic semiconductor, due to the varying polarization energies, has a huge influence on exciton dissociation and charge transport properties. The charge carrier mobility in the OsCs is closely associated with both the temperature and strength of the electric field. The measurement of the TPC in an OsC provides information on charge carrier dynamics including incorporate transport, charge recombination, charge trapping and detrapping, and charge carrier lifetime,42] providing an insight to investigate the material purity and structuralinduced defects in the photoactive blend layer in the OsCs.43] The TPC, $I _ { \mathrm { T P C } } .$ can be expressed by[19,20]

$$
I _ { \mathrm { T P C } } = I _ { \mathrm { L } } - I _ { \mathrm { D } }
$$

where $I _ { \mathrm { D } }$ is dark current measured for the device in the absence of light, whereas $I _ { \mathrm { L } }$ is the photocurrent generated in the presence of light, measured for the device under the different external biases. The photogenerated charge carriers are drifted toward the respective contacts under an internal effective electric field. The internal effective field in the cells is a result of a combination of the $V _ { 0 }$ and an external bias. The $V _ { 0 }$ in the OSCs can be measured using TPC method. The TPC, $I _ { \mathrm { T P C } }$ approaches zero when the $V _ { 0 }$ across the cell is compensated by an offset external bias. The charge carrier drift mobility $( \mu )$ is given by[44]

$$
\mu = \frac { 2 d ^ { 2 } } { 3 A t _ { \operatorname* { m a x } } ^ { 2 } ( 1 + 0 . 3 6 \Delta j / j ( 0 ) ) }
$$

where $\Delta j$ is the maximum current due to photoexcitation, $j ( 0 )$ is the capacitive displacement current, $A$ is a voltage ramp, and $t _ { \mathrm { m a x } }$ is the time taken for the TPC reaching to its maximum. By introducing a time delay between the start of a voltage ramp and a laser pulse in the PhotoCELIV measurements, the quantity of the extracted charges is dependent on the time delay, and hence the lifetime of the photogenerated carriers can be examined.[45]

A comprehensive study on the charge transport properties on the stability of OsCs has been studied using TPC and PhotoCELIV measurements. The degradation mechanism induced by temperature and light are identified as the demixing phenomenon in the metastable amorphous state in organic materials.15,16] The moisture encroachment is one of the critical factors leading to the deterioration in stable operation of the OSCs. It shows that cathode/organic interface in the OsCs is thermally unstable, caused by gradual interfacial oxidation due to the oxygen and moisture encroachment. The inevitable interfacial oxidation at the cathode/organic interface can be suppressed by interfacial emerging and modification. It shows that the moisture encroachment also induces undesired charge traps and defects in the active area in the OsCs, increasing charge recombination and hence a gradual decrease in the photocurrent. This study provides an understanding of the underlying degradation mechanisms in OsCs and possible solutions to the stable and efficient operation of the OSCs.[17]

# 4. Built-In Potential across the BHJ

The effects of vertical stratification and morphology of a BHJ on space charge buildup, bimolecular recombination, charge transfer, and the performance of nonfullerene OsCs with regular and reverse configurations have also been examined using TPC measurements and $J { - } V$ characteristics under different illumination intensities. The TPC $I _ { \mathrm { T P C } }$ in the nonfullerene OsCs is dependent on the internal electric field, which is related to the active layer thickness $( d )$ $V _ { 0 }$ across a BHJ, and applied external bias to the cells, and can be expressed as follows[19,20]

$$
E _ { \mathrm { e f f } } ~ = ~ ( V _ { 0 } - V _ { a } ) / d
$$

$V _ { 0 }$ in a solar cell can be evaluated by measuring the change in the TPC under different external bias. By applying an increasing compensating external bias to offset the $V _ { 0 }$ according to Equation (3), the internal electric field gradually vanishes and the photocurrent approach zero in the absence of $E _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ For example, as shown in Figure 6a, the $I _ { \mathrm { T P C } }$ of a PTB7-Th:ITIC-based regular configuration OsC approaches zero under a $V _ { a }$ of ${ \approx } 0 . 8 4 \mathrm { V }$ corresponding to the $V _ { 0 }$ in the OsC. Using the same approach, it reveals clearly that a higher $V _ { 0 }$ of $1 . 0 \mathrm { V }$ was obtained for a reverse configuration PTB7-Th:ITIC OSC, as shown in Figure 6b. The TPC measurements suggest that the OSCs with a reverse configuration possess a higher $V _ { 0 }$ compared with that in the regular configuration nonfullerene OsCs. The results show that an $8 0 \mathrm { n m }$ -thick BHJ is optimal for the inverted nonfullerene OSCs, whereas a thicker BHJ of $1 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ was optimized for the regular configuration OsCs. According to Equation (3), a thinner active layer thickness coupled with a higher $V _ { 0 }$ produces a stronger $E _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ in the reverse configuration OsCs than that in the regular structure. It becomes clear that the reverse-configuration nonfullerene OsCs are more favorable for charge transport and reduced charge recombination, assisted with a relatively high $E _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ across the PTB7-Th-ITIC blend layer, leading to an efficient charge extraction in the nonfullerene OSCs.

The charge recombination and charge collection are competing processes, and are closely associated with $E _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ in the solar cells.15,16] The charge recombination processes in OSCs mainly include the trap-assisted recombination (or geminate recombination) and bimolecular recombination. Because of the intimate miscibility of polymer donor and acceptor molecules, efficient exciton dissociations occur, leading to charge separation. The charges are then transported out of the BHJ by drift conduction under $E _ { \mathrm { e f f } } .$ Thus, a clear understanding on the suppression of bimolecular recombination and enhancement of charge collection in the OsCs are required. The effect of the $V _ { 0 }$ across the BHJ on charge transfer and recombination processes has been investigated to understand its impact operational stability of OSCs. The light intensity-dependent $J { - } V$ characteristics measured for the nonfullerene OsCs with a regular configuration have been examined. The correlation between the short-circuit current density $( J { \mathrm { s c } } )$ and intensity of the incident light $( I )$ measured for the OsCs, follows a power law dependence, i.e., $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \propto I ^ { \beta }$ . The exponent $\beta _ { : }$ extrapolated by plotting the $J _ { \mathrm { s c } } \propto I ^ { \beta }$ approaches unity for complete charge extraction without loss to the charge recombination. A smaller $\beta$ is often observed, due to the presence of the charge recombination.

Different charge loss processes, e.g., charge recombination,[54] space-charge accumulation,15] and unbalanced charge mobility in the cell,16] are responsible for resulting a smaller $\beta$ [45,55] The correlation between the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ of the OsCs and illumination intensity can be expressed by $\begin{array} { r } { V _ { 0 \mathrm { C } } \approx [ ( 1 - 2 ) \cdot \frac { k T } { q } ] \cdot \ln { I } } \end{array}$ [19] The charge recombination in the OsCs with regular and reverse configurations can be analyzed by examining the variation of $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ under different illumination intensities. The $V _ { \mathrm { O C } ^ { - } }$ logarithmically $( \ln I )$ plot produces a slope of $\frac { k T } { g }$ if the charge recombination in the cell is dominated by the bimolecular recombination. A slope of $2 \cdot { \frac { k T } { q } }$ is observed for the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } { - } \mathrm { l n } I$ characteristics measured for the conventional crystalline or polycrystalline silicon solar cells, implying that the monomolecular recombination is dominated in the solar cell. A reduction of the bimolecular recombination loss is essential for efficient operation of the OSCs.

The charge collection properties are closely related to the $V _ { 0 }$ or effective bias $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } ,$ defined as $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } = V _ { 0 } - V _ { \mathrm { a } } ,$ across the BHJ, and can be analyzed using the $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } - V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ characteristics of OSCs, where photocurrent density $( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } ) { = } J { - } J _ { \mathrm { D } }$ , where $J$ denotes the photocurrent density and $J _ { \mathrm { D } }$ represents the dark current density. When the OsCs are operated near the short-circuit condition $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } = V _ { 0 } )$ , the trap-assisted (first-order) charge recombination is the main recombination process. The bimolecular (secondorder) recombination gradually becomes the dominant recombination process when the internal effective electric field in OSCs approaches zero, e.g., the cells are operated near the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ condition $\left[ V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } = 0 \mathrm { V } \right]$ . The capability of the charge extraction and recombination processes in the solar cell can be analyzed using the charge collection efficiency $( \eta _ { \mathrm { C C } } )$ . Bimolecular recombination is a main factor limiting the charge extraction in the OsCs operated under low $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } . \ \eta _ { \mathrm { C C } }$ can be analyzed using the following equation[56]

$$
\eta _ { \mathrm { c c } } \left( I , \ : V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } \right) = \frac { J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \left( I , \ : V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } \right) } { J _ { \mathrm { p h , s a t } } \left( I \right) }
$$

A clear understanding of the $\eta _ { \mathrm { C C } }$ and charge recombination in OSCs under different $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } ,$ e.g., from short-circuit to open-circuit conditions, is critical for achieving high-performance OsCs. Attaining a high $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ or $V _ { 0 }$ is certainly desired for suppressing the bimolecular recombination loss, improving the performance of the OSCs.

The correlation between $\eta _ { \mathrm { C C } }$ and the operation stability of the cells operated under different $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ has been analyzed. For example, the normalized $\eta _ { \mathrm { C C } }$ of the regular configuration and inverted nonfullerene OsCs, operated under $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ of $0 . 1 3 \mathrm { V }$ (near $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ condition) and $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ of $0 . 2 2 \mathrm { V }$ (corresponding to the maximum power output condition), as a function of the aging time is shown in Figure 7a,b. The results show that the $\eta _ { \mathrm { C C } }$ of the nonfullerene OSCs with a reverse configuration undergoes a slow deterioration compared with that of the OsCs with a regular configuration in the same aging experiment. A close look at $\eta _ { \mathrm { C C } }$ obtained for cells operated under $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } }$ of $0 . 2 2 \mathrm { V }$ suggests that the nonfullerene OSCs with a regular configuration exhibit a $\eta _ { \mathrm { C C } }$ of $90 \%$ , which is considerably higher than that of the reverse structure $( 6 0 \% )$ after a 120 day aging test. The loss due to the bimolecular recombination is the main charge recombination loss. Enhanced charge extraction and reduced charge recombination are essential for achieving a high charge extraction efficiency in the OsCs. The results of the $J { - } V$ characteristics measured for the cells under different illumination intensities suggest that the inverted OSCs have a higher effective internal electric field, due to a high $V _ { 0 }$ in comparison with the regular configuration. The OsCs with a higher built-in potential is a prerequisite for attaining efficiency and stability of the OSCs.

The stability of the nonfullerene OsCs under different aging conditions has been tested. As an example, the evolutions of normalized $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } , \ J _ { \mathrm { S C } } ,$ fill factor (FF), and PCE measured for the nonfullerene OsCs with the regular structures and reverse configurations are shown in Figure 7c-f. The inverted OSCs experienced slow degradation in FF and $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ , leading to a moderate decrease in the efficiency in comparison with that of the regular configuration OsCs. A $2 . 6 \%$ decrease in $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } } ,$ a $7 \%$ drop in FF, and an $1 1 \%$ reduction in overall efficiency were observed in inverted PTB7-Th:ITIC OSCs after a 120day aging test. A rapid reduction in the PCE, FF, and $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ was observed for the regular structure of OsCs in the same aging test (i.e., a $2 9 \%$ drop in PCE, a $23 \%$ drop in FF, and a $7 . 7 \%$ drop in $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } } )$

Despite the recent development of high-quality photoactive polymers and nonfullerene acceptors, the substantial advancement in organic PV stability is needed if OsCs are adopted for practical applications. It has been identified that a gradual vertical phase separation of the polymer and donor in the BHJ occurs, e.g., forming a nonfullerene acceptor-rich zone and a polymer-rich top region during the aging test. The interfacial reaction between ITIC and PEDOT:PSS HEL is responsible for a continuous decrease in $V _ { 0 }$ in the nonfullerene OsCs. The quinoidal PEDOT in PEDOT:PSS has a characteristic vibrational peak at $1 4 5 6 \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ . A shift in the quinoidal PEDOT peak position has been observed for the bilayer ITIC/PEDOT:PSS film using Raman spectrum measurements. The shift in the PEDOT peak position is a result of the charge transfer between the PEDOT unit in PEDOT:PSS and INCN moieties in the ITIC molecules at the ITIC/PEDOT:PSS interface, supported by the UV-vis absorption measured for the ITIC layers, deposited on the quartz and PEDOT:PSS/glass substrates that aged under over the AM1.5 illumination period from 1 to $^ { 3 \mathrm { h } }$ . The absorption peak at $7 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ observed for the ITIC layer is closely associated with the $\pi - \pi ^ { * }$ transition and intramolecular charge transfer between the INCN and indacenodithieno[3,2-b]thiophene moieties in the ITIC molecules. The combination of the Raman spectrum and UV-vis absorption measurements reveals that the interfacial reaction between the INCN moieties in ITIC and PEDOT chains in PEDOT:PSS HEL at the BHJ/PEDOT:PSS HEL interface is the main reason responsible for the gradual reduction in the $V _ { 0 }$ in nonfullerene OsCs. Maintaining a stable $V _ { 0 }$ . realized through device engineering and interfacial modification, is critical for stable and efficient operation of the nonfullerene OSCs.[19,20]

# 5. Conclusions

The intimate miscibility of nonfullerene acceptor and polymer donor molecules in the BHJ promotes the efficient exciton dissociations, leading to charge transporting in the photoactive blend layer by drift conduction under the effective internal electric field in OsCs. Retaining a high $V _ { 0 }$ across the BHJ and a clear understanding of its effects on charge transfer and recombination processes are required. In the near future, the emerging

OSC products will be adopted for applications in windowpanes, offices, indoor appliances, and automobiles for converting light into electricity. The application of OsCs is also in line with the energy conservation, environmental protection, and sustainable development. It is anticipated that the progresses made in the development of stable and efficient nonfullerene OsCs will offer an attractive opportunity for a variety of new applications.
