# Photoinduced Charge Transfer and Recombination Dynamics in Star Nonfullerene Organic Solar Cells

Zeng Chen and Haiming Zhu\*

Cite This: J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 2022, 13, 1123-1130

ABSTRACT: Nonfullerene acceptors (NFAs) are regarded as star candidates for efficient organic solar cells with power conversion efficiency (PCE) over $1 8 \%$ . In contrast to the rapid development of NFA materials, however, the underlying excited-state dynamics which fundamentally govern the device performance remains unclear. In this Perspective, we discuss recent advances and provide our insights on photoinduced charge transfer and combination dynamics in NFA-based organic solar cell (OsCs), including the biphasic hole-transfer process and its correlation with morphology, the role of driving force and Marcus normal region behavior on interfacial hole-transfer properties, and charge recombination energy loss by NFA triplet formation. We also discuss our understanding of how to control the charge-transfer and recombination processes by phase morphology and molecular design to improve OSC performance. Finall, we suggest a few research directions, including the interfacial charge transfer and separation mechanism, the origin of low ill factor, and complex exited-state dynamics in multicomponent OSCs.

Since 2015, encouraging progress has been made in OsCs driven by the rapid development of emerging nonfullerene acceptor (NFA) materials,1-5 with the milestone power conversion efficiency (PCE) of single-junction solar cells over $1 8 \%$ 6-8 As shown in Figure 1a, compared with traditional fullerene acceptors (FAs, e.g., $\mathrm { P C } _ { 7 1 } \mathrm { B M } ,$ , state-of-the-art NFAs (e.g, IT-4F, IXIC-4Cl, and Y6) with alternating acceptordonor-acceptor (A-D-A) structures exhibit broad absorption for high short-circuit current density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \right)$ and suitable energy alignment with the donor for high open-circuit voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { O C } } )$ enabling PCEs of over $2 0 \%$ to be within sight.9-11

From a fundamental point of view, the photovoltaic performance of OSCs is ultimately governed by the underlying excited-state dynamics after light absorption. The general photoinduced process in organic bulk heterojunction (BHJ) blend films is displayed in Figure 1b. Because the dielectric constant of the organic semiconductor is quite small, photoexcitation generates singlet excitons with electron-hole pairs bound by Coulomb attraction. Singlet excitons in the donor (D) and acceptor (A) bulk regions (i.e., away from interface) will diffuse to the D/A interface where electron transfer (ET) or hole transfer (HT) can occur, forming charge transfer state (CT state) excitons at the interface.12,13 After that, the CT excitons can dissociate into electron and hole charge carriers, which transport to cathode and anode, respectively. On the other hand, charge recombination between electron and hole charges may also occur, reducing the photovoltaic efficiency. In contrast to the rapid development of materials and PCE, the understanding of the photophysical process, especially the photoinduced charge-transfer and recombination processes, remains unclear, which has hindered the rational design and optimization of NFA OSCs.

Fortunately, transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy with high temporal and species resolution has been shown to be a very powerful technique to investigate the photoinduced excited-state process in organic BHJ systems.14-26 Compared to FAs, the bandgaps of NFAs are quite narrow and their absorption is quite strong12, Therefore, we can always selectively excite NFAs with a low-energy pump light and monitor the spectral evolution and dynamics afterward, providing a real time view of hole-transfer and recombination processes in NFA-based BHJ blends.23,25-28 In this Perspective, considering our and other groups' recent TA spectroscopy results, we discuss a few fundamental photophysical questions in NFA blends, including (1) biphasic hole transfer, (2) role of driving force on interfacial hole transfer, and (3) charge recombination energy loss.

Biphasic Hole Transfer. In D/A BHJ blends, donor and acceptor materials always form a fibrous interpenetrating network structure with phase aggregation on the order of tens of nanometers. The domain size can be determined by grazing incidence small-angle X-ray scattering (GISAxs), grazingincident transmission small-angle X-ray scattering (GTSAXS), atomic force microscopy (AFM), or photoinduced force microscopy (PiFM) measurements.20,23,29 Shown in Figure 2a is the PiFM image of a representative PM6/IT-4F BHJ blend with $5 0 \%$ IT-4F weight percentage (wt $\%$ ), where the bright region corresponds to the IT-4F domain and the dark region to PM6.23 Clearly, they form a nanofbrillar network with IT-4F domain size of ${ \sim } 3 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$

Such nanoscale phase segregation has a strong effect on photoinduced charge carrier dynamics in BHJs. As shown schematically in Figure 2b, after photoexcitation of acceptor materials, while excitons near the $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ interface can dissociate quickly by direct HT, excitons embedded in the acceptor domain away from the interface have to transport to the interface prior to charge transfer. Therefore, photoinduced chargetransfer dynamics in BHJs generally exhibits a biphasic behavior with an ultrafast interfacial process and a slower diffusionmediated process.1,20-22,30 In FA-based BHJ films, Heeger et al. reported a biphasic electron-transfer process with $7 0 \%$ ultrafast interfacial process and 30% diffusion-mediated process.8,30 Similar biphasic charge-transfer behavior has also been observed in NFA-BHJ flms.3,31-34 Recently, we have thoroughly investigated the hole-transfer dynamics in PM6:IT-4F BHJ blends. By varying the IT-4F wt $\%$ (thus the domain size) (Figure 2c) and temperature (Figure 2d), we unambiguously demonstrated the ultrafast $[ \sim 1 5 0$ fs) interfacial HT process followed by a slow diffusion-mediated process $\left( 1 { - } 1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { p s } \right)$ in PM6:IT-4F BHJ.23 As shown in Figure 2c,d, the fraction and dynamics of the diffusion-mediated HT process depends strongly on temperature and IT-4F wt $\%$ confirming the biphasic HT behavior.

Interestingly, compared to electron transfer in FA BHJs with major ultrafast interfacial process, the HT process in highefficiency NFA BHJs is generally dominated $( { \sim } 6 0 \% )$ by a slow yet effcient diffusion-mediated proces (Figure 2e).3,33,35,36 This contrast strongly suggests the major contribution of exciton diffusion on photocarrier generation in NFA OSCs. NFAs have rigid, planar A-D-A structures, and preferential molecular orientations thus tend to form nanodomains with low disorder and good crystallinity, which prolongs the exciton lifetime and diffusion length.25,37-40 Indeed, a few research groups have measured the exciton diffusion length in a wide range of NFAs and revealed a diffusion length of $3 0 { - } 4 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ for the state-of-theart NFAs in high-ficiency OSCs.37,41 This diffusion length is much longer than polymer donors and FAs.37,41 Therefore, compared to FA OSCs, the diffusion-mediated HT process contributes to photocarrier generation significantly in NFA OSCs.

This exciton diffusion length also sets the rule for optimizing domain size and phase segregation in NFA-based BHJs. As we show by adjusting NFA wt $\%$ and thus domain size in PM6:IT4F blend (Figure 2f), a small domain size ( $[ \sim 1 0 \mathrm { n m }$ wt $\% = 5 \%$ of IT-4F yields high hole-transfer efficiency but rather low electron mobility; thus, the photon-to-charge internal quantum efficiency (IQE) is unsatisfactory. Alternatively, a large IT-4F domain size ( $\sim 6 0 \mathrm { n m }$ wt $\% = 7 0 \%$ ) increases electron mobility but reduces hole-transfer efficiency; thus, IQE also drops. A compromised NFA domain size of ${ \sim } 3 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ (wt $\% = 5 0 \%$ ), on the scale of exciton diffusion length, can lead to the best device performance by balancing the photogenerated charge-transfer and transport properties." Therefore, it is essential to optimize the nanoscale morphology and make full use of the exciton diffusion length of NFAs to increase photon-to-electricity conversion efficiency. In that regard, thermal annealing treatment and solvent additives have been shown to be effective tuning knobs to control morphology and phase aggregation, imposing a direct effect on the biphasic hole-transfer dynamics and leading to optimized Jsc. and PCE of devices.435,42\*

Role of Driving Force in Interfacial Hole Transfer. Now we focus on the interfacial hole-transfer process, which is critical for photogenerated exciton dissociation and charge carrier generation. In traditional FA-based OSCs, a general consensus has been reached that a ${ \sim } 0 . 3 \mathrm { e V }$ energy offset is required to drive efficient charge separation.13,2,43 However, this empirical relationship seems to be questionable when considering a large amount of results on NFA-based OSCs.1,3,6,10,28,44 Although the detailed mechanism remains unclear, many studies have reported D/A BHJs with small or even negative energy offset between the highest occupied molecular orbitals (HOMOs) $\left( \Delta E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } } = \mathrm { H O M O _ { D } } - \mathrm { H O M O _ { A } } \right)$ for efficient hole transfer and light-to-electricity conversion. $^ { 2 \dot { 5 } , 2 7 , 3 1 , 4 4 - 4 6 }$ For example, Yan et al. and Li et al. recorded a fast HT and high photocurrent generation with a $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } } \approx 0 . 0 5 \mathrm { e V . }$ 14,47 Ginger et al. and Chen et al. further showed efficient HT with near-zero or even negative $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } }$ 28,46 These results suggest that the role of driving force in NFA OSCs seems to be fundamentally different from that in FA OSCs.

A feasible way to understand the effect of driving force on charge-transfer rate is through Marcus electron-transfer theory where the charge-transfer rate $k$ is given by22,43

$$
k = \frac { 2 \pi } { \hbar \sqrt { 4 \pi \lambda k _ { \mathrm { B } } T } } V ^ { 2 } \mathrm { e x p } \Bigg ( - \frac { ( \lambda + \Delta G ) ^ { 2 } } { 4 \lambda k _ { \mathrm { B } } T } \Bigg )
$$

Here, $V$ is the electronic coupling between initial and final states, $k _ { \mathrm { B } }$ the Boltzmann constant, $T$ the temperature, $\lambda$ the total reorganization energy $( \sim 0 . 5 \mathrm { e V } )$ , and $\Delta G$ the Gibbs free energy change (-AG is the driving force and usually positive).1322 Calculating the driving force associated with the HT process in D/A BHJs is nontrivial but has to be done correctly for meaningful discussion. The commonly used $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } }$ is based on a single-particle approximation which neglects the excited state and excitonic nature of the initial and final states. Alternatively, driving force can be estimated from the energy difference between the charge-transfer state energy and optical bandgap of NFAs. However, the electroluminescence of the charge-transfer state in NFA solar cells can be dominated by the emission from singlet excitons of NFAs, and the real chargetransfer state emission is too weak to be cleanly detected. Considering the overall HT process, $\mathrm { ~ D ~ + ~ A ^ { * }  D ^ { + } { \cdots } A ^ { - } }$ , the driving force can be given by (Figure 3b)

$$
- \Delta G = E _ { \mathrm { A ^ { * } / A ^ { - } } } - E _ { \mathrm { D ^ { + } / D } } + E _ { \mathrm { b , C T } }
$$

Here, $E _ { \mathrm { D } ^ { + } / \mathrm { D } }$ is the ionization energy or HOMO level of donor material which can be obtained from ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPs) or cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements. $E _ { \mathrm { A ^ { * } / A } ^ { - } }$ is the electron affinity of excited-state NFA, which is fundamentally different from its ground-state HOMO level (i.e., $E _ { \mathrm { A ^ { ' } / A } } )$ . Considering the optical excitation process, $E _ { \mathrm { A ^ { * } / A } ^ { - } }$ can be estimated from its ground-state electron affinity $\left( E _ { \mathrm { A / A } ^ { - } } \right)$ and singlet exciton energy $\bar { ( }  { E _ { \mathrm { o p t } } } )$ by $E _ { \mathrm { A } ^ { * } / \mathrm { A } ^ { - } } = E _ { \mathrm { A } / \mathrm { A } ^ { - } } + E _ { \mathrm { o p t } }$ (Figure 3b). $E _ { \mathrm { A / A } } { } ^ { - }$ can be determined from inverse photoemission spectroscopy or CV measurement, and $E _ { \mathrm { o p t } }$ can be determined from the intersection between normalized absorption and emission spectra. $E _ { \mathrm { b , C T } }$ is the binding energy $( \sim 0 . 2 5 \mathrm { e V } )$ for the charge-transfer state.12,13,48

Recently, we systematically investigated the HT process in a large group of donor/NFA BHJs (22 in total) with a wide range of driving forces $_ { ( - 0 . 0 2 }$ to $0 . 4 8 \ \mathrm { e V } ,$ ) using diluted $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ blend films to eliminate the diffusion contribution and thus focus on the interfacial process.27 The obtained HT rates are between 1.7 $\times ~ 1 0 ^ { 1 0 }$ and $3 \mathrm { \hat { . } { 1 } \times 1 0 ^ { 1 2 } \ s ^ { - 1 } }$ for all blends and show a general increase with $- \Delta G ,$ regardless of $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ materials (Figure 3c). The dependence of HT rate on $- \Delta G$ can be well-described by the Marcus electron-transfer model, exhibiting a normal region behavior. Therefore, apparently, driving force acts as an effective energetic gauge for HT rate in NFA OSCs.

Compared to the HT rate, HT quantum efficiency (QE) is a more important and relevant parameter for OsC device performance. The HT QE is determined by the competition between the HT process and the intrinsic relaxation (the excited state to the ground state) of NFAs. Therefore, a certain driving force is required to ensure a high HT QE. As shown in Figure 3d, despite a slower HT rate with decreasing $- \Delta G ,$ HT QE remains above ${ \sim } 8 0 \%$ as long as the driving force is larger than $5 0 \ \mathrm { m e V } .$ The high QE plateau at larger driving force is due to a much faster HT rate than intrinsic relaxation of NFA. A comparison between panels c and d of Figure 3 indicates that the HT rate is not the gauging parameter for highly efficient charge transfer, and a high HT QE can be always achieved as long as it can compete with intrinsic relaxation. As a comparison, the plot of HT rate or QE as a function of $\Delta E _ { \mathrm { H O M O } }$ does not exhibit a clear trend or correlation.27 Therefore, compared to EHOMO, $- \Delta G$ provides a better indicator to access charge-transfer properties in NFA BHJs.

The driving force that drives interfacial charge transfer also constitutes a voltage loss in OSCs.27 Therefore, a minimized $- \Delta G$ with a high HT QE sets the optimization norm that can simultaneously enable photocurrent generation and mitigate voltage loss, thus leading to OSC devices with high PCE. In that regard, $- \Delta G$ of $5 0 ~ \mathrm { m e V }$ sets a critical threshold for choosing appropriate donor or acceptor materials to optimize the performance of the devices.

Charge Recombination Energy Loss. Although NFAbased OSCs have enhanced light absorption and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ compared to FA systems, the PCE, especially the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } ,$ still has much room for improvement.1,1I The $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ loss $\left( V _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } \right)$ in state-of-the-art OSCs is ${ \sim } 0 . 6 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ , which is much larger than that of traditional inorganic semiconductor (e.g, Si and GaAs) and emerging perovskite photovoltaics with $V _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ less than $0 . 4 \mathrm { V }$ and PCE over $\bar { 2 } 4 \%$ 25,38 Therefore, reducing $V _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ is no doubt an essential direction to further improve the PCE of OSCs.

Based on the detailed balance model. $^ { 4 9 , 5 0 } V _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ in OSCs can be divided into three parts as in the following (Figure 4a):

$$
\begin{array} { l } { { { \displaystyle V _ { \mathrm { l o s s } } = \frac { 1 } { q } ( E _ { \mathrm { g } } - q V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ) } } } \\ { { \displaystyle ~ = \frac { 1 } { q } ( E _ { \mathrm { g } } - V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ^ { \mathrm { s Q } } ) + \frac { 1 } { q } ( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ^ { \mathrm { s Q } } - V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ^ { \mathrm { r a d } } ) } } \\ { { \displaystyle ~ + \frac { 1 } { q } ( V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ^ { \mathrm { n d } } - V _ { \mathrm { o c } } ) } } \\ { { \displaystyle ~ = \frac { 1 } { q } ( \Delta E _ { 1 } + \Delta E _ { 2 } + \Delta E _ { 3 } ) } } \end{array}
$$

where $q$ is the elementary charge, $E _ { \mathrm { g } }$ the bandgap of the lower bandgap component which can be determined from the external quantum efficiency (EQE) curve of device, $\tilde { V _ { \mathrm { O C } } } ^ { S \mathrm { Q } }$ the maximum voltage according to the Shockley-Quessier limit, and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } ^ { \mathrm { r a d } }$ the theoretical open-circuit voltage with only radiative recombination. Apart from $\Delta E _ { 1 }$ and $\Delta \bar { E } _ { 2 } ,$ which both are related to the radiative recombination loss, $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ is the nonradiative recombination loss and equals $- k _ { \mathrm { B } } T \ln ( \mathrm { E Q E _ { E L } } ) ,$ where $k _ { \mathrm { B } }$ is the Boltzmann constant, $T$ the temperature, and $\mathtt { E Q E } _ { \mathrm { E L } }$ electroluminescence external quantum efficiency. At present, $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ in NFA OSCs shows a relatively large value of $0 . 2 \mathrm { - } 0 . 3 \mathrm { ~ e V }$ higher than that of other high-efficiency photovoltaic devices.10,25

Therefore, exploring the origin of nonradiative recombination loss in $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ blends is one of the hottest research fields in OSCs.

In BHJ OSCs, the separated electron and hole charge carriers from interfacial charge transfer can reform singlet CT $\left( ^ { \mathrm { { I } } } \mathbf { C } \mathbf { T } \right)$ and triplet CT $\left( ^ { 3 } \mathrm { C T } \right)$ excitons in the ratio of 1:3 driven by Coulomb interaction and spin statistics.'4 If energetically allowed, $^ 3 { \mathrm { C T } }$ can relax further to lower-lying localized triplet states in $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ materials. In traditional FA systems, because of the large band gap of fullerene and its derivatives, $^ 3 { \mathrm { C T } }$ in polymer donor/ fullerene blends will relax to the triplet state of the polymer donor, acting as a nonradiative recombination channel.'' In contrast, in polymer donor/NFA systems, NFAs usually have a lower band gap; thus, $^ 3 { \mathrm { C T } }$ tends to relax to the triplet state of NFA with lower energy (Figure 4b). This nonradiative recombination channel may limit the performance of OSCs, especially the $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ 14,51

Recently, we thoroughly investigated the $V _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ and nonradiative recombination process in representative polymer donor/NFA BHJ systems, PBDBT:IXIC-4Cl and PM6:IXIC4Cl.25 As shown in Figure 4c, compared with PBDBT:IXIC-4Cl, the PM6:IXIC-4Cl device shows higher $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and less $V _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ and the difference can be mainly attributed to the reduced nonradiative recombination loss, i.e., $\Delta E _ { 3 }$ . Near-infrared TA spectroscopy studies on these blend films exhibit a conversion of electron/hole charge polarons to triplet excitons of NFAs (Figure 4d), providing direct experimental evidence of nonradiative recombination channels in high-efficiency NFA OSCs. The TA kinetics (Figure 4e) shows faster formation of IXIC-4Cl triplet excitons in PBDBT:IXIC-4Cl than PM6:IXIC-4Cl, which is consistent with larger nonradiative recombination loss in the former. The difference can be attributed to a higher dielectric constant in fluoridated PM6, which retards the capture of electron and hole charges to CT excitons. The same nonradiative recombination loss channel has been reported by other groups with joint experimental and theoretical studies.24,26

To design NFA OSCs with lower $V _ { \mathrm { l o s s } }$ and higher PCE, it is critical to mitigate the nonluminous NFA triplet exciton formation by managing the energetics and dynamics of the NFA triplet states. In that regard, several approaches can be taken into future consideration. First, decreasing the binding energy of the CT states (e.g., by changing the dielectric constant of BHJ $\mathrm { { f l m } } ^ { 2 5 }$ ) and increasing the delocalization of CT states (e.g, by improving $\pi - \pi$ stacking37) can facilitate the dissociation of CT excitons and hinder the formation of CT states from charge carriers and thus the localized NFA triplet states. Second, enhancing the radiative QE of NFAs (e.g, by enhancing $J$ aggregations? with higher dipole moment of $\mathrm { N F A } ^ { 5 3 }$ ) can effectively reduce nonradiative recombination loss in small $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ energy offset OSCs systems.1 Third, decreasing the energy difference between the NFA triplet state and CT state or increasing the hybridization of them is worth exploring to enable the back transfer of NFA triplet states to the CT state and thus charge carriers. Because the energy difference between the NFA $\mathrm { S } _ { 1 }$ state and CT state is already small in high-efficiency NFA OSCs, this means the energy difference between $S _ { 1 }$ and T of NFAs should be minimized. Fourth, NFA materials with the characteristics of thermally activated decay fluorescence (TADF) or triplet-triplet upconversion are promising candidates because the NFA triplet excitons can be further reused rather than decay nonradiatively. Recently, we showed the side-chain fluorination can effectively inhibit the formation of the NFA triplet state by increasing the dielectric constant and reducing the electron-hole Coulomb intraction,25 in good agreement with the results shown by Jen et al. that high dielectric constant side-chain polymer donors exhibit reduced nongeminate recombination in OsCs.54 Zhang et al. demonstrated that the suppression of the NFA triplet state in side-chain fluoridated NFA blend films can be attributed to more stable lowest-lying CT/NFA hybrid triplet state.26 These works provide good examples for triplet-state management when designing NFA BHJ materials.

In summary, star NFAs have opened the door to the bright future of OsCs because of not only their breakthrough in efficiencies but also their intriguing excited-state dynamics. In this Perspective, considering time-resolved spectroscopy studies, we have provided our point of view on photoinduced chargetransfer and recombination processes in NFA OSCs for light-toelectricity conversion, including the biphasic hole transfer and its correlation with nanoscale morphology, the role of driving force in interfacial hole-transfer rate and efficiency, and charge recombination energy loss by NFA triplet formation. Despite extensive efforts and progress regarding material development and device fabrication, theoretical and experimental research on excited-state dynamics in NFA-based OSCs is still in its infancy, and many debates and unsolved problems require further investigation.

(1) The ultrafast charge-transfer process at the donor/NFA interface is not fully understood. A few reports show the coherence feature and ultrafast structural dynamics in the blends at the initial charge-transfer process.s,s Further experimental and theoretical research is needed to reveal the mechanism of the femtosecond ultrafast chargetransfer process, e.g., coherent or incoherent process, and the nature of photoexcitation at the interface.

(2) How CT excitons at the interface separate into electron and hole charges contributing to photocurrent is unclear for NFA OSCs. The CT state binding energy is about 300 meV, which is about 1 order of magnitude larger than thermal energy at room temperature ${ \bf \tilde { \Omega } } _ { \mathrm { \Delta } } { \sim } 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m e V } )$ . A few mechanisms have been proposed in FA- and NFA-based BHJs, including charge transfer to hot and delocalized states, polarization-assisted charge separation, and effect of disorder.15,3748,\$7 What is the ultimate origin of charge separation in NFA OSCs? Is there any difference between FA-based OSCs and NFA-based OSCs? Definitive answers with clear results from a buried interface are highly desirable.

(3) In parallel to hole transfer from NFAs, the electron transfer from polymer donors to NFAs is equally important for device operation, which is yet to be illustrated. There, the selective photoexcitation of the donor is difficult to achieve because of the absorption overlap. Therefore, clearly identifying the exciton species and polaron species in donors and NFAs with, for example, a near-infrared probe is extremely important. The spectral overlap between donors and NFAs potentially can also open the resonant energy-transfer channel.

(4) In addition to $J _ { \scriptscriptstyle \mathrm { S C } }$ and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } ,$ it is also important to explore the relationship between fill factor (FF) and charge carrier dynamics in NFA OSCs. The best FF value is ${ \sim } 7 7 \%$ for the state-of-the-art NFA OSCs, which still has a certain distance to cover compared with that of perovskite photovoltaics (over $8 5 \%$ ). Understanding its origin and the limiting factors will undoubtedly help further improvement of OsC performance.

(5) Because the current record PCE comes from multicomponent (e.g., ternary) OSCs, investigating_the underlying excited-state dynamics becomes urgent.7,8,36 Compared with the binary D/A OsCs, the multicomponent OsCs can exhibit complex and synergistic charge- and energy-transfer processes\$45,5 dependingon the energy level alignment and spatial distribution of different components, which deserve combined spectroscopy and morphology research to reveal and control the complex excited-states dynamics.
