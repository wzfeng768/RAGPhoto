# Over 13% Efficient Single-Component Organic Solar Cells Enabled by Adjusting the Conjugated-Length of Intermediate PBDB-T Block

Xinrong Yang, Yao Wu,\* Yuan Gao, Jing Guo, Wei Wang, and Jie Min\*

Batch-to-batch variation widely exists in conjugated donor-acceptor (D-A) block copolymer materials and plays a crucial role in photovoltaic performance of organic solar cells (OsCs). To investigate the influence of conjugated-length of the intermediate block on the performance of singlecomponent OSC (SCOsCs), herein, four batches of conjugated block copolymers (CBCs, PB-b-PY-1, PB- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { b }$ -PY-2, PB- $\mathbf { \nabla } . \mathbf { b }$ -PY-3, and PB-b-PY-4) are synthesized, which possess different D/A block lengths. As the conjugation length of intermediate D-block increases, the lamellar packing order of these CBCs shows a monotonically increasing trend, leading to stronger absorption spectra in the visible region, efficient charge transfer, and suppressed carrier recombination Ioss. Consequently, the PB- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { b }$ -PY-4 device yields a higher efficiency of $1 3 . 2 8 \%$ than those of other CBCs. This study demonstrates the effectiveness of the conjugated length of intermediate D-blocks in designing high-performance conjugated D-A block copolymers, which paves the way toward developing high-performance SCOSCs.

# 1. Introduction

Organic solar cells (OsCs) can work as a promising candidate for clean energy sources and are widely investigated due to the potential they possess for flexible, lightweight, and low-cost semitransparent devices.l Since the introduction of the bulk heterojunction (BHJ) concept, this donor:acceptor (D:A) mixture combination has been extensively studied in OsCs, leading to great breakthroughs in device performance, with power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of over $1 9 \%$ [2] For an optimal BHJ blend, it is necessary to form bi-continuous networks by critical morphology optimization to achieve high photovoltaic performance.3l However, this quasi-equilibrium morphology is usually sensitive to many intrinsic factors (including intermolecular interactions, interfacial surface, and crystallization behaviors, etc.) and extrinsic factors (such as light stress, thermal power, mechanical strength, and so on),(4] generally leading to severe phase separation and/or small-scale isolated domain with aging, hampering photoinduced charge transfer in devices.[1a,5] Compared with the binary or ternary photovoltaic system in which the donor and acceptor materials are in physical contact at the interface, constructing a single-component material in which the donor and acceptor moieties are chemically bonded can be an effective approach to suppress the large-scale de-mixing of polymer domains, molecular orientation misalignment at D/A interfaces, and crystalline polymer structures with unfavorable orientation and order, which might be originated from the abovementioned factors during BHJ device operation.[6]

Consequently, quite a number of single-component materials have been developed, where the D/A moieties are chemically bonded, including dual-functional molecular dyads that bind small molecule donors and acceptors via flexible spacers,7] double-cable conjugated polymers grafting acceptor units as pendants in the polymer donor chain,[8] and conjugated block copolymers (CBCs) connecting the donor blocks (DBs) and acceptor blocks (ABs) in one polymer chain.9l This strategy has been proven to be effective in solving the inherent disadvantages of BHJ microstructure, especially in the terms of storage stability, photostability, and thermal stability.7-9] In addition to preventing significant molecular aggregation or recrystallization, and large-scale phase separation in a singlematerial layer, the single-component materials with suitable intramolecular charge transfer (CT) channels can also effectively weaken the dominant effect of crystallite or phase size on charge transfer.l7a,9] Thus, these unique merits of singlecomponent materials, including good photothermal stability, superior intramolecular CT behaviors, and simplified device fabrication process, have already aroused the interest of many researchers in such photovoltaic materials.[8a] Despite this, the design and development of single-component materials lag far behind the development of donor and acceptor materials used in BHJ structures in the last two decades. This phenomenon is mainly caused by two reasons. On the one hand, some types of single-component materials (like molecular dyads and double-cable conjugated polymers) generally have complex molecular structures and many synthesis steps, which leads to huge difficulties in synthesis. 7a,8a,10] On the other hand, the structural units or moieties in single-component materials are usually limited in molecular design and have poor light-harvesting properties.7a,8a,11] With the development of near-IR nonfullerene acceptor materials,[2a,b,12] it is believed that the device performance of single-component materials will be greatly improved.[13]

To effectively improve the light-harvesting properties ot CBCs as well as their device performance, recently various types of CBCs have been investigated. [9,14] For instance, many suitable ABs, including poly(diketopyrrolopyrrole-terthiophene), [11,15 oly(pyrene bisimide) segments,16] and naphthalene dicarboximide-selenophene (PNDIS) blocks,[14,17] etc.[14b,18] have been introduced to enhance the light-harvesting properties of reported CBC materials. In our previous work, we reported a highly-efficient CBC PBDB-T $b$ PYT containing a wide-bandgap donor (PBDB-T) as a DB and a near-IR (NIR) acceptor (PYT) as an AB by performing the entire reaction in one-pot from start to completion that exhibited a PCE of $1 1 . 3 2 \%$ [9a] which greatly improves the photovoltaic performance of CBC-based OSCs. Despite constant progress being achieved,[9] the photovoltaic performance of SCOsC devices is still far below those of stateof-the-art BHJ OSCs,2a,b,12a-c] mainly resulting from the inferior short-circuit current density $( J _ { \mathsf { S C } } )$ and fll factor (FF) values.[9,14a] It is should be noted that the inferior $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF values in CBC-based devices can be attributed to unfavorable nanoscale morphology and poor charge transport channels. Nevertheless, the abovementioned issues can be improved by adjusting conjugated lengths of DBs and ABs, and optimizing coating conditions and processing techniques. Thus, apart from involving new NIR acceptor blocks into single-component materials, new strategies, including but not limited to the abovementioned strategies, need to be further developed to promote the photovoltaic performance of SCOSCs.

For donor-acceptor linear copolymers, the batch-to-batch variation is presented in the form of differences in molecular weight size and molecular weight distribution, which have been frequently analyzed in polymer acceptors and polymer donors. [19]As for the CBCs for SCOsCs, the batch-to-batch variation is more complicated and has been rarely investigated, because it does not only result in the difference of $M _ { \mathrm { w } } \mathrm { s }$ but also leads to variation in block length and DB/AB distribution. The latter is closely related to the photoelectric properties and aggregation behaviors of CBCs. Based on this view, to systematically investigate the relationship between the polymerization time of DBs and ABs and the molecular weight of the PBDB-T block, we synthesized four batches of PBDB-T-b-PYT, namely PB- $b$ -PY-1, PB-b-PY-2, PB- $b$ PY-3, and PB- $b$ PY-4. Relevant active layer morphology, charge transfer, transport, and recombination dynamics were carefully investigated to explore the effect of conjugated length of intermediate DBs on the photovoltaic performance of SCOsCs. Among these four CBC materials, SCOsCs based on PB- $\boldsymbol { \mathbf { \mathit { b } } }$ PY-4 as an active layer shows the highest PCE of $1 3 . 2 8 \%$ , which is not only higher than those of the other three CBCs but also is among the top values of SCOsCs reported so far. Additionally, the PB-b-PY-4 devices also have better photostability than those of the other investigated devices, further highlighting the significant influence of DB/AB length on device performance.

2. Results and Discussion The synthetic routes of these four CBCs are depicted in Figure 1a. First, the DBs are prepared by polymerization of monomers D1 and A1, which are also the donor and acceptor monomers for commercially available polymer donor PBDBT.20] Through the control of the polymerization reaction time $\left( \mathrm { T } _ { 1 } \right)$ in this step, we were able to select the $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ of the DB we wanted. Then the monomers of ABs (D2 and A2, which are the monomers for the reported polymer acceptor $\mathrm { P Y T } ^ { [ 2 1 ] }$ ) were added for extending the conjugated main chain within a certain period of time $( \mathrm { T } _ { 2 } )$ to form conjugated block copolymers with different conjugated lengths of DBs and ABs. Additionally, the ${ } ^ { 1 } \mathrm { H N M R }$ of the monomer A2 was shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information). Note that the one-pot polymerization time was controlled at 6 h $( \mathrm { T } _ { 1 } + \mathrm { T } _ { 2 } = 6 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ , and four batches of PBDB-T $b$ PYT copolymers, including PB- $b$ PY-1 $[ \mathrm { T } _ { 1 } = 0$ min), PB- $b$ PY-2 $( \mathrm { T } _ { 1 } = 8 0 \ \mathrm { m i n } _ { \it }$ , PB $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { b }$ PY-3 $( \mathrm { T } _ { 1 } = 1 6 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ ), and PB$b$ PY-4 $( \mathrm { T } _ { 1 } = 2 4 0 \ \mathrm { m i n } )$ , were achieved by adjusting the T1 periods. It has to be admitted that the component of the unpurified conjugated block copolymer is quite complex. To make sure the single-component polymer PB- $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { b }$ PY is the principal component in the final CBCs, we purified the crude CBCs by Soxhlet extraction. The monomers, by-product low $M _ { \mathrm { w } }$ polymer donor PBDB-T and PYT blocks, and residual palladium catalyst were removed from the primary CBC materials due to their superior solubilities in methanol, acetone, and dichloromethane solvents. All single-component polymers have good solubility in common solvents such as toluene, chloroform, and di-chlorobenzene. As shown in Figures S2-S5 and Table S1 (Supporting Information), the average molecular weight $( M _ { \mathbb { W } } )$ was obtained by gel permeation chromatography (GPC) with dichlorobenzene as the eluent at $1 4 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ The $M _ { \mathbb { W } } \mathrm { s }$ of PB-b-PY-1, PB- $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { b }$ PY-2, PB- $b$ PY-3, and PB- $\boldsymbol { \mathbf { \mathit { b } } }$ PY-4 were measured as 16.9, 55.3, 50.5, and $3 0 . 3 \mathrm { \ k D a }$ with polydispersity index (PDI) values of 1.74, 2.12, 2.42, and 2.94, respectively. Moreover, the $M _ { \mathbb { W } } \mathrm { s }$ of DBs with polymerization periods of 80, 160, and $2 4 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ were also measured, as shown in Figure 1b. As the polymerization time increases, the proportion of PBDB-T DBs with high molecular weight increases (Table S1, Supporting Information). It indicates that in CBC with a longer polymerization period $\mathrm { T } _ { 1 }$ the PBDB-T block tends to have a longer conjugation length, also supported by the absorption spectra of these DBs in solutions and thin films (Figure S6, Supporting Information). Additionally, the molar ratio of D/A moieties was further determined using the content of nitrogen, obtained from elemental analyses (EA), because the nitrogen only exists in monomer A2. As shown in Table S2 (Supporting Information), the EA results confirmed that the molar ratios of the PBDB-T and PYT repeating units in PB- $\boldsymbol { \mathbf { \mathit { b } } }$ PY-1, PB- $\boldsymbol { \mathbf { \mathit { b } } }$ -PY-2, PB$b$ PY-3, and PB-b-PY-4 were similar and roughly determined to be 1:1.10, 1:1.11, 1:1.28, and 1:1.21, respectively. Systematic analysis of GPC and EA results found that as compared with PB $\mathbf { \nabla } . \ b$ PY-3 and PB-b-PY-4, the shortest $\mathrm { T } _ { 1 }$ of PB-b-PY-2 led to the highest $M _ { w }$ while accompanied by the lowest nitrogen content, indicating its increased molecular weight mainly attributed to the extended DB lengths. This phenomenon is mainly attributed to the decreased chain propagation reactivity when the length of the PBDB-T oligomer gets longer. The limited $M _ { w }$ of ABs is also responsible for this result which was illustrated by the low $M _ { w }$ of the polymerized small molecular acceptors.

The UV-vis absorption spectra of these polymers in dilute chloroform solutions and thin films are depicted in Figure S7 (Supporting Information) and Figure 1c, respectively, with the corresponding parameters, are summarized in Table S3 (Supporting Information). As shown in Figure 1c, all the conjugated block copolymers have broad absorption with two welldefined peaks located ${ \approx } 6 2 0 $ and $7 8 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ . The peaks ${ \approx } 6 2 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ can be attributed to the PBDB-T DBs while the peaks ${ \approx } 7 8 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ are associated with the PYT ABs. Although the molar ratios of $\mathrm { D } / \mathrm { A }$ blocks are similar to the elemental analysis results investigated, the profiles of these copolymers are rather different. As $\mathrm { T } _ { 1 }$ increases, the peaks for DB get sharper and the ratios of intensity for DB and AB $( I _ { \mathrm { D } } / I _ { \mathrm { A } } )$ increase gradually. $I _ { \mathrm { D } } / I _ { \mathrm { A } } < 1$ was observed in the absorption spectra of PB- $\mathbf { \nabla } _ { \cdot } b$ PY-1, PB- $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { b }$ -PY-2, and PB- $\mathbf { \nabla } _ { \cdot } b$ -PY-3, whereas the intensity of DB was stronger than that of AB for PB- $b$ -PY-4. Unlike PBDB-T DB in which the push-pull effect forms after polymerization of D1 and A1, the monomer A2, which shows the A-D-A-D-A structure itself, has a strong push-pull effect and thus has a red-shifted absorption with high extinction coefficient, as a result of which the intensity of absorption has lower dependence on block length in PYT AB as compared to the PBDB-T DB. Therefore, it can be referred that the $I _ { \mathrm { D } } / I _ { \mathrm { A } }$ variation in these copolymers originates from different block lengths in these polymers, and with low $\mathrm { T } _ { 1 } ,$ the block length is much shorter and while the distribution is more random. In addition, according to the onset of absorption curves,[22] the optical bandgaps of these CBCs are calculated at ${ \approx } 1 . 4 2 { - } 1 . 4 3 \ \mathrm { e V }$ (Table S3, Supporting Information). To evaluate the molecular energy levels of these CBCs, cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were used and the corresponding CV curves were provided in Figure S8 (Supporting Information). All these CBCs have similar highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels which were calculated by their onset oxidation and reduction potentials.19b] The LUMO and HOMO energy levels were determined to be $- 3 . 6 9 / - 5 . 5 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for PB-b-PY-1, $- 3 . 6 8 / - 5 . 5 8 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for PB $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { b }$ -PY-2, $- 3 . 7 2 / - 5 . 5 2 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for PB-b-PY-3, and $- 3 . 7 4 / - 5 . 5 1 \mathrm { e V }$ for PB-b-PY-4, respectively.

The photo-induced charge transfer properties in these single-component films were first investigated by steady-state PL measurements. The quenching efficiencies were calculated by comparing the integrated areas of the PL spectra for the CBCs with that of polymer acceptor PYT.[19b] As shown in Figure 4a, the PL quenching efficiencies of PB-b-PY-2, PB$b$ -PY-3, and PB-b-PY-4 was $7 1 . 6 \%$ $8 0 . 9 \%$ , and $8 5 . 4 \%$ , respectively. The increasing quenching efficiency indicated that as the DB conjugation length increases, charge transfer from acceptor block to donor block gets more effective in the film. To further gain insight into the exciton dissociation and charge extraction dynamics in these SCOsC devices, the dependence of the photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { p h } } \right)$ on the effective voltage $( V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } )$ of the relevant devices were measured (Figure 4b), and the corresponding data are calculated and listed in Table S5 (Supporting Information). We also investigated the saturation photocurrent density $\left( J _ { \mathrm { s a t } } \right)$ values at a high effective voltage $V _ { \mathrm { e f f } } = 4 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ ), which are 11.53, 19.76, and $2 1 . 0 9 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ for the PB- $\boldsymbol { \mathbf { \mathit { b } } }$ PY-2, PB- $\mathbf { \nabla } _ { b }$ -PY-3, and PB- $b$ -PY-4 devices, respectively. Notably, the $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ value of the PB$b$ PY-4 device increases in proportion to the voltage at low $V _ { \mathrm { i n } } .$ but gradually saturates at high $V _ { \mathrm { i n } }$ $( { \approx } 0 . 5 ~ \mathrm { V } )$ . In contrast, the $J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ value of the PB-b-PY-3 device saturates at higher $V _ { \mathrm { i n } }$ $( > 1 . 0 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ due to the extraction limited effects. In addition, there is no obvious saturation regime for the PB- $b$ -PY-2 devices, which is consistent with its low $J _ { \mathrm { s c } }$ and FF values. The exciton dissociation and charge collection probabilities of relevant devices could be estimated by the values of $J _ { \mathrm { p h } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ under maximum power point $\left( J _ { \mathrm { M P P } } \right)$ and short circuit $( J _ { \mathsf { S C } } )$ . The $J _ { \mathrm { M P P } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } / J _ { \mathrm { s a t } }$ values were calculated as $3 0 . 7 9 \% / 6 3 . 3 1 \%$ for PB- $b$ -PY-2, $5 7 . 7 9 \% / 8 6 . 3 7 \%$ for PB- $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { b }$ -PY-3, and $7 4 . 4 4 \% / 9 4 . 8 3 \%$ for PB-b-PY-4, respectively, indicating more efficient exciton dissociation and/ or charge extraction properties in the devices based on PB$b$ PY-4 with a higher DB conjugation length in comparison to other CBC materials.

To evaluate the charge transport properties of these CBCs, the space charge limited current (SCLC) method was employed in characterizing the hole-only mobilities and electron-only mobilities (Figure S10, Supporting Information). Generally, the charge mobilities showed an increasing trend as the block length, correlating with the $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF trends in SMOSCs well. As provided in Figure $_ { 4 \mathrm { c } }$ all of the single-component films have balanced mobilities with $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ of $3 . 0 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of $2 . 6 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } \approx 1 . 1 3 )$ for PB $\mathbf { \nabla } _ { \cdot } b$ PY-3, $\mu _ { \mathrm { h } }$ of $4 . 2 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 }$ and $\mu _ { \mathrm { e } }$ of $4 . 2 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } ( { \mu } _ { \mathrm { h } } / { \mu } _ { \mathrm { e } } \approx 1 )$ for PB $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { b }$ PY-4, in exception of PB- $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { b }$ PY-2, which has significantly higher hole mobility $( 3 . 7 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ than electron mobility $( 2 . 2 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { V } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ . This phenomenon might be attributed to high molecular weight of PB- $\mathbf { \nabla } _ { \cdot } b$ -PY-2 than other conjugated block polymers. This unbalanced charge transport $( \mu _ { \mathrm { h } } / \mu _ { \mathrm { e } } \approx 1 . 6 7 )$ of the PB-b-PY-2-based SMOSC might lead to more bimolecular recombination and worse FF values as compared with the other two CBC systems. As depicted in Figure 4d,e, the dependence of both $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ and $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ on light intensity $( P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } )$ was studied to get further insight on the carrier recombination losses in these CBCs based devices. The correlation between light intensity and $V _ { \mathrm { O C } }$ data is fitted to the linear law: $V _ { \mathrm { O C } } \propto$ $\mathrm { n } k T / q \mathrm { l n } P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } ,$ where $T , \ q _ { \ i }$ and $k$ are the temperature in Kelvin, the elementary charge, and the Boltzmann constant, respectively. The PB-b-PY-2 and PB-b-PY-3 devices have slopes approaching $2 \mathrm { k T } \mathrm { q } ^ { - 1 }$ , suggesting that these devices were dominated by trapassisted recombination. The slope of the PB-b-PY-4 device was $1 . 3 9 \mathrm { ~ k T ~ q ^ { - 1 } }$ which is much lower than those of the PB. $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot b$ -PY-2 and PB-b-PY-3 devices. Moreover, the dependence of Jsc on Plight fitted by the power-law $J _ { \mathrm { S C } } \propto P _ { \mathrm { l i g h t } } ^ { \alpha }$ was also plotted in Figure 4e. The $\alpha$ values are 0.935 for PB- $b$ -PY-2, O.961 for PB-b-PY-3, and 0.983 for PB-b-PY-4, respectively, suggesting lower bimolecular recombination loss in the PB- $\mathbf { \nabla } _ { \cdot } b$ -PY-4 devices. These results indicate that as the conjugation length of DB increases, charge carrier recombination loss in the corresponding device decreases significantly, which contributes to higher $J _ { \mathrm { S C } }$ and FF in devices based on CBCs with high $T _ { 1 }$ periods.

# 3. Conclusion

In summary, we synthesized four batches of conjugated block polymers varied with block length via adjusting the polymerization method. Systematic studies on the film morphology, and charge recombination as a function of block length are conducted to understand the influence of block length on device performance. As the donor block length increases, the lamellar stacking gets more ordered and the charge recombination was significantly suppressed. Consequently, SMOsC devices based on PB- $b$ -PY-4 achieved the highest PCE over $1 3 \%$ among these conjugated polymers and exhibited the best operation stability. This work provides a strategy to develop efficient and stable CBCs by mediating the lengths of DB and AB, which is meaningful for understanding the batch-to-batch variation in conjugated donor-acceptor block copolymer.
